Counties hit hardest by the housing bust have fully recovered in dollars, but in real terms only just broken even

Answer

In price terms, the hardest-hit counties have recovered fully in nominal dollars and roughly broken even in real terms. I ranked 2,395 counties by FHFA house-price fall from the 2005-08 peak to the 2008-14 trough. The worst fifth (479 counties) fell a median 30%. By 2025 their median price was 56% above the old peak and 135% above the trough. After CPI adjustment (about +58% since 2006-07) that is about -1% real: back to the peak in purchasing power, no more. The mildest-hit fifth is about 17% above its peak in real terms.

Within the worst quintile, median 2025 level vs peak: NV +52%, FL +54%, AZ +57%, CA +33%, MD +26%, VA +38%, GA +82%, ID +117%. Trough declines were deepest in NV (-53%), FL (-45%), CA (-45%), AZ (-40%). National 90+ day mortgage delinquency peaked at 4.7% (Dec 2009), fell to 0.5% in 2021-22 and was 1.0% in Dec 2025 (CFPB).

Exclusions

Filter hpi_2000_base IS NOT NULL: this excluded counties with no 2000 index value, leaving 2,420 counties. A further 25 were excluded because they lacked a positive peak, trough or 2025 value, leaving 2,395 (479 per quintile). These are small-sample counties FHFA does not publish in some years. I did not rerun the headline including them, since they cannot be scored; the quintile ranking would shift only at the margins.

Caveats

  • Hardest hit is defined by price decline, not foreclosure counts; they overlap but are not identical.
  • County grain, not neighbourhoods; pockets within counties may have recovered less.
  • FHFA measures price recovery only, not income, employment or wealth recovery of displaced owners.
  • Peak (2005-08) and trough (2008-14) windows are my choice, to cover market-specific timing. The real-terms figure uses a CPI ratio of about 1.58 (2006-07 vs 2025 average, CPIAUCNS).

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Neighbourhood grain: County is the finest price grain found; ZIP/tract not available.
