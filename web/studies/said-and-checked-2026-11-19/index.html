Wednesday's political news centered on three threads. House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin testified to a House committee about last week's string of technology outages and about his flight to a college football game the day after the Nov. 13 nationwide ground stop. This page lists 78 statements by 17 officeholders. Thirty-four made a checkable factual claim, or rested a prediction on one, and every one of them was checked. Most of the checks turned on a single official document: the FAA's account of the ground stop, a GAO report on AI platforms, the CMS health spending figures, the Senate's cloture rule. The rest relied on news reports where no primary record was available, and each finding says which applies.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores. Statements are grouped two levels deep, by speaker and then topic or by topic and then speaker, and every group is collapsed. A topic with a single statement is listed under Other. The counts beside a name cover only that person's statements on this page, and are not a rating of the person. Statements that could not be checked are folded under each speaker. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 18 or 19, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, as read on Nov. 19 at about 7:57 ET, less the stories earlier pages already used. No transcript from the fixed transcript sources was dated in that window; transcripts from Nov. 12 to 17 were searched only to confirm quotes, and none matched. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. Quotes under five words were left out, as were commentators' accounts of what an official said and statements by officials outside the offices in scope. Statements are worded as the outlet reported them; entries beginning "Reported as" are the reporter's wording.
