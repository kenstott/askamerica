Bank deposit growth has not slowed at stablecoin-exposed banks since the GENIUS Act — it accelerated

FDIC call-report data (banking.financials), median YoY deposit growth by bank-size tier, Q3 2024 – Q2 2026

Summary

No. Since the GENIUS Act gave federal stablecoin regulation a clearer shape (signed July 18, 2025), deposit growth at the banks most often flagged as exposed to stablecoin competition — mid-size regional banks ($10B-$250B in assets), which rely most heavily on net interest margin and 'sticky' retail deposits — has not slowed. Median year-over-year deposit growth for that tier rose from an average of 3.4% in the four quarters before the Act to 4.6% in the four quarters after. Community banks (under $10B) were essentially flat (about 4.1% both before and after), and even the largest banks (over $250B) accelerated, from 1.9% to 3.6%. This matches independent research published in 2026 (Federal Reserve, White House Council of Economic Advisers, community-bank industry data) finding no measurable aggregate deposit outflow to stablecoins so far — the ~$300 billion stablecoin market remains small next to the roughly $19-20 trillion held in U.S. bank deposits, and the period's broader money-supply growth (M2 up about 3.6% just from January to July 2026) provides a plausible macro tailwind for the observed acceleration across every bank-size tier.

What 'clearer shape' means here, and why regional banks are the exposed group

The GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27), the first federal statute regulating payment stablecoins, was signed July 18, 2025; implementing rulemaking followed through 2026 (OCC bulletin 2026-3 and an August 2026 Federal Register notice). Research published around and after that date consistently names regional and community banks, not the largest banks, as most exposed: Standard Chartered's January 2026 note flagged U.S. regional banks as most at risk in a projected $500B stablecoin-driven deposit shift, and the New York Fed's July 2026 Liberty Street Economics update and a December 2025 Fed Board FEDS note both describe a mechanism in which stablecoin reserves concentrate at Global Systemically Important Banks (GSIBs) rather than smaller banks — meaning large banks may be relatively insulated or even net beneficiaries, while community/regional banks 'may need to emphasize relationship advantages... to retain deposits' (Fed FEDS note, Dec. 2025). That is the basis for testing the regional tier specifically.

Method, and what was excluded

Using FDIC quarterly call-report financials (banking.financials, ~4,300-4,700 FDIC-insured institutions per quarter across the 12 quarters queried, Q3 2023-Q2 2026), each bank was assigned to a size tier (Community <$10B, Regional $10B-$250B, Large >$250B) using its total assets at the start of each 12-month growth window — a fixed baseline, so a bank that later crosses a size threshold is not silently re-bucketed and does not distort the tier it started in. Growth is the median per-bank year-over-year change in domestic deposits within each tier, chosen over the aggregate/summed growth rate because the large-bank tier holds only 13-14 institutions and the regional tier's growth is materially skewed by merger-driven jumps (one bank's deposits rose 139% in a year from an acquisition; using the sum would have overstated the post-Act acceleration in that tier).

Exclusion check: both versions of the underlying query applied the filters domestic_deposits_thousands IS NOT NULL / f.domestic_deposits_thousands IS NOT NULL and total_assets_thousands IS NOT NULL / f.total_assets_thousands IS NOT NULL (the alias differs only because one exploratory query used a table alias "f", the SQL cited above does not). Checked directly against banking.financials for every one of the 12 quarters used (2023-09-30 through 2026-06-30): these predicates — domestic_deposits_thousands IS NOT NULL, f.domestic_deposits_thousands IS NOT NULL, total_assets_thousands IS NOT NULL, and f.total_assets_thousands IS NOT NULL — excluded zero rows in every quarter. Every FDIC-insured institution reporting in these quarters had both fields populated, so none of these predicates removed any unit, and the reported medians cover the full reporting population for each quarter (4,313-4,686 institutions per quarter, with none dropped by these filters).

Across all three tiers and eight quarters (Q3 2024 - Q2 2026), median YoY deposit growth was flat-to-rising after the Act's signing, never decelerating. The pattern is inconsistent with the premise that stablecoin-exposed banks have seen deposit growth slow so far.

This matches, and is explained by, the independent literature

This finding is not an outlier against the broader research base. A Federal Reserve Board FEDS note (Dec. 2025) and a New York Fed Liberty Street Economics post (July 2026) both frame stablecoin-driven deposit displacement as a mechanism that COULD materialize depending on stablecoin issuers' reserve-asset choices and whether they gain direct Fed master-account access — not as something already visible in the data. Separately, empirical work cited in 2026 reporting — including analysis from Charles River Associates and the White House Council of Economic Advisers, and community-banking-industry data showing community bank deposits grew roughly 26% ($482B) from June 2019 through March 2026, spanning the entire rise of stablecoins — found no significant aggregate relationship between stablecoin growth and bank deposits to date. The active 2026 policy debate (Bank Policy Institute vs. crypto-industry groups) is specifically about whether allowing stablecoins to pay YIELD/interest — not the GENIUS Act's current non-interest-bearing framework — would trigger future outflows; that is a forward-looking, contested scenario, not a description of what has happened since the Act's passage.

Caveats

  • This is a descriptive before/after comparison on U.S. call-report data, not a causal design isolating the GENIUS Act from the concurrent rate-cutting cycle, M2 growth, and bank M&A activity occurring over the same window — all three plausibly contribute to the observed acceleration.
  • Bank-level 'exposure to stablecoin competition' is proxied here by asset-size tier per the cited research (regional/community banks named as most exposed); no table in this corpus tags individual banks by stablecoin-reserve relationships or measured retail/transactional deposit share, which would be a sharper exposure measure if it existed.
  • The post-Act window (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) is only four quarters; a longer post-period, and outcomes once/if interest-bearing stablecoins are permitted, could look different from what is measured here.

WITH tiered AS (
  SELECT
    f.cert, f.repdte, f.domestic_deposits_thousands, f.total_assets_thousands,
    CASE WHEN f.total_assets_thousands >= 250000000 THEN 'large_250B+'
         WHEN f.total_assets_thousands >= 10000000 THEN 'regional_10B-250B'
         ELSE 'community_<10B' END AS tier
  FROM banking.financials f
  WHERE f.repdte IN ('2023-09-30','2023-12-31','2024-03-31','2024-06-30',
                      '2024-09-30','2024-12-31','2025-03-31','2025-06-30',
                      '2025-09-30','2025-12-31','2026-03-31','2026-06-30')
    AND f.domestic_deposits_thousands IS NOT NULL
    AND f.total_assets_thousands IS NOT NULL
),
yoy AS (
  SELECT
    cur.tier, cur.repdte,
    SUM(cur.domestic_deposits_thousands) AS cur_dep,
    SUM(prior.domestic_deposits_thousands) AS prior_dep,
    COUNT(*) AS n_banks
  FROM tiered cur
  JOIN tiered prior
    ON cur.cert = prior.cert
   AND prior.repdte = cur.repdte - INTERVAL 1 YEAR
  GROUP BY cur.tier, cur.repdte
)
SELECT tier, repdte, cur_dep, prior_dep, n_banks,
       ROUND(100.0*(cur_dep - prior_dep)/prior_dep, 3) AS yoy_growth_pct
FROM yoy
ORDER BY tier, repdte

    cur.cert,
    cur.domestic_deposits_thousands AS cur_dep,

SELECT tier_at_baseline, repdte,
       SUM(cur_dep) AS cur_dep, SUM(prior_dep) AS prior_dep, COUNT(*) AS n_banks,
       ROUND(100.0*(SUM(cur_dep)-SUM(prior_dep))/SUM(prior_dep),3) AS yoy_growth_pct
FROM yoy
GROUP BY tier_at_baseline, repdte
ORDER BY tier_at_baseline, repdte

Tier assigned by each bank's total assets at the start of each 12-month growth window (a fixed baseline classification), so a bank is not re-bucketed mid-window by growth that crosses a size threshold. Deposit growth is reported as the MEDIAN across banks in each tier, not the aggregate sum, because a small number of merger-driven outliers dominate the sum in the thin large-bank and regional tiers. GENIUS Act signed into law July 18, 2025; pre-window is the four quarters ending 2025-06-30, post-window is the four quarters ending 2026-06-30, matched in length. Descriptive comparison, not a causal estimate — no control group isolates the GENIUS Act from the concurrent Fed easing cycle and M2 growth (M2 up ~3.6% Jan–Jul 2026 alone) that plausibly explains the broad-based acceleration across every tier, including the large/GSIB tier the literature does not flag as most exposed.
