Fewer than 1 in 4 completed trials post results, and sponsor compliance ranges from 0% to over 85%

ClinicalTrials.gov interventional studies via health.clinical_trials (AskAmerica), cross-checked against peer-reviewed FDAAA compliance literature

Summary

Of the 289,629 interventional trials in this snapshot, 47.4% have reached COMPLETED status, and of those completed trials, only 25.4% (34,906 of 137,182) have results actually posted on ClinicalTrials.gov — about 12% of the full registered population. The gap between registering/completing a trial and reporting its results is large and highly sponsor-dependent: among sponsors with at least 150 completed trials, reporting rates range from 0% (Riphah International University, Pakistan) to 85.3% (the VA Office of Research and Development). Industry sponsors as a group report at roughly double the rate of the broad academic/hospital/foreign-institution category (43.1% vs 18.6%), consistent with the peer-reviewed compliance literature on FDAAA 801, though the specific numeric split differs by data source and metric (see caveats).

OTHER is by far the largest bucket (69% of all completed trials) and drags the overall average down — it mixes elite US academic medical centers reporting near 50% with large volumes of trials from institutions such as Cairo University (0.6% of 1,242 completed trials) and Riphah International University in Pakistan (0% of 1,223 completed trials) that register on ClinicalTrials.gov but almost never post results.

How this compares to the published FDAAA compliance literature

This corpus's has_results flag records whether results have EVER been posted, as of today — it is not the same measure as the peer-reviewed literature's headline statistic, which is typically compliance WITHIN the legally required 12-month window after primary completion. Anderson et al. (NEJM, 2015, 10.1056/NEJMsa1409364) found that industry-funded trials were far more likely to report within the mandated window than NIH-funded or other-government/academic trials in the years just after FDAAA took effect. DeVito, Bacon and Goldacre's FDAAA TrialsTracker cohort study (Lancet, 2020, summarized via PubMed 31958402 and The Lancet) found a median 424-day delay from primary completion to results submission (59 days over the legal limit) and reported that non-industry sponsors — universities, hospitals, and government — were considerably more likely to be non-compliant than industry sponsors, a direction that matches this warehouse's industry-vs-OTHER gap (43.1% vs 18.6%) even though the two studies measure different things (ever-posted vs. on-time). A 2025 survey of US academic PRS administrators (Keyes et al., J Clin Transl Sci, PMC12695459) found academic centers have meaningfully increased dedicated compliance staffing and policy since 2016-17 (results-reporting policies up from 35% to 68% of surveyed institutions), which is consistent with this corpus showing large elite US academic centers (Mayo, Stanford, MGH, Johns Hopkins) clustering around 41-55%, well above the 18.6% OTHER-category average that is pulled down by non-US institutions with far less compliance infrastructure.

One search result (a domain called realclearjournals.org/publichealth) surfaced a specific headline figure of 'industry 73.7%, NIH 71.0%, academic 25.5%' for sponsor-level FDAAA compliance. That figure is NOT used here: the domain is unfamiliar, is not an established peer-reviewed or agency outlet, and its precise percentages could not be independently corroborated against a primary source in this session, so it is flagged as unverifiable rather than cited as fact.

Caveats

  • Snapshot restriction: health.clinical_trials is restricted by default to studies with a last-update-post-date on or after 2020-01-01. This slightly favors sponsors with active, well-maintained records and may exclude some old completed trials that were never subsequently updated, which is unlikely to change the direction of the sponsor gaps shown but should not be read as the full historical ClinicalTrials.gov universe.
  • has_results is not a timeliness measure. It only indicates whether results have EVER been posted, not whether they were posted within the FDAAA-mandated 12-month window — the published literature's headline compliance numbers use the latter and are not directly comparable in magnitude to this corpus's figures, only in direction.
  • funder_type OTHER_GOV's 3.7% figure is small-n and heterogeneous (2,984 completed trials, mostly non-US/state government sponsors) and should not be read as describing the US federal government, which is captured separately as FED (62.7%) and NIH (53.8%).
  • All sponsor-level figures above are restricted to sponsors with 150+ completed trials to avoid small-sample noise; smaller sponsors were not examined individually.
