This question cannot be answered at county grain — no public source pairs VA loan concentration, mortgage delinquency, and veteran unemployment there; the national relationship is directionally positive but weak evidence

AskAmerica corpus + BLS CPS + MBA National Delinquency Survey + JCHS research, checked live 2026-09-15

Summary

The question as asked cannot be answered from public data. No source — in AskAmerica's corpus or on the open web — publishes the three things a county-level answer needs: (1) VA-guaranteed loan share of originations by county, (2) mortgage delinquency by county broken out by loan type, or (3) a veteran-specific unemployment rate by county measured with enough precision to trend. Mortgage servicing performance data at fine geography and by loan type (Black Knight McDash, CoreLogic) is proprietary and not publicly released; the one public source that breaks delinquency out by VA vs. FHA vs. conventional — the Mortgage Bankers Association's National Delinquency Survey — publishes only national and state-level rates, never county. Veteran-specific unemployment is a Current Population Survey supplement published by BLS almost exclusively at the national level, because the veteran population is too small a share of most counties' labor force for CPS's county-level sample to support a reliable veteran-only rate.

What can be shown, as a national-level proxy rather than a county answer: veteran unemployment (BLS CPS) and the VA loan delinquency rate (MBA survey) have moved in the same direction in three of the last four years for which comparable data exists (2021–2024), consistent with the general finding in mortgage-default literature that income loss/unemployment is the leading driver of VA loan default. But this is a 4-point national annual series, not a county-level relationship, and it should not be read as evidence about "counties with a large VA loan share" specifically — no data exists to identify those counties' delinquency or veteran-unemployment paths at all.

What was checked in AskAmerica's corpus

search_catalog was run for VA-guaranteed loans, mortgage delinquency, and veteran unemployment by county. Results: housing.hmda_loans carries HMDA aggregates by state and year only (one row per state x action_taken x loan_purpose) — not county grain, and the CFPB aggregations API it is built on does not expose VA vs. conventional loan-type as a breakout dimension at all in this table. census.acs_veterans gives veteran population counts by county (no employment/unemployment status). econ.employment_statistics carries a genuine veteran-specific unemployment series (BLS CPS series LNU04049526 and related codes) — but only at the national level, monthly, not seasonally adjusted, 2020–2026. The catalog search's own unmatched-terms flag confirmed "delinquency" appears in no table, column, or description anywhere in the corpus — there is no mortgage-performance/delinquency table of any kind, at any grain, for any loan type.

What exists publicly outside the corpus, and why it still doesn't close the gap

  • VA loan geographic concentration: a Joint Center for Housing Studies working paper (Spitzer & Lambie-Hanson, presented at the 2019 JCHS/Fannie Mae symposium) used county-level HMDA microdata to show VA loan origination share is driven strongly by proximity to a military installation, and that veteran-dense but high-cost coastal counties are systematically under-served relative to their veteran population. This establishes that a "high VA-loan-share county" list is buildable in principle from HMDA loan-level microdata (which carries loan_type=3 for VA loans and county via census tract) — but that requires processing raw HMDA LAR files, which AskAmerica's hmda_loans table does not carry, and even then gives loan origination share, not the county's delinquency outcome.
  • VA loan delinquency by loan type: published only by MBA's National Delinquency Survey, and only at national/state grain, quarterly. In Q1 2026 the VA delinquency rate was 4.99% vs. 2.75% for conventional and 11.88% for FHA — VA loans run meaningfully higher than conventional but well below FHA. VA loan delinquency rose from 3.97% to 4.18% in one recent month-over-month reading and 4.50% (Q3 2025) to 4.60% (Q4 2025, +10bp). None of this is broken out by county anywhere public.
  • Veteran unemployment by county: the ACS 5-year table B21005/S2101 ("Veteran Status") does publish an estimated employment status by veteran status at county grain, including small counties — but with margins of error that are frequently as large as the estimate itself for a county where veterans are, e.g., 5% of a 20,000-person labor force. BLS's own CPS veteran-employment supplement, the methodologically preferred source and the one used above, is explicitly national-only.

The Pearson correlation across these four annual pairs is r = 0.90 — but with n=4, this is descriptive only and carries no statistical power; it is reported to illustrate direction, not to claim a tested relationship. 2020 is excluded from the correlation because MBA's COVID-era "delinquency" figures were distorted by pandemic forbearance accounting in a way that makes them not comparable to later years (VA loans reached a 90+ day "seriously delinquent" rate of 5.96% in Q4 2020, a different, narrower metric than the total delinquency rate used above).

This directional co-movement is consistent with the general finding in the mortgage-default literature (see, e.g., research summarized around VA loan performance during the Global Financial Crisis) that loss of income — including unemployment — is the leading reason cited for VA loan default and forbearance. It does not establish, and cannot establish from data available here, how tightly the relationship holds specifically within counties where VA loans make up an unusually large share of the mortgage market, because no source ties VA-loan-concentration, delinquency, and veteran unemployment together at any geography finer than the nation as a whole.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Identify counties with a large share of VA-guaranteed home loans: No AskAmerica table or public source publishes VA loan share of originations at county grain on a routine basis; hmda_loans is state-grain only and carries no loan-type (VA/FHA/conventional) breakout. A county list is in principle buildable from raw HMDA LAR microdata (loan_type=3), which is outside this session's data access.
  • Measure mortgage delinquency at the county level (ideally by loan type): No public source publishes mortgage delinquency broken out by loan type at county grain. MBA's National Delinquency Survey, the only public VA-specific delinquency source, stops at national/state grain. Fine-geography delinquency-by-loan-type data (Black Knight McDash, CoreLogic) is proprietary.
  • Measure the local (county-level) veteran unemployment rate: BLS's veteran-specific unemployment rate (CPS supplement) is published essentially at the national level; ACS table S2101/B21005 gives a county-grain veteran employment-status estimate but with margins of error too large to trend reliably in most counties, and was not pulled for this answer given the delinquency variable's absence makes it moot.
