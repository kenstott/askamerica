Nearly 3.4 million U.S. schoolchildren attend school within a quarter mile of a known environmental hazard site, and those schools are disproportionately urban, poorer, and serve more Black, Hispanic, and Native students

PNAS 2026 national study plus an AskAmerica replication restricted to EPA Final Superfund (NPL) sites, NCES CCD 2024

Summary

The best available national estimate comes from a 2026 PNAS study (Malik, Kraft & Falken) that geolocated every U.S. pre-K-12 school against every EPA-tracked environmental hazard site (Superfund sites, brownfields, and Toxics Release Inventory facilities): nearly 10,000 schools (8% of all US schools), enrolling 3.36 million students and employing 480,000 teachers and staff, sit within a quarter mile of a hazardous site — the distance at which exposure risk is most acute. A much larger share, about 44% of all pre-K-12 schools, are within one mile, a distance at which health and academic-achievement effects have also been documented in the literature. These schools are not a random cross-section of American communities: the study found Native, Black, Hispanic, and low-income students are 124%, 86%, 43%, and 40% more likely, respectively, to attend a school within a quarter mile of a hazard site than other students.

To add an independently computed, checkable figure rather than only relaying the published one, AskAmerica ran its own geolocation match between EPA's Superfund database and the NCES Common Core of Data school directory, restricted to the ~1,337 sites carrying EPA's most serious designation (Final National Priorities List). This is a narrower and non-overlapping cut of the same underlying phenomenon — it excludes brownfields and TRI facilities, which make up the bulk of the PNAS total — and found 1,848 schools enrolling 911,332 students within one mile of a Final NPL Superfund site, and 71 schools enrolling 27,616 students within a quarter mile. Consistent with the national study's community-type finding, schools near a Superfund NPL site are far more urban (80% in city/suburb locale codes vs. 59% nationally) and somewhat poorer (59% free/reduced-price lunch vs. 56% nationally) than U.S. schools as a whole.

What the published literature found

The primary source is Malik, Kraft & Falken, "US schools' proximity to environmental hazard sites: A national analysis," PNAS (2026), also circulated as an EdWorkingPaper (ai26-1384). The study is a nationwide geospatial match of every U.S. pre-K-12 public and private school against three EPA hazard-site inventories: Superfund sites, brownfields, and Toxics Release Inventory (TRI) facilities. Headline figures, as reported in coverage of the study (Futurity, The 74, Education Week, Medical Xpress):

  • ~10,000 schools (8% of all US pre-K-12 schools) are within a quarter mile of a hazard site, enrolling 3.36 million students and employing 480,000 teachers/staff.
  • ~44% of all pre-K-12 schools are within one mile of a hazard site — a distance at which negative health/academic effects have been documented in prior exposure research.
  • Native, Black, Hispanic, and low-income students are 124%, 86%, 43%, and 40% more likely (respectively) than other students to attend a school within a quarter mile of a hazard site.
  • Documented health/academic consequences associated with these hazard sites include elevated cancer risk, immune and cardiovascular effects, birth defects, poorer academic performance, and higher special-education needs.

A related, narrower study — Oliva & Som, "National-Scale Assessment of Environmental Justice Trends in Public School Proximity to Industrial Lead Sources" (2023) — found that schools within 1.5 km of industrial lead facilities have significantly higher Black (22% vs. 17%) and Hispanic (30% vs. 26%) enrollment shares, and a lower White enrollment share (37% vs. 47%), than other schools in the same statistical area, corroborating the race/ethnicity disparity direction found in the national PNAS study.

What AskAmerica computed directly (Superfund-only replication)

To extend the literature with an independently reproducible figure, AskAmerica joined EPA's Superfund site inventory (environment.superfund_sites, filtered to npl_status_code='F', EPA's Final National Priorities List — the most severe designation, ~1,337 sites nationally with usable coordinates) to the NCES Common Core of Data school directory (edu.ccd_schools, school year 2024, the latest complete year in this warehouse) using a great-circle (haversine) distance calculation between each school's and each site's latitude/longitude.

This is a deliberately narrower cut than the PNAS study: it counts only the most severe class of Superfund site and excludes brownfields and TRI facilities, which the PNAS study shows make up the large majority of the 10,000-school/3.36M-student total. The two figures are not meant to reconcile to the same number — they measure overlapping but different hazard universes. AskAmerica's Superfund-only results:

  • Within 1/4 mile: 71 schools, 27,616 students enrolled.
  • Within 1 mile: 1,848 schools, 911,332 students enrolled.

Community-type comparison for the 1-mile Superfund-NPL group vs. all US schools nationally (both computed from the same 2024 CCD snapshot, sentinel/missing codes excluded per NCES's own coding convention):

  • Poverty proxy (free/reduced-price lunch rate): 59.3% near a Superfund NPL site (1 mi) vs. 56.2% nationally; 56.9% within a quarter mile.
  • Urban locale share: 80.1% of schools near a Superfund NPL site (1 mi) are in city/suburb locale codes vs. 58.9% of all US schools nationally.
  • Charter share: 11.3% of schools near a Superfund NPL site (1 mi) are charters vs. 8.1% nationally.

These directional findings — more urban, somewhat higher-poverty communities cluster around the most contaminated sites — are consistent with, and add a second independently-computed data point supporting, the PNAS study's disparity findings, even though this analysis cannot itself measure race/ethnicity (NCES CCD's school directory table used here does not carry a race/ethnicity breakdown; that would require a further join to CRDC or ACS block-group data, which was out of scope for this replication).

Caveats and what this does not show

  • AskAmerica's own computed figures (71/1,848 schools) are a Superfund-NPL-only subset and substantially smaller than the PNAS total (10,000 schools) because they exclude brownfields and TRI facilities, which the PNAS study indicates account for most of the exposure count.
  • Straight-line (haversine) distance was used, not walking/travel distance or site-boundary distance to the actual contamination footprint; this is the same simplification most proximity studies in this literature use, but it is an approximation.
  • The NCES CCD table used here (edu.ccd_schools) does not carry student race/ethnicity; the race/ethnicity disparities reported above are drawn from the PNAS and Oliva & Som studies directly, not recomputed by AskAmerica.
  • "Near a hazard site" is a proximity measure, not a direct exposure or health-outcome measurement; the health and achievement effects cited are the published literature's findings, not something computed here.

    Show SQL
    WITH sf AS (SELECT site_id, latitude AS flat, longitude AS flon FROM environment.superfund_sites WHERE npl_status_code='F' AND latitude IS NOT NULL AND longitude IS NOT NULL), s AS (SELECT ncessch, latitude AS slat, longitude AS slon, enrollment, free_or_reduced_price_lunch AS frpl, urban_centric_locale, charter FROM edu.ccd_schools WHERE "year"=2024 AND latitude IS NOT NULL AND longitude IS NOT NULL AND enrollment>=0) SELECT ncessch, MIN(3958.8*2*ASIN(SQRT(POWER(SIN(RADIANS(sf.flat-s.slat)/2),2)+COS(RADIANS(s.slat))*COS(RADIANS(sf.flat))*POWER(SIN(RADIANS(sf.flon-s.slon)/2),2)))) AS min_dist_mi FROM s JOIN sf ON TRUE GROUP BY ncessch HAVING min_dist_mi<=1
  9. National FRPL rate and urban locale share, all US schools 2024, AskAmerica computed
    Show SQL
    SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN free_or_reduced_price_lunch>=0 THEN free_or_reduced_price_lunch ELSE 0 END) AS frpl_sum, SUM(CASE WHEN free_or_reduced_price_lunch>=0 THEN enrollment ELSE 0 END) AS enr_sum, AVG(CASE WHEN urban_centric_locale IN (11,12,13,21,22,23) THEN 1.0 ELSE 0.0 END) AS urban_share FROM edu.ccd_schools WHERE "year"=2024 AND enrollment>=0

AskAmerica's own computed figures are restricted to EPA Final NPL Superfund sites only (excluding brownfields/TRI) and school year 2024 (NCES CCD); they are a narrower, non-overlapping complement to the PNAS national total, not a replication of it.
