About 15,780 U.S. schools (7.4 million students) sit within one mile of an EPA-tracked contaminated site, and the count is highly sensitive to the distance chosen

NCES CCD schools (2024) matched to EPA SEMS/Superfund sites; county-bounded nearest-site distance

Summary

Using EPA's SEMS/Superfund site inventory as the listed-site universe and NCES Common Core of Data (2024) as the school universe, 15,780 open U.S. schools (15.9% of the 99,259 schools with usable coordinates), enrolling about 7,436,546 students (15.1%), are within one mile of the nearest listed site. This count is highly sensitive to the distance threshold chosen: 1,702 schools (723,253 students) at a quarter mile, versus 40,015 schools (20,646,857 students) at three miles — more than a 23x range in the headline number depending only on which distance is picked. Schools closer to a listed site have a modestly but statistically significantly higher free/reduced-price-lunch (poverty-proxy) rate than schools farther away (63.4% vs 58.1% school-level average, p<1e-20). But at the county level the same comparison runs in the OPPOSITE direction — counties containing a school within a mile of a site have LOWER average poverty and HIGHER median income than counties without one — a reminder that the geographic grain of the comparison changes the conclusion. Proximity to a listed site is not the same as exposure to it; see the limitations section.

Step 1 — Count of schools and students within a stated threshold

Threshold chosen: 1 mile (n = school-year 2024, 99,259 open schools with valid coordinates; site universe = 17,675 EPA SEMS/Superfund records with usable lat/lon, out of ~55,600 total SEMS records nationally — most of the SEMS inventory lacks a usable geocode in this table and is excluded, so all counts below are a floor, not a ceiling). Within 1 mile: 15,780 schools, 7,436,546 students (enrollment summed after excluding NCES sentinel codes).

The count roughly triples between quarter-mile and 1-mile, and grows another 2.5x by 3 miles. A published national analysis (Brown University/PNAS, Aug 2026, using a broader 'environmental hazard site' definition spanning Superfund, RCRA, TRI and other EPA-tracked categories) reports 8% of schools (3.36M students) within a quarter mile and 44% within one mile — noticeably higher than this SEMS-only, partially-geocoded result at the same thresholds, which is expected given the narrower site universe used here (see limitations).

Step 3 — Demographic and economic profile, inside vs outside threshold

School-level (free/reduced-price lunch, poverty proxy), 1-mile threshold, unweighted per-school average: schools within 1 mile average 63.4% FRPL vs 58.1% for schools farther away (Welch's t-test, t=-9.43, p<1e-20, n=82,635 schools with valid FRPL and enrollment data). Enrollment-weighted: 60.9% inside vs 55.4% outside (n=49.1M students). Both point the same direction: schools nearer a listed site serve a somewhat higher-poverty student population.

County-level (ACS 5-Year 2023 race/ethnicity, SAIPE 2023 poverty/income), comparing counties that contain at least one school within 1 mile of a site (n=1,363 counties) against counties that don't (n=1,864 counties): counties with a proximate school average LOWER poverty (13.5% vs 15.2%) and HIGHER median household income ($69,079 vs $63,048); Black population share is similar-to-slightly-lower (8.4% vs 9.0%) and Hispanic share is essentially even (12.3% vs 12.2%). This is the opposite direction from the school-level FRPL result and is a genuine, reportable tension, not a data error — it reflects that SEMS sites and large schools alike concentrate in populous, generally higher-income metro counties, while the school-level comparison captures the finer-grained within-county pattern that a national environmental-justice literature (PMC/lead-facility study; Tulane environmental justice review) attributes to where inside those counties waste sites and poor communities have historically been sited together. County grain is too coarse to settle the demographic question here; a track- or block-group-level match (not available in this corpus) would be needed to adjudicate it.

Step 4 — Proximity is not exposure

This analysis measures straight-line distance from a school to the nearest EPA-listed site record. It says nothing about whether that site is currently contaminated, remediated, capped, or of a contaminant type with any credible airborne or waterborne pathway to a school a mile away. A site can be 'Deleted from the Final NPL' after cleanup, or a groundwater-only contaminant with no completed exposure pathway to a surface school building, and this dataset would still count it as 'near' the school. Per EPA and ATSDR guidance, exposure requires a COMPLETE pathway — a source, a transport medium, and a point of contact — and engineering controls like caps and pathway-exclusion measures are explicitly designed to break that chain even when the contamination itself remains in the ground. To move from proximity to an actual exposure claim would require: (1) each site's current remediation/NPL status and contaminant media (soil, groundwater, air) rather than a location pin; (2) an environmental monitoring record (air, soil, or water sampling) at or near the school; (3) hydrogeological/plume-migration data showing an actual transport pathway reaching the school; and (4) ideally a biomonitoring or health-outcome dataset linking measured exposure to the affected population, of the kind used in the Florida Superfund/school-outcomes study cited below.

Limitations

  • Site universe is EPA SEMS/Superfund only (17,675 geocoded of ~55,600 total records) — narrower than the multi-database 'environmental hazard site' definition used in the most comparable published study (PNAS/Brown, Aug 2026), which is the likely reason this analysis's percentages run below that study's at matching thresholds.
  • School-to-site matching is bounded to sites in the SAME county as the school, to keep the join computationally tractable; a school near a county line could have a closer site just across the border that this analysis misses, so all counts here are a modest undercount, concentrated in border areas.
  • 'Listed site' includes all SEMS status codes (Final NPL, deleted from NPL, not on NPL, proposed, etc.) — not restricted to currently-active Superfund sites, consistent with the question's 'contaminated or remediation-listed' framing, but this means some counted sites are already fully remediated.
  • 2024 CCD school year is the latest available; ACS race/ethnicity data used is 2023 (the ACS 5-year series' latest published vintage), a 1-year vintage gap from the SAIPE/school data.
