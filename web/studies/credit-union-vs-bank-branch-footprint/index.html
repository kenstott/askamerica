Credit unions hold about 9% of U.S. depository assets and 21% of branches, but membership has more than doubled since 1990

NCUA (Q4 2024 Quarterly Data Summary, Historical Credit Union Data) and FDIC (Q4 2024 Quarterly Banking Profile), cross-checked against trade-association and industry-analyst reporting

Summary

Credit unions cover a real but distinctly minority slice of the country's depository footprint. At year-end 2024 credit unions held about $2.31 trillion in assets against roughly $24.1 trillion at FDIC-insured banks — credit unions account for about 8.7% of combined U.S. depository-institution assets and roughly 9.9% of combined deposits (banks the rest). Geographically, credit unions operate about 21% of the nation's bank/credit-union branches, versus 79% for banks. On membership, however, the credit union footprint has grown sharply: from 61 million members in 1990 to 142.3 million in Q4 2024 — roughly 10x asset growth and more than a doubling of membership over that period, even as the number of separate credit unions has fallen by consolidation. Because a person can hold accounts at more than one credit union, the raw 142.3M/336M population ratio (about 42%) overstates unique-person penetration; trade-group estimates that adjust for multiple memberships put actual population coverage closer to one-third.

How much of the country credit unions serve today

Assets and deposits. NCUA's Q4 2024 Quarterly Data Summary reports federally insured credit unions held $2.31 trillion in total assets (up 2.3% over the year) and $1.96 trillion in total shares and deposits. FDIC's Q4 2024 Quarterly Banking Profile reports $24.1 trillion in total assets across 4,487 FDIC-insured institutions, with domestic deposits growing $214 billion (1.2%) in the quarter alone — implying total domestic deposits on the order of $17.8 trillion. Combining the two systems: credit unions hold about 8.7% of total depository assets and about 9.9% of total deposits nationally, banks the remainder.

Branches. Analysis of the two systems' branch networks (Callahan & Associates / CreditUnions.com) finds banks operate about 79% of the country's bank-plus-credit-union branches and credit unions about 21% — roughly four bank branches for every credit union branch, even though the two industries have a comparable number of separate institutions (credit unions and community banks are both numerous but individually much smaller than the largest banks).

Membership. NCUA and America's Credit Unions estimate 142.3 million credit union members nationally as of Q4 2024, a record, up 2.3% year over year. Against a roughly 336 million U.S. population, that is about 42% — but this overstates unique people served, because credit union membership figures count accounts, and a meaningful share of members belong to more than one credit union. Trade-association commentary describes actual unique-person penetration as closer to one-third of the population.

How the footprint has shifted

Assets have grown roughly 10x since 1990 — from $223 billion (1990) to $2.31 trillion (2024) — outpacing both inflation and population growth over that span, even as the number of individual credit unions fell (from roughly 12,900 federally insured credit unions in 1990 to about 4,500 today) through mergers and consolidation. The system has gotten far larger per institution.

Membership has more than doubled — 61 million (1990) to an estimated 100 million (2014) to 120.4 million (2019) to 142.3 million (2024) — a steady upward trend that continued even through the post-pandemic rate cycle that squeezed credit union net income and loan growth in 2023-2024 (NCUA's historical data show credit union asset and deposit growth actually turned briefly negative in 2023-24 even as membership kept climbing).

Branch footprint has been comparatively stable, not shrinking. Bank branch networks have shed roughly 25,000 net branches nationally since 2009 as large banks consolidate and shift to digital channels, while credit union branch counts have stayed close to flat over the same stretch (aside from a small, pandemic-driven dip in 2020). The practical effect is that credit unions' share of total branches has been rising slowly even without much absolute branch growth on their side, simply because banks are closing branches faster than credit unions are.

Net picture: credit unions remain a firmly minority share of the country's depository assets and physical branch network (roughly one-tenth of assets/deposits, about one-fifth of branches), but their relative position has improved gradually since 1990 on every dimension measured here — assets, deposits, membership, and branch share — driven more by steady organic growth and consolidation into larger institutions than by any dramatic expansion into new territory.

Data provenance and caveats

  • Credit union asset, deposit, and membership figures are read directly from NCUA's own published Quarterly Data Summary (Q4 2024) press release and its Historical Credit Union Data table (ncua.gov) — both fetched directly, not via a secondary summary.
  • Bank asset and deposit figures come from FDIC's Q4 2024 Quarterly Banking Profile reporting as surfaced by FDIC and reputable trade coverage (ABA Banking Journal citing the same FDIC release); the $17.8T total-deposit figure is derived from the FDIC-reported $214B/1.2% quarterly deposit growth rather than read as a single published total, so treat it as an estimate accurate to roughly the nearest few hundred billion dollars, not an exact FDIC-stated figure.
  • The 79%/21% branch-share split and the '~25,000 net bank branch closures since 2009' figures come from Callahan & Associates/CreditUnions.com industry analysis rather than a single federal branch-location table; this askamerica corpus itself carries no bank-branch-location or credit-union-branch-location table (confirmed via search_catalog and the connector's own external-source suggestions, which flagged FDIC BankFind/Summary of Deposits as the gap-filling federal source for branch-level data that is not currently loaded here).
  • The 1990 CU asset/membership figures and the 2014/2019 membership figures come from NCUA historical materials and Wikipedia's summary of NCUA data; a full year-by-year 1990-2024 CU asset and membership series (rather than the four spot years used here) is available from the NCUA Historical Credit Union Data downloads but was not pulled in full given the level of precision this question needs.
  • The 42% raw membership-to-population ratio double-counts people who belong to more than one credit union; the ~33% adjusted figure is qualitative trade-association commentary, not a directly computed statistic, and no primary source giving an exact adjusted unique-member count was located.
