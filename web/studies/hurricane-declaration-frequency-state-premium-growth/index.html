Hurricane disaster declarations in Atlantic and Gulf coastal states have risen since the 1970s, but the corpus has no state premium series to test whether premiums track them

Summary

Part 1: yes, with caveats. Major-disaster (DR) hurricane declarations in 18 Atlantic/Gulf states: 8 in the 1970s, 16 in the 1980s, 37 in the 1990s, 44 in the 2000s, 50 in the 2010s, 38 in 2020-2025. The linear trend 1980-2024 is +0.13 declarations per year (R2 = 0.16, n = 45), so it is noisy and driven by spikes (1999: 13, 2004: 12, 2021: 16). Declaration counts also reflect policy and political practice, not only storm frequency.

Part 2: could not be tested. The warehouse has no homeowners-premium-by-state series, so I computed no correlation. From the sources read: Treasury's FIO report (2018-2022) finds premiums per policy positively correlated with expected-loss risk categories, with hurricanes the largest share (61.4%) of building value at risk, but it does not report a state-level link between declaration counts and premium growth. Premium growth also reflects reinsurance costs, litigation (Florida reforms 2021-23), and insurer exits. National average premiums rose 10.5% (NAIC) or 11.2% (III) in 2022 alone. Declaration counts are lumpy, so a clean match is unlikely.

Method

Table disasters.disaster_declarations; incident_type='Hurricane', declaration_type='DR'; distinct disaster_number x state; ME, NH, MA, RI, CT, NY, NJ, DE, MD, VA, NC, SC, GA, FL, AL, MS, LA, TX. Excludes Typhoon, Tropical Storm, Coastal Storm types and territories (including those would raise counts). 2025 may be partial. Decades computed from the annual query.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Whether homeowners premium growth tracks that frequency: No state-level homeowners premium series in the warehouse (searched catalog); NAIC PDF returned 403; only Treasury FIO report text and national NAIC figures were read. No correlation computed.
