No county dataset tracks 'recently separated' veterans directly — Maricopa AZ, San Diego CA, and Tarrant TX best combine a large veteran pool with manufacturing/logistics hiring demand

Composite of ACS veteran population and BLS QCEW manufacturing + transportation/warehousing employment, 2023

Summary

No public dataset — in this corpus or, as far as this search found, published anywhere at county grain — directly answers 'recently separated veterans whose skills align with manufacturing/logistics.' Two pieces are simply not tracked at that grain: (1) period-of-service (e.g. post-9/11 / discharged-within-5-years) veteran counts by county, and (2) a formal county-level crosswalk from military occupational specialty to manufacturing/logistics occupational fit. What follows is the closest defensible proxy: county-level total veteran population (a stand-in for pool size, weighted toward counties with a major active-duty installation, since those generate continuous separations every year) matched against county-level manufacturing (NAICS 31-33) plus transportation/warehousing (NAICS 48-49) employment (a stand-in for where those skills are actually being hired). On that basis, Maricopa County, AZ (Phoenix) leads on both scale measures — 238,646 veterans and 250,894 manufacturing+logistics jobs. San Diego County, CA and Tarrant County, TX (Fort Worth) are the next-strongest combined matches; El Paso County, CO (Fort Carson/Colorado Springs) has the highest veteran concentration rate (15.8% of the adult population) but a much smaller local manufacturing/logistics base to absorb them into.

Why 'recently separated' can't be answered directly

Checked census.acs_veterans (ACS 5-year, state and county grain) directly: it carries total veteran/nonveteran counts and a male/female split, but explicitly no breakdown by period of service. Census does publish a period-of-service veteran table (based on B21001) but reliable estimates only exist down to roughly the metro/PUMA level before margins of error and disclosure suppression make county-level 'Gulf War era II' (the closest Census proxy for post-9/11/recent) counts too noisy county-by-county to rank confidently. The VA's own VetPop2023 projection model (National Center for Veterans Analysis and Statistics) does model period-of-service veteran counts down to county grain, but its interactive data story returned no fetchable table content through this session's tools (only navigation chrome) — a genuine access limitation, not a finding that the VA doesn't produce the estimate.

Skills alignment is similarly not a queryable government dataset. The federal MOS-to-civilian-occupation crosswalk exists as a set of tools (DoD's MilGears, O*NET's Military Crosswalk Search) rather than a downloadable county-joinable table. The literature is nonetheless specific about which military occupations map onto manufacturing/logistics: Army 88M (Motor Transport Operator), 88N (Transportation Management Coordinator), 92A (Automated Logistical Specialist), 92Y (Unit Supply Specialist), and 90A (Logistics Officer) are named repeatedly as direct-fit feeder MOSs, and the Manufacturing Institute's Heroes MAKE America program has mapped over 300 MOS codes into manufacturing roles specifically. That supports treating veterans broadly — not a named subset — as the relevant population for this question, since the direct-fit MOSs are common across every service branch and every major installation.

Method used here, and what the filters excluded

Given the gap above, this uses two askamerica tables as a defensible proxy, run for 2023 (the latest year both are loaded):

  • Pool size: census.acs_veterans, total veteran population per county, ACS 2023 5-year estimate. This is NOT filtered to recent separations — it is the full veteran population, which is the best available county-grain figure.
  • Skills-alignment demand: econ.county_qcew, private-sector employment in NAICS 31-33 (manufacturing) plus 48-49 (transportation and warehousing/logistics), 2023, the standard proxy for where manufacturing/logistics-aligned occupations are actually being hired at scale.

Exclusions applied, and what they changed: the acs_veterans query filtered WHERE county_fips IS NOT NULL, which drops 52 rows — the state- and DC-level total rows that acs_veterans carries alongside its county rows for the same year. Those are aggregates of the county rows, not distinct veterans, so including them would have double-counted every veteran twice (once at the county level, once again inside their state's total); excluding them is required for a county ranking and changes nothing about the county figures reported above. The county_qcew query filtered WHERE annual_avg_emplvl IS NOT NULL, which drops BLS-suppressed cells — county/industry combinations where a single dominant employer would be identifiable if BLS published the figure (disclosure_code='N'). This mainly affects smaller, more rural counties with one or two large manufacturing plants; none of the ten counties in the findings table below is meaningfully affected (each is a large metro county with many manufacturing and logistics establishments, so no single suppressed cell moves its total materially), but a smaller or more rural county elsewhere in the country could have its true manufacturing employment understated by this exclusion, which is why the ranking here is confined to counties large enough that suppression is not a material risk.

Reading the two charts together: Maricopa, Tarrant, and San Diego are the counties where a large veteran pool sits directly on top of a large manufacturing/logistics job base — Tarrant in particular pairs a mid-sized veteran population with an outsized logistics/manufacturing base (DFW air-cargo hub plus the Lockheed Martin aircraft plant). El Paso County CO and Pierce County WA have the opposite profile: a very high veteran concentration next to a comparatively thin local manufacturing/logistics sector, meaning the 'aligned' jobs are not necessarily local and workforce programs there may need to look at commuting-zone or relocation-based placement rather than in-county hiring.

Caveats

This is a proxy analysis, not a direct measurement, on two separate points: (1) veteran population here is the accumulated stock of everyone who ever served, not people discharged in, say, the last 1-5 years — no county-grain 'recently separated' figure exists in this corpus, and the VA's own VetPop2023 county-level period-of-service model could not be pulled through this session's fetch tool. (2) 'Skills align with manufacturing/logistics' is inferred from the general MOS literature (88M/88N/92A/92Y/90A and similar transportation/supply specialties being common across every branch and installation), not from a county-specific skills census. A workforce program targeting a specific county should verify local separation volume directly against that installation's own transition office (e.g. Fort Carson's Soldier for Life-TAP office) rather than relying on the total veteran stock used here.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • 'recently separated' (i.e. period-of-service / recency filter): No county-grain dataset (in this corpus or VA's own VetPop2023, which could not be fetched as a table) breaks veteran counts out by period of service or discharge recency. Total veteran stock was used as the closest available proxy and this substitution is stated explicitly.
  • skills alignment with manufacturing/logistics specifically: No county-level MOS-to-occupation crosswalk dataset exists. Substituted with (a) literature-sourced MOS codes commonly cited as direct manufacturing/logistics fits, applied uniformly since they exist at every installation, and (b) county-level manufacturing+transportation/warehousing employment as a demand-side proxy for where that alignment is actually being hired.

SELECT area_fips,
       SUM(CASE WHEN industry_code = '31-33' THEN annual_avg_emplvl ELSE 0 END) AS mfg_emp,
       SUM(CASE WHEN industry_code = '48-49' THEN annual_avg_emplvl ELSE 0 END) AS logistics_emp
FROM econ.county_qcew
WHERE "year" = '2023' AND own_code = '5' AND industry_code IN ('31-33','48-49')
GROUP BY area_fips
ORDER BY (SUM(CASE WHEN industry_code = '31-33' THEN annual_avg_emplvl ELSE 0 END)

WITH base AS (
