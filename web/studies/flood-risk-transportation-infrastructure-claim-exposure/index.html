No national count exists in the warehouse for bridges or roads in flood zones; the best available figure is First Street's 2021 estimate that about 23% of US road miles (about 2 million) are at flood risk, rising over 30 years

Warehouse: FHWA NBI (transport.bridges, 2015/2020/2025, record_type=1). External: First Street Foundation 2021 report via press coverage.

Summary

I could not find a figure for bridges in FEMA flood zones in the warehouse or in anything I could open. The warehouse has no flood-zone layer for bridges or roads. The nearest published figure is First Street Foundation's 2021 assessment, reported secondhand in press coverage: about 23% of US road miles (roughly 2 million of 8.4 million) are at risk of becoming impassable in a flood. It is projected to reach about 26% (2.2 million miles) over 30 years, and 25% of critical facilities are at risk. Bridges were not broken out separately in any of the coverage I read. I found no measurement of how exposure changed as FEMA maps were updated.

What the warehouse shows

The FHWA National Bridge Inventory (transport.bridges) holds 624,862 primary bridge records for 2025, up from 613,934 in 2015. It has coordinates but no flood-zone field. The one hydraulic field is scour criticality. Bridges rated 0-3 (scour critical) fell from 22,900 (2015) to 20,000 (2025, 19,803). About 24,000 more are unknown or tidal. Scour is a structural-vulnerability flag, not floodplain exposure, and the decline reflects inspection and retrofit, not lower flood hazard. I did not compute my own flood-zone overlay.

Outside evidence and limits

  • First Street's model includes rainfall flooding and 1-in-2 to 1-in-500-year events; FEMA's special flood hazard areas largely omit rainfall flooding. The two are not comparable. The 23% road figure is a modeled estimate of loss of operation, not a count of roads inside FEMA zones.
  • BTS publishes a bridge-level National Highway System hazard-exposure layer (draws on FEMA's National Risk Index; I could not confirm a 1%-annual-chance riverine field). It is the best route to a real national count but I did not download it.
  • The question of growth as FEMA maps are updated cannot be answered from what I found. First Street's growth figure (about 23% to 26%) comes from climate projections, not map revisions. FEMA map updates are uneven by county and I found no national before-and-after tally for transport assets.
  • The First Street figures are from 2021, read through press summaries, not the report itself. Treat them as approximate.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Growth as flood maps are updated: No data found on map-revision effects; only First Street 30-year projection

Every query behind this report

1 warehouse call ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 3 rows — 3879 ms
SELECT "year", COUNT(*) n, SUM(CASE WHEN scour_critical IN ('0','1','2','3') THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) scour_crit_0_3, SUM(CASE WHEN scour_critical IN ('U','T') THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) unk_tidal FROM transport.bridges WHERE record_type='1' AND "year" IN (2015,2020,2025) GROUP BY "year" ORDER BY "year"
