Federal research money has shifted away from life sciences: about 13 points of share lost FY2005 to FY2025

NSF NCSES Survey of Federal Funds, federal obligations for research by field; research.nsf_rd_by_field; current USD, real via CPI-U

Summary

Life sciences fell from 52.3% of federal research obligations in FY2005 to 39.2% in FY2025, the biggest shift. Gainers: physical sciences (10.2% to 13.1%), engineering (15.9% to 17.2%), psychology (3.5% to 5.5%), social sciences (2.0% to 3.2%), and the residual 'other' bucket (3.9% to 9.3%). Total obligations rose from $53.7B to $95.2B nominal, about +7.5% in real terms (CPI-U), so life sciences fell about 19% in real terms while everything else grew.

Caveats

  • Research only (basic+applied), not development; defense development is excluded, so engineering and computing are understated versus total R&D tallies.
  • NCSES revised the field taxonomy in 2021: computer sciences and mathematics were split, and 'Other sciences nec' became 'Other fields' (likely absorbing reclassified items), so part of the 'other' jump is reclassification. I combined CS and math for comparability.
  • Endpoints are noisy: life sciences peaked at $42.5B in FY2020 (pandemic funding) and was 39.2% in FY2025; FY2025 is preliminary. Deflation uses CPI-U with an 11-month 2025 average, not a research-specific price index (biomedical research prices rose faster, so the real life-sciences drop would be larger).
