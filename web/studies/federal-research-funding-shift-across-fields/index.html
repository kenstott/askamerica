Federal research money has shifted about 8-9 points between fields since FY2004, mostly out of life sciences, but only about 7.6 points is measured on a consistent field list

NSF NCSES Survey of Federal Funds, federal research obligations (basic + applied, all agencies and performers), current dollars, FY2004-FY2025

Summary

Using merged field groups that mean the same thing before and after NSF's 2021 field revision, about 9.0 percentage points of federal research obligations moved between fields from FY2004 to FY2024 (the last final year). The big mover is life sciences plus 'other' (including NEC), down from 55.6% to 47.8% of the total. Physical sciences (9.8% to 12.8%), psychology (3.5% to 5.3%), social sciences (2.0% to 3.2%) and computing plus math (5.5% to 7.6%) gained. Engineering was roughly flat (16.6% to 17.6%) and geosciences fell (7.0% to 5.7%).

The shift is not smooth. Only 2.9 points occurred inside the old taxonomy (FY2004-FY2020). The FY2020 to FY2021 revision year alone moves 4.3 points, and FY2021-FY2024 moves 4.7 points. Summing the two consistent segments gives about 7.6 points. Treat 8-9 points as the answer, with the 2021 break as a caveat on how much is real.

Points shifted (merged groups): FY2004-FY2024 = 9.0; FY2004-FY2020 = 2.9; FY2020-FY2021 = 4.3; FY2021-FY2024 = 4.7; FY2004-FY2025 (preliminary end) = 7.8. Unmerged life sciences alone fell from 52.0% to 38.8% (FY2004 to FY2024), but part of that is reclassification into 'other fields'.

Merging does not fully neutralize the break: life plus other still drops 4.3 points in FY2020-21, so some money genuinely moved in classification between life sciences and physical/engineering/psychology categories. The 9.0 headline therefore likely overstates real change by some amount; 7.6 (the two clean segments, omitting the break year) is the lower bound.

In dollars, total research obligations grew from $53.4B to $99.6B (current dollars, nominal; not inflation-adjusted), so even shrinking-share fields such as life sciences grew in dollars ($27.7B to $38.6B). FY2025 is preliminary and fell back in total ($95.2B).

Scope caveats

This covers research (basic and applied) obligations, not development. Development, which is dominated by defense, has no field breakdown, so the shift among sciences excludes most DOD spending. Field assignment is by agency-reported categories, and a few agency reclassifications can move shares. I did not run an independent second source (for example AAAS or the HERD university table) and did not test the sensitivity to dropping the largest category.
