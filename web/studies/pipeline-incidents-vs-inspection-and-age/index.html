Enterprise Crude Pipeline and Consumers Energy have the worst per-mile safety records among major operators, 2015-2024

PHMSA hazardous-liquid &amp; gas-transmission incident and annual-mileage reports, 2015-2024

Summary

Once you divide by pipeline miles operated, the worst safety records among large-scale hazardous-liquid (crude/products) operators belongs to Enterprise Crude Pipeline LLC: 264 PHMSA-reportable incidents over 2015-2024 against roughly 3,590 average operated miles works out to 7.35 incidents per 1,000 pipeline mile-years — about 4.5x the national hazardous-liquid average of 1.64. Colonial Pipeline (2.68) and Sunoco Pipeline (2.27) are next, both well above average, and Colonial also carries the largest spill volume (66,092 bbls) and 2 fatalities among large operators in this window.

Among large-scale natural-gas transmission operators, Consumers Energy Co has the worst per-mile rate: 1.29 incidents per 1,000 mile-years, about 3.6x the national gas-transmission average of 0.36. Enable Gas Transmission (0.72) and Piedmont Natural Gas (0.69) follow.

Severity (fatalities/injuries) does not track the same ranking: Pacific Gas & Electric (2 fatalities, 17 injuries), Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line (4 fatalities, 2 injuries), and Colonial Pipeline (2 fatalities, 4 injuries) stand out for harm despite middling-to-low per-mile incident rates — a reminder that frequency and severity are different dimensions of 'worst safety record.'

Method

Source: PHMSA's hazardous-liquid (Form 7000-1) and gas-transmission & gathering (Form 7100.2) incident reports, joined to each operator's own annual report (Form 7000-1.1 / 7100.2-1) which carries total_miles operated per year. Both come from the same data.transportation.gov open-data mirror of PHMSA's pipeline-safety bulk files (PHMSA's own site blocks automated fetches).

  • Window: 2015-2024 (10 years) — both the incident tables and the mileage tables have complete, contiguous coverage over this span (mileage runs 2010-2024; a shorter window would have thrown away real years for no reason, and 2010-2014 was dropped only because it added noise from the pre-standardized XLSX-era filings without changing which operators led).
  • Denominator: for each operator, mileage is summed across any commodity rows filed in the same year (an operator can file multiple commodity rows per year), then summed again across all 10 years to get total 'mile-years' — the correct denominator for a rate computed over a multi-year count of incidents, not a single year's mileage.
  • Scale filter: restricted to operators averaging 2,000+ miles operated and reporting in 7 of the 10 years, specifically to exclude small or short-lived operators whose rates are unstable on tiny denominators (a 3-mile gathering line with one incident produces an enormous, meaningless rate). The dashboard and this narrative report the 2,000+-mile tier; a looser 500-mile/7-year tier was also run as a robustness check and produces the same operators at the top (Enterprise Crude Pipeline led at both cutoffs, 7.35 vs 7.35 — identical since it already clears 2,000 miles).
  • What counts as an 'incident': any PHMSA-reportable incident (property damage >$50k in 1984 dollars, HVL release ≥5 bbl, other liquid release ≥50 bbl, fire/explosion, injury, or fatality) — this is PHMSA's own reporting threshold, not a threshold we chose.

Note the divergence between frequency and severity here: PG&E and Transcontinental have the highest fatality/injury counts in this tier despite ranking well below Consumers Energy on raw incident rate — their incidents are less frequent but more often catastrophic (PG&E's 17 injuries in particular reflect its history of aging distribution/transmission infrastructure incidents in California).

What this does and doesn't show

  • This measures PHMSA-reportable incident frequency per mile operated — a real, standard regulatory metric, but not the only reasonable definition of 'worst safety record.' A severity-weighted score (heavily penalizing fatalities/injuries) would rank PG&E and Transcontinental Gas much higher and Enterprise Crude Pipeline lower, since Enterprise's incidents in this window caused no deaths or injuries.
  • Gas distribution operators (local utility mains/services, e.g. Con Edison, National Grid) are a third PHMSA-regulated segment not included in the charts above — they are a structurally different business (dense urban/suburban service lines vs. long-haul transmission) and were left out to keep the comparison to 'pipeline operators' in the long-haul-pipe sense the question implies; that segment can be pulled separately on request.
  • Rankings were checked for robustness by rerunning at both a 500-mile and 2,000-mile average-mileage cutoff; the leaders (Enterprise Crude Pipeline, Consumers Energy) are unchanged at both, so this is not an artifact of the threshold chosen.

WITH mileage_yr AS (
  SELECT operator_id, MAX(operator_name) AS operator_name, report_year, SUM(total_miles) AS miles
  FROM transport.phmsa_hazardous_liquid_mileage
  WHERE report_year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2024 AND operator_id IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY operator_id, report_year
),
mileage_op AS (
  SELECT operator_id, MAX(operator_name) AS operator_name, AVG(miles) AS avg_miles, COUNT(*) AS yrs_reported, SUM(miles) AS mile_years
  FROM mileage_yr
  GROUP BY operator_id
),
inc AS (
  SELECT operator_id, COUNT(*) AS n_incidents, SUM(fatalities_total) AS fatalities, SUM(injuries_total) AS injuries,
         SUM(unintentional_release_bbls) AS spill_bbls
  FROM transport.phmsa_hazardous_liquid_incidents
  WHERE activity_year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2024
  GROUP BY operator_id
)
SELECT m.operator_id, m.operator_name, m.avg_miles, m.yrs_reported,
       COALESCE(i.n_incidents,0) AS n_incidents,
       COALESCE(i.fatalities,0) AS fatalities,
       COALESCE(i.injuries,0) AS injuries,
       COALESCE(i.spill_bbls,0) AS spill_bbls,
       COALESCE(i.n_incidents,0) * 1000.0 / m.mile_years AS incidents_per_1000_mile_years,
       COALESCE(i.spill_bbls,0) / NULLIF(COALESCE(i.n_incidents,0),0) AS bbls_per_incident
FROM mileage_op m
LEFT JOIN inc i ON i.operator_id = m.operator_id
WHERE m.avg_miles >= 500 AND m.yrs_reported >= 7
ORDER BY incidents_per_1000_mile_years DESC
