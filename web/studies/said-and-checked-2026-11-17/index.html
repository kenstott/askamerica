This page lists 111 statements by 13 officeholders, drawn from NPR and PBS NewsHour stories and from Senate floor speeches printed in the Congressional Record for Nov. 16. They concern judicial and executive nominations, an energy permitting bill and data centers, the war in Ukraine, the farm bill, Nvidia and chip exports to China, the Nov. 13 nationwide air traffic ground stop, the federal deficit and the Senate Democratic leadership race.

80 statements could be checked. None has been checked yet; all 80 are marked not yet checked and will be updated as their checks return. Unsettled means evidence was found but does not decide the claim; unsupported means no evidence was found, not that the statement is false.

The remaining 31 statements are opinions, predictions, promises and personal attacks. They are listed because they were said, not because they can be checked. 2 pairs of statements by different officeholders are linked as counter-statements.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores. Statements are grouped two levels deep, by speaker and then topic or by topic and then speaker, and every group is collapsed. A topic with a single statement is listed under Other. The counts beside a name cover only that person's statements on this page, and are not a rating of the person. Statements that could not be checked are folded under each speaker. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 16 or 17, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, and in the Senate floor speeches printed in the Nov. 16, 2026 Congressional Record, as read on Nov. 17 at about 7:20 ET, less the stories the Nov. 15 and Nov. 16 pages already used. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. One NPR story listed in the feed had no readable text and was skipped. Quotes under five words were left out, as were commentators' accounts of what an official said. Statements are worded as the outlet reported them unless a transcript is linked; entries beginning "Reported as" are the reporter's wording.
