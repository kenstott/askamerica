Redefinitions and discontinuations of federal statistical series have accelerated sharply since mid-2024, driven mainly by budget cuts

A qualitative/historical survey of BEA, BLS, Census, and USDA discontinuation logs, 2024-2026

Summary

Outright redefinition of a major federal statistical series (a new methodology, a rebased index, a redesigned survey) happens routinely — on the order of once every year or two for any given big series, mostly as planned modernizations (e.g. CPI's 2025 shift toward phone/web/scraped data collection, BLS's annual CPS seasonal-factor revisions). But full discontinuation of series or data products was rare before 2024 and has become frequent since: at least three agencies (BEA, BLS, USDA/NASS) and parts of Census have each announced multiple rounds of cuts in 2024-2026, driven almost entirely by budget and staffing shortfalls rather than by methodological obsolescence. BEA alone logged roughly 9 separate discontinuation/delay announcement batches between July 2024 and September 2025, touching well over 100 individual tables and three whole products. The most recent, largest breaks as of this writing (September 2026) are: BEA's September 2025 elimination of a dozen more national wage/employment-by-industry tables and its July-August 2025 gutting of the multinational-enterprise and direct-investment detail tables; USDA/NASS's August-September 2025 discontinuation of the Agricultural Labor Survey and the 30-year Household Food Security Survey; and the October-November 2025 government shutdown, which caused the Current Population Survey to lose an entire month (October 2025) of household data outright — the first full missing CPS month in the survey's history — and forced BLS to cancel that month's CPI and employment releases.

How often: redefinition vs. discontinuation are different processes with different base rates

Redefinition/methodology change is the routine, ongoing kind of change and happens to some part of nearly every major series every few years: the CPI has had recurring "initiatives" (chained-CPI research series, geometric-mean formula changes in the 1990s, the 2025 collection-mode shift to phone/web/scraping to offset falling in-person response), the CPS revises its seasonal adjustment factors and reweights annually, and GDP undergoes a comprehensive ("benchmark") revision roughly every five years. These are disclosed in advance through BLS's own Recent and Upcoming Methodology Changes notice archive and CPI Notices pages, and are generally improvements rather than losses of data.

Discontinuation — a series or product simply stops being produced — was historically an infrequent event tied to genuine obsolescence (the Decennial Census long form was replaced by the American Community Survey after 2000; the ACS's 3-year estimates were dropped in 2014 for budget reasons, one of very few precedents). Since mid-2024, however, discontinuation announcements have clustered densely enough to look almost monthly in aggregate across agencies: BEA's own Discontinued or Delayed Statistics: 2024-2026 tracking page lists roughly 9 distinct announcement dates (07/2024, 09/2024, 11/2024, 03/2025, 07/2025, 07/22/2025, 08/22/2025, 09/2025) covering well over 100 individual national and regional tables plus 3 full products (Digital Economy, Near Real-Time Spending, Economic Well-Being). The American Statistical Association's February 2025 letter to Congress and its "The Nation's Data at Risk" 2025 report describe this as a systemic erosion across all 13 principal federal statistical agencies since July 2024 — declining purchasing power, hiring freezes, and deferred modernization, not a one-off event.

Which series broke most recently (ordered by announcement date, most recent first)

  • USDA/NASS Household Food Security Survey (Sept 20, 2025): USDA terminated the 30-year Current Population Survey Food Security Supplement, calling it "redundant" with other data; advocates and researchers dispute that characterization since it is the primary source for U.S. food-insecurity rates. (USDA press release; Investigate Midwest)
  • BEA national-accounts tables (Sept 25, 2025): Discontinued roughly a dozen more Section 6 tables — wages and salaries, compensation, and full-time/part-time employment by industry, at multiple levels of industry detail. (BEA discontinuation log)
  • USDA/NASS Agricultural Labor Survey and Mink Survey (Aug 28, 2025): NASS discontinued the only federal survey of agricultural employers, plus several county-level crop/livestock estimate programs, citing an 11% in-year budget cut and a 39.5% staff decline FY24-FY26. (NASS notice; EPI)
  • BEA multinational-enterprise and direct-investment detail tables (Jul 11 - Aug 22, 2025): Multiple rounds cutting country/industry-level detail on U.S. direct investment abroad, foreign direct investment in the U.S., and activities of multinational enterprises. (BEA discontinuation log)
  • BLS Producer Price Index (announced May 14, 2025; effective with the July 2025 data release, Aug 14, 2025): BLS stopped calculating and publishing about 350 of roughly 10,000 individual PPI indexes (under 1% of the index by weight, but the first PPI-index cut of this kind in years), plus suspended CPI in-person data collection entirely in Lincoln NE, Provo UT, and Buffalo NY. (BLS PPI notice)
  • CPS October 2025 data loss (Oct 1 - Nov 12, 2025 lapse in appropriations): Not a permanent discontinuation but the single most disruptive recent break — the October 2025 Current Population Survey was never completed, so BLS lost the entire month of household labor-force data and canceled that month's employment and CPI releases outright, the first time this has happened to CPS in this way. Consumer Expenditure Survey collection also halted Oct 1-Dec 5, 2025. (BLS CPS shutdown impact notice; BLS CPI shutdown impact notice; Atlanta Fed analysis, April 2026)
  • Census SIPP redesign (FY2026, field test planned for 2027): Not a discontinuation yet, but the Survey of Income and Program Participation is being restructured away from its 2014-vintage design toward a smaller sample blended with administrative data — the kind of redefinition that, if it proceeds, will break comparability with the pre-2027 series. (The Census Project, Aug 2026)

Frequency estimate and its limits

Treating each BEA announcement date as one "batch," the 9 batches from July 2024 to September 2025 (a 451-day span) worked out to a batch roughly every 24 days on average (median 5 days within a cluster, longer gaps between clusters) — this is a Python-computed statistic over the dated entries scraped from BEA's own log, not an estimate from memory. That cadence describes BEA specifically; BLS's PPI and CPI actions, USDA/NASS's survey terminations, and Census's SIPP redesign are separate, less frequent, agency-specific events layered on top of it, so the true cross-government rate is higher than any single agency's log shows and is not captured by any one comprehensive federal tracker — no agency maintains a combined cross-government discontinuation index. This is a change in note: the felt frequency of headline-series breakage (CPS, CPI, PPI, GDP detail) is genuinely new since mid-2024, driven by the FY2024-FY2026 budget environment, not by researchers concluding old measures were wrong.
