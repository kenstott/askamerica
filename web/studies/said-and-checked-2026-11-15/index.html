Said & Checked

What officeholders said, and what the data shows

2026-11-15 · 56 statements from 15 speakers · 8 factual

Fifty-six statements by 15 officeholders appeared in two days of NPR and PBS NewsHour politics coverage. Most were about three things: the Nov. 13 nationwide ground stop and what it will cost to fix air traffic control, the Iran war and talk of ending it, and what Democrats plan to do with the majorities they won on Nov. 3.

Eight of the 56 were factual claims that data or documents could test. Three were supported, including the transportation secretary's statement that air traffic control has seen decades of underinvestment. Three were unsettled: evidence was found but does not decide them. They are the president's statement that much of the air traffic control system is 50 years old, the Florida governor's statement about what a state law requires, and the transportation secretary's reported account of what caused the ground stop, where the FAA's published account differs and its investigation is still open. Two were unsupported, meaning no evidence was found for them; that is not a finding that they are false.

The other 48 are opinions, predictions, promises and one personal attack. They are listed because they were said, not because they can be checked.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Contradicted
Data exists and says something different.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or on an investigation that has not reported.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores and no totals per person. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 14 or 15, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, as read on Nov. 15. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. Two NPR audio segments with no transcript were skipped. Quotes under five words were left out, and two entries that were a commentator's account of what an official said were removed. No original source (transcript, release or post) could be read for any statement, so all wording is as the outlet reported it.

UnsettledFactual claim

AP's account of Duffy's statements on the cause of the nationwide ground stop; L3Harris is an FAA contractor.

Reported as: He has said the problem began when L3Harris was replacing a section of old copper wire with new fiber wire, somehow causing the system to overload.

The FAA's own account differs. It says data from two sites, a radar site in Amarillo, Texas and a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where L3Harris had deployed a new router, flooded the network; it attributes the instability to the legacy system, not to upgrade work, and says the root cause is still under investigation. L3Harris has not named a cause.

Evidence: FAA, Nationwide Ground Stop: Questions and Answers (Nov. 15, 2026), L3Harris statement on the Nov. 13 ground stop (Nov. 14, 2026)
