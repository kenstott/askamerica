Auto physical-damage loss cost per insured car rose a median 8% by state in 2019-2021 (collision severity +27%), and national repair-price inflation explains only about a quarter of the severity jump

NAIC 2021/2022 Auto Insurance Database Report (total business, Texas n/a) vs BLS CPI motor vehicle maintenance and repair; 50 states + DC

Summary

Using NAIC incurred losses per earned exposure, collision-plus-comprehensive loss cost per insured car rose a median 8.3% across 50 states and DC from 2019 to 2021, with a huge spread: Louisiana +49.6%, New Jersey +43.7%, Oklahoma +22.0% at the top; Wyoming -28.6%, Montana -25.7%, Colorado -18.5% at the bottom (the bottom and several top moves are comprehensive/hail swings; collision alone is steadier, median +8.4%). Collision severity (cost per claim) rose a median 27.0% in nearly every state (range 12.6% to 38.7%), while the national CPI for vehicle maintenance and repair rose only 7.4% over the same two years. So repair-price inflation as measured by the CPI tracks roughly a quarter of the severity increase in 2019-2021; the rest came from things the CPI does not capture (used and new vehicle prices, which the CPI shows +30.7% for used cars, newer and costlier-to-repair vehicles, total-loss valuations, and ADAS/sensor content). Repair CPI then accelerated: +28.1% from 2021 to 2024, so the gap probably narrowed in later years, but state loss data for those years was not accessible to me.

Frequency fell in 2020-21 (national collision frequency 6.03 to 5.21 per 100), which is why pure premium rose far less (+9.9% ex-Texas) than severity.

What was measured

  • Loss cost = pure premium = incurred losses / earned exposures, NAIC Table 29C (collision) and 32C (comprehensive), total business; severity from Table 29D. Parsed from the Maryland Insurance Administration's mirror of the NAIC report (the NAIC site returned 403).
  • Physical damage only (collision + comprehensive), the coverages that repair and parts prices touch directly. Liability (bodily injury, property damage) is not in the state table here.
  • National collision pure premium: $289.77 (2019), $233.80 (2020), $320.37 (2021) incl. Texas; ex-Texas $265.53 to $291.81 (+9.9%). Collision severity ex-Texas $4,404 to $5,601 (+27.2%).
  • Repair CPI (CUSR0000SETD annual average, FRED): 296.0 (2019), 306.0 (2020), 317.9 (2021), 344.1 (2022), 383.8 (2023), 407.3 (2024).

How much tracks repair inflation

BLS publishes repair and parts indexes nationally only, so a state-by-state regression of loss cost on repair inflation cannot be run. The state severity growth is tightly clustered (standard deviation 5.3 points around a 27% median), which itself says the driver is national, not state-specific. 7.4 / 27.2 is about 27%: the CPI repair component accounts for roughly a quarter of 2019-21 severity growth. This is a descriptive ratio, not a causal estimate; severity also moves with the mix of claims (fewer minor claims in 2020-21 raises average severity mechanically) and with vehicle prices. Parts CPI could not be retrieved, so the parts-specific share is not separated.

State differences in total loss cost (Louisiana, New Jersey, Oklahoma, Florida, Connecticut) are mainly frequency, comprehensive (weather/hail), and state-specific rules, not repair-price differences; no state repair price series exists to test that.

Limits

  • Window ends 2021 because the newer NAIC 2022/2023 edition could not be opened; losses lag about three years and Texas is not reported.
  • Comprehensive loss cost is volatile (hail): CO, MT, WY, NE declines largely reflect an unusually bad 2019 for hail.
  • The national severity and repair comparison uses two end years; 2020 is a pandemic-distorted year.
