Auto insurance loss costs rose roughly 19-57% since 2019 depending on measure; repair/parts inflation explains only part of it, and this corpus has no state-grain series for either

NAIC/ISO/BLS CPI, national and state-flag data, 2019-2025 vintage

Summary

Nationally, the average auto-insurance expenditure per insured vehicle rose from $1,075 in 2019 to $1,282 in 2023 (+19.2%, NAIC), and filed rate increases continued at 12.0% in 2023, 16.5% in 2024, and a projected 7.5% in 2025 (S&P Global RateWatch/NAIC). The BLS Consumer Price Index for motor vehicle insurance rose 56.5% from 2019 to 2025 - a bigger move than premium expenditure because it prices a constant policy rather than what people actually bought (which shifted toward lower coverage as prices rose).

Vehicle repair and parts inflation explains part, not most, of this. BLS's CPI for motor vehicle body work/repair rose about 38 index points (2019=100 basis) over 2019-2025, versus the insurance CPI's roughly 57-point rise over the same years - repair inflation covers roughly two-thirds of the insurance CPI's move on a simple index-point comparison. Underlying claim data (ISO) tells the same story more directly: average collision-claim severity (own-vehicle repair) rose 43% and property-damage-liability severity (paying for the other driver's vehicle) rose 56% from 2019 to 2024 - both outpacing the ~33-point repair-CPI move over the same years, meaning something beyond straight repair-price inflation (vehicle complexity/ADAS calibration costs, rising total-loss frequency at higher used-vehicle values, and shifting claims mix) is also pushing per-claim cost up. On the liability side, bodily-injury claim severity rose 48% over the same years, which has nothing to do with parts or repair labor at all - it reflects medical-cost inflation and litigation/settlement trends ("social inflation").

State-by-state loss cost, and any state-level repair-cost index, is a genuine data gap. Neither this corpus nor any freely available public source publishes a clean multi-year 'loss cost per insured vehicle' series broken out by state, and BLS does not publish its motor-vehicle-repair or motor-vehicle-insurance CPI subindices below the national level. What IS available at state grain: NAIC average premium EXPENDITURE per vehicle by state (single-year 2023 snapshot; premium, not loss cost, so it also carries underwriting expense and margin) and average filed RATE increases by state for 2023-2025 (S&P/RateWatch), which vary enormously - Nevada's 2024 filed increase (28%) was roughly four times North Carolina's near-zero 2025 change. Those figures are reported below as the best available proxy, labeled as such.

The gap between the NAIC expenditure figure (+19.2% through 2023) and the CPI figure (+56.5% through 2025) is partly a window mismatch (NAIC's latest published state-level year is 2023) and partly definitional: CPI prices a fixed policy design over time, while NAIC's expenditure figure reflects what people actually bought, including a shift toward thinner coverage (fewer optional coverages, higher deductibles) as prices rose - a form of quality/coverage downgrade that mutes the expenditure number relative to the pure price index.

How much tracks repair and parts inflation

Indexing to 2019=100: the insurance CPI rose to 156.5 by 2025 while the motor-vehicle-repair (body work) CPI rose only to 138.0. Repair inflation (38 of the roughly 57 points of insurance-CPI movement) covers about two-thirds of the insurance CPI's rise on this simple index-point comparison - not a weighted or causal decomposition, since insurance CPI blends liability (bodily injury + property damage) and physical-damage (collision + comprehensive) coverages that have very different cost drivers.

The ISO claim-severity data (average dollar size of a paid claim) is a more direct test, because it separates coverage types: collision-claim severity (which pays almost entirely for parts and labor on the policyholder's own vehicle) rose 43.4% from 2019 to 2024, and property-damage-liability severity (which pays largely for repairing the OTHER driver's vehicle) rose 56.5% - both above the ~33-point rise in the repair CPI over the same years (2019-2024, before 2025's further increase). That gap points to cost drivers beyond straight parts/labor price inflation: growing vehicle complexity and ADAS sensor recalibration costs that outpace the general repair-CPI basket, and a higher share of claims settling as total losses as used-vehicle values rose, both of which push average severity up independent of the per-hour/per-part price. Bodily-injury liability severity, which has no repair-cost component at all, rose 47.7% over the same period - driven by medical-cost inflation and litigation/settlement trends (commonly called 'social inflation' in the industry) - underscoring that repair/parts inflation is a real but partial driver of the total loss-cost increase, not the whole story.

State variation: what is (and is not) available

No source found - inside this corpus or in open published NAIC/BLS data - carries a multi-year, all-50-state loss-cost-per-vehicle series, and BLS publishes its motor-vehicle CPI subindices (insurance and repair) at the U.S. city-average level only, with no state or even regional breakout. This is a genuine coverage gap, confirmed by search_catalog returning no match for 'repair' anywhere in the corpus and by the BLS/NAIC public releases themselves not publishing sub-national detail for these specific series.

What state-level data does exist, and what it shows: NAIC's average premium expenditure per insured vehicle for 2023 ranged from $691.77 (North Dakota, lowest) to roughly $1,865 (Florida, highest per Triple-I/ValuePenguin's most-expensive-state citation) - a more than 2.5x spread. Filed rate increases for 2024 were sharply uneven: Nevada (28%), Washington (18%), Arizona (17%), Connecticut (17%), Louisiana (16%) and Georgia (16%) saw the largest jumps against a 16.5% national average, while for 2025 New Jersey and Washington (+17.2% each) and California (+16.2%) are projected largest, with North Carolina essentially flat (-0.1%). These are premium/rate figures, not loss cost, and should not be read as a state-by-state repair-cost-inflation ranking - no such ranking exists in published form.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Auto-insurance loss cost per vehicle, by state: Neither this corpus nor any free public source publishes a clean 'loss cost per insured vehicle' series broken out by all 50 states over multiple years. NAIC/state insurance-department loss-cost filings (via ISO/NCCI-style advisory loss costs) are the actuarial source for this exact figure but are not freely republished state-by-state in machine-readable form; what IS publicly available at state grain is average PREMIUM EXPENDITURE per insured vehicle (NAIC Auto Insurance Database, most recent full state table is 2023, single-year snapshot in what I could access) and average FILED RATE CHANGES by state (S&P Global RateWatch via ValuePenguin, 2023-2025). I used these two premium-side proxies and said explicitly that premium is not loss cost - it also embeds underwriting expense, profit margin and reinsurance cost, so a state's premium rise can outrun or lag its actual loss-cost rise.
  • State-level breakdown of the repair-cost tracking specifically: No state-level CPI or repair-cost index exists in any source found (BLS CPI motor vehicle repair/insurance subindices are U.S. city average only, not published by state). This is a genuine, confirmed public-data gap, not a search failure - flagged as such rather than proxied.
