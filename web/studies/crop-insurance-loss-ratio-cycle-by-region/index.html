Worst crop-insurance loss ratios sit in WA, FL, TX, AR, CA and the Mountain West, and losses have spread wider since 2016, largely because the years got worse

USDA RMA Summary of Business (ag.rma_crop_insurance), crop years 2016-2025; loss ratio = indemnity / total premium

Answer

Over 2016-2025 the worst premium-weighted loss ratios were in Washington (1.48), Florida (1.36), Texas (1.32), Arkansas (1.32), California (1.29), Utah (1.29), New Mexico (1.24), Arizona (1.22), Georgia (1.15), Montana (1.14) and Louisiana (1.14): Pacific/specialty-crop states, the Southwest and Mountain West, the Gulf/Delta South and Texas. The Corn Belt was best (IN 0.31, IL 0.31, IA 0.44, WI 0.45, OH 0.50). Texas matters most in dollars: $18.8B indemnity on $14.3B premium.

Losses have become more spread out, not more concentrated: states with a ratio above 1 averaged 15.6 per year in 2016-20 and 22.6 in 2021-25 (28 of 43 in 2024); the premium share in counties with a ratio above 1 rose from 25% to 34%; and the HHI of states' excess indemnity (indemnity above premium) fell from about 2,500 to 1,750. Caveat: breadth correlates r=0.99 with the national loss ratio (0.72 in 2016-20 vs 0.88 in 2021-25), so most of the spreading is worse years (2019, 2022-24), not a clean structural shift. 2025 (national 0.69) already retreated.

Correlation with national LR (2015-2025, n=11): states above 1 r=0.82; county premium share above 1 r=0.99; HHI r=-0.73. The HHI decline is the mirror of the same severity effect: in mild years (2016-17) one or two states account for over half of excess indemnity; in bad years it is spread across many. Shifts by state between halves: WA 0.96 to 1.76, CA 0.93 to 1.52, MT 0.75 to 1.35, OK 0.66 to 1.29, KS 0.40 to 1.09, NE 0.35 to 0.88; NC fell 1.42 to 0.81 and AR 1.42 to 1.26. So persistently bad regions (TX, FL, AR, NM, AZ, UT, LA, GA) are stable, while the Plains and West got worse.

Limits

State grain, not USDA farm-resource regions (the table has county rows, so a county-to-region rollup is possible but not done). Loss ratio is not risk-adjusted: premium subsidies, crop mix (specialty crops, citrus, hurricane/freeze exposure) and premium rate-setting differ by state, and premiums rose with commodity prices in 2021-22, which dampens ratios. Ten-year window chosen as the latest ten complete crop years (2025 is loaded); the 2015-2024 window gives the same pattern. No leave-one-out or causal test run; descriptive only.

WITH c AS (SELECT year, state_abbr, county_fips, SUM(total_premium_amount) p, SUM(indemnity_amount) i FROM ag.rma_crop_insurance WHERE year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2025 GROUP BY year, state_abbr, county_fips HAVING SUM(total_premium_amount)>0),
s AS (SELECT year, state_abbr, SUM(p) p, SUM(i) i FROM c GROUP BY year, state_abbr),
st AS (SELECT year, SUM(CASE WHEN i>p THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) states_gt1, SUM(CASE WHEN i>p THEN p ELSE 0 END)/SUM(p) prem_share_states_gt1, SUM(i)/SUM(p) nat_lr FROM s GROUP BY year),
ex AS (SELECT year, state_abbr, GREATEST(i-p,0) x FROM s),
hh AS (SELECT year, SUM(POWER(100.0*x/NULLIF(t,0),2)) hhi_excess, MAX(100.0*x/NULLIF(t,0)) top_share FROM (SELECT year, state_abbr, x, SUM(x) OVER (PARTITION BY year) t FROM ex) GROUP BY year),
cn AS (SELECT year, COUNT(*) n, SUM(CASE WHEN i>p THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) cty_gt1, SUM(CASE WHEN i>p THEN p ELSE 0 END)/SUM(p) prem_share_cty_gt1, SUM(CASE WHEN i>2*p THEN p ELSE 0 END)/SUM(p) prem_share_cty_gt2 FROM c GROUP BY year)
SELECT st.year, ROUND(nat_lr,3) nat_lr, states_gt1, ROUND(prem_share_states_gt1,3) ps_states, n, cty_gt1, ROUND(prem_share_cty_gt1,3) ps_cty, ROUND(prem_share_cty_gt2,3) ps_cty2, ROUND(hhi_excess) hhi_excess, ROUND(top_share,1) top_state_share FROM st JOIN hh USING(year) JOIN cn USING(year) ORDER BY year
