Western and Gulf-South states run the worst crop-insurance loss ratios; concentration has drifted up but is driven by drought-year spikes, not a steady trend

USDA RMA Summary of Business (askamerica ag.rma_crop_insurance), 2015-2024 (the most recently complete decade; 2025 is mid-season and excluded)

Summary

Over 2015-2024, the worst-performing federal crop-insurance regions were the Pacific/Southwest states (Washington, California, Oregon, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico) and the Gulf/Southeast (Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, Florida, Georgia) — every major farm state in these two clusters paid out more in indemnities than growers and taxpayers paid in premium over the decade, with loss ratios from 1.16 (Nevada) up to 1.54 (Washington). By contrast, the Corn Belt / Heartland (Illinois 0.33, Indiana 0.38, Iowa 0.43, Wisconsin 0.46, Ohio 0.50) ran deeply profitable for the program, subsidizing the losses elsewhere. Nationally the decade loss ratio was 0.83 ($104.3B paid on $125.3B in premium).

Concentration of indemnity dollars among a few states has drifted up over the decade — the top-5 states' share of national indemnities rose from about 42% (2015-2019 average) to about 50% (2020-2024 average), and the Gini coefficient of state-level indemnity dollars (computed with the gini_coefficient tool for each year) rose from 0.62 in 2015 to an extreme 0.71 in 2022, before falling back to 0.58 by 2024. But the trend is noisy, not smooth: it is driven almost entirely by an extreme concentration spike in 2021-2022 (Texas alone took 23.9% of the entire nation's indemnity dollars in 2022, versus about 9% in 2015, amid the historic Southern Plains drought), and by 2024 the top-5 share and Gini had both fallen back to roughly where the decade started. A linear trend fit across the 10 annual HHI values (a second, independent concentration measure computed directly from the same data) is positive but not statistically significant (slope +24.5 HHI/year, r=0.49, p≈0.15) — there is directional evidence of rising concentration, but at only 10 annual observations it cannot be called a confirmed trend distinct from drought-driven volatility.

Data and method

Source: USDA Risk Management Agency (RMA) Summary of Business, as loaded in askamerica's ag.rma_crop_insurance table (observed coverage 1995-2025; 2025 is only partially loaded/mid-season and was excluded, so the analysis window is the ten complete years 2015-2024). Loss ratio is defined exactly as RMA defines it: total indemnity paid divided by total premium (grower-paid plus federal/state subsidy combined), aggregated at the state level across all crops, plans, and coverage levels.

"Farming region" is operationalized at the state level rather than USDA ERS's county-based Farm Resource Regions (Heartland, Northern Great Plains, Prairie Gateway, etc.), because those nine regions are built from NASS Crop Reporting Districts that cross state lines with no simple state crosswalk available in this session (confirmed by fetching ERS's own AIB-760 definitional publication, which describes the regions narratively but does not include a downloadable county list in the two pages fetched). States are close analytical proxies here since RMA itself only reports at state/county grain, and the state clustering found (Pacific/Southwest vs. Gulf/Southeast vs. Corn Belt) lines up with the commodity specializations ERS describes for its Fruitful Rim, Prairie Gateway/Basin-and-Range, and Heartland regions respectively.

To avoid small-state noise, the worst/best rankings are restricted to the 36 states with more than $500 million in cumulative 2015-2024 premium volume (a materiality filter); several tiny-premium states (Rhode Island, Hawaii, Utah) showed even higher nominal loss ratios but on premium bases under $350 million, too thin to support a "worst region" claim, and are excluded from the headline ranking for that reason.

Concentration was measured three ways on each year's cross-state distribution of indemnity dollars: the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (sum of squared state shares × 10,000, computed directly from the same query results), the Gini coefficient (computed year-by-year with the gini_coefficient tool), and the top-5 states' combined indemnity share — all three move together and tell the same qualitative story (up on average, but volatile and spike-driven rather than a smooth secular rise).

query — 500 rows — 3768 ms
SELECT year, state_abbr, SUM(indemnity_amount) AS indemnity, SUM(total_premium_amount) AS premium
FROM ag.rma_crop_insurance
WHERE year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2024
GROUP BY year, state_abbr
query — 36 rows — 5566 ms
SELECT state_abbr,
       SUM(indemnity_amount) AS indemnity,
       SUM(total_premium_amount) AS premium,
       SUM(indemnity_amount)/SUM(total_premium_amount) AS loss_ratio,
       SUM(policies_sold_count) AS policies
FROM ag.rma_crop_insurance
WHERE year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2024
GROUP BY state_abbr
HAVING SUM(total_premium_amount) > 500000000
ORDER BY loss_ratio DESC
