Defense R&D money does not predict later patent growth across states: the leaders were not ahead, and did not pull ahead

NSF NCSES (HERD 2010-2023; Federal Funds FY2024), USPTO PatentsView (first-inventor state), ACS population; 50 states

Answer

Neither story shows up in this data. Across the 50 states, DoD R&D per capita is essentially uncorrelated with patents per capita before (r = -0.04 for 2010 university DoD R&D vs 2010 patents) and with patent growth afterward (r = -0.03 vs 2010-2023 growth). The big defense-money places are a mixed bag: Virginia, Colorado, Massachusetts and California grew patenting 28-55 percent, while Alabama (about $1,290 DoD R&D per person in FY2024) was flat (0.5 percent) and New Mexico fell 6 percent. Confidence: moderate-low; 50 units and a patent-count outcome cannot rule out modest effects, and this is descriptive, not causal.

Exclusions

Predicate p.state_fips <> '11' excluded the District of Columbia (FIPS 11; a city and extreme per-capita outlier). Predicate l.state_fips IS NOT NULL dropped patents whose first inventor has no US state (foreign inventors, which are not part of a state comparison); territories (Guam, Puerto Rico, Virgin Islands) were also left out by the state filter. With DC included (n=51), the 2010 university DoD R&D vs 2010 patents correlation is -0.07 and DoD vs growth is +0.14; both remain small, so the null reading does not depend on the exclusion.

What I ran

  • Outcome: utility patents granted per 100k residents, assigned to the first inventor's state (inventor basis, not assignee), 2010 vs 2023, plus 2012/2015/2018/2021 for a panel.
  • Exposure A: DoD-funded university R&D per capita (NSF HERD), 2010 onward. Exposure B: all-performer DoD R&D obligations per capita, FY2024 (only vintage available at state x agency grain; includes contractors and federal labs).
  • Were they already ahead? Corr(2010 DoD univ R&D pc, 2010 patents pc) = -0.04; log FY2024 DoD pc vs log 2010 patents pc = +0.21 (weak, not significant at n=50).
  • Did they pull ahead? OLS of log patents-pc growth 2010-2023 on DoD pc and initial log patents pc: DoD coefficient -0.0004 (SE 0.0019, p = 0.84) for HERD; +0.007 (SE 0.026, p = 0.79) for FY2024 all-performer log DoD pc. Initial patent level has a mild convergence sign (-0.07, p = 0.11). R-squared about 0.05.
  • Within-state: two-way fixed-effects panel (50 states x 6 years, clustered by state) of patents pc on DoD university R&D pc: coefficient 0.020 (SE 0.018, p = 0.29). Not distinguishable from zero.

Caveats

  • Patents are a narrow innovation measure; defense work is often classified or unpatented, so DoD-heavy places may understate innovation by this yardstick.
  • State grain, n=50, one growth window: low power. I did not run leave-one-out tests; the FY2024 all-performer figure is a snapshot taken after the growth window, so it proxies a persistent allocation pattern rather than the timing of funds.
  • Money goes where capacity already exists (selection) and to places with contractors but few inventors (Alabama, Virginia), so a null is consistent with both weak effects and offsetting ones.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Historical pre-2010 baseline or multi-year all-performer DoD series: State x agency DoD obligations exist only for FY2024 in the warehouse; I used university-performed DoD R&D (2010-2023 panel) for timing and FY2024 all-performer DoD as a persistent-allocation proxy.
