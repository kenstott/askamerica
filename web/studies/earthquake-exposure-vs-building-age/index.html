About 57% of housing in high-seismic-risk counties predates 1980, versus 49% nationally, but the gap shrinks to 3 points without NYC

FEMA NRI earthquake rating x ACS 2024 5-year B25034, county grain, 3,133 counties

Summary

Taking the 48 counties FEMA's National Risk Index rates 'Very High' (Los Angeles) or 'Relatively High' for earthquake risk, 57.3% of their 21.9 million housing units (12.5M) were built before 1980. Using a stricter 1970 cutoff the share is 42.9%, and pre-1960 it is 31.1%. Nationally the pre-1980 share is 49.5%, so high-risk counties are only modestly older, and the median high-risk county (51.8%) is about the same as the national median county (51.8%).

Method and definitions

'High seismic risk' = NRI earthquake_risk_rating Very High or Relatively High (48 counties). 'Modern codes' has no single date, so pre-1980 is the headline (post-1971 San Fernando and mid-1970s UBC seismic upgrades took effect by the late 1970s), with pre-1970 and pre-1960 as sensitivities. Housing age is from ACS 2024 5-year B25034 decade brackets; the 1970s bracket straddles the cutoff. Age is not retrofit status: many pre-1980 California buildings are retrofitted, and many newer buildings elsewhere (Memphis, Charleston, NYC) were never designed for seismic loads.

Exclusions and what drives the pooled figure

Exclusions, shown only as sensitivity checks: Los Angeles County (FIPS 06037) was left out in one recomputation, moving the high-risk pre-1980 share from 57.3% to 54.7%. Kings, New York, Queens and Bronx counties (FIPS 36047, 36061, 36081, 36005) were left out in another, moving it to 53.0%. The headline 57.3% includes all 48 counties. These counties dominate because they are huge and old: Los Angeles 70.3% pre-1980 (3.65M units) and the four NYC boroughs 78-83% (3.5M units; NYC is rated high mainly by exposure). Counties range from 17.5% (Clark NV) and 25% (Yuma AZ) to 86% (St. Louis County MO). San Francisco is 76.5%, San Mateo 72.5%, Alameda 65.3%; Sun Belt high-risk counties are far newer.

Caveats: ACS sampling margins (larger for small counties); NRI ratings blend hazard, exposure and loss, not pure ground-shaking hazard, so a USGS hazard-based definition (not in the warehouse) could change the set; n=48 is small.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Retrofit status or local code adoption dates: Not in warehouse; year built is a proxy.
