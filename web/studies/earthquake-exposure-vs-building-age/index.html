Housing in high-seismic-risk U.S. counties is centered on the pre-1980 building-code era, not dramatically older than the nation as a whole

Census ACS 2019-2023 5-yr median year built, 43 counties across California, Cascadia, the Wasatch Front, the New Madrid Seismic Zone, Charleston SC, Alaska and Hawaii

Summary

Using a hand-built list of 43 counties in the nation's highest-known seismic-hazard zones (California's San Andreas/Hayward corridor, Cascadia-exposed Puget Sound/Willamette Valley, Utah's Wasatch Front, the New Madrid Seismic Zone in Missouri/Arkansas/Tennessee, Charleston SC, Anchorage/Mat-Su Alaska, and Honolulu/Hawaii County), the occupied-unit-weighted median year of housing construction is 1977 -- before 1980, the year by which the 1976 Uniform Building Code's strengthened seismic provisions had become the operative national model code. About half of the 14.4 million occupied housing units in this sample sit in counties whose median year built is itself before 1980, meaning that in those places, more than half the housing stock predates that threshold by definition.

Two of the corpus tables built specifically to answer this question exactly -- FEMA's own earthquake-risk-by-county classification and Census's decade-bracket year-built table -- were both unreachable this session (one returned an S3 404 on every query, the other returned 0 rows after a routine schema rebuild). Both are reported as defects below. The figures here are computed from a working substitute table (median year built) and a hand-built county list, not from those two purpose-built tables, and are presented as a proxy, not an exact replacement.

For context, the same weighted-median calculation run over every U.S. county gives 1978 -- so the high-seismic sample is only modestly older than the nation's housing stock as a whole. The 'old housing in earthquake country' story is real but concentrated: it is driven by specific places (San Francisco 1945, Los Angeles 1965, Alameda 1969, Multnomah/Portland 1971) and the rural Mississippi Delta/New Madrid counties (1971-1976), not a uniform pattern across every seismically hazardous county -- several Wasatch Front, Puget Sound and Charleston-area counties in the same sample have median year-built figures in the 1980s-1990s.

Why 1980 as the code threshold

There is no single 'modern seismic code adoption date' nationally -- codes tightened in steps. The two most consequential steps for existing wood-frame and unreinforced-masonry housing stock were the 1976 Uniform Building Code (adopted following the 1971 San Fernando earthquake, phased in through the late 1970s) and the string of post-1989 Loma Prieta / post-1994 Northridge amendments that produced the 2000 International Building Code / ASCE 7 seismic design categories used today. This report uses 1980 as the cut because it is the point by which the 1976 UBC's provisions were in force across most seismic jurisdictions and is the threshold used in California's own legislative background material on seismic resilience (cited below) when discussing 'homes built before 1980' and raised-foundation retrofit eligibility. A pre-1940 cut is also meaningful -- California's own Earthquake Brace + Bolt program specifically targets pre-1940 housing as its highest-priority vulnerability tier -- but the corpus table that would give a precise pre-1940 county share (census.acs_housing_year_built_detail) was unreachable this session, so that narrower figure could not be computed here.

Method and the two broken corpus tables

What should have answered this directly: disasters.fema_nri_earthquake_by_county carries FEMA's own county-level earthquake_risk_rating, and census.acs_housing_year_built_detail carries Census ACS table B25034's exact decade brackets (including 'built 1939 or earlier' and 'built 1970-1979'), which sum directly to a 'percent built before 1980' or 'before 1940' at county grain. Both are exactly the tables this question calls for.

What happened: census.acs_housing_year_built_detail returned "The specified key does not exist (S3 404)" on every query attempt, including a bare SELECT of one column. disasters.fema_nri_earthquake_by_county's describe_table call succeeded and matched its own documented prior verification, but a subsequent COUNT(*) returned 0 rows, and this persisted after running update_schema to force a reconnect -- the update_schema call itself may have broken this particular lazily-loaded ArcGIS-backed table rather than fixing the first one. Both were filed as defects during this session.

Substitute used: census.acs_housing_tenure, which works normally and carries median_year_built per county (ACS 2019-2023 5-year, 3,222 county rows for 2023, 2009-2023 observed coverage overall). A median is a coarser instrument than a decade-bracket share -- it tells you whether more or less than half a county's housing predates a given year, not the exact percentage -- so the 49.9%-of-units figure above is a lower-bound-flavored proxy for 'more than half,' not a precise national percentage.

No rows excluded in the national comparison figure: the national weighted-median query filtered WHERE county_fips IS NOT NULL AND median_year_built IS NOT NULL as a defensive predicate, but checking directly: of the 3,222 county-grain rows for 2023, zero have a null county_fips and zero have a null median_year_built. The filter therefore excluded no rows at all -- the national figure of 1978 is computed over the full set of 3,222 counties, not a subset.

County list: disasters.fema_nri_earthquake_by_county could not be queried to pull FEMA's own high-risk county list, so the 43-county sample was built from published USGS/FEMA-HAZUS seismic hazard geography instead: all major population centers in the San Andreas/Hayward fault corridor and the broader California seismic zone (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Alameda, Santa Clara, San Diego, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, Sacramento, Kern), the Cascadia-exposed Puget Sound and Willamette Valley (King, Pierce, Snohomish, Thurston WA; Multnomah, Washington, Clackamas OR), the Wasatch Front in Utah (Salt Lake, Utah, Davis, Weber), the New Madrid Seismic Zone spanning Missouri/Arkansas/Tennessee (New Madrid, Pemiscot, Scott, Dunklin, Cape Girardeau MO; Mississippi, Craighead, Poinsett, Clay AR; Shelby, Madison, Haywood, Lake TN), the Charleston SC seismic zone (Charleston, Berkeley, Dorchester), Anchorage/Mat-Su Alaska, Washoe/Douglas Nevada, and Honolulu/Hawaii County HI. This list is the USGS-FEMA HAZUS report's own named top-10-exposure states translated into specific high-population/high-hazard counties within each -- a defensible proxy, but not FEMA NRI's own per-county classification, which the broken table would have supplied precisely.
