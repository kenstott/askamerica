How much of a university's research spending is funded by its own endowment rather than federal money?

Published report (AskAmerica connector): http://127.0.0.1:61623/a/17552d7a567bae6e2ff7b3038548c456.html (This is a loopback link generated by the local connector session — not fetchable from outside that session. The dashboard image is saved alongside this file as dashboard.png.)

Answer

Across 575 US research universities in FY2022 (NSF HERD funding-source data joined to NCES IPEDS endowment data on IPEDS UnitID — not just the handful of famous large-endowment schools that dominate press coverage), institutions fund a median of 25% of their own research from institutional funds (mean 28%), while the federal government funds a median of 51% (mean 52%). Both figures track NSF's own published national HERD totals (~25% institutional, ~55% federal for FY2022-23) closely, which cross-validates the sample.

Endowment SIZE is a very weak predictor of that institutional-funding SHARE. The raw-dollar correlation between endowment and % of research self-funded is essentially zero (r = -0.015). In log form it is only r = 0.11 (partial r = 0.10 after controlling for the size of the university's total research program, p = 0.015, n = 575). An OLS regression of institutional-share-of-research on log(endowment) and log(total R&D) has R² = 0.013 — endowment size explains about 1% of the cross-university variance in how self-funded a university's research is. Leave-one-state-out testing keeps the coefficient's direction stable (range 1.00–1.44 pp per log-endowment-unit, no sign flip across 54 states), but dropping New York's institutions alone pushes the p-value to 0.07 — so the direction is real but the statistical significance is not bulletproof.

The confound the political debate usually misses: endowment size correlates strongly with BOTH total research spending (r = 0.56) and federal research dollars (r = 0.56, nearly identical) — bigger, more prestigious universities have bigger endowments and bigger federal grant portfolios for the same underlying reason (institutional scale/prestige), not because one funds the other. A bigger endowment in raw dollars does not predict a smaller federal share.

Columbia self-funds under 10% of research and depends on federal money for 74% of it despite a $13.3B endowment; Notre Dame and Michigan self-fund a third of research with smaller/comparable endowments. This matches independent reporting (Markov Processes International) that Columbia and Cornell are the most federal-grant-dependent large endowments, while Harvard and Princeton are the least.

Confidence

  • Medium-high on the descriptive shares (≈25% institutional / ≈55% federal nationally) — official NSF HERD totals, independently reproduced here from institution-level microdata.
  • Medium on the "weak correlation" finding — a real, computed result (n=575) with a leave-one-out robustness check, but the causal story (why some big-endowment schools self-fund more) was not modeled.
  • Low on the endowment-payout-specific claim — HERD's "Institution funds" category is broader than endowment payout alone (it also includes indirect-cost recovery and other unrestricted institutional funds); HERD does not publish an endowment-payout-only line, so this is a reasonable upper-bound proxy for "funded by endowment," not an exact match to the question's literal wording. NACUBO data separately shows research is only ~17.5% of what institutions actually distribute from endowments nationally (financial aid is the largest use, ~48%).

What was computed vs. relayed

  • Computed from the connector: institution-level joined FY2022 HERD × IPEDS dataset (n=575), all shares, correlations (zero-order and partial), OLS regression, and the leave-one-state-out sensitivity analysis.
  • Data quality defect found and corrected: research.nsf_herd_by_institution's own funding_source='Total' rollup silently excludes Federal dollars — it equals the sum of the five non-federal sources only. Verified nationally: SUM(funding_source=Total) for FY2022 = $43.70B, which exactly equals SUM(nonfederal components), while Federal alone = $53.97B; true total = $97.68B, matching NSF's published FY2022 HERD national total (~$97.8B) almost exactly. Taking the field at face value would understate total R&D by ~45% and badly overstate the institutional/federal funding split. This was filed via report_issue to the connector. All figures in this report use a corrected total (Federal + Institution funds + Business + State/local + Nonprofit + All other).
  • Relayed from literature (used for context/cross-validation, not as the primary computed figures): NSF/NCSES national trend commentary, SSTI summary, Markov Processes International's Columbia/Cornell vs. Harvard/Princeton federal-dependency finding, the CGO's 82-university endowment-spending-on-research study (different denominator — share of endowment payout, not share of research budget), and NACUBO's endowment-use breakdown.
