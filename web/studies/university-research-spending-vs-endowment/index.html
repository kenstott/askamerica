Federal money funds about 55% of university research; institutions' own funds (endowment income plus other internal sources) fund about 25% — pure endowment income alone is a smaller, unpublished slice of that 25%

NSF NCSES Higher Education R&D (HERD) Survey, FY2013-FY2024

Summary

Nationally, in FY2023 (the latest full HERD survey year), universities' own institutional funds financed about 25% ($27.7 billion) of the $108.8 billion in academic R&D spending, versus 55% ($59.6 billion) from the federal government — roughly a 1-to-2 ratio. But 'institution funds,' the actual NSF/NCSES survey category, is not the same thing as endowment spending: it is a composite of endowment income, unrestricted gifts, general-purpose institutional funds, cost-sharing commitments, and unrecovered indirect costs. NCSES does not publish a separate, pure 'endowment income only' dollar figure, so the true endowment-specific share is smaller than 25% and cannot be stated exactly from public federal data. The institution-funds share has been essentially flat (22-26%) for over a decade, while federal share drifted down slightly from 59% (2013) to about 55% (2023-24). It varies enormously by institution: federally grant-heavy research powerhouses like Johns Hopkins (4%) and MIT (7%) rely on institutional funds far less than endowment-rich or teaching-oriented universities like Harvard (34%) and Michigan State (36%).

What the data actually measures

The primary US source for this question is the National Science Foundation's National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics (NCSES) Higher Education Research and Development (HERD) Survey, an annual census of all US colleges and universities that spend at least $150,000/year on separately budgeted R&D. It breaks total R&D expenditure into six funding-source buckets: Federal, Institution funds, State and local government, Business, Nonprofit organizations, and All other sources.

Per NCSES's own survey instructions (confirmed via a search of NYU's and other institutions' HERD reporting guidance), the 'Institution funds' bucket is defined to include: general institutional/current operating funds separately budgeted for research, funds from a university's affiliated 501(c)(3) foundation, unrestricted gifts designated for research, endowment income, general-purpose state/local appropriations not tied to a specific grant, and unrecovered indirect costs / cost-sharing commitments the institution absorbs itself. Endowment income is one ingredient in this bucket, not the whole of it — NCSES does not publish an endowment-only line in its public tables, so the pure-endowment share of research funding cannot be isolated from HERD alone.

The national numbers, verified against the warehouse and the primary source

AskAmerica's research.nsf_herd_by_institution table (loaded from NCSES's own per-year HERD institution microdata) was queried directly and cross-checked line-for-line against NCSES's own FY2023 InfoBrief (NSF 25-313). Both agree: FY2023 total academic R&D was $108.8 billion, of which Federal funded $59.6B (54.8%) and Institution funds $27.7B (25.4%). The remaining ~20% split among State & local government (5.0%), Business (5.7%), Nonprofits (6.1%), and all other sources (2.9%).

Over FY2013-FY2024, the federal share declined modestly from 58.9% to about 55%, while the institution-funds share rose only slightly, from 22.3% to 25.7% — essentially flat for a decade, not a story of endowments rapidly displacing federal grants.

Data-quality note on the query mechanics: the raw table stores federal totals broken out by agency (HHS, DOD, NSF, DOE, NASA, USDA, other) alongside an agency='Total' rollup row, and stores a separate funding_source='Total' row that (after inspection) turned out to represent the sum of NON-federal sources only, not the grand total. Both had to be excluded/handled explicitly to avoid double- or under-counting; the final institution-level total (Federal[agency=Total] + each non-federal leaf source) reproduces NCSES's own published figures to within rounding, and Johns Hopkins' resulting FY2023 total ($3.80B) matches its known position as the #1 R&D-spending university in HERD rankings, corroborating the query logic.

How much this varies by institution

The 25% national average masks very wide variation. Universities whose research portfolios are dominated by large federal grant programs (biomedical, defense, aerospace) draw the least on institutional funds because sponsors pay directly: Johns Hopkins (largely NIH/DoD-funded, #1 in total R&D spending) used institution funds for only about 4% of its $3.8B FY2023 research spending; MIT, about 7% of $1.0B; Stanford, about 12% of $1.5B. By contrast, Harvard (about 34% of $1.4B) and Michigan State (about 36% of $844M) draw much more heavily on institutional sources — for large, wealthy private endowment universities like Harvard, this reflects real endowment-funded research capacity; for a large public land-grant university like Michigan State, 'institution funds' is more likely to include unrecovered indirect costs and general appropriations pass-through rather than endowment payout specifically. This split cannot be disentangled further without institution-level financial statements, which lie outside HERD.

Bottom line

At the sector level, federal money outfunds universities' own resources by roughly 2-to-1 (55% vs 25%) for research specifically, a ratio that has been stable for over a decade. But because HERD's 'institution funds' bucket bundles endowment income together with gifts, general funds, and absorbed indirect costs, the true endowment-only contribution to research spending is smaller than 25% nationally and is not separately reported in public federal statistics — answering the narrower 'endowment alone' version of this question precisely would require pulling audited financial statements or NACUBO endowment-spending breakdowns institution by institution, which was outside the scope of what NCSES publishes.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Quantify the share funded by the university's own endowment specifically: NCSES HERD, the primary US data source for university R&D funding by source, does not publish an endowment-only line item. Its closest category, 'Institution funds' (25.4% of FY2023 spending), bundles endowment income together with unrestricted gifts, general institutional funds, and unrecovered indirect costs. This is disclosed explicitly and the composite figure (25%) is reported as an upper bound, with the true endowment-only share stated as smaller and not isolable from public federal statistics.

    Show SQL
    SELECT institution, SUM(CASE WHEN funding_source='Federal' AND federal_agency='Total' THEN rd_expenditure_usd_thousand ELSE 0 END) AS federal, SUM(CASE WHEN funding_source='Institution funds' THEN rd_expenditure_usd_thousand ELSE 0 END) AS institution_funds FROM research.nsf_herd_by_institution WHERE "year"=2023 AND rd_field='All' GROUP BY institution

Figures are NCSES HERD survey dollars (nominal, not inflation-adjusted); 'institution funds' includes endowment income but also gifts, general funds, and unrecovered indirect costs, so it overstates true endowment-specific research funding. FY2024 figures are the most recent published (NSF 26-305).
