Workplace death rates span about 30-fold across industries and 12-fold across states; the worst combinations are agriculture, transportation and mining in sparsely populated states

BLS CFOI fatal work injuries per 100,000 FTE workers, state x industry sector, 2018-2024 (econ.cfoi_state_industry_rates)

Summary

Industry matters more than state. Across state-industry cells in 2022-2024, mean rates run from 0.9 per 100,000 (education and health) to 27 (agriculture and mining), roughly 30-fold. All-industry state rates (2020-2024 average) run from 1.2 (Rhode Island) to 13.2 (Wyoming), about 11-fold. Industry alone explains most of the variation in log rates (ANOVA F=451, n=1,711 sector cells, p<0.001). The worst persistent combinations (2020-2024 average, at least 4 years published) are Wyoming transportation and utilities (50.5), Wyoming agriculture (49.7), New Mexico mining and oil and gas (46.3), then agriculture in Indiana (41.8), Montana (40.6) and North Dakota (39.9). The single highest cell, Alaska agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting, hit 198.7 in 2024, but it is a small-employment cell.

Exclusions and rollup handling

The 'All industries' value in industry_sector is a rollup row holding each jurisdiction's overall rate, alongside the 13 sector rows. I excluded it from every sector ranking and from the sector ANOVA so it is not double counted; it is used only for the state-level ranking and appears as its own row in the industry table (mean 4.2). New York City (jurisdiction_type CITY) was excluded everywhere because it is a subset of the New York row. Excluding the rollup removes 153 of the 2022-24 rows, but does not change any sector mean; excluding NYC changes no state figure because its rows are separate.

States

Highest all-industry rates, 2020-2024 average: Wyoming 13.2, North Dakota 8.0, Alaska 7.3, West Virginia 6.5, Montana 6.3, Arkansas 6.2, Louisiana 6.1. Lowest: Rhode Island 1.2, Connecticut 1.9, New Jersey 2.3. High-fatality states are rural and weighted toward agriculture, mining, oil and trucking, so much of the state gap is industry mix.

Worst combinations

Top state-industry pairs by 2020-2024 average (4+ years published): Wyoming transportation 50.5; Wyoming agriculture 49.7; New Mexico mining 46.3; Indiana agriculture 41.8; Montana agriculture 40.6; North Dakota agriculture 39.9; Missouri agriculture 37.5; Louisiana agriculture 36.4; Michigan agriculture 35.2; New Mexico transportation 35.0. Single-year peaks are higher (DC construction 72.0 in 2022; North Dakota mining 63.7 in 2022) but small cells are volatile.

Caveats

  • Most state-by-sector cells are unpublished (BLS suppresses small counts), so rankings cover only published cells; mining exists for 9 states only.
  • Rates are not mix-adjusted; BLS warns state rates are not comparable across industry mixes. Death counts are not held here.
  • Small cells swing year to year (Wyoming agriculture 29 to 83).
  • Some years label education and health slightly differently, splitting that sector's ANOVA group in two (13 rows); conclusions are unaffected.
  • The ANOVA tests industry differences only; no formal state-versus-industry variance decomposition was run.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Formal variance decomposition of state vs industry: Only industry ANOVA run; state share not computed.
