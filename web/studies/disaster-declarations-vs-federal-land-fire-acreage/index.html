Federally managed land burns about 4.5x the annual rate of state/private land per acre

CRS/NIFC Wildland Fire Statistics, 2015-2022; federal land ~640M acres (28%) vs. state/local/private ~1,630M acres (72%) of the 2.27B-acre US land base

Summary

Federally managed land burns at roughly 4.5 times the annual rate of state, local, and private land, acre for acre. Over 2015-2022, an average of about 5.1 million acres of federal land burned each year against a federal land base of roughly 640 million acres (about 0.80%/year), while about 2.9 million acres of nonfederal land burned each year against a nonfederal base of roughly 1,630 million acres (about 0.18%/year). The year-by-year ratio ranged from about 2.8x to 7.0x, with no year showing nonfederal land burning at a higher rate than federal land. Raw acreage totals tell a similar but cruder story: federal land, only 28% of the US land base, accounted for about 64% of all acres burned nationally over this period.

Important limitation: this is a national-level comparison and has not been adjusted for the fact that federal land is heavily concentrated in the arid West, which burns more regardless of who owns it — so part of the federal/nonfederal gap likely reflects geography and climate as well as ownership and management. See the Limitations section.

Source: NIFC/NICC Wildland Fire Summary and Statistics annual reports, compiled in CRS IF10244 'Wildfire Statistics' (versions dated Jan. 2021 and June 2023). 'Federal' includes Forest Service, DOI (BLM, NPS, FWS, BIA), and other federal agencies; 'Nonfederal' is state, local, and private land. Federal land base of ~640 million acres (28% of the 2.27 billion acre US total) is from CRS R42346 'Federal Land Ownership: Overview and Data' and is held fixed across years — it changes only slightly year to year.

Two things the raw fire counts complicate rather than clarify: nonfederal land actually has more fires — roughly 80% of the nation's wildfire count occurs on nonfederal land — but federal fires run much larger on average (CBO's own analysis put the average federal fire at more than 5x the size of an average nonfederal fire over 1991-2021). So the federal/nonfederal gap in burned acreage is driven by fire size, not fire frequency: nonfederal land ignites more often but each fire stays smaller (closer to people, water, and suppression resources); federal land, especially in Alaska and the interior West, has fewer ignitions that grow far larger before they are contained.

Limitations — a corpus defect blocked the finer-grained analysis this question deserves

The one AskAmerica table built to answer exactly this question — disasters.wildfire_perimeters (NIFC WFIGS perimeters, carrying per-fire acres burned alongside point-of-origin landowner_kind: Federal/Private/Other) — is broken this session. Every query that projects any column (SELECT *, a single named column, or a GROUP BY) fails with Binder Error: table "unnamed_subquery" has 17 columns available but 20 columns specified. Only a bare count(*) succeeds (returning a suspiciously round 10,000 rows against the table's documented ~39,000). A live schema rebuild did not fix it. This has been filed as a defect.

That table mattered here specifically because the right way to test an ownership effect is a within-state paired comparison — computing each state's own federal-vs-nonfederal burn rate and running a paired significance test across states — rather than a single national ratio, precisely because federal land is disproportionately Western and arid, and the West burns more regardless of who owns it. CRS's own regional breakdown shows this confound qualitatively: in 2022, 64% of the acreage burned in the West was federal, versus only 15% of the acreage burned in the East. With the corpus table broken, that state-level paired test could not be run this session, so the 4.5x figure above should be read as a national association, not a causally isolated ownership effect.
