Federal land burns roughly 4.7x more acreage per year, relative to its size, than state and private land

NICC Wildland Fire Summary data via CRS Report IF10244; federal land base via CRS R42346

Summary

Federally managed land burns considerably more, both in total acreage and per acre owned, than state and private land. Averaged over 2017-2021, federal land (managed mainly by the Forest Service and Interior Department agencies) accounted for 65% of all wildfire acreage burned nationally despite generating only 22% of the fire count — meaning federal fires are much rarer but run far larger, averaging 392 acres per fire versus 62 acres per fire on nonfederal land, a 6.3x difference. Once each ownership class's burned acreage is scaled against how much land it actually holds (roughly 640 million federal acres versus 1.63 billion state/local/private acres), federal land burns at an estimated 0.82% of its acreage per year, versus 0.18% per year on nonfederal land — about 4.7 times the rate.

Why federal land burns more, and more per fire

The gap is not mainly about ignition frequency — nonfederal land actually catches fire more often (roughly 3.4x as many fires as federal land in this period). The gap is about fire SIZE: federal land, concentrated in the more remote, higher-elevation, and more heavily forested West and Alaska, tends to host fires that grow far larger before containment, partly by design (many federal wilderness/backcountry fires are managed for resource benefit rather than suppressed immediately) and partly because of terrain, fuel loads, and lower population density delaying detection and access. CRS's own regional breakdown confirms this: in 2021, 80% of the acreage burned in the West (where most federal land sits) was on federal land, while 73% of the acreage burned in the East (mostly nonfederal land) was on nonfederal land — ownership and region are heavily confounded, so this comparison should not be read as pure land-management-quality difference; it is partly a geography effect.

Method and sourcing

Acres burned by ownership comes directly from Table 1 of CRS Report IF10244 ("Wildfire Statistics," version 65, updated December 2, 2022), which itself compiles NICC (National Interagency Coordination Center) Wildland Fire Summary and Statistics annual reports. Data used: 2017-2021, the five most recent complete years in that CRS vintage. Federal fires/acres = Forest Service (FS) + Department of Interior (DOI, including BLM/NPS/FWS/BIA) + Other; nonfederal = state, local, and private land, which states are responsible for suppressing except under federal cooperative agreements.

Total federal fires (2017-2021): 67,000; total federal acres burned: 26.3 million. Total nonfederal fires: 231,200; total nonfederal acres burned: 14.4 million. Average fire size = total acres ÷ total fire count within each ownership class.

Land-area denominators for the per-acre burn rate come from CRS Report R42346 ("Federal Land Ownership: Overview and Data"): the federal government owns approximately 640 million acres, about 28% of the 2.27 billion acres of land in the United States. Nonfederal land is the remainder, approximately 1.63 billion acres. Annual burn rate = (total acres burned in that ownership class ÷ 5 years) ÷ (that ownership class's total land base).

Corroborating cross-check: this connector's own wildfire-perimeter table

The AskAmerica warehouse's own disasters.wildfire_perimeters table (NIFC WFIGS interagency fire perimeters, point-of-origin landowner_kind) shows the same DIRECTION of effect — federal-origin wildfires average 522 acres versus 297 acres for private-origin fires in its loaded sample — but at a much smaller magnitude (1.75x vs. the CRS-sourced 6.3x) and should not be treated as an independent confirmation of the exact ratio. This table is capped at 10,000 total rows (versus roughly 39,000 perimeters described in its own catalog entry), spans only fire_year 2018-2023, and its `final_acres` field is populated for only a small fraction of rows (821 of 4,390 Private-origin fires; 26 of 4,443 Federal-origin fires) — all reasons the CRS/NICC national annual totals, not this table, are used as the primary source above. This is a genuine data-coverage gap in the connector worth flagging, not a contradiction of the CRS finding.

Caveats

  • The per-acre burn-rate comparison uses each ownership class's TOTAL land base (all federal acres nationwide vs. all nonfederal acres nationwide), not just fire-prone/vegetated land. Since federal land is disproportionately concentrated in fire-prone Western forests and rangeland, and nonfederal land includes large amounts of land that essentially never burns (urban, suburban, cropland), this comparison is a genuine national aggregate but is not a controlled, like-for-like fuels/climate comparison — it does not isolate a pure "federal management" effect from the geography federal land happens to occupy.
  • Figures are a 2017-2021 average (the most recent 5-year window in the fetched CRS vintage); the ratio varies year to year (e.g., federal share of acreage burned ranged from 66% in 2017-2018 to 70% in 2020) but federal land consistently burns a majority of total acreage in every one of these years.
  • "Federal" fire responsibility in the CRS/NICC data is defined by AGENCY jurisdiction (Forest Service + Interior Department units), not a spatial land-ownership map; "nonfederal" bundles state, local, and private land together and cannot be split into state-only vs. private-only from this source.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Distinguish state and private land as the comparison group: The primary CRS/NICC source only splits fires into federal vs. combined 'nonfederal (state, local, private)' — no source found in this session breaks nonfederal further into state-only vs. private-only acreage burned nationally, so that finer split could not be reported
