In Texas, Shell's Deer Park chemical plant and Huntsman's Port Neches C4 plant lead in EPA enforcement actions for air/water permit violations

EPA ECHO facility compliance snapshot, current as of 2026; Texas chosen as a representative large industrial state

Answer

Texas was used as the representative state (the question did not name one). Among Texas industrial facilities EPA's ECHO database currently flags as being in 'Significant Violation' of the Clean Air Act and/or Clean Water Act, the plants with the most formal enforcement actions on record are:

  1. Shell Chemical, Deer Park — 11 formal enforcement actions, 11 inspections, $393,340 in penalties; flagged for both CAA ('Violation Unaddressed; State Has Lead Enforcement') and CWA ('Violation Identified')
  2. Huntsman Petrochemical Corp – C4 Plant, Port Neches — 10 formal actions, $12.6 million in penalties
  3. LyondellBasell Chemical, Channelview — 9 formal actions, $3.7 million in penalties
  4. Safety-Kleen Systems, Denton — 9 formal actions (24 inspections, the most of any facility on the list)
  5. Port Arthur Plant (Motiva), Port Arthur — 8 formal actions, $3.7 million in penalties

Independent verification: a 2024 investigative report (Oil & Gas Watch / Sierra Club litigation record) found Shell's Deer Park chemical plant had roughly 1,946 recorded environmental violations over the prior decade, with 12 of the last 12 quarters in "high priority violation" status under the Clean Air Act — corroborating that this facility is a genuine chronic violator, not an artifact of the proxy metric used here.

Method and an important data-gap caveat

Source: environment.epa_facilities / its facility_compliance view — EPA's ECHO Exporter, a weekly-refreshed current-status snapshot per facility (~1.5M facilities nationally). This table does NOT carry a literal 'number of times exceeded permit limits' figure — EPA's own richer metric for that (Quarters in Noncompliance / effluent exceedance counts from DMR data, e.g. Shell Deer Park's '12 of 12 quarters') is not loaded in this corpus. As the best available proxy, this analysis ranked facilities by formal_action_count (the number of formal EPA/state enforcement actions on record) among facilities currently flagged compliance_status = 'Significant Violation', which combines CAA, CWA, RCRA and SDWA status. This is a proxy, not a raw violation-frequency count, and is called out as such rather than presented as the number the question literally asked for.

A separate defect: the table's snc_flag (significant-noncompliance flag) column returned only the value 'N' for every one of the 170,713 Texas rows — it appears unpopulated/stuck for this state in the loaded snapshot, so severity was judged from the text compliance_status field instead, which does vary as expected.

Recency: date_last_inspection for the top-ranked facilities falls in 2025–2026 (e.g. Shell Chemical 05/12/2026, Huntsman 07/22/2026), confirming these are active, recently-inspected records, not stale ones.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Exceeded ... the most times (a frequency count): The loaded EPA ECHO table is a current-status snapshot without a per-facility count of how many times limits were exceeded (EPA's own quarters-in-noncompliance/DMR-exceedance metric for that isn't loaded here). Ranked instead by formal_action_count among facilities flagged 'Significant Violation', explicitly labeled as a proxy, and cross-checked against an independent published violation count for the top facility.
