Highest overdose-death / treatment-supply mismatch: Mason Co WA, Scioto Co OH, Cecil Co MD lead among mid-to-large counties

CDC VSRR county overdose deaths (12 months ending Dec 2025) vs. CMS behavioral-health facility counts, counties ≥65,000 population

Summary

Using CDC provisional county overdose-death data (12 months ending December 2025) and a CMS facility-count proxy for behavioral-health/addiction treatment capacity, the counties with the largest gap between overdose burden and treatment supply — among counties with population 65,000+ — are Mason County, WA (61.2 deaths/100k, 0 listed facilities), Scioto County, OH (49.2/100k, 0 facilities), Cecil County, MD (45.0/100k, 0 facilities), Roanoke city, VA (43.6/100k, 0 facilities), and Marion County, OH (43.0/100k, 0 facilities). Every county in the top 15 by this mismatch score has zero facilities in our proxy category despite death rates roughly 2-3x the concurrent national average. This pattern — rural and small-metro counties with high overdose mortality and near-zero visible treatment infrastructure — closely matches the peer-reviewed "risk-availability mismatch" finding of Rizk et al. (PLOS ONE, 2024), which used the actual SAMHSA Behavioral Health Treatment Locator and found 35.1% of U.S. counties had no listed opioid treatment facility in 2019.

Important limitation: This corpus (AskAmerica/govdata) does not carry the SAMHSA Behavioral Health Treatment Locator or the N-SSATS/N-MHSS facility census that the published literature on this exact question uses — confirmed by a catalog search returning no match for "SAMHSA," "licensed," or "locator." The facility count used here is a proxy: CMS Provider-of-Services (POS) categories 06 (psychiatric/behavioral residential treatment) and 19 (community mental health center), which is narrower than SAMHSA's facility count (2,716 facilities nationally here vs. 11,111 in the SAMHSA-sourced 2019 academic study) and omits standalone methadone clinics (opioid treatment programs) and office-based buprenorphine prescribers entirely. The ranking below should be read as directionally consistent with — but not a replacement for — the SAMHSA-based literature.

Method and exclusion disclosure

Overdose deaths: health.cdc_county_overdose_deaths (CDC VSRR, provisional, 12-month-ending window), year=2025, month-ending 2025-12-31.

Exclusion disclosure (provisional_drug_overdose IS NOT NULL): the filter provisional_drug_overdose IS NOT NULL was applied because CDC suppresses (sets to NULL) any county's 12-month death count under 10, for privacy. Of the 3,144 county rows in the 2025-12-31 window, this predicate excludes 1,630 rows (52%) — these are the smaller-death-count, generally lower-population counties. Without this filter, no death rate is computable for those rows at all (NULL, not zero), so they could not be included in either the headline ranking or the '265 of 844' zero-facility statistic below; both are therefore computed only over the 1,514 counties (3,144 minus the 1,630 excluded) that report a non-suppressed count. This exclusion is not a random sample: it disproportionately removes the smallest, most rural counties, which independent rural-access literature (cited below) identifies as the places most likely to be severe treatment deserts — meaning the true worst-mismatch counties nationally are plausibly smaller places this analysis cannot see at all.

Exclusion disclosure (county_fips IS NOT NULL, applied twice, on two different tables): on health.cms_pos_facilities (filtered to provider_category_code IN ('06','19')), this predicate excludes 1 facility record nationally (out of 2,717 in that category) that lacks a resolvable state/county FIPS code; that 1 excluded facility is simply absent from whichever county it should have counted toward, which can only understate a facility count, never overstate one, and with n=1 nationally has no material effect on any ranking here. On census.acs_population (year=2023, geography='county'), the same-named predicate excludes 0 rows — every county row in that vintage already carries a non-null county_fips, so this filter changed nothing on the population side.

Treatment-facility proxy: health.cms_pos_facilities, provider_category_code IN ('06','19') (psychiatric/behavioral residential treatment facilities and community mental health centers), counted per county.

Population: census.acs_population, 2023 5-year ACS vintage (most recent available; the overdose-death data is 2025, so there is a ~2-year vintage gap between numerator and denominator years — a real limitation, though county population changes slowly enough that this does not materially change rankings).

Mismatch score: for each county with population ≥65,000, computed the percentile rank of its overdose-death rate per 100k minus the percentile rank of its facility rate per 100k, following the same directional logic as the SAMHSA-locator-based "risk-availability mismatch" definition in Rizk et al. (2024) (national thresholds: 20.67 deaths/100k, 4.72 facilities/100k; this analysis uses within-sample percentile ranks rather than those exact thresholds, since our facility base is a different, narrower census). Restricting to population ≥65,000 avoids small-county rate instability but means true rural "treatment deserts" are further undercounted here; see Limitations.

Cross-checked against the literature: of 844 counties with population ≥65,000 in this corpus, 265 (31%) have zero facilities in our narrower proxy category — comparable in direction, if not in exact magnitude, to the 35.1% of all 3,130 U.S. counties found to have zero SAMHSA-listed OUD facilities in the peer-reviewed study.

What the published literature adds

The closest matching peer-reviewed study — Rizk, Saini, Kim, Pathan & Qato, "County-level factors associated with a mismatch between opioid overdose mortality and availability of opioid treatment facilities," PLOS ONE, April 2024 — asks essentially this question directly, using SAMHSA's own Behavioral Health Treatment Services Locator (11,111 facilities providing or allowing medications for opioid use disorder, 2018-2019) against CDC NVSS county overdose mortality. Their national thresholds: mean overdose mortality of 20.67/100k and mean facility rate of 4.72/100k in 2018-2019; 1,203 of 3,130 counties (38.4%) had above-average mortality, and 1,098 (35.1%) had zero facilities. Their adjusted regression found metropolitan counties, not rural ones, had higher odds of being "mismatch" counties (OR 1.85) after controlling for other factors — counter to the simple rural-desert narrative — along with higher shares of white residents, unemployment, and uninsured residents.

Separately, rural-access research (PMC9019866) and the American Institutes for Research review report that roughly 88.6% of large rural counties lack sufficient opioid treatment programs, 30% of rural residents (vs. 2% of urban residents) lack a buprenorphine provider within reasonable reach, and methadone clinics in rural counties studied averaged a 49-minute drive versus 17 minutes for primary care — a distance/access dimension this connector's facility count (which has no location/drive-time data joined) cannot capture at all.

A companion operations-research paper (Sun et al., Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 2025) models optimal statewide allocation of new treatment centers specifically to minimize this same county-level mismatch, reinforcing that mismatch, not aggregate national capacity, is the actionable policy lever.

Limitations

  • Facility proxy is narrow and undercounts true treatment supply. This corpus has no SAMHSA Behavioral Health Treatment Locator, N-SSATS, or N-MHSS facility census — confirmed absent by catalog search. The CMS POS categories used (psychiatric/behavioral residential treatment, community mental health center) are CMS-certified facilities only; they exclude standalone opioid treatment programs (methadone clinics), office-based buprenorphine prescribers, and non-CMS-certified outpatient counseling — all central to the SAMHSA-based definition of "opioid treatment facility" in the cited literature. This is why the top-15 list above is saturated at zero facilities: 2,716 facilities nationally in this proxy vs. 11,111 in the SAMHSA-sourced academic study.
  • Vintage mismatch. Overdose deaths are the 12-month period ending December 2025; population is 2023 ACS (most recent available in this corpus); the CMS facility snapshot is the current 2026 quarter (CMS replaces this file wholesale each quarter and does not retain history).
  • DC is included as a county-equivalent alongside real counties; it is a fully urban city-state, not comparable to rural or suburban counties on this list.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • 'Licensed addiction-treatment providers' specifically (not a general health facility count): This corpus has no SAMHSA Behavioral Health Treatment Locator, N-SSATS, or licensed-addiction-treatment-provider table (confirmed via catalog search: 'samhsa' and 'licensed' return zero matches). Substituted a CMS Provider-of-Services proxy (psychiatric/behavioral residential treatment + community mental health center categories), which is narrower and excludes standalone opioid treatment programs and buprenorphine prescribers -- explicitly labeled as a proxy throughout, with the true SAMHSA-based national study (Rizk et al. 2024) cited for comparison and methodology.
  • 'Nearby' — implies some geographic/distance dimension, not just same-county presence: No facility location/drive-time data is joined here; 'nearby' is operationalized as 'within the same county' only, which the cited rural-access literature notes can still miss real distance barriers (e.g. a facility located at the county's edge, 45+ minutes from residents on the other side). This is a coarser operationalization than true proximity.

WITH od AS (

  FROM health.cdc_county_overdose_deaths
  WHERE year='2025' AND monthendingdate='2025-12-31' AND provisional_drug_overdose IS NOT NULL
),
pop AS (
  SELECT county_fips, total_population AS population
  FROM census.acs_population
  WHERE "year"='2023' AND county_fips IS NOT NULL AND geography = 'county'
),
fac AS (
  SELECT county_fips, count(*) AS n_facilities
  FROM health.cms_pos_facilities
  WHERE provider_category_code IN ('06','19') AND county_fips IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY county_fips
)
SELECT od.fips, od.countyname, od.state_name, od.deaths, p.population,
       COALESCE(f.n_facilities,0) AS n_facilities,
       od.deaths / (p.population/100000.0) AS deaths_per_100k,
       COALESCE(f.n_facilities,0) / (p.population/100000.0) AS facilities_per_100k,
       od.deaths / (COALESCE(f.n_facilities,0)+0.5) AS deaths_per_facility
FROM od
JOIN pop p ON p.county_fips = od.fips
LEFT JOIN fac f ON f.county_fips = od.fips
WHERE p.population >= 50000
ORDER BY deaths_per_facility DESC
