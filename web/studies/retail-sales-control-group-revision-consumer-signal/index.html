Control-group retail sales are routinely revised by a few tenths of a point, enough to flip a month's read, but no public series computes the exact average

Sources: Census MARTS advance report (Apr 2016 Table 3), Reuters via Finance & Commerce (Nov 17, 2026), news-reported prior-month revisions; percent month-over-month

Answer

Revisions to the control group (retail sales excluding autos, gasoline, building materials and food services) are typically a few tenths of a percentage point in the month-over-month change, and they can be large relative to the change itself. The clearest recent case: September 2026 was first printed at +0.1% and revised to +0.5%, a 0.4 point swing that turned a weak read into a solid one. I could not find a published, systematic average-absolute-revision statistic for the control group specifically, and the warehouse holds only current-vintage retail sales (econ.fred_indicators RSXFS), not real-time vintages, so I did not compute one. Treat the sizes below as indicative, not measured averages.

What the evidence shows

  • September 2026: first print +0.1%, revised to +0.5% in the October report (Reuters, Nov 17, 2026, read directly). Consensus for October was +0.4% and the actual was +0.6%.
  • August 2026: first print +1.4%; search summaries report a revision to about +1.2%, but another source says +1.1% to +1.3%. Conflicting, unverified.
  • January 2020 report: December control group revised from +0.5% to +0.2% (forexlive via search snippet, not opened).
  • Census's own advance report publishes Table 3 with 'average absolute revision' of the advance-to-final month-over-month change by industry. In the April 2016 report, total retail and food services averaged 0.2 points, building materials 0.7, gasoline 0.5, nonstore 0.4, general merchandise 0.3, health and personal care 0.3. Control group is not a published line, but it is made of these components, so a typical revision of roughly 0.2 to 0.4 points is plausible. That is my inference, not a Census number.
  • Direction is not one-sided: the examples include both upward and downward revisions. A search-found analysis of headline retail sales claimed a downward skew, but I could not open it (HTTP 403).

Does it matter for reading consumer strength?

Yes, for single months. Monthly control-group growth in a normal month is around 0.3 to 0.5%, so a 0.2 to 0.4 point revision is the same size as the signal. A first print of +0.1% and a revised +0.5% are different stories. The control group feeds the goods component of GDP consumption, so revisions change the quarter's tracking estimates; Goldman raised its Q4 GDP forecast after the October report. Practical reading: use three-month averages (Econoday makes the same recommendation), look at the prior-month revision as part of each release, and remember the annual benchmark revision can shift levels more than any monthly revision. Also, retail sales are nominal and exclude services, so revisions are only one limit on reading consumer strength.

Limits

Small, non-systematic sample (three data points, one with conflicting sources). The Census revision table is from 2016 and covers advance-to-final changes for industries, not the control group. A proper answer needs Census's real-time vintages (advance versus third estimate for each month, 2010 onward), which are not in this warehouse. A data request would be needed.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Systematic computed revision statistic from real-time vintages: Warehouse has no control group vintages; Census vintage files not retrieved.
