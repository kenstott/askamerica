Federal PILT payments to counties averaged $305,137 per county in FY2025, ranging up to $232.59 per resident

DOI Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT), FY2025, joined to Census PEP 2023 county population

Summary

The federal government paid counties (and equivalent local governments) $644.8 million nationwide in FY2025 through the Department of the Interior's Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT) program — the mechanism that compensates local governments for the property-tax base that tax-exempt federal land removes. Across the roughly 2,279 counties, boroughs, and parishes that received a payment, the average was $305,137 per county. Per resident, the amount varies enormously: it depends on a formula combining federal acreage, population, and other federal payments already received, subject to a statutory ceiling of $232.59 per resident for FY2025 — a cap that ten different sparsely-populated, heavily-federal-land counties hit exactly (Esmeralda County NV, four Alaska boroughs, Custer and Lincoln Counties, Clark County ID, and Rich and Wayne Counties UT). Densely populated counties with proportionally less federal land receive far less per person; averaged across every PILT-receiving county's residents, the figure is $2.52 per resident.

What PILT is and how much it totals

PILT is separate from federal mineral-royalty revenue sharing (ONRR) — it compensates counties purely for the property tax they cannot collect on land the federal government owns within their borders (national forests, BLM land, national parks, wildlife refuges, etc.), independent of whether that land generates any resource revenue. Payments are disbursed annually via pilt.doi.gov.

Queried directly from lands.pilt_county_payments (DOI's own PILT table, FY1999–2026 loaded): FY2025 total payments were $644,769,260 across 2,279 county-level areas (all 50 states plus DC, Guam, the Virgin Islands, and Puerto Rico), covering 606.7 million federal acres. FY2026 payments (already disbursed by June 2026, per PILT's usual cycle) total $733.0M, up from FY2025 — FY2025 is used here as the most recently complete prior-year figure with matching FY2023 Census population data for the per-capita calculation.

Average payment per county

Matching PILT's county-level area names to Census TIGER county geography (state + normalized county name) matched 1,971 of the 2,279 FY2025 areas — 93% of total dollars ($601.4M of $644.8M); the unmatched remainder is mostly New England towns, independent cities, and island territories that PILT reports at a different area grain than standard counties. Among the 1,971 matched counties, the average payment was $305,137, but the distribution is extremely skewed: the highest-paid counties (Yuma County AZ, Natrona County WY, Mohave County AZ, Washoe County NV, and others) all cluster near $4.5–4.65 million — evidence of hitting PILT's statutory per-county payment ceiling — while many small counties receive only a few thousand dollars.

Payment per resident

Joining to Census Population Estimates Program county population (2023, the most recent vintage loaded in census.pep_population) and dividing FY2025 payment by county population: the national average across the 1,971 matched, PILT-receiving counties is $2.52 per resident ($601.4M / 239.0M residents of those counties). If spread instead across the entire U.S. resident population (338.1 million in 2023, including residents of counties with no federal land and therefore no PILT payment), the figure falls to about $1.91 per resident — the more meaningful number is the $2.52 figure, since PILT dollars flow only to counties that actually hold federal land.

Per-resident amounts range from a small fraction of a cent in large, mostly-private-land counties to a hard ceiling of $232.59 per resident in the smallest, most federally-dominated counties — Esmeralda County NV (population 736), four Alaska boroughs (Aleutians East, Petersburg, Wrangell, Yakutat), Custer County ID, Lincoln County NV, Clark County ID, and Rich and Wayne Counties UT all land on that exact ceiling for FY2025, confirming it as PILT's statutory per-capita payment cap rather than a coincidence of the formula.
