Federal PILT payments to counties: $733M in FY2026, averaging $2.87 per resident nationally — but far more in sparsely populated land-heavy counties

DOI Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT), FY2026, joined to ACS 2023 county population

Summary

The federal government does not pay counties rent or a market price for land it holds; instead it makes an annual Payment in Lieu of Taxes (PILT), administered by the Department of the Interior, that compensates local governments for the property-tax base that tax-exempt federal land removes. In FY2026 DOI paid out $733.0 million nationwide across 2,281 reporting areas, covering about 606.7 million acres of federal land. Averaged over the residents of the counties that receive a payment, that comes to $2.87 per resident nationally — but this national average badly understates the burden/benefit at the local level: the median receiving county gets only about $0.85 per resident, while the county with the highest per-capita payment, sparsely populated Kenedy County, TX (population 52), receives roughly $1,515 per resident because a fixed-formula payment is divided across almost no people.

How PILT payments work

PILT is not a per-acre rent. The payment to each county is set by a statutory formula (31 U.S.C. §6902) that factors in the county's federal acreage, its population, and payments the county already receives from other federal revenue-sharing programs (e.g. Forest Service and BLM receipt-sharing) — subject to a population-based per-county ceiling. That ceiling is visible in the data: the ten counties with the largest total payments (Yuma County AZ, Mohave County AZ, Natrona County WY, Clark County NV, Kenai Peninsula Borough AK, Washoe County NV, Eddy County NM, Pima County AZ, Otero County NM, Gila County AZ) all land in a narrow $4.70M-$4.77M band regardless of how much federal acreage they hold, because they have each hit the formula's population-based cap.

Total payment vs. per-resident payment

Because the payment scales partly with population, the counties receiving the largest checks are populous Western counties near their statutory cap. The counties receiving the most per resident are the opposite: small-population counties with substantial federal acreage, where even a modest total payment divides into a large per-capita figure. Kenedy County, TX ($78,769 total / 52 residents = $1,515/resident) is the extreme outlier; the rest of the per-capita top ten (Yakutat Borough AK, Petroleum & Garfield Counties MT, Daggett County UT, Harding County SD, Mineral & San Juan Counties CO, Eureka County NV, Lincoln County ID) cluster around $250-$325 per resident, still roughly 100x the median.

National and typical figures

Across the 2,013 counties that both received a PILT payment and matched to a standard county FIPS with ACS 2023 population data:

  • Population-weighted national average: $2.87 per resident ($705.3M paid / 245.6M residents in receiving counties)
  • Simple average across counties: $22.63 per resident — much higher than the weighted figure because small rural counties with high per-capita payments pull the unweighted mean up sharply
  • Median county: $0.85 per resident — most residents of PILT-receiving counties live in populous counties where the payment is a small fraction of a dollar per person

The gap between the $2.87 population-weighted figure and the $22.63 simple average is itself a finding: PILT dollars are large in per-resident terms almost entirely in a long tail of thinly populated counties, not for the typical resident of a receiving county.

Coverage and what was excluded from the per-capita figures

Of $733.0M in FY2026 PILT dollars nationwide, 265 of the 2,281 reporting areas, carrying $27.8M (3.8% of the national total), were excluded from every per-capita and county-level figure in this report because they do not resolve to a standard 5-digit county FIPS code (the SQL filter county_fips IS NOT NULL) — these are New England towns/plantations paid below the county level, independent cities, and DC/Guam/Puerto Rico/Virgin Islands territory-wide rows for which no standard county-FIPS equivalent exists in Census geography. This is a real gap in standard county geography for those area types, not a data defect or an arbitrary drop, and it means the $705.3M / $2.87-per-resident figures above describe the 2,016 FIPS-matched areas (96.2% of national dollars), not literally every dollar DOI disburses. Including the excluded $27.8M would raise the national total to $733.0M but cannot be converted to a per-capita figure without those areas' own population counts, which the standard county population series does not carry for sub-county towns and territory-wide rows.

Separately, population is ACS 2023 5-year estimates (the most recent vintage loaded; ACS itself lags about 2 years), while PILT figures are FY2026 (the payment year running Oct 2025-Sep 2026) — a roughly 2-3 year vintage gap between numerator and denominator that is standard for this kind of ratio and does not materially change the ranking, since county populations move slowly relative to PILT's fixed-formula payment jumps.
