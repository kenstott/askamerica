PILT is the mechanism: DOI pays counties $3.55 (or $0.51) per acre of federal land, capped by population — nationally $733.0M in FY2026, but per-resident payments range from under $1 to nearly $70

Source: U.S. Department of the Interior, Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT) program, FY2026 National Summary (doi.gov/pilt)

Summary

The federal government pays counties (and equivalent local governments) for the untaxable federal land within their borders through the Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILT) program, administered by the Department of the Interior. In FY2026, DOI distributed $733.0 million to more than 1,900 counties and local governments across 49 states, DC, Guam, Puerto Rico, and the Virgin Islands, covering 606.7 million acres of federal entitlement land (national forests, BLM land, national parks, wildlife refuges, Army Corps/Reclamation water projects, and more).

There is no single flat rate. Each county's payment is computed from a statutory formula — the higher of (A) $3.55 per acre of qualifying federal land, minus certain other federal land-revenue payments the county received the prior year, or (B) $0.51 per acre with no deduction — subject to a population-based ceiling. Because the formula runs on acreage divided against population, the per-resident payment varies enormously: from under a dollar per person in populous, low-federal-acreage states like Alabama, to roughly $57.66 per resident in Alaska and $68.65 per resident in Wyoming — states with vast federal acreage and small populations. Even within one state, county-to-county results with identical formula rates differ by 5-6x, as illustrated below with four real Alabama counties.

How the payment is actually calculated

PILT (31 U.S.C. Chapter 69, enacted 1976, amended 1994) is not a lease or rent payment — it is compensation for the county's loss of property-tax base and for the county services (roads, fire, police, search-and-rescue) that federal land still requires. For each unit of local government, DOI computes:

  • Alternative A: $3.55 (FY2026 rate) × entitlement acres, minus certain federal revenue-sharing payments (e.g., Forest Service timber receipts, Mineral Leasing Act payments, Secure Rural Schools) the county received the prior fiscal year.
  • Alternative B: $0.51 (FY2026 rate) × entitlement acres, with no deduction.

DOI pays whichever alternative is larger, subject to a population ceiling that caps the payment per resident on a sliding scale — from $238.87/resident for populations of 5,000 or fewer down to $95.55/resident at a population of 50,000 (Table 2 of the FY2026 National Summary). Both the per-acre rates and the population-ceiling table are adjusted every year for inflation (CPI). Separate, smaller Section 6904/6905 payments cover land recently added to national parks or wilderness areas, based on 1% of the land's fair market value at acquisition. In FY2026, prior-year deductions dropped sharply (a lapse in Secure Rural Schools authorization), which is why national PILT payments jumped from $644.8 million (FY2025) to $733.0 million (FY2026) even though acreage barely changed.

For comparison, spreading the entire $733.0 million national total over the whole U.S. population (~336 million) works out to about $2.18 per American — but that framing is misleading, since the money goes only to the specific counties that host federal land, not to all residents nationally.

Some Alabama counties receive far less: e.g. Conecuh County received $0 in FY2026 (no qualifying acreage above the payment floor), and several counties (Coffee, Elmore, Jackson, Lamar) received only a few hundred dollars because their federal acreage is minimal. The DOI report lists every one of the roughly 1,900 recipient counties and local governments nationwide by name; the examples above are drawn directly from the published FY2026 Alabama schedule as an illustration of the range within a single state.

Where to look up any specific county

DOI publishes the full county-by-county payment table (and the underlying acreage) for every fiscal year at doi.gov/pilt, with a searchable lookup at pilt.doi.gov/counties.cfm and the full FY2026 PDF (304 pages, county payments start on report page 22, acreage detail starts on report page 212) at the National Summary link cited below. This report quotes the national totals, the formula and rate table, the full 53-jurisdiction state table, and a full state's (Alabama's) county rows directly from that primary document; per-resident figures were computed here using AskAmerica's census.acs_population table (2023 ACS 5-year estimates) joined to the DOI payment figures — this is the only computed step layered on top of DOI's own published numbers.
