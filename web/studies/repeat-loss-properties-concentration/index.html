A small slice of NFIP properties drives roughly half of all flood-claim dollars

FEMA/GAO, GAO-26-109045 (March 2026) plus a live 2026-09-26 pull from FEMA's OpenFEMA NFIP Multiple Loss Properties dataset

Summary

Federal flood claims are highly concentrated in a small number of repeat-flooding properties. According to FEMA, properties that have flooded and been paid on two or more claims make up only about 2.5% of NFIP policies but have received 48% of all NFIP claim dollars ever paid (as of December 2021, the most recent figure FEMA has published). An even narrower tier — "severe repetitive loss" properties, with 4+ claims over $5,000 each or losses exceeding the property's value — is more concentrated still. A live pull from FEMA's own database today (2026-09-26) found 96,392 currently-insured repetitive-loss properties and 13,523 currently-insured severe-repetitive-loss properties nationwide, and the total population of unmitigated repetitive-loss properties has grown from about 131,000 in 2009 to about 227,000 in 2026 — up 73% — despite decades of federal buyout and mitigation spending.

The headline concentration figure

The Government Accountability Office's March 2026 testimony on flood risk mitigation (GAO-26-109045) states plainly: "unmitigated repetitive loss properties make up about 2.5 percent of NFIP policies, but 48 percent of NFIP claims by dollar value have been paid to properties with two or more losses," with the 48% figure dated to December 2021 — GAO notes this is the most recent figure FEMA has reported and it has not been refreshed since. This is roughly a 19x overrepresentation: a property in the repetitive-loss group is, on average, receiving about nineteen times its "fair share" of claim dollars relative to its share of the insured book.

As of January 2026, FEMA counted 112,640 NFIP-insured repetitive-loss properties nationwide, against 4.58 million NFIP policies in force as of December 2025 — consistent with the ~2.5% figure. Separately, a live query run today against FEMA's OpenFEMA "NFIP Multiple Loss Properties" dataset (as-of date 2026-08-02, a few months later than GAO's January 2026 snapshot) returned 96,392 currently-insured properties flagged as NFIP Repetitive Loss and 13,523 currently-insured properties flagged as NFIP Severe Repetitive Loss — the same order of magnitude as GAO's figure, with the modest difference plausibly explained by vintage (7 months later) and by GAO's figure covering only 'unmitigated' properties while this count includes any currently-insured RL-flagged property regardless of mitigation status.

The concentration is worsening, not improving

GAO's trend data shows the population of unmitigated repetitive-loss properties has grown steadily despite federal mitigation spending: from about 131,000 in 2009, to 176,000 in 2018, to 227,000 in 2026 — a 73% increase over 17 years. From 1989 through 2025, FEMA mitigated (via acquisition, elevation, relocation, or floodproofing) a total of 95,762 properties nationwide, of which only 12,972 were repetitive-loss properties. In other words, mitigation has been retiring repetitive-loss properties far more slowly than new ones are being added to the list — new flood events keep creating new repeat-loss properties faster than the acquisition/elevation programs can remove them.

Severe repetitive loss: an even more concentrated tier within the tier

FEMA and Congress define a stricter category, Severe Repetitive Loss (SRL): a structure with either (a) 4+ separate claims exceeding $5,000 each, cumulatively exceeding $20,000, or (b) 2+ claims whose cumulative amount exceeds the fair market value of the structure. A live pull found 34,996 properties nationwide have ever met this SRL threshold, of which 13,523 are currently insured. These are a small fraction of the roughly 220,000 all-time repetitive-loss properties, but by definition each one has an especially extreme claims history — e.g. the highest single case surfaced in this sample was a Tampa, FL property with 12 total losses, and a Charlotte, NC property with 13 losses as of a 2025 date. This SRL group is the population FEMA's Flood Mitigation Assistance grant program specifically targets for buyouts.

What askamerica's own data could and could not confirm directly

Askamerica's disasters.nfip_claims table holds the full history of paid claims — 2,755,527 claims totaling $95.37 billion paid since 1978 — but it is a claim-level table without a property-level identifier (no address or building ID; latitude/longitude are rounded to one decimal degree, roughly 11 km, far too coarse to re-identify individual repeat-flooding addresses). That means the 48%-of-dollars concentration figure cannot be independently recomputed from this table alone; it is FEMA's own reconciliation, reported here as such rather than as an askamerica-derived statistic. Separately, this session queried FEMA's purpose-built "NFIP Multiple Loss Properties" file directly and live via the OpenFEMA API, which does carry a property-level repetitive/severe-repetitive-loss flag and an insured-status flag, providing the current national counts above as an independent, same-day corroboration of the FEMA/GAO figures.

Every query behind this report

2 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 3 rows — 9286 ms
SELECT "year", COUNT(*) AS n_policy_rows FROM disasters.nfip_policies WHERE "year" IN ('2024','2025','2026') GROUP BY "year" ORDER BY "year"

SELECT COUNT(*) AS n_claims, SUM(amount_paid_building+amount_paid_contents+amount_paid_icc) AS total_paid FROM disasters.nfip_claims
