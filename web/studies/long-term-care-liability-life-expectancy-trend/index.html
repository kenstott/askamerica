US life expectancy at 65 rose about 2.1 years since 2000, but the state gap (3.9 years in 2022) and uneven COVID-era losses matter for LTC reserving

NCHS period life tables (national trend tables; State Life Tables 2018, 2021, 2022) plus Census ACS 65+ population from the warehouse

Summary

US life expectancy at 65 rose from 17.6 years in 2000 to 19.6 in 2019 (+2.0), fell to 18.4 in 2021, and had recovered to 19.7 by 2024 (+2.1 vs 2000). Most of the gain came by about 2010 (19.1); 2010 to 2019 added only 0.5. States differ materially: in 2022 e65 ran from 16.6 (West Virginia) to 20.5 (Hawaii), a 3.9-year spread, up from 3.6 in 2018. From 2018 to 2022 all 51 jurisdictions lost ground (mean -0.55, range -0.1 Rhode Island to -1.0 West Virginia), and weaker states lost most. The difference is large enough to matter for pricing and reserving, though the state time series I could retrieve is short.

National trend

NCHS: 17.6 (2000), 19.1 (2010), 19.6 (2019), 18.4 (2021, US row of the 2021 state table), 18.9 (2022), 19.5 (2023), 19.7 (2024). 2020 not retrieved. From the warehouse (census.acs_age, ACS 5-year), the 65+ population grew from 39.3M (12.8% of the population) in 2010 to 58.4M (17.3%) in 2024: longer lives and a much larger exposed block both raise LTC liabilities. Exclusion note: this query summed county rows only (county_fips IS NOT NULL); the table's 52 state-level rows were left out to avoid double counting, and a state-rows-only sum gives the identical 65+ total (39,267,370 in 2010; 58,391,714 in 2024), so the exclusion changed nothing.

State differences, 2018 vs 2022 (my computation from NVSR Table B, n=51 incl. DC)

  • Spread (max-min): 3.6 (2018) to 3.9 (2022); standard deviation across states 0.83 to 0.93.
  • The 12 lowest-ranked states (KY, WV, MS, AL, OK, AR, TN, LA, IN, GA, OH, MO) averaged 17.9 in 2018 and 17.2 in 2022 (-0.7); the 9 top states (HI, CT, NY, CA, MN, NJ, MA, CO, FL) went 20.2 to 19.7 (-0.45).
  • Largest losses: WV -1.0; OR, DC, AR -0.9; AZ, NM, VA, NV, OK, MS -0.8. Smallest: RI -0.1, MA and ND -0.2.
  • Ranking shifts: Oregon, Arizona, New Mexico, DC, Virginia and Nevada fell sharply in rank; Connecticut, Massachusetts and North Dakota rose. The 2018-2022 change includes COVID mortality, so it is not a secular trend.

Does it matter for LTC reserving?

A 3.9-year spread is about 20% of the national figure, so state-blind mortality will misstate duration: a Kentucky or West Virginia block has materially shorter life expectancy than a Hawaii or Connecticut one. But LTC cost depends on disability incidence, claim duration and facility cost, and shorter life expectancy partly offsets higher morbidity, so mortality alone does not give the net effect. The state ordering is persistent (same bottom states in 2018, 2021 and 2022), arguing for state or regional mortality assumptions rather than a uniform improvement trend. Do not extrapolate the 2000-2010 gain, which largely stalled before COVID. Standard errors are 0.01-0.05 for large states but 0.06-0.12 for small ones.

Limits

The warehouse has no life-table or e65 series, so e65 figures come from NCHS publications (NVSR 70(1), 73(7), 74(12); Health, United States LExpMort table; Mortality in the United States data briefs 2023 and 2024), read with a client fetch because the engine fetch returned 403. A state series for 2000-2017 was not retrieved, so a full two-decade state trend is not shown. These are period tables for the general population, not insured lives, which are healthier on average, and not mortality-improvement projections.

Every query behind this report

3 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 2 rows — 1691 ms
SELECT "year", SUM(age_65_and_over) AS pop65, SUM(under_5_years+age_5_to_17+age_18_to_64+age_65_and_over) AS pop, SUM(age_65_and_over)*1.0/SUM(under_5_years+age_5_to_17+age_18_to_64+age_65_and_over) AS share FROM census.acs_age WHERE county_fips IS NOT NULL AND "year" IN (2010,2024) GROUP BY "year" ORDER BY "year"
query — 4 rows — 730 ms
SELECT "year", county_fips IS NULL AS no_county, COUNT(*) n, SUM(age_65_and_over) AS pop65 FROM census.acs_age WHERE "year" IN (2010,2024) GROUP BY "year", county_fips IS NULL ORDER BY "year"
query — 2 rows — 606 ms
SELECT "year", SUM(age_65_and_over) AS pop65, COUNT(*) n FROM census.acs_age WHERE "year" IN (2010,2024) AND geography NOT LIKE '%state%' GROUP BY "year" ORDER BY "year"
