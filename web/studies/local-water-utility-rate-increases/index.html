Water and sewer rates are up about 60% since 2010 against 44% for all prices, and state-level utility revenue growth ranges from -3% to +91%

Summary

National water and sewer prices (BLS CPI, series CUUR0000SEHG02) rose about 60% from 2010 to 2024 and about 69% to 2025 (annual averages), versus 44% and 48% for all items. Between communities the spread is large: state-level local water-utility revenue (Census A91) grew from -3% to +91% between 2012 and 2022 (median +44%, 10th to 90th percentile +19% to +72%). That is revenue, not a per-customer rate, so it mixes rate changes with growth in customers and usage.

National rise (computed)

Annual-average index: 384.4 (2010), 404.7 (2012), 549.3 (2022), 615.5 (2024), 647.9 (2025). 2012 to 2022: +35.7% for water and sewer versus +27.5% for all items.

Exclusions

The CPI query dropped the annual-average pseudo-period M13 so only monthly rows are averaged; the state query left out the District of Columbia (FIPS 11, a single city unit). The 50-state figures exclude DC; DC's own revenue grew 86% (137,352 to 255,977 thousand dollars), inside the state range, so excluding it changes neither the min, max nor the median materially.

Variation between communities

BLS publishes no local water index. Census unit data (fiscal.govt_finance_by_unit, A91, local governments, 50 states, 2012 vs 2022) show the state-level spread above. Caveats: it is a stratified sample survey, covers revenue not rates, and I could not match individual utilities across years, so city-level dispersion is not measured here.

External surveys point the same way. Bluefield Research found 2024 to 2025 increases of 6.0% for water and 4.8% for wastewater across 50 cities, and a 24.2% cumulative five-year rise for 2020 to 2025. AE2S Nexus reported 2025 average water increases of 7.5% for systems with 5,000 or more people versus 6.3% for smaller ones, and wastewater 6.6% versus 11.1%. Circle of Blue's 30-city survey reported a 41% rise from 2010 to 2015. I read these only from search summaries, not the primary documents.
