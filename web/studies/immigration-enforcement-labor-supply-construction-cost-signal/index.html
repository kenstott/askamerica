Construction jobs fell in 2025 mainly in the Pacific West (OR, CA, WA); wages kept rising, and the state data cannot isolate enforcement

BLS QCEW private NAICS 23 annual (2023-2025) and BLS SM monthly construction jobs (to Sep 2026); ACS 2024 non-citizen share

Summary

Partly. Private construction employment (QCEW) fell in 2025 in Oregon (-3.0%), California (-2.3%), Washington (-2.1%), Colorado (-1.3%) and Arizona (-1.3%), while Texas (+5.2%) and Virginia (+5.2%) kept growing. California and Arizona are the clearest breaks from their 2024 trend (California +0.2% to -2.3%; Arizona +6.4% to -1.3%). Wages did not fall or spike anywhere: construction weekly pay still rose 4-5% in those states. But the pattern does not track immigrant exposure: across 34 states, non-citizen population share has no significant relation to the 2025 employment change, so these data show a regional construction slowdown, not an enforcement effect.

Evidence

  • Monthly SM data: first-half 2025 vs first-half 2024 California -2.1%, Oregon -3.3%, Washington -1.0%, Arizona -0.5%; second half 2025 California -2.4%, Arizona -1.8%. First-half 2026 vs first-half 2025: California -1.2%, Oregon -1.7%, Arizona -0.9%, Washington -0.9%, Florida -1.0%, Georgia -0.6%, Texas +2.3%. California and Oregon were already soft in H1 2025, before the second-half enforcement escalation; Oregon and Washington were also falling in 2024 (-1.1%, -3.0%), so their weakness predates enforcement.
  • Immigrant-heavy states are not uniformly worse: the 11 states with 6.5%+ non-citizen share averaged +0.6% employment growth in 2025 versus +2.1% for the rest, but the change relative to 2024 is almost identical (-0.67 vs -0.93 points; Welch p=0.80). Slope of 2025 growth on non-citizen share is -0.24 points per point (p=0.10, r=-0.29, n=34); wage growth slope -0.07 (p=0.37). Texas and Florida, big immigrant-labor states, kept adding construction jobs.
  • Confounds not held fixed: high mortgage rates and weak housing starts, tariffs on materials, the Los Angeles fires, and 2025 QCEW preliminary status.

Exclusions: rows where annual_avg_emplvl IS NOT NULL were kept; the rest were excluded. The units removed by that filter are Alaska, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Indiana, Louisiana, Maine, Massachusetts, Missouri, North Carolina, Puerto Rico, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Utah, Vermont and Wisconsin, all 2025 cells suppressed by BLS (disclosure code N, employment NULL). The headline statistic without them cannot be computed because their 2025 values do not exist; the monthly SM series covers them and was used only as a cross-check. The regression also left out states lacking a matching non-citizen share (n=34 of the remaining states).

Limits

The warehouse has no state enforcement-intensity series (ICE arrests, worksite raids, 287(g) agreements) and no construction-worker nativity or unauthorized-worker estimate. State QCEW also undercounts off-the-books and 1099 labor, which is where enforcement would hit first, so a null in payroll data does not prove no effect. Best narrower follow-up: county QCEW for specific raid sites, or CPS/ACS construction-occupation microdata. Several queries timed out on the data store, so the regression was recomputed from the query outputs in Python rather than with the warehouse tools.
