President Donald Trump posted on social media on Nov. 26 about the Commerce Department's revision of third-quarter GDP growth from 3.4% to 4.3%, saying the numbers published before the election were fake, that a correction had been issued the day before, and that he was asking for an investigation.

Both factual parts of the post were checked. The Bureau of Economic Analysis did issue its revised second estimate on Nov. 25, the day before the post. How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Trump posted on social media after the Commerce Department revised third-quarter 2026 GDP growth upward from 3.4% to 4.3%, referring to economic numbers published before the November midterm elections.

It was just revealed that the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE

The Bureau of Economic Analysis's Nov. 25 second estimate, read directly, revised third-quarter growth up 0.9 point from the 3.4% advance estimate of Oct. 29 and attributes the change to updated source data, chiefly revised BLS producer prices for software and Census retail and services surveys, in its scheduled release sequence. What remains open is the investigation the president requested; no finding from it has been published.

How this was tested True if the third-quarter GDP estimate published before the Nov. 3 election was fabricated or knowingly misstated by the agency. Cannot be determined if the record does not show whether the pre-election figure was a routine estimate.

Trump posted on social media on Nov. 26, 2026, about the Commerce Department's revision of third-quarter 2026 GDP growth from 3.4% to 4.3% issued the day before.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis released its second estimate of third-quarter 2026 GDP on Wednesday, Nov. 25, the day before the post, revising growth to 4.3% from the 3.4% advance estimate; BEA calls it a revised estimate rather than a correction. AskAmerica's econ.real_gdp_growth shows third-quarter growth of 1.05% at a quarterly rate, consistent with the revised figure.

How this was tested True if the Bureau of Economic Analysis issued a revised third-quarter 2026 GDP estimate on Nov. 25, 2026, the day before the post. False if no revision of that figure was issued on that date. Cannot be determined if the release date cannot be established.
