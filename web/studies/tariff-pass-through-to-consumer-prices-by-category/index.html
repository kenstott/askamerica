Roughly 90% of the 2025 tariffs reached US import prices, but only about a quarter showed up in consumer prices after a year; the rest is being absorbed by US firms

NBER/NY Fed (Amiti-Heise-Weinstein, Sept 2026), Gopinath-Neiman (NBER 34620), ECB 2/2026, Goldman; USITC DataWeb duties; BLS CPI via warehouse

Summary

Nearly all of the 2025 tariff (about 90-95%) was paid by US importers at the border, because foreign exporters cut their prices very little. Much less has reached shelf prices: the best matched-goods estimate (NY Fed and Columbia, Sept 2026) is that about 26% of the tariff increase had passed into consumer goods prices after 12 months. The difference is being absorbed mainly by US importers, producers and retailers through thinner margins, with a small share borne by foreign exporters (roughly 5-14%). Consumer pass-through is still building, and Goldman and the ECB expect the consumer share to rise toward one-third to more than half.

Confidence: high that foreign exporters bear little; moderate on the consumer share, since estimates of 22-37% (and projections of 50-67%) differ by method and date.

What I computed myself

From USITC DataWeb in the warehouse, calculated duties on imports for consumption were $72.2B (2023), $76.6B (2024) and $259.4B (2025), lifting the effective rate from 2.4% to 7.6% of customs value on $3.39T of imports. That is about $183B of added annual cost, the pool to be divided. The warehouse carries 2025 at most, so 2026 duties are not in it. I also pulled CPI: core CPI rose 2.65% from Dec 2024 to Dec 2025 and 4.7% from Jan 2025 to Oct 2026, but that cannot be attributed to tariffs without a comparison group, and 2026 prices were also moved by a large energy and producer-price surge (PPI finished goods up about 9% from Dec 2025 to Oct 2026). I therefore rely on published estimates that make such comparisons.

Published evidence

  • Border: Gopinath and Neiman estimate 94% pass-through to tariff-inclusive import prices for 2025 (80% in 2018-19). The ECB puts it at 0.95, with Amiti et al. falling from 0.94 to 0.86 by November. Foreign firms bear about 5%.
  • Shelf: Amiti, Heise and Weinstein find about 26% of the tariff increase reaches consumer goods prices relative to less-exposed goods; 64% of that is the direct effect on imported varieties and 36% indirect (imported inputs and higher domestic markups). Direct effects arrive quickly; indirect ones take 9-12 months.
  • Cost shares: The ECB finds US firms bear about a third and consumers about a third now, rising for consumers to over half long term. Goldman estimated consumers at 22% (June 2025) and 37% (Aug 2025), with foreign exporters at 14% in June, and later projected 67%.
  • Policy change: Per Fortune/Goldman (Feb 2026), the Supreme Court ruled IEEPA cannot support the tariffs, creating about $180B in potential refunds; a 10-15% Section 122 global tariff replaced them, keeping levels comparable. Goldman says tariff-driven inflation (0.7% added) has likely peaked and does not expect prices to fall. Refunds are unlikely to reach consumers.

Caveats

The studies measure different things: 26% is the price effect relative to the tariff rate on goods CPI, while the shares of cost come from surveys and models, so they are not additive. Statutory rates overstated actual duties (14.1% paid vs 27.4% statutory in Sept 2025, per Gopinath-Neiman), and I did not independently reproduce the 26% estimate; the CPI-to-tariff concordance it needs is not in the warehouse. The VoxEU column returned HTTP 403, so its content comes from search summaries; the NBER paper itself was read directly.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Goods-level (affected goods) pass-through: Warehouse has no CPI-to-HTS concordance; relied on the NY Fed paper that built one. Apparel, furniture and appliance CPI are noisy and not decisive.
