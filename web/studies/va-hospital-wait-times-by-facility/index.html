VA neurology wait times remain far above the 28-day standard even where they've fallen back from early-2026 peaks; systemwide, most facilities are still getting worse, not better

VA Access to Care (accesstocare.va.gov, live Sep 13, 2026) · Government Executive FOIA analysis (Apr 2026) · Vet Voice Foundation tracking study (Aug 2025-Feb 2026)

Summary

There is no single official VA ranking of "longest wait time by hospital" published in one table — VA's own Access to Care site (accesstocare.va.gov) publishes current wait times per facility per specialty, but not a national leaderboard, and VA has not released facility-by-facility comparative data on its own initiative. The two best sources that fill that gap are (1) a Government Executive/Nextgov investigation that obtained FOIA data covering 134 of VA's 170 medical centers across 10 specialties and compared Oct 2025-Jan 2026 to the same period a year earlier, and (2) a Vet Voice Foundation study that tracked 21 VA Medical Centers daily for six months (Aug 2025-Feb 2026).

Facilities/specialties currently standing out for the longest new-patient waits include neurology at Omaha VA Medical Center and the Dallas VA Medical Center (both were flagged as having the largest increases nationally, and both still post neurology waits of roughly 80-105 days as of September 2026 versus VA's 28-day specialty-care standard); group mental-health therapy at Hampton, VA (30 to 87 days) and Togus, ME (25 to 68 days); and mental health at East Orange, NJ and Washington, DC VA Medical Centers, where veterans calling directly were told wait times of 35-45 days and roughly 4 months respectively, well above the numbers posted online.

Has it gotten better or worse recently? Mixed, trending negative on balance. Systemwide, the Vet Voice Foundation found wait times rose at 71% of the 21 medical centers studied and in 64% of specialties, over Aug 2025-Feb 2026. Government Executive's FOIA analysis found 42% of facility-specialty combinations got worse (by 2+ days) versus 37% that improved, comparing Oct 2025-Jan 2026 to Oct 2024-Jan 2025. Neurology is the worst-off specialty nationally: only 7% of facilities met the 28-day standard at the start of FY2026, and neurology waits increased at 13 of 21 sites in the Vet Voice sample (average 76.3 days). At the same time, both Omaha and Dallas neurology waits, and the systemwide primary-care average, have come down somewhat from their worst points in late 2025/early 2026 — VA itself points to this partial recovery as evidence of progress, while the two independent trackers show it has not been enough to restore pre-cut performance or meet VA's own access standards at most sites.

What VA's own current data shows (live spot-check, Sep 13, 2026)

VA's Access to Care site (accesstocare.va.gov) publishes current average wait times per facility and specialty for both new and established patients, refreshed roughly monthly to daily depending on the measure. What the two systematic investigations found

Government Executive / Nextgov (published April 16, 2026): Using VA wait-time data VA provided to the group American Bridge under FOIA, reporters compared new-patient average wait times at 134 of VA's 170 medical centers across 10 major specialties (primary care, mental health individual and group, substance use, PTSD, neurology, physical therapy, pulmonary, oncology, urology) for Oct 2025-Jan 2026 versus the same four months a year earlier. Findings: 486 facility-specialty combinations got worse by 2+ days, 427 improved by 2+ days (42% vs 37%, on a per-facility-per-specialty basis). Neurology was the worst performer — just 7% of facilities met the 28-day standard, and Omaha and Dallas posted the largest increases in the country. Physical therapy, substance-use treatment, and oncology were the only specialties where the number of facilities meeting the standard actually fell versus a year earlier; seven of ten specialties improved on that count. VA disputed the analysis as incomplete (it excludes appointment volume and existing-patient wait times, which VA says have fallen).

Vet Voice Foundation (published April 27, 2026): An advocacy group (not independent of a policy position — it opposes VA workforce cuts and privatization) tracked daily wait-time postings at 21 VA Medical Centers spanning 13 of VA's 18 regional networks, Aug 2025-Feb 2026. Wait times rose, averaged across specialties, at 15 of 21 sites (71%) and in 16 of 25 specialties (64%). Primary care averaged 35.9 days (vs the 20-day standard) — better than the 37.2-day start of the period but still nearly double standard, and only 3 of 21 sites met the standard (down from 5 earlier in the period). Neurology averaged 76.3 days, rising at 13 of 21 sites. Oncology averaged 46.6 days (up 6+ days); pulmonology 45.1 days (up 2.7 days). Group mental-health therapy, reported at only 8 of 21 sites, rose an average of ~12 days to 29 days, with Hampton, VA jumping from 30 to 87 days and Togus, ME from 25 to 68 days. The report also documents cases (East Orange, NJ and Washington, DC VA Medical Centers) where the wait time veterans were told by phone was much longer than what was posted online.

Context: why this is happening, per both sources

VA has lost a net roughly 18,600+ VHA employees since early 2025 amid a workforce-reduction push (an original target of 80,000 was pared back to roughly 30,000 achieved in FY2025), including a net loss of about 1,100 physicians, nearly 3,000 nurses, and 800 medical-support-assistant scheduling staff, alongside roughly 500 psychologists/psychiatrists lost in one year per Vet Voice's sourcing. At the same time, the PACT Act (2022) has driven record enrollment and completed-appointment volume — VA reports 82.08 million direct-care appointments completed in FY2025, up 4.1% from FY2024 — so demand has been rising as capacity has been cut, which VA's own former VHA chief operating officer (through Sep 2025) characterized as "a demand versus capacity mismatch that won't be fixed by efficiency or productivity improvements." VA leadership (Secretary Doug Collins) maintains wait times overall have stabilized or improved and that existing patients, who are more than 80% of those seen, now wait less than in FY2024; VA has not published the overall data to substantiate that claim independently of the facility-specialty figures analyzed above.

Data and methodology notes

  • No table in the AskAmerica warehouse (askengine) covers VA appointment wait times — confirmed via search_catalog, which returned no matching schema/column for "wait" or "appointment", and via find_recipe, which returned no recipe on this topic (this answer uses no warehouse SQL, so no recipe applies); this answer relies entirely on VA's own live public site and independent journalism/advocacy analyses of VA-supplied data.
  • accesstocare.va.gov numbers are VA's own calculation, refreshed on a rolling basis (VA states appointments are drawn from the past month); they are not independently audited, and Vet Voice's report documents specific cases where they understated the wait time a veteran was actually quoted by phone.
  • Vet Voice Foundation is an advocacy nonprofit that opposes VA staffing cuts and privatization — its report should be read as an advocacy-driven but methodologically transparent tracking exercise (daily data collection across 21 named sites over 6 months), not a neutral government release.
  • Government Executive's analysis is the most systematic and least advocacy-driven source (134 of 170 facilities, 10 specialties, year-over-year comparison), but VA itself disputes it as incomplete because it excludes appointment volume weighting and existing-patient data.
  • No comprehensive current national ranking of all ~170 VA medical centers by wait time was available to reproduce in the time available; Omaha and Dallas are cited because both investigations independently identified them as among the worst for neurology specifically. A full facility-by-facility ranking would require pulling accesstocare.va.gov's per-facility tables individually or via its underlying CSV export for all ~170 hospitals, which was outside this session's scope.
