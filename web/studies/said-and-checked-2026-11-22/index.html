Said & Checked

What officeholders said, and what the data shows

2026-11-22 · 28 statements from 10 speakers

8 checked: 5 supported, 3 unsettled · 1 not yet checked · 19 not checkable

Ten officeholders in scope were quoted in the Nov. 21 and 22 political coverage of NPR and PBS NewsHour, for 28 statements in all. Most came from a White House news conference at which President Trump, Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio recapped Trump's trip to Asia and his meeting with the leaders of China and Russia, and took questions about the Iran war and the 2028 Republican field.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent spoke about export restrictions on Nvidia chips to China and about the Commerce Department's handling of Megaspeed International. Trump spoke in a Fox News Channel interview about the apparent hack of an FBI criminal justice database and about capping credit card interest rates, a proposal Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Josh Hawley have also backed.

Reps. Robert Garcia and Jamie Raskin responded to Trump's order telling federal contractors to check with the White House before answering Congress. Rep. Greg Murphy described how North Carolina's redrawn congressional map changed his district, and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries addressed voters after the midterm elections.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores. Statements are grouped two levels deep, by speaker and then topic or by topic and then speaker, and every group is collapsed. A topic with a single statement is listed under Other. The counts beside a name cover only that person's statements on this page, and are not a rating of the person. Statements that could not be checked are folded under each speaker. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 21 or 22, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, as read on Nov. 22 at about 08:07 ET, less the stories earlier pages already used, plus every transcript from the fixed transcript sources dated in that window (none was). Transcripts from Nov. 15 to 20 were searched only to confirm quotes. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. Two of the eleven stories quoted no officeholder in scope. Greetings and procedural remarks, quotes under five words, commentators' accounts of what officials said, and statements by officials outside the offices in scope were left out. Statements are worded as the outlet reported them; entries beginning "Reported as" are the reporter's wording.

SupportedFactual claim

Bessent, the Treasury Secretary, spoke to CNBC after the Asia trip about Chinese firms' remote access to Nvidia chips.

And the remote access has been curtailed.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security published an interim final rule, "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (2026-23529), in the Federal Register on Nov. 19, 2026. It requires licenses for exports to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant computing power to companies headquartered in China, Macau or other Country Group D:5 nations. No withdrawal was found, and the rule's effective date was not read.

How this was tested True if the administration took a published action in November 2026 restricting Chinese firms' remote access to advanced Nvidia chips. False if no such restriction was issued or it was withdrawn. Cannot be determined if only reports of a planned action exist with no official text.

Evidence: Federal Register 2026-23529 (Nov. 19, 2026)

AP reported what Bessent said about which department controlled export restrictions on Megaspeed International.

Reported as: In an unusually public rebuke of a fellow Cabinet member, Bessent said his department wanted to place export controls on Megaspeed, but Commerce was in charge of the matter.

The Export Control Reform Act, 50 U.S.C. 4813(a), gives the Secretary of Commerce authority over the export control lists, in consultation with State, Defense and Energy. BIS Entity List rules describe additions as decided by an interagency End-User Review Committee, in which Treasury takes part where appropriate but does not hold the authority.

UnsettledFactual claim

Murphy, the Republican congressman for North Carolina's 3rd District, described his district after the Republican-drawn map moved four counties into it.

It made us the weakest Republican district in the state.

Figures attributed to the North Carolina General Assembly, as reported by Roll Call and WFAE (the Assembly's district file could not be read directly), put Trump's 2024 margin in the redrawn 3rd District at about 14 points, down from 22, but about 12 points in the 1st and 10 points in the unchanged 11th. Whether the 3rd is the weakest depends on whether 'Republican district' means seats Republicans held or seats the map leans Republican, and on the measure used.

How this was tested True if, under the congressional map in force for the 2026 election, North Carolina's 3rd District has the lowest Republican vote share or partisan lean of the state's Republican-leaning districts. False if another Republican-leaning North Carolina district is weaker for Republicans on the same measure. Cannot be determined if no district-level vote or lean data for the new map is available.

SupportedFactual claim

Trump, in a Fox News Channel interview, spoke about credit card companies while discussing a credit card interest rate cap.

These credit card companies have made a lot

The Federal Reserve's report to Congress on credit card profitability (latest edition read: July 2019, 2018 data) shows large credit card banks earned 3.79% of assets before taxes against 1.46% for all commercial banks, and lost money in only one year (2009) of 2001 to 2018. AskAmerica's data show the commercial bank credit card interest rate at about 21% from 2023 through August 2026 (21.19% latest), but no official profitability figure after 2018 was read.

How this was tested True if credit card issuers' profits or interest income are high by official measures (for example Federal Reserve or FDIC data on card-bank returns or card interest income). False if official data show credit card lending was unprofitable or earned unusually little. Cannot be determined if the loose term 'a lot' leaves the data open to more than one reading.

Evidence: AskAmerica data econ.fred_indicators (card interest rate TERMCBCCALLNS and charge-off rate CORCCACBS, 2023 to 2026) , Federal Reserve, Profitability of Credit Card Operations of Depository Institutions (July 2019)
