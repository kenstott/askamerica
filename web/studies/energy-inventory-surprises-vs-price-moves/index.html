How much do energy prices move on the weekly EIA inventory report, and how long does the move last?

Published report: http://127.0.0.1:51725/a/b3e0212c9a985122b38f91fe56f17ccb.html (local to the host machine; images copied alongside this file)

Dashboard: dashboard.png · Charts: chart_report_vs_other_day.png, chart_inventory_vs_price_scatter.png · Report card: report_card.png

The report and the trade

EIA's Weekly Petroleum Status Report releases every Wednesday at 10:30am ET (Thursday after a Monday holiday), and its headline crude number — U.S. commercial crude stocks ex-SPR (EIA series WCESTUS1) — is the number the market watches. Over the 26 report weeks from 2025-08-08 through 2026-02-20, the weekly change ranged from a 9.3M-barrel draw to a 16.0M-barrel build, averaging roughly a 0.5M-barrel build with a standard deviation around 5.9M barrels — a genuinely volatile weekly series.

Report-day move vs. a normal day (real numbers)

Using WTI daily close-to-close price data (econ.global_conditions), the mean absolute daily percent move on the 26 EIA report days in this calm window was 1.11%, versus 1.38% on the 108 other trading days in the same window. A Welch's t-test found this difference is NOT statistically significant (t=-1.22, p=0.23) — and directionally the report-day average was actually smaller, not larger, than an ordinary day's close-to-close move.

An OLS regression of the same-day price change ($/bbl) on the raw weekly inventory change (MMbbl) found essentially no linear relationship: coefficient 0.0008 $/bbl per MMbbl, R²=0.00002, p=0.98 (n=26).

Important caveat: this null result is about the full day's close-to-close return net of everything else that happened that day, using the raw weekly change — not the consensus-vs-actual "surprise" the literature says actually moves prices; a genuine analyst-consensus series (e.g. Bloomberg/Reuters survey expectations) is not present in this corpus. Published intraday event-study literature (Ederington et al., ScienceDirect 2016; Karali 2019 AJAE "Mixed Event Response Model") finds WTI/Brent do move within seconds of the release on a surprise vs. consensus, sometimes $1-3/bbl on a big miss, with the volatility/volume response burning out in roughly 20-30 minutes — a real signal, but on a timescale and against a benchmark (consensus surprise) this corpus's daily/raw-change data cannot resolve or reproduce.

Does the move persist, or reverse?

Correlation between the report-day price change and the subsequent 4-trading-day continuation (day+5 close minus report-day close) was 0.058 (n=27) — statistically indistinguishable from zero, meaning this data shows neither meaningful momentum (the move extending) nor meaningful reversion (the move unwinding) over the following week. Combined with the literature's finding that the volatility/volume response is concentrated in the first 20-30 minutes post-release, the practical read for a trader is: whatever edge exists is almost entirely in the immediate intraday reaction, not in a multi-day drift that shows up in daily closes.

A major confound: the 2026 Iran-war price shock

energy.eia_petroleum_stocks stops at report_date=2026-06-05 — about 12 weeks stale versus the WTI price series, which runs to 2026-08-21 (both askamerica facts; logged as a data-quality issue via report_issue). More importantly, WTI itself is not well-behaved across the full window: it traded ~$57-66/bbl from Aug 2025 through Feb 2026, then surged to an intraday high near $119/bbl by March 9, 2026 and stayed elevated (~$85-113/bbl) through May, driven by the US-Israel-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz shipping risk (EIA's own "Crude oil and petroleum product prices increased sharply in the first quarter of 2026" Today in Energy note; Forbes Advisor/Polymarket coverage). That regime shift dwarfs any inventory-report signal, so the significance test and regression above deliberately use only the calm Aug-2025-to-Feb-2026 window; a report-day move measured inside the war period would really be measuring the war, not the inventory print.

Natural gas / Henry Hub

The question also allows Henry Hub natural gas. This corpus's energy schema has EIA weekly underground gas storage volumes (eia_natural_gas_storage, weekly_gas_storage_signal) but no daily/weekly Henry Hub spot PRICE series — logged as a second data-quality gap via report_issue — so a quantitative price-reaction regression parallel to the crude analysis above could not be computed from this corpus. From EIA's own published commentary: natural gas price reaction to the storage report follows the same surprise-vs-expectation logic as crude (bigger-than-expected build = bearish, bigger-than-expected draw = bullish), and Henry Hub daily volatility itself trended down over H1 2025 (quarterly realized vol fell from ~81% in Q4 2024 to ~69% by mid-2025) — narrative citation, not a number computed here.

Bottom line for the trade

In this recent, non-war window, the EIA report-day WTI move is not distinguishable from an ordinary day's volatility on a daily-close basis, and the raw weekly inventory change alone explains none of the day's price move (R²≈0.00002) — the real driver, per the literature, is the surprise relative to consensus, which resolves in the first 20-30 minutes and does not show a detectable multi-day continuation or reversal in this data. A trader positioning purely on the headline print without an independent read on consensus expectations, and expecting the edge to persist past the release window, is not supported by either this corpus's numbers or the cited event-study literature.

Confidence

MEDIUM. The crude-oil report-day/persistence statistics are computed directly from askamerica (EIA + WTI price series) with formal significance tests, but the sample is modest (n=26-27 report weeks) and uses raw inventory change rather than a true consensus-surprise measure (unavailable in this corpus), and uses daily closes rather than intraday ticks (the literature's real edge is intraday). Natural gas coverage is narrative-only (no Henry Hub price series in corpus) and should be treated as lower confidence.
