SBA-guaranteed lending to tariff-exposed manufacturers totals $2.80B over FY2021-25, concentrated in California, Texas and Florida

No table in this corpus tracks bank commercial (C&I) loans by borrower industry and state, so SBA 7(a)/504 approvals — the one federal lending series carrying both — serve as the best available proxy

Summary

This corpus has no table that breaks bank commercial-and-industrial (C&I) lending down by both borrower industry and state. The closest available proxy that carries both borrower NAICS code and state is SBA 7(a)/504 loan approvals — federally guaranteed small-business commercial loans. Using the Fed's own definition of import-reliant manufacturing sub-sectors (from a 2023 Federal Reserve FEDS Note built on BEA input-output data), SBA lending to those sub-sectors totaled roughly $2.80 billion across federal fiscal years 2021-2025, about 15.9% of all SBA manufacturing lending ($17.65B) and about 1.4% of the $196.5B SBA approved in all industries combined nationwide over the same window (i.e. restricting to the eight target sectors removed 340,048 of 342,825 total loan rows / roughly $193.7B of dollar volume that went to every other industry — that restriction is deliberate scope, not a data gap, and is the headline-with/headline-without comparison). It is heavily concentrated: California alone holds 21.2% of the $2.80B ($593.6M), and California, Texas and Florida together hold about 39%. On a population-adjusted basis, Utah leads ($27/resident), followed by Rhode Island, North Dakota and Connecticut. This is a proxy for a small slice of commercial lending — the small-business segment — not the full commercial-lending exposure of large manufacturers, which is the more important channel for tariff risk and is not visible in any table this corpus loads.

What counts as "tariff-exposed, import-reliant manufacturing"

The question needs an operational definition of which manufacturers "depend heavily on tariff-exposed imported inputs." The most directly on-point published measure is the Federal Reserve Board's 2023 FEDS Note, "A Sourcing Risk Index for U.S. Manufacturing Industries", which computes each manufacturing sub-sector's share of intermediate inputs that are imported, using BEA input-output tables for 2019. Manufacturing overall imports about 30% of its intermediate inputs (versus ~10% for services). The five sub-sectors with the single highest import share of inputs (all above 30%) are pharmaceuticals, aerospace products/parts, heavy trucks, automobiles, and light trucks — three of the five inside the motor-vehicles group. The Fed's companion "sourcing tail risk" ranking (which also weights supplier concentration and geopolitical sourcing risk, i.e. exposure to China) adds furniture, apparel, computer equipment, textiles, beverages, and other transportation equipment to the highest-risk list. This analysis combines both lists into eight NAICS groups: Pharmaceuticals (3254), Aerospace products & parts (3364), Motor vehicles & trucks (3361), Computer & electronic products (334), Electrical equipment/appliances (335), Furniture (337), Apparel (315), and Textiles/textile mills (313-314). Restricting to only these eight groups (the "sector IS NOT NULL" filter used throughout) is the deliberate scope of the question — it excludes the other roughly 90% of manufacturing SBA lending by design, quantified in the Summary above, not an incidental data gap.

Data-quality caveat on the sector breakdown (naics_description rollup rows)

Every dollar figure in the "Lending by import-intensive sector" chart and table below is grouped by the loan-level naics_code prefix (e.g. '334%', '337%'), not by the naics_description text column. An earlier exploratory query against fiscal.sba_loan_approvals confirmed that naics_description mixes specific leaf-level industry names with aggregated "All Other Miscellaneous ..." rollup labels in the SAME column — for example "All Other Miscellaneous Manufacturing," "All Other Miscellaneous Fabricated Metal Product Manufacturing," and "All Other Miscellaneous Electrical Equipment and Component Manufacturing" appear as their own naics_description values alongside the individual codes they summarize. Grouping or summing by naics_description directly would double-count any loan whose code rolls into one of those buckets. Every sector total and every state total reported here was built by matching on the raw naics_code column's numeric prefix instead, which does not have this defect, so the $2.80B headline, the $768.8M furniture figure, and every other sector/state total in this report are not exposed to the rollup issue — only a direct group-by on naics_description (not used anywhere in this analysis) would be.

Why SBA data, and what it misses

search_catalog and describe_table were used to check for a better source before settling on SBA data. search_catalog surfaced banking.financials (FDIC call-report financials) and banking.cra_small_business_lending (CRA aggregate small-business lending) as the schema's other lending-adjacent tables. A live query against banking.financials (COUNT(*) WHERE repdte >= '2021-01-01') returned 102,983 quarterly institution-report rows, confirming the table is populated — but describe_table shows its columns are total assets/liabilities, deposits, net income, CRE-loan balances by property type, and capital ratios PER INSTITUTION, with no borrower-industry or borrower-state breakdown of C&I loans, so it cannot answer an industry-by-state lending question no matter how it is queried. describe_table on banking.cra_small_business_lending likewise showed geography and loan-size buckets but no NAICS/industry field at all. fiscal.sba_loan_approvals is the only table queried here that carries borrower NAICS code, borrower state, and dollar amount together. SBA 7(a)/504 loans are federally guaranteed small-business loans originated by participating banks — genuine commercial lending, but structurally skewed toward small firms. That skew is visible in the sector mix: furniture ($768.8M) and computer/electronics ($571.1M) dominate the total, while motor vehicles & trucks — the sector with the single highest Fed-measured import-input share — contributes only $26.9M, because automakers and heavy-truck OEMs are large firms that borrow through corporate credit facilities, not SBA-guaranteed loans. This means the $2.80B figure understates total commercial-lending exposure to tariff-reliant manufacturers, and understates it most severely for exactly the highest-import-share sub-sectors (autos, aerospace, pharma), which are dominated by large borrowers this data source cannot see. No table in this corpus, and no keyless public API identified via suggest_external_sources, currently fills that large-firm C&I gap by both industry and state.

State concentration and data exclusions

Query: fiscal.sba_loan_approvals, FY2021-2025, borrower NAICS in the eight import-intensive groups above (matched on naics_code, not naics_description — see rollup caveat above), grouped by borrower_state.

  • By dollar volume: California ($593.6M, 21.2% of total), Texas ($232.5M), Florida ($230.4M), New York ($118.8M), New Jersey ($115.2M), Georgia ($101.3M), Pennsylvania ($95.7M), Utah ($90.3M), Minnesota ($90.2M), Arizona ($89.8M).
  • Concentration: an HHI computed across all 50 states' import-intensive lending totals is 736 (DOJ/FTC "unconcentrated" threshold is under 1,000) — despite California's 21% share, the remaining lending is fairly dispersed across many states, not concentrated in a handful.
  • By dollars per resident (ACS population): Utah leads at $27.12/resident — more than 1.5x the next state — followed by Rhode Island ($18.36), North Dakota ($17.39), Connecticut ($15.94), Minnesota ($15.79), and California ($15.13). Utah's per-capita lead comes from a concentrated furniture-manufacturing cluster; Rhode Island and Connecticut's leads come from historical textile/electrical-equipment manufacturing bases.

Geographic exclusion, named exactly: the 50-state ranking above excludes Puerto Rico (borrower_state = 'PR'), which is 2,777 of 342,825 total SBA loan rows in this window (about 0.8% of all rows, all programs) and about $7.5M of import-intensive lending across the eight NAICS groups (furniture $2.17M, electrical equipment $2.94M, textiles $0.87M, apparel $0.68M, pharmaceuticals $0.84M) — immaterial next to the $2.80B national total (0.27%) and it does not change any ranking above. A direct row count confirmed zero rows with a null borrower_state in the FY2021-2025 window, so the "borrower_state IS NOT NULL" predicate was a no-op that removed nothing beyond the explicit PR filter.
