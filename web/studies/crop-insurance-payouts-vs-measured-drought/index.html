Drought-coded crop insurance payouts track measured drought moderately (r about 0.46 by county-year) and diverge most in irrigated Western counties

RMA Cause of Loss (drought indemnity / liability) vs US Drought Monitor DSCI, Apr-Sep mean, county-years 2010-2025, n=30,642

Summary

Drought-coded crop insurance indemnities track measured drought only moderately. Across 30,642 county-years (2010-2025), drought indemnity as a share of insured liability correlates r=0.46 with the growing-season (Apr-Sep) mean Drought Severity and Coverage Index. Dollars rather than rates give 0.40. Excluding six Western states (AZ, CA, NM, NV, OR, UT) the correlation rises to 0.55. At the county level, using 1,844 county means over 10 or more years, it falls to 0.30. The match is tight in the Corn Belt and northern Plains (ND 0.83, IL 0.74, SD 0.71, KS 0.70) and loose or absent in the irrigated and specialty-crop West (UT 0.06, NM 0.13, AZ 0.13, CA 0.18).

Drought is the largest single cause: $54.1B of $167.7B total indemnity in the sample (32%).

Where they diverge most

In county-years with DSCI of 150 or more (very dry), drought indemnity per dollar of liability was about 0.0001% in Arizona, 0.04% in Nevada, 0.65% in California and 1.3% in Florida, against 6% in Oregon and New Mexico.

California almonds, pistachios, cherries and tomatoes show zero drought indemnity over 2010-2025 despite 26-47% overall loss ratios; Arizona pasture/rangeland shows zero drought indemnity yet a 53% overall loss ratio. Rollup caveat for these commodity_name figures: the column mixes a rollup row ('All Other Crops') with individual crops, so summing across commodities would double count; each figure here is a single crop's own row, so it is unaffected, and the rollup row is not quoted. Irrigated or water-allocated crops evidently lose to other causes; with no irrigated-practice column this is suggestive, not a direct test.

The opposite divergence is rare: a few Texas and Georgia county-years (for example Texas 48317 in 2017) show loss ratios above 60% with DSCI under 40, which fits short flash droughts, drought outside Apr-Sep, or drought on part of a county.

Year by year

Annual cross-sectional r ranges from 0.64 (2011) to 0.16 (2015) and -0.03 (2025). The 2025 value is likely depressed by incomplete settlement, which I did not verify. The biggest drought payout years were 2012 ($12.9B) and 2022 ($7.6B), matching DSCI peaks.

Exclusions and caveats

Exclusions: I filtered county_fips IS NOT NULL, but a check found 0 rows with a null county FIPS in 2010-2025, so that filter removed nothing (0 rows, $0 indemnity) and the headline is identical with and without it. County-years with under $1M of insured liability were dropped (a deliberate size floor), as were county-years with no drought record. The six Western states were left out only in the stated sensitivity (r 0.46 to 0.55). This is association, not causation; no leave-one-out or fixed-effects tests were run. Drought indemnity uses RMA causes starting 'Drought' (heat and hot wind are separate). Indemnity is by crop year, the index by calendar year, and Apr-Sep suits spring crops.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Test of irrigation mechanism directly: No irrigated/non-irrigated practice column in the table; commodity mix used instead.
