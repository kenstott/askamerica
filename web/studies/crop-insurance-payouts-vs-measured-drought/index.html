Crop insurance payouts track drought closely at the national level (r≈0.79) but diverge sharply by state and by peril

USDA RMA Summary of Business indemnities vs. NOAA/USDM Drought Severity and Coverage Index (DSCI), county/state/year, 2010-2024

Summary

At the national, year-to-year level, crop insurance payouts track measured drought closely. Comparing USDA RMA's annual crop-insurance loss ratio (total indemnities ÷ total premium, all 50 states) against the U.S. Drought Monitor's mean Drought Severity and Coverage Index (DSCI) for 2010-2024, the correlation is r=0.79 for total indemnity dollars and r=0.70 for the loss ratio (r=0.76 for indemnity dollars even with the historic 2012 drought excluded, so the relationship is not an artifact of one extreme year). 2012 (the worst U.S. drought in decades) and 2022 stand out as years when both drought and payouts spiked together.

The match falls apart at finer grain and in specific years/places. At the state-year and county-year level the correlation drops to roughly r=0.3-0.4 — most of the variance in what a given place is paid in a given year is NOT explained by measured meteorological drought. The biggest divergences fall into three patterns: (1) years/states where non-drought perils (hurricanes, spring flooding) drove large payouts despite low or moderate measured drought — Florida 2018 (Hurricane Michael), and Ohio/Arkansas 2019 (historic Midwest flooding, DSCI near its 15-year low); (2) 2012 Midwest drought states (Illinois, Indiana, Missouri) where payouts ran far ABOVE even what their own severe drought readings would predict, indicating the loss-severity relationship is convex — losses accelerate faster than DSCI once drought becomes extreme; and (3) severely-drought-flagged states/years with comparatively LOW payouts — California in 2013-2016 (the state's historic drought, but irrigation and perennial/specialty crops buffer yield loss) and Iowa/Indiana/Illinois in 2021-2022 (moderate Corn Belt drought that modern hybrids and soil moisture reserves largely absorbed without triggering large claims).

Data and method

Sources: USDA RMA Summary of Business crop-insurance experience (ag.rma_crop_insurance: indemnity, premium, liability by state/county/crop/plan/year, 2009-2025 observed) and NOAA/USDM weekly county drought categories (weather.drought_monitor_weekly: D0-D4 percentages and DSCI 0-500, 2009-2026 observed), both queried through the AskAmerica connector. Analysis window: 2010-2024, the full overlap of both tables' measured coverage.

Metric: Loss ratio (indemnity ÷ premium) is used alongside raw indemnity dollars because it is scale-independent — it isolates whether a place/year paid out MORE than its premium base implies, rather than just reflecting which states insure the most acreage. DSCI is averaged across all weekly readings in a year per county/state as the drought-severity measure (0=no drought, 500=entire area in exceptional drought).

Key limitation: this connector's RMA table carries no cause-of-loss field, so "indemnity" here is the TOTAL payout across all perils (drought, excess moisture, hail, freeze, disease, wind, price) — it cannot isolate drought-caused claims the way RMA's own cause-of-loss detail files or USDA ERS's published cause-of-loss series can. That is precisely why divergence is expected and is the main analytical value here: a state/year with a high payout and low DSCI is very likely a non-drought-peril event, and a state/year with high DSCI and low payout is very likely a place where measured meteorological drought didn't translate into an indemnified yield loss (irrigation, drought-tolerant varieties, or crops less exposed).

USDA's own Economic Research Service, using RMA's cause-of-loss detail (a data cut this connector does not carry), reports that drought and high temperature were the leading cause of indemnified losses in 14 of the 25 years since 2000, averaging 41% of total indemnity payments, with excess moisture responsible for another 27% — confirming both that drought is the single largest driver nationally AND that roughly 3 in 5 dollars of indemnity are NOT drought (source parsed directly: ERS "Crop Insurance at a Glance," Federal Crop Insurance Program indemnities by cause of loss, updated 9/23/2025).

Caveats

  • This is an observational correlation, not a causal attribution — no cause-of-loss field is available in this connector's RMA table, so "divergence" is inferred from known events (hurricanes, floods) rather than measured directly.
  • DSCI is a meteorological/soil-moisture index; it does not itself measure crop yield loss, irrigation availability, crop mix, or insurance take-up, all of which mediate how drought severity translates (or doesn't) into an indemnified loss.
  • County-year and state-year correlations (r≈0.3-0.4) are the more decision-relevant grain for "how closely do payouts match drought" locally, and they show a much weaker match than the national aggregate — averaging across many counties/perils each year is largely what produces the strong national-level r.
  • ag.rma_crop_insurance is declared through 2025 but late years may still be mid-backfill (publication lag ~1 year); 2010-2024 was used to avoid partial recent years.
