No dataset ranks states by growth in undisclosed-source legislative outside spending; the evidence is state-by-state and points to Oklahoma, Virginia and Colorado as places to look

Answer

I cannot give a ranked list of states. I found no dataset that measures undisclosed-source outside spending on legislative races across states and cycles. What can be said: Oklahoma shows the clearest 2026 signal, with outside groups spending more than $5.5M on midterm races (almost half with no traceable donor per Oklahoma Watch's headline), including nearly $1.3M by PACs on legislative races since Sept. 1. Virginia (about $1.7M of mostly dark-money-backed independent expenditures in the 2023 cycle) and Colorado (about $13M of super PAC spending on 2024 legislative races, with some money from non-disclosing nonprofits) are also active. None of these is a measured growth rate. Arizona's Prop 211, upheld on appeal Oct. 1, 2026, now forces donor disclosure there, which would shrink what counts as undisclosed.

What the sources say

  • Oklahoma Watch, Nov. 2, 2026: more than $5.5M outside spending on midterm races; funders often conceal identity through chains of nonprofits. The 'almost half' figure is from the headline; I did not see the underlying split.
  • Oklahoma Watch, Dec. 2023: $2.3M outside spending in 2023, about 90% on a ballot question; Oklahoma law does not require nonprofits to disclose donors.
  • VCIJ/WHRO with OpenSecrets: about $1.7M spent in Virginia through Sept. 12 of the 2023 cycle; the top three spenders, three-quarters of the total, were largely or entirely dark-money funded.
  • Colorado Sun, Nov. 2024: nearly $13M of state super PAC spending on legislative races; the Sixteen Thirty Fund, which does not report donors, gave $250,000 to one Democratic caucus PAC.
  • Brennan Center (2016): in six states (AK, AZ, CA, CO, ME, MA) dark money rose on average 38 times from 2006 to 2014, the only multi-state growth measure found, and it is old. California had the most outside spending but little dark money.
  • Sludge (2020): Alabama, Indiana and New Mexico required no independent-expenditure disclosure as of 2018.

Warehouse check

California Form 496 independent expenditures (all spenders, candidates and ballot measures, no dark-money flag) by calendar year: 2018 $207.9M, 2020 $84.9M, 2022 $113.0M, 2024 $134.0M, 2026 so far $343.5M. This is total IE, not undisclosed-source money, and California is known for strong disclosure, so it does not answer the question. It is shown only to document what the warehouse holds. I did not exclude outlier rows beyond amounts under $50M.

Next step

A real ranking needs state independent-expenditure data classified by whether the donor chain ends in a disclosed source, such as National Institute on Money in Politics data. I could not reach that data, so the question stays open here.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Rank states by growth in undisclosed-source outside spending on state legislative races: No consistent multi-state series was found. FEC tables are federal-only; the only state-level IE table in the warehouse is California Form 496, which has no dark-money flag and mixes ballot measures with candidate races. A 50-state source (National Institute on Money in Politics or OpenSecrets state data) was not reachable.
