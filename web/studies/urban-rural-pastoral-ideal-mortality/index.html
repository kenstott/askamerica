Rural America's health gap is large and has widened: premature death ~13% higher than metro in 1999, ~34% higher by 2022

Summary

NCHS Data Brief 417: rural all-cause death rate 7% above urban in 1999 (923.8 vs 865.1 per 100,000), 20% above in 2019 (834.0 vs 693.4). My county YPLL under 75 per 100k, nonmetro vs metro: 8,506 vs 7,524 in 1999, 10,828 vs 8,067 in 2022 (ratio 1.13 to 1.34; absolute gap 983 to 2,761). Cause-specific (CDC MIOV, nonmetro vs metro per 100k): suicide 19.6 vs 13.9 and firearm deaths 17.5 vs 13.5 in 2023; metro is higher for homicide and overdose, though rural overdose grew faster 2019 to 2023 (+63% vs +49%).

Exclusions

The filter t.premature_death_rate IS NOT NULL excluded 309 county-years (about 5% of 6,264), all small counties such as Morgan County UT and Teton County ID. Without it (zero numerator, a floor) the 1999 nonmetro-to-metro ratio is 1.115 versus 1.131 and 2022 is 1.340 versus 1.342. The filter r.population IS NOT NULL excluded 2 counties. The filters t.premature_death_numerator IS NOT NULL and t.premature_death_denominator IS NOT NULL excluded county-years before 2013 (no denominators). The filter s.rate IS NOT NULL excluded about 11% of counties for MIOV suicide in 2019.

Coverage caveat: geo.rural_urban_continuum holds only the 2023 classification (declared window 2022 is stale; observed 2023), so for the 1999, 2019 and 2024 figures the 2023 codes and populations were applied to all years; counties that changed metro status are classified by current status, which can bias the historical gap. The warehouse has no all-cause rate or life expectancy by urbanization, so the all-cause figures are from NCHS Data Brief 417 (cdc.gov), not computed.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Life expectancy by rurality: not available by urbanization in warehouse
