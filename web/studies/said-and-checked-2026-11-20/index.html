Said & Checked

What officeholders said, and what the data shows

2026-11-20 · 47 statements from 18 speakers

17 checked: 7 supported, 8 unsettled, 2 unsupported · 30 not checkable

Nov. 19 and 20 brought President Trump's three-way summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, a Senate vote on ending the war in Iran, Senate passage of a bipartisan permitting bill, and a House Homeland Security Committee hearing with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin on the FAA ground stop, recent tech outages and a reported Iranian dirty-bomb plan. This page lists what officeholders in scope were quoted saying in that coverage, 47 statements from 18 speakers.

Seventeen statements made checkable factual claims or rested on a stated factual premise. Checks against AskAmerica's warehouse found U.S. gasoline and diesel prices above their pre-war levels, CISA's workforce down about 35% since January 2025, and Oklahoma receiving about 1.6% of federal highway formula funds. A House roll call confirmed how Mullin voted on the 2021 infrastructure law, and the Senate's record confirmed the permitting bill's Nov. 19 passage.

Several claims could not be decided from the record. No official root-cause finding has been published for the outages, no on-record agency assessment of the dirty-bomb threat's imminence was found beyond the secretary's own testimony, and China's published readouts of the summit do not mention Iran or the Strait of Hormuz. Each statement's test, finding and sources are shown with it.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores. Statements are grouped two levels deep, by speaker and then topic or by topic and then speaker, and every group is collapsed. A topic with a single statement is listed under Other. The counts beside a name cover only that person's statements on this page, and are not a rating of the person. Statements that could not be checked are folded under each speaker. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 19 or 20, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, as read on Nov. 20 at about 7:40 ET, less the stories earlier pages already used, plus the one transcript from the fixed transcript sources dated in that window (the White House text of the Nov. 19 U.S.-Russia-China declaration). Transcripts from Nov. 13 to 18 were searched only to confirm quotes. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. Quotes under five words were left out, as were commentators' accounts of what an official said and statements by officials outside the offices in scope. Statements are worded as the outlet reported them; entries beginning "Reported as" are the reporter's wording.
