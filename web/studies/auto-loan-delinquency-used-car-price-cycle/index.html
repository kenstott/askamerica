Auto delinquency tracks used-car prices only loosely (r about -0.4); lenders are not facing a collapse in collateral, but 2021-22 vintages are underwater-prone

NY Fed Consumer Credit Panel (warehouse econ.household_debt_credit, 2003Q1-2026Q3) vs BLS CPI used cars and trucks CUSR0000SETA02 (FRED, quarter-end months)

Answer

Loosely, and with the wrong sign for a simple story. Across 2003Q1-2026Q3 (95 quarters) the year-over-year change in used-car CPI correlates about -0.40 with the year-over-year change in both the 90+ day delinquent share of auto balances and the flow into 30+ day delinquency: falling prices go with rising delinquency, but only ~16% of variance is shared. Delinquency is slow-moving and mostly follows credit quality, interest rates and the labor market. Today the used-car index is down about 14% from its Dec 2021 peak but still about 30% above Dec 2019, so on this measure lenders are not facing collapsing collateral. The stress is in borrowers: 90+ day delinquent share is 5.66% in 2026Q3, the highest in this 2003-2026 series, up from 3.73% in 2022Q4.

The link is unstable: near zero for the 90+ share before 2020, strong only in the post-2021 boom-and-bust. Levels: price vs 30+ flow r=-0.44 (all), but price level vs 90+ share is +0.33 over the full sample (spurious trend overlap). Lags: price YoY leads the 90+ share change most strongly at 1-3 quarters (r about -0.45 to -0.46), faint for the 30+ flow. Quarterly overlapping YoY changes are autocorrelated, so p-values would be overstated; treat these as descriptive, not causal. In 2008-09, delinquency rose while used prices fell sharply into early 2009 (index 122 vs 139 in 2008Q1), consistent with the link, but recession job losses coincided.

Current exposure

  • Used CPI: 179.9 (Oct 2026) vs peak 211.7 (Dec 2021): -14%; vs Dec 2019 (140.1): +30%. Price YoY has hovered within roughly +/-3% since 2025, so there is no current price slide.
  • Delinquency is rising without a price drop: 90+ share 5.66% (2026Q3), flow into 90+ 3.02%, flow into 30+ 7.96%; auto balances $1.73T (record).
  • Exposure concentrates in loans written 2021-23 at peak prices: the index fell about 9-10% YoY through 2022-24, so those borrowers likely have negative equity, raising loss severity on repossession. That is an inference from index levels; the corpus has no loan-level LTV, vintage or recovery data.

Caveats

CPI used cars is a retail, quality-adjusted index, not wholesale auction prices (Manheim), which move faster and lead; Manheim is not in the corpus. NY Fed data cover all lenders and all credit tiers, so subprime stress is diluted. The used-car series was fetched from FRED (BLS CUSR0000SETA02, seasonally adjusted, quarter-end months), not the warehouse; I typed quarter-end values into the analysis from that fetch. A 2020-21 stimulus/forbearance period depressed delinquency while prices spiked, which drives the post-2021 negative correlation.
