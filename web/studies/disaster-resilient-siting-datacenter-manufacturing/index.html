213 counties pair few major-disaster declarations with above-median power, gas, highway and water infrastructure; Maricopa AZ, Fresno CA, Clark NV, Pinal AZ and Mohave AZ lead

Summary

Screening all 3,235 county records, 213 have 5 or fewer major-disaster (DR) declarations since 2000 (the bottom quartile; national median is 8) and sit at or above the national median on all four infrastructure measures: highway miles, gas pipeline miles, installed power capacity and water withdrawals. Top by average infrastructure percentile: Maricopa AZ (4 DRs), Fresno CA (4), Clark NV (1), Pinal AZ (3), Mohave AZ (3), Imperial CA (5), Pecos TX (5), Sweetwater WY (1), Allegheny PA (4), San Juan NM (4). Many with only 0-1 declarations are in the interior West (Sweetwater, Carbon, Lincoln, Converse WY; Moffat CO; Klamath OR; Humboldt NV).

Method and exclusions

Exposure: distinct disaster numbers with declaration_type DR, 2000 onward, per county (disasters.disaster_declarations). Excluded: rows with incident_type Biological (the COVID-19 declarations issued once per state; left in, nearly every county would register a disaster) and rows with a null county_fips (statewide/tribal entries). I did not rerun the screen with those rows included; the Biological rows are DR-type for nearly all counties in 2020, so including them would add roughly one declaration to most counties and shift the cut-off, which is why they were removed. Three counties (Lorain, Madison, Ottawa, by name; state ambiguity unresolved) were dropped by the statistical tooling for a null value in the display join, not by my screen's logic.

Infrastructure: NHS route-miles (transport.nhs_highway_county_access); gas transmission miles (energy.county_gas_pipeline_presence); operating nameplate MW, EIA-860 2023 (energy.eia_power_plants); total water withdrawals, USGS 2015 (environment.water_use_county). Counties with no row count as 0. Pass = DR count <=5 and every measure at or above its median; ranked by mean percentile of the four.

Caveats

  • Declarations measure federal response, not physical hazard; arid counties escape flood/storm declarations but face heat, drought, wildfire and water scarcity. Declaration counts also reflect state request behavior.
  • Water is 2015 withdrawals (a track-record proxy, not legal rights or headroom). Power MW is existing generation, not spare grid capacity or industrial load service; rail, substations and industrial rates at county grain were not found (utility data is state-level). Gas miles are spatially prorated estimates.
  • Median thresholds and the 5-DR cut are my choices; large metros (Maricopa, Clark) pass on scale. Not tested for sensitivity to thresholds, and no site-level suitability (land, labor, permitting) is assessed.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Rail, transmission capacity, industrial power price at county grain: No county-grain rail, substation or load-headroom table found; utility data is state-level. Interconnection queue not used.
  • Non-declaration hazards (heat, drought, seismic): Declarations proxy only; NRI/wildfire tables exist but were not combined.
