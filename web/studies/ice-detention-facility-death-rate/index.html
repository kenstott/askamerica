Deaths in ICE custody have hit a two-decade high, with several newly opened or expanded facilities — Adelanto (CA), Krome (FL), and Camp East Montana (TX) — accounting for a disproportionate share

No single askamerica table covers immigration detention; findings below are drawn from ICE's own detainee-death reporting, Human Rights Watch/Physicians for Human Rights, KFF, AILA, and news investigations (NPR, ProPublica/Texas Tribune, ACLU)

Summary

Yes — it has clearly gotten worse, and the worsening is concentrated. 33 people died in ICE custody in calendar 2025, the highest annual total in at least two decades, and 24 more deaths had already been recorded by September 1, 2026 — on pace to meet or exceed 2025. Human Rights Watch/Physicians for Human Rights counted 52 deaths in the 500 days between Trump's January 20, 2025 inauguration and June 4, 2026, a mortality rate more than double the rate under the first year of Trump's own first term and about four times the Biden-era rate — an increase disproportionate to the roughly 70-80% growth in the detained population over the same period. No single facility accounts for a majority of deaths, but a handful of large or newly opened sites are clear outliers: Adelanto ICE Processing Center (California) and Krome North Service Processing Center (Miami, Florida) each recorded 4 deaths since late 2024/2025, and Camp East Montana, a tent facility on Fort Bliss in El Paso, Texas that opened only in August 2025, reached 3 deaths within about six months — the fastest accumulation of any single site — and has drawn ICE's own inspectors finding 49 compliance violations, congressional calls for closure, and an autopsy ruling one January 2026 death (Geraldo Lunas Campos) a homicide by asphyxia even though ICE reported it as a suicide.

This entire answer is therefore built from primary and secondary public sources outside that warehouse: ICE's own Detainee Death Reporting page and detainee death reports, the Human Rights Watch/Physicians for Human Rights report "Dying in Detention" (June 2026), KFF's March 2026 analysis of ICE death reporting, the American Immigration Lawyers Association's (AILA) chronological detainee-death tracker (sourced directly to ICE press releases and death reports), Wikipedia's independently sourced "List of deaths in ICE detention," and NPR's on-the-ground reporting from Camp East Montana. No ICE facility publishes a single, comprehensive death-by-facility count; the facility figures below are assembled by reading location fields off each individual death disclosure and should be read as a documented lower bound, not an exhaustive census.

Which facilities have seen the most deaths / serious medical incidents

  • Adelanto ICE Processing Center, Adelanto, CA — 4 deaths between September 2025 and March 2026 (Ismael Ayala-Uribe, Gabriel Garcia-Aviles, Alberto Gutierrez-Reyes, José Guadalupe Ramos-Solano). Long-running litigation (Public Counsel, California AG Bonta's office) alleges inadequate medical/mental health care predates this period; a federal judge ordered a preliminary injunction in Feb 2026 requiring improved medical staffing and access to specialists.
  • Krome North Service Processing Center, Miami, FL — at least 4 deaths (including deaths at hospitals immediately following Krome custody) since October 2024, part of 6 deaths across Florida facilities in that window. Amnesty International and HRW documented related abuse allegations at Krome and the nearby "Alligator Alcatraz" facility in a December 2025 report.
  • Camp East Montana, Fort Bliss, El Paso, TX — 3 deaths (Francisco Gaspar-Andres, Dec 2025; Geraldo Lunas Campos, Jan 2026 — ruled a homicide by the El Paso County Medical Examiner; Victor Manuel Diaz, Jan 2026) within roughly 6 months of the facility's August 2025 opening — the fastest death rate of any single facility identified. NPR reported it is now the largest ICE detention facility in the country and "one of the facilities with the most detainee deaths." A February 2026 ICE inspection found 49 detention-standard violations, including failure to document suicide-prevention checks; the facility's first operating contractor (Acquisition Logistics, which had never run a detention center) was replaced in March 2026 after congressional and ACLU pressure, though members of Congress continue to call for the facility's closure.
  • Miami Correctional Facility, Bunker Hill, IN — 2 deaths (Lorth Sim, Feb 2026; Tuan Van Bui, Apr 2026).
  • Delaney Hall, Newark, NJ — 2 deaths (Jean Wilson Brutus, Dec 2025; Edwin Lopez-Cornejo, Aug 2026); this facility has also been the site of a documented 2025 oversight-access incident and a 2026 hunger strike/protest.
  • Winn Correctional Center, Winnfield, LA — 2 deaths (Alejandro Cabrera Clemente, Apr 2026; Mamuka Artmeladze, Jun 2026).

Beyond fatalities, PHR's clinical review of 39 of the 2025 deaths found a "high suspicion of inadequate or delayed health care" in several cases — including a stroke victim (Maksym Chernyak) whose emergency transfer was delayed despite obvious neurological symptoms, and a man (Ismael Ayala-Uribe, Adelanto) whose repeated requests for care for an infected abscess were "recurrently mishandled" before he died of septic shock. Seven people died by apparent suicide in ICE custody from January 2025-January 2026, versus one reported suicide in all of 2024 — a signal of serious mental-health-crisis handling failures more than of medical-only incidents. AILA's tracker and a 2020 House Oversight report separately document a pattern of falsified medical records, missed suicide-watch checks, and delayed medication at detention facilities predating the current surge, and a June 2024 ACLU/PHR/American Oversight report ("Deadly Failures") reviewed 52 deaths from 2017-2021 and found DHS consistently failed to investigate or impose consequences — i.e., the failure modes are longstanding, but their frequency has sharply worsened since January 2025.

Has it gotten worse — the trend

Yes, on every reported measure:

  • Raw annual counts: roughly 3-11 deaths/year from 2021-2024 (a 2020 COVID-driven spike of ~21 aside), jumping to 33 in 2025 and 24 more through just the first eight months of 2026.
  • Rate, not just volume: HRW/PHR found the mortality rate in ICE custody is now higher than at any point in over a decade — including higher than during the COVID-19 pandemic — and about 2.5x the first-term Trump-era rate and roughly 4x the Biden-era rate. Detention population grew from about 39,000 (Dec 2024) to over 71,000 (Jan 2026), a ~70-80% increase, but deaths rose far faster than that population growth: HRW calculated an approximately 140% year-over-year increase in the annual mortality rate from January 2025 to January 2026 — i.e., the increase is not simply proportional to more people being held.
  • Facility-level pattern: HRW's report found most of the 39 deaths it clinically reviewed occurred at facilities with populations significantly elevated above their prior three-year average in the two weeks before the death — consistent with overcrowding as a contributing mechanism, and consistent with newly opened, unvetted facilities like Camp East Montana becoming outliers almost immediately.
  • Oversight has weakened, not strengthened, over the same period: HRW and KFF both document that DHS oversight offices that historically investigated in-custody deaths were dismantled or rendered less effective early in the second Trump administration (later subject to court orders to partially restore), medical-reimbursement claims processing for offsite care lapsed for months after an October 2025 contract termination, and a POGO investigation found ICE facility inspections fell even as detention numbers rose.

Methodology and limitations

Deaths were tallied from ICE's own required 48-hour/30-day disclosures, cross-checked across three independently compiled trackers — AILA's chronological list (sourced directly to ICE press releases and Detainee Death Reports), Wikipedia's "List of deaths in ICE detention," and KFF's independent count of 46 deaths as of March 18, 2026 — which are internally consistent with each other and with HRW's count of 52 as of June 4, 2026 after accounting for the different cutoff dates. Facility names were read directly off each entry's disclosed location. Facility counts should be read as a floor, not a ceiling: advocacy groups (American Immigration Council, ACLU) have documented cases where ICE released detainees while hospitalized/comatose specifically to avoid counting a subsequent death as an in-custody death, and at least two additional deaths (Jaime Alanis Garcia, who died fleeing a raid; Daphy Michel, who died three days after release) are excluded from official in-custody counts entirely despite direct ICE-enforcement involvement. "Serious medical incidents" short of death — the second half of the question — have no comprehensive public dataset at all; the qualitative findings above (PHR's clinical case reviews, ICE's own 49-violation Camp East Montana inspection, litigation-documented conditions at Adelanto, and the 2023 NPR/inspection-report and 2020 House Oversight investigations) are the best available substitute, not a count.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Identify facilities with the most serious medical incidents (not just deaths) in custody: No comprehensive public dataset counts non-fatal serious medical incidents by facility. Substituted qualitative evidence: PHR's clinical case reviews of inadequate/delayed care, ICE's own 49-violation Camp East Montana inspection finding, litigation-documented conditions at Adelanto, and prior NPR/House Oversight investigations of falsified records and missed suicide checks. This is documented evidence of the problem, not a facility-level incident count.
