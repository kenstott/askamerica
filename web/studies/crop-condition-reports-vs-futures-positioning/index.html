Speculative Grain-Futures Positioning Does Not Reliably Move Ahead of USDA Crop Condition Reports

CFTC Commitments of Traders (managed money) vs. USDA NASS weekly crop condition, corn 2014-2017

Summary

No, not reliably. Both the peer-reviewed literature on Commitments of Traders (COT) data and an original computation run for this report find essentially no evidence that speculative (managed-money) positioning in grain futures moves systematically ahead of USDA crop condition news. Using CFTC managed-money net corn positions and USDA NASS weekly Illinois corn condition ratings for 2014-2017 (48 weekly report-to-report changes), the correlation between a speculative position change in the week before a Monday USDA Crop Progress report and that report's condition surprise is essentially zero and not statistically significant (r = -0.13, p = 0.38; year-fixed-effects coefficient p = 0.82; sign is unstable across a leave-one-year-out check). A slightly larger, but still only marginally significant, relationship (r = -0.26, p = 0.07) exists between the condition change and the position change spanning the report's release — consistent with funds reacting to news rather than anticipating it. This matches the standing academic finding (Sanders, Irwin & Merrin, and earlier work) that non-commercial/managed-money COT positions are trend-following and do not Granger-cause grain futures returns; it is the reverse, price/news leads positioning, not positioning leading price.

What was tested and why

"Crop condition reports" refers to USDA NASS's weekly Crop Progress report (released Mondays during the growing season, rating each state's corn/soybean/wheat crop Very Poor to Excellent). "Speculative positioning" refers to the CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders (COT) Disaggregated report, which breaks out Managed Money long/short futures positions each Tuesday, published the following Friday.

The literal timing: a Crop Progress report drops Monday afternoon; the next COT snapshot is taken the very next day (Tuesday), so it already captures one day of any reaction. To separate "anticipation" from "reaction" this analysis computed, for each week's report:

  • Lead change: the change in managed-money net corn position over the week strictly before the report (Tuesday-to-Tuesday, ending the Tuesday before the Monday release) — this predates any information from that week's report.
  • Reaction change: the change in managed-money net position from that pre-report Tuesday to the Tuesday immediately following the report.
  • Condition change: the week-over-week change in the state's Good+Excellent percentage.

If speculators were "moving ahead of" the report — i.e., correctly anticipating that conditions were about to deteriorate or improve and trading on it in advance — the lead change should correlate with the coming condition change (negative sign expected: funds add net length ahead of a coming decline in condition, since worse crops mean less supply, higher prices).

The 2016 season (dashboard, bottom panels) illustrates the pattern found in the pooled data: as Illinois corn's Good+Excellent rating climbed steadily from 68% in mid-May to 85% by September (a record crop developing week after week, no surprises), managed money did the opposite of "getting ahead of" that improving picture — it swung from a large net long (+268,000 contracts in mid-June, when the crop still looked only middling) to a large net short (-174,000 contracts by late August, once the bumper crop was already obvious). The funds' big position swing followed the unfolding condition data and the price decline it drove; it did not front-run it.

How this fits the published research

The classic reference here is Sanders, Irwin and Merrin's bivariate Granger-causality tests across ten agricultural futures markets: they found very little evidence that non-commercial (largely managed-money-type) trader positions forecast or lead returns, and substantial evidence of the reverse — that these traders are trend-following, reacting to price changes rather than causing them. An earlier study reached a similar conclusion from a different angle: positions and returns are contemporaneously correlated, but returns Granger-cause positions, not the other way around.

Separately, market-microstructure research on WASDE/Crop Production releases finds that (a) report-day price variance for corn and soybeans runs roughly 7x normal, (b) only about 31-45% of the price move on a Crop Production report day is explained by the surprise in the number itself, and (c) the weekly Crop Progress report's own market impact has shrunk over time relative to the monthly/quarterly WASDE and Crop Production reports, even as those bigger reports' impact has grown. Documented cases of large managed-money position swings occurring in the days immediately before a scheduled report (e.g., a roughly 25% cut to the corn net long in the week before the January 2023 WASDE/Grain Stocks releases, or a full net-long-to-net-short reversal in the seven days before the August 2023 WASDE) show that funds actively reposition ahead of known report dates — but no peer-reviewed study or extension publication was found linking how crowded that pre-report positioning is to the size or direction of the post-report price move, and the September 2022 example in that same literature explicitly shows the position build and the price rally happening together (contemporaneously), not the position leading the price.

Caveats

  • Grain and geography scope: this analysis is corn, and uses Illinois as a Corn Belt proxy rather than a national production-weighted Good+Excellent index (which this warehouse's NASS table does not carry as a single pre-built national series). Illinois tracks the national number closely in most years but is not identical to it.
  • Sample size: n=48 weekly observations pooled across only 4 growing seasons is a real constraint — the leave-one-year-out check shows the sign and significance of the "lead" correlation are not stable, which is itself part of the finding (there is no robust effect to report, not merely an underpowered test of one).
  • 2025 data gap: the CFTC COT series in this warehouse has a near-total gap for the 2025 growing season (only one weekly print loaded), a real 2025 CFTC reporting disruption, so the most recent season could not be added to the pooled sample.
  • Weekly Crop Progress vs. monthly WASDE: this test is specific to the weekly condition ratings. The literature's own finding that Crop Progress's market impact has shrunk relative to WASDE/Crop Production suggests a parallel test on monthly WASDE surprises (which this table also does not carry as a clean surprise series) could show a different, and possibly stronger, relationship — that is a narrower, unanswered question this corpus could not settle here.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Cover more than one grain / national scope: Analysis is corn-only, Illinois-proxy rather than a national multi-grain index, because that is the only slice of this warehouse's NASS condition table with complete gap-free weekly coverage matching a complete COT series (2014-2017). Soybean/wheat and national-index versions were not run given that data constraint; flagged explicitly as a caveat rather than silently generalized.
