Speculative funds do reposition sharply ahead of individual USDA reports, but the typical weekly swing is not systematically larger during the report season

CFTC Disaggregated Commitments of Traders (managed money), CORN and SOYBEANS futures, weekly Tuesday reports, 2006-09-06 through 2026-09-01

Summary

Grain funds visibly reposition ahead of individual, high-conviction USDA releases — the clearest live example is corn right now: managed-money net long in CBOT corn futures went from a 74,819-contract net SHORT in mid-June 2026 to a 401,003-contract net LONG by September 1, 2026, adding 83,555 net-long contracts in the single week before USDA's September 11 WASDE/Crop Production report, which the trade widely expects to cut the corn yield estimate. That single-week move sits around the 90th percentile of every weekly move in the 20-year CFTC record for corn.

But that kind of visible, newsworthy buildup is an EVENT, not a persistent statistical regularity. Measured across the full 2006-2026 history, the average size of week-to-week managed-money repositioning in corn and soybean futures is statistically indistinguishable between weeks that fall in the growing season (when USDA issues a weekly Crop Progress/Condition report) and weeks that don't (Welch's t-test: corn p=0.26, soybeans p=0.61). In other words, there is no detectable systematic amplification of speculative positioning simply because a crop-condition report is imminent — positioning swings large enough to make headlines cluster around specific, high-conviction information events (a surprising WASDE, an extreme weather pattern), not around the report calendar itself.

What the literature says

Trade press consistently documents funds adjusting positions in the days before scheduled USDA releases, but describes this as event-specific rather than mechanical. Recent 2026 examples: managed money built a record gross-long corn position in the week ended Aug 25, 2026, ahead of the widely anticipated Sept 11 WASDE yield cut (Reuters-sourced coverage via Ag Optimus and Barchart); earlier in 2026, ahead of the August WASDE/acreage report, funds instead trimmed to a comparatively small net-short position (16K contracts) because of unusual uncertainty over the record acreage surprise that followed — the opposite behavior, driven by low conviction rather than high. Karen Braun's Reuters commentary (via Agri-Pulse and Barchart) repeatedly frames these as funds positioning on their own private read of yield/weather/acreage risk relative to the trade's survey-based expectation, not a mechanical pre-report ritual. None of the sources found quantify "how much, on average, ahead of every report" — that number only exists by computing it directly from CFTC data, which is what this analysis does above.

Method and what was and wasn't computed

Computed directly from CFTC data (cftc.cot_disaggregated_futures): weekly managed-money net long (money_manager_long_all minus money_manager_short_all) as a share of open interest, for the CORN and SOYBEANS contracts, every Tuesday report from 2006-06-13 to 2026-09-01 (1,055 corn weekly changes, 1,055 soybean). Growing season was defined as April-October, when USDA issues weekly Crop Progress/Condition reports; November-March was treated as off-season, when USDA issues no weekly crop-condition report (only the monthly WASDE). A Welch two-sample t-test compared the average absolute week-over-week change in net-long share between the two periods for each commodity.

What could not be precisely computed: aligning fund positioning changes to the exact release date and content of each week's Crop Progress/Condition report at the day level. USDA's crop-condition table in this corpus (ag.nass_crop_production) carries only a "WEEK #nn" label, not a calendar report date, and there is no verified public crosswalk from that week number to an exact Monday release date in this session's tool access, so a day-precise "funds moved X ahead of the specific condition print" regression was not attempted — doing so with an approximate, self-constructed date mapping risked misattributing which week's condition change a given COT report actually preceded. The growing-season/off-season contrast above is the more conservative test this data supports without that mapping, and a day-of-month proxy for the WASDE calendar (Tuesday report dated the 3rd-9th of the month, i.e., shortly before a typical 8th-12th WASDE release) was run as a secondary check and also showed no material difference (1.81% vs. 1.78% average absolute weekly change).

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Specifically 'crop condition reports' (weekly Crop Progress) rather than only monthly WASDE: The growing-season vs. off-season contrast (when weekly Crop Progress/Condition reports are and aren't issued) is the test actually run, because ag.nass_crop_production carries only a 'WEEK #nn' label with no calendar release date and no verified crosswalk was available in this session to align it day-precisely with COT report dates. A day-of-month WASDE proxy was run as a supporting check instead of a condition-report-specific one.
