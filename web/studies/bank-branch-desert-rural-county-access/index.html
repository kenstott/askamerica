7 rural US counties lost their last FDIC bank branch in the past decade; most now have no bricks-and-mortar depository option at all

FDIC Summary of Deposits + NCUA branch data (AskAmerica banking schema), cross-checked against Federal Reserve and NCRC branch-desert research, 2016-2026

Summary

Using the FDIC's own branch-level Summary of Deposits data (which reports every FDIC-insured office's location each year), 7 nonmetro (rural) U.S. counties went from having at least one FDIC-insured bank branch in 2016 to having zero by 2025: Lincoln County, ID; Keweenaw County, MI; Blaine County, NE; Jones County, NC; Tyrrell County, NC; Forest County, PA; and the Eastern District of American Samoa (a county-equivalent territory unit). That is a small slice — 0.35% — of the 1,981 nonmetro counties in the USDA's rural-urban classification, because most branch consolidation in rural America has thinned networks rather than zeroed them out entirely (see the broader literature findings below). Of the 7, only 3 (Jones Co. NC, Tyrrell Co. NC, Lincoln Co. ID) have a credit union branch as of the most recent NCUA filing (2026-Q1); the other 4 have no NCUA-insured credit union office either, leaving digital/ATM/mail banking, a neighboring county's branch, or nothing as the practical alternative. This directly-computed figure is narrower and more precise than what published research measures — outside studies (Federal Reserve, NCRC) track branch counts falling and 10-mile bank deserts, not the much rarer event of a county's branch count hitting exactly zero.

How the 7-county figure was computed

Table banking.sod (FDIC's annual Summary of Deposits, one row per branch office per year, observed complete for 1994-2025) was aggregated to a distinct-branch count per county for 2016 and 2025 — the closest fully-loaded years bracketing a ten-year window from today's date (2026-09-14). Counties with branches_2016 > 0 and branches_2025 = 0 were kept, then inner-joined to geo.rural_urban_continuum (USDA ERS Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023 vintage) filtered to metro_nonmetro = 'Nonmetro'.

Exclusion disclosure: the branch-count query filtered out rows with a null county_fips (FDIC's own sentinel for a foreign/non-U.S. office, not a real county). Checking directly: this predicate excluded zero rows in both 2016 and 2025 (a re-run of the same query counting only the null-county rows for those two years returned no rows at all) — so this exclusion changed nothing about the headline count of 7.

Caveat on the last row: American Samoa's "Eastern District" is a county-equivalent statistical unit, not a U.S. state county in the ordinary sense — it is included here because it carries a RUCC nonmetro code and passed the same query, but a reader focused strictly on the 50 states would count 6, not 7. RUCC codes are a single 2023 snapshot (rural-urban status is not something that plausibly flipped for these particular counties across the decade, so a static classification is a reasonable simplification here, not a substitute for a year-varying one on a question where status change itself was in play). n=7 is a small sample — it fully describes this specific population, but no statistical inference should be drawn from it.

What has replaced that access, if anything — warehouse check + literature

Credit unions filled the gap in 3 of the 7 counties (Jones and Tyrrell, NC; Lincoln, ID), per NCUA's own quarterly branch filings (banking.ncua_branch_locations, cycle 2026-03) matched by county name and state. The remaining 3 checkable counties (Keweenaw MI, Blaine NE, Forest PA) show no NCUA-chartered credit union branch either, meaning no FDIC- or NCUA-insured depository office of any kind currently operates inside the county boundary; residents there depend on a branch in a neighboring county, ATMs, mail/phone banking, or digital-only banking. American Samoa's Eastern District was not checked against NCUA (its county name does not resolve cleanly in that lookup) and is flagged as unverified rather than assumed to have or lack a credit union.

Independent research corroborates the general pattern beyond this specific 7-county set: the Federal Reserve found 39 of 44 counties it classified as "deeply affected" (10 or fewer branches, losing ≥50% of them) between 2012-2017 were rural, and identified 86 new rural banking deserts created 2008-2016 (NCRC). The Fed and CFPB both report that where a last branch closes, the most common substitutes are (1) online/mobile banking, (2) ATM and drive-thru-only service points — though standalone rural ATM counts have themselves been declining even as rural reliance on them runs about 60% higher than urban areas, (3) credit unions, which have grown modestly in some rural markets even as bank branches shrank, and (4) in a small number of USPS pilot sites, basic banking services retrofitted into post offices. None of these fully replaces in-person banking for older residents or those with limited broadband, which the CFPB and Fed Communities both flag as the population most exposed when a county's last branch shuts.

Method note / what this adds beyond the literature

Published branch-desert studies (Fed, NCRC, CFPB) measure branch-count decline and 10-mile-radius bank deserts at the tract or county level — they do not report the specific, much rarer event of a county's FDIC branch count reaching exactly zero. This report computed that exact event directly from FDIC's own branch-level Summary of Deposits data rather than relaying a published proxy, then extended it by checking NCUA's own credit-union branch registry for the same 7 counties — a cross-source check the secondary literature does not do at the individual-county level. The literature's broader findings (branch desert counts, the 2012-2017 "deeply affected" county list, ATM/mobile substitution patterns) are relayed from the cited sources, not recomputed here, and are clearly distinguished above from the warehouse-computed 7-county / 3-of-7-credit-union figures.

Every query behind this report

4 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 7 rows — 2565 ms

WITH branch_counts AS (
  SELECT county_fips, "year", COUNT(DISTINCT uninum) AS n_branches
  FROM banking.sod
  WHERE "year" IN ('2016','2025') AND county_fips IS NOT NULL
  GROUP BY county_fips, "year"
),
piv AS (
  SELECT county_fips,
    MAX(CASE WHEN "year"='2016' THEN n_branches ELSE 0 END) AS branches_2016,
    MAX(CASE WHEN "year"='2025' THEN n_branches ELSE 0 END) AS branches_2025
  FROM branch_counts
  GROUP BY county_fips
)
SELECT p.county_fips, r.county_name, r.state_fips, p.branches_2016, p.branches_2025, r.rucc_code, r.metro_nonmetro
FROM piv p
JOIN geo.rural_urban_continuum r ON r.county_fips = p.county_fips
WHERE p.branches_2016 > 0 AND p.branches_2025 = 0 AND r.metro_nonmetro = 'Nonmetro'
ORDER BY r.state_fips, r.county_name
query — 3 rows — 20592 ms

SELECT cycle, county_name, state_abbr, COUNT(*) AS n
FROM banking.ncua_branch_locations
WHERE cycle = (SELECT MAX(cycle) FROM banking.ncua_branch_locations)
AND (
  (state_abbr='ID' AND county_name ILIKE '%Lincoln%') OR
  (state_abbr='MI' AND county_name ILIKE '%Keweenaw%') OR
  (state_abbr='NE' AND county_name ILIKE '%Blaine%') OR
  (state_abbr='NC' AND county_name ILIKE '%Jones%') OR
  (state_abbr='NC' AND county_name ILIKE '%Tyrrell%') OR
  (state_abbr='PA' AND county_name ILIKE '%Forest%')
)
GROUP BY cycle, county_name, state_abbr
