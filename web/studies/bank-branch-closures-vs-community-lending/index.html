Branch closures cut local small-business lending by roughly 10%, for up to six years, within a six-mile radius

Primary evidence: Nguyen (2019), AEJ:Applied Economics, merger-driven branch closures 2000s. Extension: FDIC branch-history + FFIEC CRA county lending, 2019-2020 (askengine banking schema).

Summary

The best causal evidence (Nguyen 2019, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics) finds that when a bank branch closes, small-business loan originations in that immediate area (census tract) fall by about 9.6% per year ($453,000 off a $4.7 million baseline), and the decline persists for up to six years (cumulative loss ~$2.7 million per closed branch), with effects dissipating within about six miles. The lending contraction ultimately shows up as roughly a 2 percentage-point drag on local employment growth, mainly through smaller entering firms. Other studies find broader gross effects (up to ~19-22% of lending volume) that shrink toward Nguyen's ~10% figure once other lenders (smaller banks, credit unions, fintechs) partially backfill the gap - i.e. the ~10% is closer to the net, economy-wide effect, while headline ~20% figures are often gross, single-lender effects before substitution.

Step 1 - What the peer-reviewed literature finds

Nguyen (2019) is the strongest causal study: she instruments for branch closings using post-merger consolidation between large banks (both partners >$10B in assets), comparing census tracts inside the same county that did vs. did not have both merging banks' branches beforehand - a design that sidesteps the core problem that banks tend to close branches where local demand is already weak. Findings, quoted directly from the paper: "Annual originations fall by $453,000 after a closing, off a baseline of $4.7 million, and remain depressed for up to six years... This amounts to a cumulative loss of $2.7 million in forgone loans. These effects are very localized, dissipating within six miles... the decline in lending leads to a 2 percentage point reduction in local employment growth rates." She also reports that during the 2008 financial crisis the effect was especially severe, and that only about 20% of closings create true "banking deserts" (no other branch in the tract) - meaning her estimate is a lower bound on what happens in markets that lose their only local branch.

Other work (NCRC) finds gross small-business lending volume falls by as much as ~19-22% in the immediately affected area, and that a subsequent paper on mobile banking found ~19% of that decline is offset when the closing bank has a mobile app, i.e. roughly half the gross shock is absorbed by substitute channels/lenders - consistent with Nguyen's smaller, longer-run, more locally-specific net figure once other lenders in the same tract partially step in. The World Economic Forum and NCRC both note the contraction concentrates in low-income and majority-minority tracts, and that closing all branches in a tract cuts new business formation by about a quarter of a standard deviation.

Step 2 - Extending the question with askamerica's own banking data

We attempted to reproduce a version of this relationship directly from this corpus: FDIC branch-closing events (banking.history, changecode = 'Branch Closing') aggregated to the county in 2019, regressed against the percent change in FFIEC CRA aggregate small-business loan counts (banking.cra_small_business_lending, report_level='200' county totals) from 2019 to 2020, across 2,927 U.S. counties with at least 50 loans in 2019.

Result: the coefficient on branch closings is essentially zero and not statistically significant (coefficient 0.0034, p=0.88, R²<0.001). This is not evidence against the branch-closure effect - it reflects a genuine limitation of the only lending window this corpus currently holds (FFIEC's aggregate CRA small-business file is loaded for 2019-2020 only). 2020 small-business loan counts were massively inflated nationwide by emergency PPP lending, which swamps any signal from the much smaller, longer-horizon effect Nguyen documents. A clean replication of her design needs (a) branch-merger events as an instrument, not raw closing counts, and (b) tract-level lending data spanning several years before and after each closing - neither of which the CRA table here currently provides outside 2019-2020. We flag this explicitly rather than presenting the null result as if it settled the question.

Step 3 - Bottom line and honest caveats

  • Best available causal estimate: local small-business lending falls ~9-10% per year for up to six years after a branch closes, within about a six-mile radius, cumulating to roughly $2.7M in forgone lending per closed branch and a ~2pp employment-growth drag - Nguyen (2019).
  • Gross, single-lender effects reported elsewhere run higher (~19-22%), but roughly half of that gap is typically absorbed by other lenders/channels, so ~10% is the better estimate of the community's net credit loss.
  • Our own attempt to extend this with FDIC/FFIEC data in this corpus was inconclusive because the only CRA lending window loaded here (2019-2020) is dominated by the COVID-era PPP lending surge - a genuine data-coverage gap, not a contradiction of the literature.
  • Any closure/lending correlation computed without an instrument (including ours) is confounded: banks disproportionately close branches where local lending demand is already declining, which is exactly the endogeneity problem Nguyen's merger-based design was built to solve.

WITH closures AS (
  SELECT sr.state_fips, UPPER(TRIM(h.office_county_name)) AS cname, COUNT(*) AS n_closures
  FROM banking.history h
  JOIN geo.state_ref sr ON sr.state_abbr = h.office_state_abbr
  WHERE h.changecode_label = 'Branch Closing'
    AND h.effective_date >= DATE '2019-01-01' AND h.effective_date < DATE '2020-01-01'
  GROUP BY sr.state_fips, UPPER(TRIM(h.office_county_name))
),
cty AS (
  SELECT state_fips, UPPER(county_name) AS cname, county_fips
  FROM geo.counties
),
closures_fips AS (
  SELECT c.county_fips, SUM(cl.n_closures) AS n_closures
  FROM closures cl JOIN cty c ON c.state_fips = cl.state_fips AND c.cname = cl.cname
  GROUP BY c.county_fips
),
lend19 AS (
  SELECT county_fips, SUM(loans_lt_100k_count + loans_100k_to_250k_count + loans_250k_to_1m_count) AS loans_2019
  FROM banking.cra_small_business_lending
  WHERE activity_year = 2019 AND report_level = '200'
  GROUP BY county_fips
),
lend20 AS (
  SELECT county_fips, SUM(loans_lt_100k_count + loans_100k_to_250k_count + loans_250k_to_1m_count) AS loans_2020
  FROM banking.cra_small_business_lending
  WHERE activity_year = 2020 AND report_level = '200'
  GROUP BY county_fips
)
SELECT f.county_fips, COALESCE(f.n_closures,0) AS n_closures, l19.loans_2019, l20.loans_2020,
  (l20.loans_2020 - l19.loans_2019)*1.0/NULLIF(l19.loans_2019,0) AS pct_change
FROM lend19 l19
JOIN lend20 l20 ON l20.county_fips = l19.county_fips
LEFT JOIN closures_fips f ON f.county_fips = l19.county_fips
WHERE l19.loans_2019 > 50
