No public data shows close calls rising specifically at short-staffed airports; nationally, severe runway incursions rose in 2025 while the total fell

FAA Controller Workforce Plan snapshot (2025-2028 plan); NTSB avall 2015-2026; FAA/DOT IG/GAO via web

Answer

Not demonstrably. The data that would settle this (airport-by-airport runway incursions and losses of separation, matched to staffing over time) is not publicly available to me. What exists is mixed. Nationally, total runway incursions fell about 1% in 2025 (to just over 1,600), while the most severe ones (Category A/B) rose 44%, from 9 in FY2024 to roughly 13. That is a tiny count, and no source I read ties it to staffing. At the facility level, short-staffed towers do not show more NTSB-recorded commercial-aviation events.

What I checked

  • Staffing: FAA's Controller Workforce Plan lists 290 terminal facilities. Certified controllers are 41% to 100% of target; the shortest are PDK, MAF, TVC, VGT and HPN. This is one snapshot with no history, so I cannot say which airports became short recently.
  • Outcome proxy: NTSB Part 121/135 accidents and incidents with the tower's airport as nearest airport, 2022-2025. Mean events per tower by staffing quartile were 0.11, 0.21, 0.43, 0.40, from most to least short (trend p=0.087, not monotonic). The shorter-staffed towers had fewer events. That mostly reflects traffic: big hubs have more traffic and are better staffed. I did not control for traffic, so this is not evidence of safety either way.
  • National NTSB trend: incident-type records rose from 124 (2019) to 204 (2025), and Part 121 records from 52 to 77. These are not close-call data, and the NTSB itself has not tied them to staffing.

What the literature says

GAO-26-107320 reports 13,164 controllers at the end of FY2025 (about 6% below 2015) and that 20 of 26 critical facilities were below 85% of target in a 2023 DOT IG review. It treats safety effects as open questions, not findings. DOT IG put FY2024 incursions at 1,758, with pilot deviations causing about 62%. FAA testimony attributed about 20% of FY2023 incursions to controller actions. Brookings notes controller staffing at Reagan National has not been ruled out as a factor in the January 2025 midair collision.

Limits and what would answer it

FAA's runway-incursion dashboard is not accessible to this corpus (govdata-ops#285). A real test would need FAA incursion and operational-error counts by airport and month, with controller headcount by facility over several years, then a before/after comparison against matched airports. Until then, the honest answer is: severe incursions are up nationally, total incursions are flat, and there is no airport-level evidence linking either to staffing.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Airport-level close calls vs staffing: Runway incursion data by airport is not available; used NTSB events as a weak proxy and national incursion figures.
  • Recent change over time at short-staffed airports: Staffing table is a single snapshot with no history.
