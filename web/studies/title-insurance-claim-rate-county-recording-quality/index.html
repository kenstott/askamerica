Nobody can show that counties with older recording systems generate more title claims, and the claim data suggests the effect is bounded

Answer

Not demonstrated, and not testable with public data. I found no study or dataset that links county recording-system age or quality to title-insurance claims. The warehouse has no title-claims table and no county recorder-technology inventory (searches returned only flood, crop, health and unemployment claims). Title claims are not published by county. So the county-level comparison cannot be built, and I did not run one.

What the published claim data does show is that most claim dollars come from causes a better county index would not prevent. This bounds the plausible effect; it does not measure it.

Bounds from published claim data

  • ALTA/Milliman (127,000+ claims, policies 2013-2022): the category most tied to records handling, endorsements, title plant, search and abstract (including take-off of public records, posting, searching and abstracting irregularities), is 7.8% of claims. Basic risks such as fraud and forgery are 24.0%, special risks (liens) 21.0%, escrow/closing 13.7%.
  • About 29% of losses and expenses arise from issues that could not be found in a public records search (fraud, forgery, escrow/closing). For refinances, fraud and forgery are about 40% of claim cost (2025 Milliman update, claims reported through 2023).
  • Texas, the one state with public claim detail, 2024 (all companies): ALTA risk group C (plant, searching and abstracting) is about $8.2M of $57.9M incurred losses (14%) and about $6.6M of $30.0M loss-adjustment expense (22%). I computed these from the TDI Form 10 table. They are statewide, with no county split, and Texas insurers use private title plants rather than raw county indexes.

Why the direct test fails and what it would need

  • Insurers search their own title plants and curate records, which can offset weak county systems and hide any effect (First American's own white paper says curation is what keeps records reliable; an interested-party source).
  • County quality correlates with size, rurality, transaction volume and fraud exposure, so a raw comparison would be confounded.
  • A real test needs insurer claims by property county (from a regulator or insurer) joined to an inventory of recorder systems (e-recording adoption date, indexing depth, digitization). I did not find either.

Caveat on process: the First American page returned HTTP 403, so its claims (claims on roughly 3-5% of policies, 3,000+ county offices) come from a search summary, not a page I read.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Test county recording-system quality/age against title claims: No county-level claims or recorder-system inventory exists in the warehouse or public sources found; delivered bounds from claim-cause shares and Texas statewide Form 10 instead.
