No county-level test links recording-system quality to title-insurance claims — the evidence that exists is indirect and, for the single largest loss category, runs the other way

Summary

This question cannot be answered with an actual county-level correlation -- the data to run that test does not exist, in this corpus or anywhere located on the open web. Title-insurance claims data is proprietary to underwriters and is published only in national industry aggregate (ALTA/Milliman's 2024 study, the most complete dataset found, breaks claims out by cause and by policy year but never by county). There is likewise no standardized public index of a county's recording-system age or quality. What the evidence that does exist suggests, indirectly: the single largest and fastest-growing loss category -- fraud and forgery, over 20% of claim dollars and averaging $143,518 per claim, more than five times other claim types -- is explicitly reported by the ALTA/Milliman study's authors as not geographically concentrated, which runs against the simple version of the 'older/worse records = more claims' hypothesis. Roughly 40% of claim costs (examination/search irregularities, liens and judgments, undisclosed heirs) are plausibly sensitive to how complete and well-indexed a county's records are, and heirs-property research independently documents severe, geographically concentrated clouded-title risk in the rural South tied to incomplete probate recording -- but that concentration tracks inheritance and demographic history, not recorder-office technology per se. Net: plausible mechanism for a subset of claim types, unconfirmed and untestable as an overall claims-rate relationship with data that exists today.

What data would be needed, and why it doesn't exist

Testing this properly needs two county-grain series: (1) title-insurance claim frequency/severity per closed transaction, by county, and (2) an objective measure of recording-system quality -- e.g. years since last index modernization, indexing method (grantor-grantee vs. tract-book vs. Torrens registration), share of historical deed images digitized, or average document turnaround time. Neither exists in comparable form:

  • Claims side: ALTA's Milliman-commissioned study covers 127,228 claims from 2013-2022 representing ~70% of industry premium -- reported only nationally, by policy year, and by claim-cause category. The one geographic check the authors ran (comparing CA, FL, NY, TX) found the fraud/forgery increase 'does not seem to be related to geography.' Claims data below the state level is commercially sensitive underwriter data that is not filed publicly.
  • Recording-quality side: County recorder modernization is documented only through scattered single-county announcements (Delaware County PA, DuPage County IL, Bergen County NJ blockchain pilot, etc.), not as a systematic, comparable index across the roughly 3,000+ recording jurisdictions. First American's industry estimate that 36% of transactions require 'significant non-routine' curative work is a national average with no county breakdown.

Roughly 40-45% of claim dollars fall into categories where a worse county record system plausibly raises risk. The single largest category is explicitly reported as unrelated to geography. Without county-grain claims data this cannot be converted into an effect size -- it bounds the mechanism's plausible ceiling, it does not measure it.

The one genuine empirical comparison that exists

The closest thing to a controlled empirical test of land-title-system quality is Miceli, Munneke, Sirmans & Turnbull's 2008 study of Cook County, Illinois, where the ordinary recording system and the Torrens registration system (a fundamentally more secure, government-guaranteed alternative) coexisted on adjacent parcels since 1897. The study finds parcels self-select into the higher-security Torrens system based on predicted claim risk -- consistent with claim risk varying meaningfully by title-system design -- but this is a within-one-county natural experiment between two legal regimes, not a cross-county comparison of recording-system 'age or quality' as the question poses it, and Torrens coverage today is a tiny, non-random remnant of the U.S. land-title landscape (parts of MN, MA, IL, HI, CO and a few others), so it cannot be extrapolated into a national county-by-county claims comparison.

Verdict

The honest answer: plausible for specific claim types tied directly to search and indexing quality (roughly 20-40% of claim dollars), not demonstrated for total claims, and the single largest cost driver -- fraud and forgery -- is documented by the industry's own analysis as unrelated to geography. No dataset, in this corpus or published anywhere located, currently supports converting that plausibility into a measured county-level effect. Closing this gap would require either a proprietary ALTA/underwriter data-sharing arrangement at county grain, or a research effort to build a genuine county recorder-office quality index from scratch and match it against claim records the industry does not currently publish that granularly.
