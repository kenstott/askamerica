MFP payments covered roughly 85-90% of USDA's own estimated trade-war losses in aggregate — but badly mismatched by crop, and independent research disputes the loss estimate itself

FSA payment microdata vs. USDA ERS ERR-304 (Jan 2022) and independent academic estimates, 2018-2019 trade war

Summary

In aggregate dollar terms, the federal government's Market Facilitation Program (MFP) came close to covering the trade-war damage USDA itself estimated: $23.16 billion in actual MFP payments (2018 and 2019 rounds combined, from FSA payment microdata) against USDA ERS's own peer-reviewed estimate of more than $27 billion in direct U.S. agricultural export losses to retaliating trade partners between 2018 and the end of 2019 — roughly 86% coverage at the national, all-commodity level.

But that headline ratio hides the real story. The MFP formula did not pay farmers in proportion to their actual losses: corn producers were overpaid by about $3 billion relative to corn's own trade-damage estimate (per GAO), while soybean, sorghum, and cotton growers — who bore the overwhelming majority of the real damage (soybeans alone were 71% of estimated losses, $9.4 billion) — were paid less than their own estimated damage. And a separate strand of independent academic research that measures losses by actual observed price effects, rather than USDA's gross export-value method, finds soybean losses were substantially smaller than the ~$8.5 billion soybean growers actually received in MFP payments — the opposite conclusion. The honest answer is: it depends heavily on which loss estimate you trust, but under the government's own most-cited methodology, payments covered most, not all, of the loss, and mis-targeted it badly by crop.

What "losses" means, and why it's contested

There is no single agreed number for what farmers lost. The most authoritative government figure is USDA's Economic Research Service report ERR-304 ("The Economic Impacts of Retaliatory Tariffs on U.S. Agriculture," Jan 2022, building on Grant et al. 2021 product-line estimates). It finds direct U.S. agricultural export losses to retaliating partners — Canada, China, the EU, India, Mexico, and Turkey — totaled more than $27 billion from 2018 through the end of 2019, with China accounting for 95% of it ($25.7 billion). Annualized losses for the commodities it studied ran about $13.2 billion/year from mid-2018 to end-2019. Soybeans made up 71% of the annualized loss ($9.4 billion), followed by sorghum ($854 million) and pork ($646 million).

This is a gross export-value measure: it counts the dollar value of exports that didn't happen to retaliating buyers, not net farm income lost. It does not net out gains some farmers realized from cheaper feed (soybean meal fell in the U.S. domestic market, which helped livestock producers), nor does it fully net out the value of sales redirected to non-retaliating buyers (though the ERS report and cited prior research find that trade-deflection offset was small relative to the direct loss). A separate academic literature, using price-based econometric methods rather than gross trade-flow accounting (e.g., Adjemian, Smith & He 2021, Food Policy), estimates soybean-specific tariff damage that comes in well below USDA's $9.4 billion trade-damage figure for soybeans — in fact, below the $8.5 billion soybean growers actually collected in MFP payments. So depending on which credible measure of "loss" is used, soybean growers were either undercompensated (USDA's own methodology) or overcompensated (the independent price-effect methodology) by several billion dollars.

What the government actually paid

Querying USDA FSA's own commodity-payment microdata for the Market Facilitation Program and its 2019 successor ("Trade Mitigation Program/MFP", disbursed under program names like TMP/MFP 2019 NON SPECIALTY CROPS) gives:

  • 2018 MFP round: $8.64 billion (crops, specialty crops, dairy/hogs/livestock combined)
  • 2019 MFP/TMP round: $14.52 billion (crops, specialty crops, livestock combined, disbursed mostly in 2019 but trickling into payments through 2023)
  • Combined total: $23.16 billion

For context, total federal government payments to the farm sector (all programs — ARC/PLC, CRP, disaster programs, dairy margin coverage, MFP, etc.) were $13.7 billion in 2018 and $22.4 billion in 2019, versus $11.5 billion in 2017 (pre-trade-war) — showing MFP was the dominant driver of the elevated payment years, not incidental to them.

The coverage ratio, and its big caveat

$23.16 billion in payments against USDA's own $27.0 billion loss estimate works out to about 86% aggregate coverage — the government came close to, but did not fully close, the gap it itself measured. That is the honest top-line number if you accept USDA's export-loss methodology as the benchmark.

But GAO's 2022 audit (GAO-22-468) found the 2019 payment formula — which set rates by county based on that county's overall crop mix rather than by the actual per-commodity damage — systematically mismatched payments to damage: corn producers nationally received about $3 billion more than corn's own estimated trade damage, while soybean, sorghum, and cotton producers were paid less than their commodities' own estimated damage. Since soybeans alone accounted for 71% of the total estimated loss, this means the crop that was hurt the worst was one of the ones under-covered, while a comparatively lightly-hurt crop (corn) was over-covered. GAO also found Southern producers of the same non-specialty crop received systematically higher per-acre rates than producers of that same crop elsewhere, for the same reason.
