Trade-war aid to farmers: 2018-19 payments exceeded measured losses in aggregate; 2025-26 bridge aid was designed to cover about 30% of net crop losses

FSA payments (ag.fsa_commodity_payments), USDA ERS farm income, GAO-22-468, Farm Bureau, farmdoc daily; retrieved 2026-11-21

Answer

There is no single trade-only loss total, so no single coverage percentage exists. The two rounds differ. 2018-19: about $23.2B was paid, and the best academic estimate (Janzen and Hendricks, 2020) says that aid exceeded measured market-price damages in aggregate, so payments covered more than 100% of short-run price losses, though not necessarily longer-run losses. Distribution was uneven: GAO found corn was paid about $3B above USDA's own damage estimate while soybeans, sorghum and cotton were paid less than theirs. 2025-26: the $12B Farmer Bridge Assistance (FBA) package (about $9.6B disbursed by April 2026) was set at roughly 30% of estimated per-acre net crop losses, so it covers about a third of losses by design, and those losses are broader than trade. Against one trade-specific measure, CSIS's estimated $5.7B of lost soybean exports to China, FBA soybean payments (about $2.3B) equal roughly 40%, but the two measures are not like for like.

Round 1: 2018-19

Warehouse (ag.fsa_commodity_payments, grouped by program_year): 2018 round $8.65B, 2019 round $14.52B, total about $23.2B, matching GAO's $23B. The recipe warning applies: the 2019 labels begin 'TMP/MFP', so a filter on 'MARKET FACILITATION%' misses them. GAO-22-468 found USDA's 2019 damage estimates were raised non-transparently, with inflated baselines for 14 of 29 commodities, and payment rates tied to county crop mix rather than crop damage. Fortune cites a $26B agricultural loss figure for 2018-2020, but that is a secondary claim I could not trace to a primary estimate, so I do not use it as a denominator.

Round 2: 2025-26

FBA: up to $11B for row crops plus $1B for specialty crops and sugar. Farm Bureau (April 2026): $9.6B disbursed; corn $3.45B, soybeans $2.27B, wheat $1.34B, cotton $874M, rice $307M. Farm Bureau says the payments cover only a share of losses and all nine principal row crops still show negative returns after aid. farmdoc daily's back-calculation (30% of net loss; this is the fetch tool's derivation, not a published figure) implies net losses of roughly $34B across corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton, rice and oats, versus about $10B of payments. Those losses stem from low prices and high costs as well as the trade war, so 30% is not a trade-only coverage rate. The warehouse does not yet label FBA disbursements; the 2025 'Emergency Commodity Assistance Program' ($9.36B) is 2024 economic relief, not trade aid.

Context

ERS national government payments (all programs, $ thousands): 2018 $13.7B, 2019 $22.4B, 2020 $45.6B, 2025 $27.9B, 2026 forecast $47.4B. This includes ARC/PLC and disaster aid, so it overstates trade aid.

Caveats

Loss estimates depend on the counterfactual (price effect only versus lost exports versus net returns). The Nebraska modelling study put hypothetical short-run corn and soybean losses from a 14% cut in Chinese purchases at only $1.3B. I did not run a regression or fully reproduce USDA's damage totals.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Single percentage answer: No authoritative trade-only loss total exists for either episode; ranges given instead.
