USDA trade-war payments covered roughly 85% of ERS's estimated direct export losses from 2018-2019 retaliatory tariffs

Market Facilitation Program disbursements vs. USDA ERS estimated agricultural export losses, 2018-2019

Summary

USDA's Market Facilitation Program (MFP) paid farmers about $23.0 billion in 2018-2019 to offset losses from the retaliatory-tariff trade war — against USDA's own Economic Research Service (ERS) estimate that U.S. agricultural exporters lost "more than $27 billion" in direct export value over that same period. That puts payment coverage at roughly 85% of the ERS-measured loss, leaving an estimated $4 billion-plus gap. The true gap is almost certainly larger: ERS's $27B figure counts only the value of lost export sales to the six retaliating trading partners, not the broader farm-income effects (domestic price depression, storage costs, lost future market share) that a full accounting of "what farmers lost" would include, and tariffs were still partly in effect as of the ERS report's 2021 cutoff, so the estimate does not capture the full multi-year damage.

What farmers lost — the loss estimate

The trade war refers to the 2018 U.S. Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods (and Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs), and the retaliatory tariffs China, Canada, the EU, Mexico, Turkey, and India imposed on U.S. farm exports in response, with individual product tariff increases ranging from 2% to 140%.

USDA's Economic Research Service directly quantified the damage in Economic Research Report No. 304 ("The Economic Impacts of Retaliatory Tariffs on U.S. Agriculture," January 2022), fetched directly from ers.usda.gov for this analysis. Its headline finding: "direct U.S. agricultural export losses due to retaliatory tariffs totaled more than $27 billion during 2018 through the end of 2019," equivalent to an annualized rate of $13.2 billion/year from mid-2018 to end-2019. China's retaliation accounted for about 95% of the total ($25.7B), with the EU ($0.6B) and Mexico ($0.5B) a distant second and third. Soybeans alone accounted for nearly 71% of annualized losses ($9.4B), followed by sorghum ($854M) and pork ($646M). At the state level, Iowa ($1.46B), Illinois ($1.41B), and Kansas ($955M) — the three biggest soybean/sorghum/pork states — bore the largest annualized losses.

This is a measure of lost export value only. It explicitly excludes possible offsetting sales to non-retaliating countries (though ERS cites prior research finding this "trade deflection" effect was small), and it does not capture the depressed domestic prices, storage/basis costs, or foregone future market share (the U.S. share of China's ag imports fell from 20% in 2017 to 10% in 2019 and had not fully recovered as of the report) that a fuller farm-income loss accounting would include. So $27B is a floor on total farmer losses, not a ceiling.

What the government paid — MFP disbursements

USDA's Farm Service Agency ran the Market Facilitation Program in two rounds, funded through the CCC's Section 5 authority. Querying the FSA county-level disbursement data directly in the warehouse (ag.fsa_commodity_payments), summing every program_description containing "MARKET FACILITATION" or "MFP" across all disbursement years (2018-2023, since 2019-round MFP payments continued trickling out for years):

  • 2018 MFP round (crops, dairy/hog, specialty crops): $8.50B, disbursed mostly in 2018 with some carryover into 2019
  • 2019 MFP/TMP round (non-specialty crops, specialty crops, livestock): $14.52B, disbursed mostly in 2019-2020 with a long tail through 2023
  • Combined total: $23.02B

This warehouse-computed total lines up closely with the officially reported figures cited in USDA/GAO documentation ($8.6B for 2018, $14.4B for 2019, $23B combined) — a useful cross-check that the FSA disbursement data in this corpus captures the program accurately. (Separately, USDA also ran a smaller Food Purchase and Distribution Program, roughly $1.2B in specialty-crop purchases for food banks and school programs, and an Agricultural Trade Promotion Program, roughly $200-300M for export-market development — these are outside the FSA commodity-payments table queried here and are not included in the $23.0B figure, so the true total federal trade-mitigation outlay was somewhat higher, on the order of $24.5B.)

Coverage: payments vs. losses

$23.0B in MFP payments against $27B+ in ERS-estimated direct export losses works out to roughly 85% coverage — a real, measurable shortfall of at least $4 billion, even before accounting for the broader income effects the $27B figure excludes. Including the smaller non-MFP trade-mitigation spending (~$1.5B more) would narrow the measured gap to something closer to 90%, but would still leave a shortfall given how much of "what farmers lost" (price depression, lost future market share) the $27B floor never counted in the first place.

This also matches independent findings from GAO's oversight reviews of MFP (GAO-22-104259, GAO-22-468): MFP payment rates were calibrated to USDA's own annual trade-damage estimates, but GAO found the distribution was uneven — the payment formulas ended up over-compensating some crops (notably soybeans, which drove most of the damage estimate but where some analyses suggest payment rates ran ahead of estimated per-unit damage) while under-compensating others, and payments concentrated heavily among the largest operations. So even a topline ~85% aggregate coverage ratio masks real variation: some farmers were closer to made whole, others were not.

Every query behind this report

2 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 32 rows — 45226 ms
SELECT year, program_description, SUM(payments) AS total_payments, SUM(farms) AS total_farms
FROM ag.fsa_commodity_payments
WHERE program_description ILIKE '%MARKET FACILITATION%' OR program_description ILIKE '%MFP%'
GROUP BY year, program_description
ORDER BY year
query — 6 rows — 9688 ms
SELECT year, amount, unit_desc, category, subcategory, variable_description_total
FROM ag.ers_farm_income
WHERE state = 'US' AND category ILIKE '%government%'
AND year BETWEEN 2016 AND 2021
ORDER BY year
