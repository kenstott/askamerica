Federal contract dollars are extremely concentrated: 12 defense/health-hub districts capture roughly a fifth of all contract spending, led by Lockheed Martin

FY2025 USAspending.gov data, 441 congressional districts, place-of-performance basis

Summary

In FY2025 (Oct 2024–Sep 2025), federal procurement contracts obligated $743.8 billion across all 441 U.S. congressional districts — but the money is heavily concentrated, not spread evenly. The top 12 districts alone (out of 441) captured about $266 billion, roughly 36% of the national total, and the distribution's Gini coefficient across all districts is 0.71, in the "extreme concentration" range. The leading districts are almost entirely defense- and health-administration hubs: Northern Virginia (VA-07, VA-11, VA-08, VA-03), the DC delegate district, Fort Worth TX (TX-12, Lockheed's F-35 line), Groton CT (submarine building), Huntsville AL, and Tucson AZ. Nationally, the single dominant contractor is Lockheed Martin ($69.8B combined across its corporate-name variants, ~9.4% of all district contract dollars), followed by Raytheon/RTX, Optum, Boeing, and Electric Boat — a roster dominated by defense primes and a small number of large federal health-claims administrators.

How much money, and where it concentrates

askamerica's fiscal.usaspending_by_district table (sourced from USAspending.gov's spending_by_geography API) carries a dedicated obligated_amount_contracts column isolating procurement contract and IDV award types (codes A, B, C, D, IDV_A–E) from grants, loans, and direct payments — the correct measure for "contract money," distinct from total federal spending. For FY2025, 438 of 441 districts show positive contract obligations (three show small net negatives from de-obligations exceeding new awards that year).

At the other end, the median district received about $521 million in FY2025 contract obligations — roughly 1.5% of what the top district (VA-07) received. This is not a smooth national spread: contract spending clusters tightly around defense installations, federal agency headquarters, weapons-system final-assembly lines, and a handful of health/pharmaceutical-distribution hubs (e.g. TX-33's McKesson presence, PA-04's AmerisourceBergen, KY-03's Humana Government Business).

The top-10 recipients above account for roughly 27% of all district-level contract dollars nationally — an extraordinary concentration for a $743.8B national program, though a caveat below tempers exactly how extreme.

Which single contractor "dominates" varies enormously by district and reflects local industrial anchors, not uniform national market share: Lockheed Martin dominates TX-12 (Fort Worth F-35 plant, $30.5B) and FL-10; Electric Boat dominates CT-02 (Groton submarine yard, $21.4B); Optum Public Sector Solutions dominates VA-07; McKesson dominates TX-33; Raytheon dominates AZ-07 (Tucson missile plant); Humana Government Business dominates KY-03; National Technology & Engineering Solutions of Sandia (LLC) and Triad National Security (LLC) dominate NM-01 and NM-03 respectively (national laboratory management contracts); Sikorsky Aircraft dominates CT-03; and Huntington Ingalls dominates MS-04 (shipbuilding).

Caveats

  • Place of performance, not headquarters. A district's contract total reflects where the work is performed (a factory, base, or shipyard), not where the company is headquartered — so this measures local economic impact, not corporate footprint.
  • Top-100 ceiling per district. The recipient table caps at USAspending's own API page-size maximum of 100 ranked recipients per district per year. In districts with concentrated spending (nearly all of the top 12 above), this fully captures the picture; in districts with many small, dispersed contractors below the 100th rank, the recipient table understates the true contractor count, though the district TOTAL (from the separate, uncapped usaspending_by_district table) is unaffected.
  • Recipient-name normalization. USAspending's own recipient registry carries Lockheed Martin under at least two distinct legal-entity name strings ("CORPORATION" vs. "CORP"); we combined them for the national ranking above. Similar minor name-variant splitting likely affects other large multi-subsidiary contractors (e.g. RTX/Raytheon/Collins Aerospace) that were not further consolidated here, so the true single-parent share for some conglomerates may be modestly understated.
  • District total vs. summed recipients don't have to reconcile exactly — the district-level total nets in large downward de-obligations from recipients outside the top 100, while each recipient row is that recipient's own gross positive sum for the year.
