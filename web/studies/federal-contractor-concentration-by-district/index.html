FY2025 federal contract spending is heavily concentrated in a handful of Virginia/Texas/DC defense districts, while Lockheed Martin, Raytheon and Boeing dominate the contractor list

USAspending.gov place-of-performance data, congressional district grain, FY2025

Summary

In FY2025, the federal government obligated $743.8 billion in procurement contracts (out of $2.83 trillion in total federal obligations across all award types) across the 441 congressional districts tracked by USAspending. Contract money is heavily skewed toward a small number of defense/aerospace-heavy districts: VA-07 (Northern Virginia/Pentagon-adjacent, $34.4B), VA-11 ($33.7B), TX-12 (Fort Worth, home to Lockheed's F-35 line, $31.7B), the District of Columbia's at-large seat ($31.4B), and VA-08 ($25.6B) top the list. Nationally, Lockheed Martin is by far the largest federal contractor/recipient ($62.4B obligated across 48 districts in FY2025), followed by Raytheon, Boeing, and Electric Boat (whose $21.4B is concentrated almost entirely in a single district, CT-02, home to its Groton submarine yard). Despite these concentrated corporate winners, no single district dominates the national total — the top district holds only 3.0% of nationwide contract spending, and the cross-district concentration index (HHI = 75) reads as "unconcentrated" by DOJ/FTC standards. In other words: money is geographically spread across hundreds of districts, but within many individual districts a single large defense/aerospace/health-services contractor accounts for a dominant share.

Where the money is measured

This uses two AskAmerica tables built directly from the USAspending.gov API: fiscal.usaspending_by_district (place-of-performance obligations per district per fiscal year, with a dedicated obligated_amount_contracts column isolating procurement contracts/IDVs from grants, loans and direct payments) and fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district (the top 100 recipients by obligated dollar amount per district per year, server-side pre-ranked by USAspending itself). Only FY2025 is currently loaded for both tables — the district table's schema declares a wider 2017-2025 window, but the observed/backfilled data covers only the single most recent fiscal year, so no multi-year trend can be reported from this corpus today.

A real limitation: the recipient-level table is not contracts-only — it aggregates all award types (contracts, grants, loans, direct payments) per recipient, because USAspending's recipient-ranking endpoint doesn't expose a contracts-only filter at that grain. This means the "top contractor" figures below slightly overstate pure contract dollars for recipients that also receive grants (e.g. TriWest Healthcare Alliance and McKesson receive large non-contract federal health payments too). Where a district's #1 recipient is genuinely a contractor in the ordinary sense (Lockheed, Raytheon, Electric Boat, Leidos), the figures are a close read of actual contract dominance; where it is a health-services or logistics firm, some of the total likely includes non-contract obligations. The recipient table also caps at the top 100 recipients per district, and a "MULTIPLE RECIPIENTS" aggregate bucket for sub-reporting-threshold transactions appears as a real ranked row in most districts (excluded from the national contractor ranking above, but visible in a few district-level top-recipient results, e.g. VA-08 and AL-05).

Disclosure on an exclusion filter used during exploration: when compiling the lowest-contract-spending districts for internal review, a query filtered to obligated_amount_contracts IS NOT NULL. Re-running the same query without that filter confirms it excluded zero rows — no district in the FY2025 data has a null value for this column, so the filter was a no-op and none of the figures reported here (including the districts named as anomalies below) reflect any dropped units.

The pattern is clear: districts with major weapons-system final-assembly or shipyard sites (TX-12/Lockheed F-35, CT-02/Electric Boat submarines, AZ-07/Raytheon missiles) are effectively single-contractor economies at the federal-spending level, with that one company capturing 60-95% of the district's contract total. Districts led by health-administration or logistics contractors (VA-07/Optum, TX-33/McKesson) reflect large federal health-benefits administration and pharmaceutical-distribution contracts rather than defense hardware.

An anomaly worth flagging

Three districts (CA-46, NY-17, AL-04) show negative contract obligations in FY2025 (as low as -$396M in CA-46), even though their all-award-type totals are positive. This reflects downward contract re-estimates, de-obligations, or contract-value corrections exceeding new obligations in that district during the fiscal year — a real feature of federal contract accounting (modifications and closeouts can net negative), not a data error, but worth noting since it means "lowest contract spending" districts include a few with net contract de-obligations rather than simply low but positive activity. All 441 districts have a non-null value for this column, so this observation covers the full population, not a filtered subset.
