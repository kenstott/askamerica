Federal contract money is not evenly distributed by congressional district — the government's own data can't say precisely how much lands in each of the 435 districts, only that it's heavily concentrated in a handful of places and a handful of firms

FY2024 obligations by state/county (AskAmerica/USAspending), FY2025 contractor rankings (GovSpend/Visual Capitalist)

Summary

Federal contract (and broader federal award) money is heavily concentrated, both geographically and among contractors — but no one, including the government's own USAspending.gov, can report exact district-by-district dollar totals with confidence, because congressional-district assignment for spending is inferred from place-of-performance street addresses that are frequently imprecise or missing (per the Congressional Research Service). What can be stated precisely, at the state and county level (which USAspending reports directly, not inferred): total federal obligations were about $3.14 trillion nationally in FY2024, up from $2.94T in FY2022. California ($306B), Texas ($232B), and Florida ($199B) lead in raw dollars; Virginia ($169B) and Maryland ($99B) rank disproportionately high relative to their population because of concentrated federal-agency and contractor headquarters presence in the DC suburbs. At the contractor level, a small handful of firms dominate: Lockheed Martin alone received $73.7 billion in FY2025 federal contract obligations — more than double the #2 contractor, General Dynamics ($35.9B), with RTX, Boeing, Northrop Grumman, and other defense primes rounding out most of the top 10; the largest non-defense contractor is UnitedHealth Group ($25.4B, mostly federal employee/military health benefits administration).

Why a precise per-district figure and per-district contractor breakdown can't be delivered here

This connector (AskAmerica) carries USAspending data at state grain (fiscal.usaspending_by_state) and county grain (fiscal.usaspending_by_county) only — there is no congressional-district table, and neither table carries recipient/contractor names at all (they are geography-by-fiscal-year aggregates, not award-level records). A county is not a substitute for a district: Los Angeles County alone contains roughly 13 congressional districts, so 'top counties' cannot be relabeled 'top districts' without falsifying the unit.

Even USAspending.gov itself, which does publish a congressional-district breakdown on its own site, flags that district assignment there is inferred from the place-of-performance address on each award record, and CRS (Congressional Research Service) reporting on USAspending.gov notes ongoing GAO-documented data-quality issues with place-of-performance fields being imprecise or missing. So even the authoritative source treats CD-level totals as a derived estimate, not a hard number — this is a genuine, source-level limitation, not just a gap in this connector.

Likewise, no table anywhere in this connector names contract recipients/vendors, so a true 'top contractor by district' cross-tab (e.g., which company dominates each specific district) cannot be built from AskAmerica data at all; the contractor ranking below is national, from a different (web) source entirely.

At county grain, the highest-obligation places of performance in FY2024 were Los Angeles County, CA ($62.1B), Washington, DC ($50.8B), Fairfax County, VA ($44.5B — home to major defense/IT contractor campuses), San Diego County, CA ($32.8B, naval/defense presence), and Maricopa County, AZ ($32.2B). These are the counties whose congressional districts would be expected to rank highest if a true district-level table existed, but the county-to-district ratio (one county to several districts) means this cannot be read as a district ranking.

What the contractor data show (national, from GovSpend/Visual Capitalist, not AskAmerica)

FY2025 federal contract obligations by top prime contractor (SAM Unique Entity ID contract data): Lockheed Martin $73.7B, General Dynamics $35.9B, RTX (Raytheon) $34.0B, UnitedHealth Group $25.4B, Boeing $23.1B, Northrop Grumman $14.8B, TriWest Healthcare Alliance $13.4B, Leidos $12.6B, McKesson $11.9B, Huntington Ingalls Industries $11.2B. Defense firms hold 8 of the top 14 slots; the largest non-defense concentration is healthcare (UnitedHealth, TriWest, McKesson, Cencora) and IT/cyber services (Leidos, Booz Allen Hamilton). Five primes (Lockheed, RTX, General Dynamics, Boeing, Northrop Grumman) account for roughly 30% of all DoD contract spending, per the same analysis.

Because most of these contractors' work is performed at specific plants, shipyards, and campuses (F-35 final assembly in Fort Worth, TX; submarine construction in Newport News/Groton; missile production in Tucson, AZ), their dollars are extremely concentrated in the specific districts hosting those facilities rather than spread evenly across a company's home state — which is exactly the kind of fact a true CD-level, recipient-level dataset would need to establish precisely, and exactly what neither this connector nor a reliable public source currently provides at that combined grain.

Bottom line for someone who wants exact numbers by district

For a specific district, the best available (though still 'inferred') source is USAspending.gov's own Profiles of States and Territories tool, which publishes award totals down to congressional district and by top recipient for that district, built from the same place-of-performance data discussed above. Treat any single-district total from that tool as an estimate, not an audited figure, per USAspending's and CRS's own caveats.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Exact dollar total of federal contract money for each of the 435 congressional districts: Neither AskAmerica (state/county grain only, no CD table) nor the authoritative source (USAspending.gov, which itself labels CD assignment as inferred from imprecise place-of-performance addresses per CRS/GAO) can produce this as a hard, audited figure. Delivered instead: precise state-level and county-level obligation rankings (AskAmerica/USAspending data) as the finest reliable geography, with the county-to-district ratio problem stated explicitly, plus a pointer to USAspending's own (caveatted) CD search tool for a specific district.
  • Which contractors dominate within each specific district: No dataset available to this session (AskAmerica or public) combines recipient name with congressional-district place of performance at the precision the question implies; this would require raw award-level USAspending records filtered by district, which is beyond both this connector's tables and the web sources consulted.
