Federal contract and award money by congressional district: top-100 recipients concentrated in defense, health-administration contractors; district rankings distorted by loan/Medicare reporting artifacts

FY2025 USAspending obligations by place of performance, 441 congressional districts

Summary

In FY2025 (Oct 2024-Sep 2025), federal agencies obligated about $2.83 trillion across all award types (contracts, grants, loans, direct payments) to the 441 U.S. congressional districts tracked by USAspending. Once loan face-value and Medicare-administrator awards are stripped out (both misreport place of performance to a servicer's office rather than where the money actually lands), the districts with the heaviest real federal-money concentration are places with a major federal agency headquarters or a large defense contractor's production site: DC-98 ($45.2B, federal agency HQs), VA-07 and VA-11/VA-08 (Pentagon-adjacent Northern Virginia, ~$28-38B each), TX-12 ($33.1B, Lockheed Martin's Fort Worth F-35 plant), and CT-02 ($25.1B, General Dynamics' Electric Boat submarine yard in Groton). At the national level, the recipients that dominate these district totals are almost entirely large defense and health-administration contractors: Lockheed Martin (~$69.5B combined across name variants), Raytheon/RTX (~$29.9B), Boeing ($22.1B), Electric Boat ($21.4B, concentrated wholly in one district), Northrop Grumman ($10.5B), and major federal-services firms Booz Allen Hamilton and Leidos. In several districts a single contractor accounts for the large majority of all measurable spending: Lockheed Martin is 92% of TX-12's non-loan/CMS total, Electric Boat is 85% of CT-02's.

What this measures, and an important scope caveat

This corpus does not carry a contracts-only breakout at congressional-district grain. The table used, fiscal.usaspending_by_district, aggregates all USAspending award types (procurement contracts, grants, loans, and direct payments) by place of performance. The companion recipient table, fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district, is similarly all-award-type, and is capped at the top 100 recipients per district per year (USAspending's own API page-size ceiling) -- it fully captures concentrated districts but undercounts districts with many small, dispersed recipients below the 100th rank.

That said, the recipients that actually dominate the national and district-level rankings -- Lockheed Martin, Raytheon/RTX, Boeing, Electric Boat, Northrop Grumman, BAE Systems, Bell Textron, Rolls-Royce, Booz Allen Hamilton, Leidos -- are genuine federal procurement contractors, so the "which contractors dominate" half of the question is well supported by this data even though the district totals themselves are not contracts-only.

The MULTIPLE RECIPIENTS artifact -- why the raw district ranking is misleading

Ranked by raw obligated_amount with no adjustment, the top districts are IN-07 ($143.7B), KY-03 ($122.1B), PA-10 ($114.7B), and ND-00 ($98.4B) -- figures far larger than DC-98's entire federal-HQ total. Querying the recipient breakdown shows why: in every one of these districts, the #1 line item by a wide margin is USAspending's own "MULTIPLE RECIPIENTS" bucket -- an aggregate of sub-reporting-threshold transactions (e.g. $138.7B of IN-07's total, $113.9B of PA-10's, $92.9B of KY-03's). This bucket is overwhelmingly loan-guarantee, benefit-payment, or program-administration money whose place of performance is recorded at wherever the servicing office or program administrator sits, not where the underlying economic activity happens. obligated_amount_excl_loans_excl_cms_admin strips out loan-type awards (07/08) and CMS/Medicare-administrator awards specifically for this reason, and under that measure these four districts fall out of the top ranks entirely (their non-loan/CMS totals run $6-13B, an order of magnitude lower). Any ranking of "which districts get the most federal contract money" that uses the raw total without this adjustment is measuring loan-servicer and benefit-administrator office locations, not contractor activity.

Two distinct dominance patterns show up. Classic defense-hardware contractors (Lockheed, Electric Boat, Boeing, Rolls-Royce at IN-07, BAE Systems at PA-10, Bell Textron at TX-12) concentrate overwhelmingly in the single district hosting their production facility -- Electric Boat is 85% of CT-02's entire non-loan/CMS total and appears in no other district's top-100 list at all. Federal-services and health-administration contractors (Booz Allen, Leidos, Northrop Grumman, TriWest, Optum) spread across 30-58 districts, reflecting distributed IT/consulting workforces or claims-processing operations rather than a single plant.

Data window and methodology notes

  • FY2025 (Oct 2024-Sep 2025) was used because it is the only recent, fully-loaded year at congressional-district grain in this warehouse for both the district-total and recipient tables; the district-total table has an interior gap for 2018-2024 and the recipient table for 2018-2022, with the most recently completed prior loaded year being 2017.
  • DC and the territories (PR, VI, GU, AS, MP) are included in district counts under USAspending's non-voting delegate district code "98"; they are not congressional voting districts in the ordinary sense and are called out explicitly above rather than silently folded into state comparisons.
  • The recipient table caps at the top 100 recipients per district per year (the USAspending API's own page limit), so national recipient totals here are a sum over district top-100 lists, not a true unconstrained national total -- a recipient with many small, dispersed sub-$100th-rank awards across many districts would be undercounted.
  • No contract-versus-grant-versus-loan award-type breakout exists at district grain in this corpus; the loan/CMS exclusion column is the closest available proxy for isolating real economic activity from reporting artifacts, and was used throughout the "real spending" rankings above.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Specifically 'contract' money as distinct from grants/loans/other award types, by district: No contract-only breakout exists at congressional-district grain in this corpus; fiscal.usaspending_by_district and usaspending_recipients_by_district aggregate ALL award types. Reported all-award-type totals and, as the closest available proxy, a loans-and-CMS-administrator-excluded figure to strip out the two reporting artifacts known to most distort place-of-performance. Stated this limitation explicitly in the report rather than presenting the all-award total as contracts-only.

WITH nat AS (
  SELECT recipient_name, SUM(obligated_amount) total
  FROM fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district
  WHERE "year"='2025' AND recipient_name <> 'MULTIPLE RECIPIENTS'
  GROUP BY recipient_name
), tot AS (
  SELECT SUM(obligated_amount) grand_total FROM fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district WHERE "year"='2025'
)
SELECT (SELECT SUM(total) FROM (SELECT total FROM nat ORDER BY total DESC FETCH FIRST 10 ROWS ONLY)) AS top10_sum,
       (SELECT grand_total FROM tot) AS grand_total
