Federal contract dollars flow overwhelmingly into a small set of defense- and health-contracting districts, led by Lockheed Martin and Northern Virginia

FY2025 USAspending obligations by place-of-performance congressional district (fiscal.usaspending_by_district, fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district)

Summary

Federal procurement contract dollars are extremely concentrated by congressional district. In FY2025, the 441 U.S. House districts (place-of-performance basis) received a combined $743.7 billion in contract/IDV obligations, but the top 10 districts alone captured roughly $237 billion (32%) of that total, and the Gini coefficient across all districts is 0.71 ("extreme concentration" in the 0-1 scale, where 1.0 means a single district holds the entire total). The top districts are dominated by defense and federal-health contracting hubs: Virginia's 7th and 11th districts (Northern Virginia/Pentagon-adjacent), Texas's 12th (Lockheed's Fort Worth F-35 line) and 33rd (Dallas-Fort Worth), the District of Columbia's at-large seat, and Connecticut's 2nd (Electric Boat submarine yard in Groton). Nationally, the largest single contractor is Lockheed Martin (~$69.5B across its two USAspending name variants), followed by Raytheon/RTX, Optum's public-sector unit (TRICARE managed care), Boeing, and General Dynamics' Electric Boat subsidiary.

How much money, and where

This confirms the pattern visible in the district ranking: Lockheed Martin, Raytheon/RTX, Boeing, and Northrop Grumman — the traditional "Big Five" defense primes minus General Dynamics's corporate parent (which shows up here through its Electric Boat subsidiary) — dominate both nationally and in nearly every high-value district. A cross-check against public reporting on FY2025 USAspending data (Visual Capitalist/GovSpend coverage) puts Lockheed Martin's total FY2025 federal award value at roughly $73.7B nationally and the top 15 contractors' combined share at about 35% ($287.7B) of total federal contract obligations — consistent with (slightly higher than, reflecting a broader all-agency, all-geography scope than this district-level recipient ranking) the figures computed here.

Caveats

  • Contract vs. all-award mismatch at the recipient level. fiscal.usaspending_by_district isolates contract/IDV obligations cleanly (obligated_amount_contracts), but fiscal.usaspending_recipients_by_district's obligated_amount column covers ALL award types (contracts, grants, direct payments) because USAspending's recipient-ranking endpoint does not offer a contracts-only filter at that grain. For the districts and firms above (Lockheed, Boeing, Electric Boat, Raytheon, Northrop) this is not a meaningful distortion — these firms' federal business is overwhelmingly procurement contracts — but it does inflate figures for recipients like Optum, TriWest, and McKesson, whose totals may include some non-contract federal health-program payments.
  • Recipient table is capped at the top 100 per district per year (USAspending's own API page-size ceiling), so a district with many small, dispersed contractors below rank 100 is undercounted relative to a concentrated one — this affects the completeness of a full national contractor total, not the ranking of the largest firms shown here.
  • DC-98 and other delegate districts (Puerto Rico, Guam, etc., code "98") are non-voting congressional districts, included here as the API reports them, not full voting House seats.
  • FY2025 is the most recently complete fiscal year in this dataset (Oct 2024-Sep 2025); the source publishes roughly one year behind real time.
