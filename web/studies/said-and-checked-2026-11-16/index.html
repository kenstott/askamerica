Said & Checked

What officeholders said, and what the data shows

2026-11-16 · 17 statements from 7 speakers

6 checked: 2 supported, 4 unsettled · 11 not checkable

This page lists 17 statements by 7 officeholders, drawn from seven NPR and PBS NewsHour stories and one CBS 60 Minutes report. They concern the Nov. 13 nationwide ground stop, the Iran war, a court order halting work at a public golf course in Washington, and what the parties plan after the Nov. 3 election. Two other 60 Minutes reports were read and quoted no officeholder in scope.

6 statements could be checked, and all 6 have been: 2 supported and 4 unsettled. Unsettled means evidence was found but does not decide the claim. The two supported statements are the transportation secretary's on the ground stop, and both rest on the FAA's published account, which describes its own findings as initial.

The four unsettled statements turn on records that are not public or on a judgment word. The Army's investigation of the March 1 drone strike in Kuwait has not been published, no official count of drones that got past U.S. defenses in the war's first days was found, and the condition of the golf course is documented only through a news account of a court filing.

The remaining 11 statements are opinions, predictions and promises. They are listed because they were said, not because they can be checked. No statement on this page is linked to a counter-statement or to a contradicting statement by the same speaker.

How to read this page
Supported
Warehouse or external primary data confirms the statement.
Unsettled
Evidence was found, but it does not decide the claim: it turns on how a law or a loose term is read, or the evidence does not speak to the specific point.
Unsupported
No evidence was found in the AskAmerica warehouse or any external factual source. This does not mean the statement is false.
Contradicted
A primary source, read directly, says something different on the specific point, but some question remains open: an inquiry is unfinished, or the source could be using a different definition or period.
False
A settled official record, read directly, rules the statement out: a published measurement, no ordinary reading of the statement that fits it, and nothing pending that could change it.
Citejacked
The speaker cites a source that does not say what the speaker claims.
Premised prediction
An opinion or prediction that rests on a factual premise. The label rates the premise only, never the opinion or prediction built on it.
Counter-statement
A link to a statement by another speaker, or to an official document, that directly addresses the same point and says otherwise. A link is not a finding about which is right, and a rating shown beside a counter-statement rates that statement only.
Said more than once
When a speaker makes the same claim several times in similar words, it is listed once, with the other wordings and where each was said shown beneath it. Versions that differ on a figure, date or cause stay separate.
Contradicts own statement
A link between two factual statements by the same speaker that cannot both be true on the same specific point. A link is not a finding about which one is right; each statement keeps its own label.
Not checkable
An opinion, prediction, promise or personal attack. No data can settle it.

Every statement is listed, whatever its kind. There are no scores. Statements are grouped two levels deep, by speaker and then topic or by topic and then speaker, and every group is collapsed. A topic with a single statement is listed under Other. The counts beside a name cover only that person's statements on this page, and are not a rating of the person. Statements that could not be checked are folded under each speaker. Selection: Every statement by an in-scope officeholder quoted in a story published on Nov. 15 or 16, 2026 in the NPR Politics and PBS NewsHour Politics feeds, and in the Nov. 15, 2026 transcripts of CBS 60 Minutes, as read on Nov. 16 at about 9:00 ET, less the stories and transcripts the Nov. 15 page already used. PBS NewsHour carries Associated Press reporting. One NPR story listed in the feed had no readable text and was skipped. A recorded clip of an official aired in a 60 Minutes report is treated as a quotation in a story. Quotes under five words were left out, as were commentators' accounts of what an official said and one reporter's summary of positions with no stated occasion. Statements are worded as the outlet reported them unless a transcript is linked; entries beginning "Reported as" are the reporter's wording.

UnsettledFactual claim

At a March 2, 2026 Pentagon briefing, about the U.S. Army command post at Port Shuaiba, Kuwait, struck by an Iranian drone the day before, killing U.S. soldiers.

And in that particular case it happened to hit a tactical operations center that was fortified.

No official description of the struck structure other than the secretary's own was found. CBS reports that the Army finished its investigation in June 2026 and that the Pentagon declined to say whether the unit was denied adequate force protection; no published report was found. In the CBS report a major from the unit says the protection there "was to stop car bombs and threats like that" with "nothing there designed to protect from an aerial attack", and describes the building as a "quadruple-wide trailer". Those are a witness's accounts, not an official record, and the answer may turn on what "fortified" covers.

How this was tested True if an official record (the Army's investigation, a Defense Department or Central Command statement, congressional testimony) describes the struck structure as a fortified tactical operations center. False if a settled official record, read directly, describes it as not fortified against the kind of attack that hit it. Cannot be determined if the only accounts are witnesses' and news reports', or if it turns on what "fortified" means.

Evidence: CBS 60 Minutes transcript, Nov. 15, 2026, Hegseth and Caine Pentagon briefing transcript (American Rhetoric copy)
