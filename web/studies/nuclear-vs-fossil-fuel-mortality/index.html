Coal is the most dangerous per unit of power, gas is in the middle, and nuclear is far safest, though modern US coal is much cleaner than the classic figures suggest

Summary

Coal is the most dangerous, natural gas is second, and nuclear is the safest by a wide margin. The standard estimates are about 25 deaths per TWh for coal, 2.8 for gas and 0.03 for nuclear. Those coal and gas numbers come from a 2007 European study. For the US, a 2023 Science study (Henneman et al.) found coal-plant pollution killed about 460,000 Medicare enrollees from 1999 to 2020, but annual deaths fell from about 50,000 in 1999 to about 1,600 in 2020 as scrubbers went in and plants closed. That is a floor (Medicare only, ages 65+), and the current US coal rate may be nearer gas than the old 25 per TWh figure. Nuclear being safest is not in doubt.

Evidence

  • Our World in Data, using Markandya and Wilkinson (Lancet 2007) and Sovacool et al. (2016): coal 24.6, gas 2.8, nuclear about 0.03 deaths per TWh, covering air pollution and accidents.
  • US scale from the warehouse (EIA, energy.eia_electricity_generation, 2024): coal about 652 TWh, gas about 1,870 TWh, nuclear about 782 TWh. Rollup caveat: the energy_source_code column mixes rollup rows (such as ALL) with leaf rows. My query flagged this, and I summed only one code per fuel (COW, NG, NUC) with no ALL double-counting, so these three figures are not contaminated. Gas produces nearly three times coal's output, so total harm from the two may be closer than per-TWh rates suggest.
  • Henneman et al. (Science, 2023): independent US data, 480 coal plants, 1999 to 2020. Coal PM2.5 was about 2.1 times as deadly per unit exposure as PM2.5 from all sources. Annual deaths fell to about 1,600 in 2020. Dividing by roughly 770 TWh of 2020 coal output gives about 2 per TWh, my own rough lower-bound derivation.

Assumptions that matter

  • Nuclear's 0.03 rests on counted accident deaths plus an estimate for Chernobyl and Fukushima. Linear-no-threshold models of low-dose radiation give far higher possible totals (thousands to tens of thousands of cancers) from the same events, and the assumption is contested. Even high modeled totals leave nuclear well below coal and at most comparable to gas.
  • The fossil-fuel baseline is 2007 European data. Newer PM2.5 epidemiology suggests the true risk is higher than the 2007 figures; OWID's own global coal estimate is 93 to 224.
  • Gas figures exclude methane and climate harm. Nuclear waste and proliferation are not health-death figures and are excluded.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Recompute death rates from warehouse: No energy-source mortality table in the corpus; only generation volumes were queried.

query — 4 rows — 12059 ms
