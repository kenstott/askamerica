How much more dangerous per mile are small trucking companies than the big carriers?

Answer

Small trucking companies are measurably more dangerous per mile than large carriers, but the size of the gap depends heavily on which figure is used.

  • Published FMCSA research (most representative): carriers with fewer than 5 power units average 3.84 crashes per 100 power units, versus 2.98 for carriers with more than 500 power units — a gap of about 25–30%, not an order of magnitude. Industry-wide average crash rate runs about 0.74–0.96 crashes per million miles; FMCSA's own audit-failure threshold is 1.5–1.7 crashes per million miles.
  • askamerica's own data (transport.fmcsa_carriers, recordable_crash_rate field): restricted to the ~43,700 carriers (of 4.47M) that have undergone an FMCSA compliance review and so carry a nonzero computed rate, small carriers (1–5 power units) show a median 13.33 vs 1.19 for large carriers (>100 power units) — an ~11x gap — and a mean 42.06 vs 19.61 — a statistically significant ~2.1x gap (Welch's t-test, t=-5.71, p=1.5e-8).

Caveat driving the discrepancy: FMCSA only computes recordable_crash_rate for reviewed carriers. Large carriers get reviewed as routine oversight; small carriers are reviewed almost exclusively because something (a crash, a violation) already flagged them. That makes the small-carrier nonzero subsample a self-selected, risk-skewed group, not a random cross-section — which is why the raw-field ratio (11x median, 2.1x mean) runs so far above the ~29% gap the full FMCSA fleet-size study reports. Treat the census-field ratios as an upper bound, and the published 25–30% figure as the best population-level estimate.

A mileage-normalized reweighting attempt (crash_rate × mcs150_mileage) was tried and discarded — it produced a non-monotonic result across fleet-size buckets, almost certainly because mcs150_mileage (self-reported annual VMT on the census form) is not the same VMT base FMCSA used to originally compute recordable_crash_rate; mixing the two denominators doesn't measure anything cleanly.

Method

  1. Read the literature first (FMCSA CSA-effectiveness reporting, FreightWaves, FleetOwner, HDT Fact Book) to establish the accepted per-unit and per-mile crash-rate figures and the audit failure threshold, before touching the warehouse.
  2. search_catalog → describe_table on transport.fmcsa_carriers (FMCSA Company Census File, ~4.47M carrier rows, DOT Socrata az4n-8mr2).
  3. Bucketed carriers by power_units (1–5, 6–20, 21–100, >100), computed mean/median recordable_crash_rate restricted to nonzero (reviewed) carriers.
  4. Ran hypothesis_test (Welch's t-test) on the 1–5 PU vs >100 PU groups to confirm significance.
  5. Attempted a mileage-weighted aggregate rate using mcs150_mileage; result was non-monotonic across buckets, flagged as a denominator mismatch, and excluded from the headline numbers.
  6. Verified the computed magnitude against the published FMCSA fleet-size study and reported both, explaining the gap via the compliance-review selection effect.
  7. Rendered charts (render_chart) and published the full narrative via publish_report with compose_dashboard inlined.
