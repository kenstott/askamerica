Small trucking carriers crash roughly 1.7-2x more often per unit of exposure than the largest fleets

FMCSA carrier-census and MCMIS crash data, normalized by fleet size; a true crashes-per-mile breakdown is not reliably computable from public data

Summary

By the exposure-normalized measures the trucking safety literature actually uses, small carriers are roughly 1.7 to 2 times more likely to crash per unit of exposure than the largest carriers. FMCSA's own carrier-size crash-rate comparison puts carriers with 5 or fewer power units at 7.64 crashes per 100 power units versus 3.72 for carriers with more than 500 power units — about 2.05x. A separate, more recent 2025 analysis of FMCSA crash data by driver count shows a similar gap: midsize carriers (250-500 drivers) averaging roughly 9.6% YTD crashes-per-driver versus roughly 5.7% for large carriers (500+ drivers) — about 1.7x. Both point the same direction and roughly agree on magnitude.

Important scope note: the question asks about danger "per mile," but no reliable, publicly available dataset breaks out crashes by true vehicle-miles-traveled and carrier size together. FMCSA itself normalizes its crash-rate metric by power units (registered trucks/tractors), not self-reported mileage, precisely because carrier-reported annual mileage (from the biennial MCS-150 form) is known to be stale and unreliable — something we independently confirmed below using AskAmerica's own FMCSA data. So "per mile" and "per power unit" are being used as substitutes for each other across this literature, and that substitution should be read as an approximation, not an exact match to the question's wording.

What the primary/industry sources show

  • FMCSA carrier-size crash-rate comparison (widely cited from a 2014 FMCSA CSA/MCSAC presentation): carriers with 5 or fewer power units average 7.64 crashes per 100 power units; carriers with more than 500 power units average 3.72 — a 2.05x ratio. We attempted to fetch the primary FMCSA PDF directly (fmcsa.dot.gov/.../CSAMCSACPresentationApril2014.pdf) and it returned HTTP 403; this figure is therefore reported as it appears in secondary industry summaries of that presentation, not as independently re-parsed from FMCSA's own document — flagged here explicitly rather than presented as a verified primary read.
  • FreightWaves/FMCSA MCMIS analysis (Nov. 2025): ranking active for-hire carriers by year-to-date crashes per driver, midsize carriers (250-500 drivers) had crash rates from about 8.5% to 11.9% (top 6 averaging 9.6%), while large carriers (500+ drivers) ranged from about 4.9% to 6.6% (top 6 averaging 5.7%) — roughly 1.7x higher for midsize than large. The article's own framing: "midsize carriers are far more likely to be in a crash by this metric than their larger counterparts," attributing the gap to large fleets' greater resources for safety programs, training, and technology.
  • Insurer portfolio-risk concentration (FreightWaves, 2026, analysis of 2.8 million insurer-carrier relationships): carriers placed with the 50 largest insurers (which skew toward larger, more established fleets) had a crash rate of 3.22 per carrier, versus 10.32 per carrier for carriers placed with the long tail of 3,680 smaller/specialty insurers — a different mechanism (underwriting selection, not fleet size directly) but pointing the same direction: smaller, less-scrutinized operators cluster the risk.
  • Industry-wide baseline for context (IIHS, citing FHWA 2026 VMT data): across all large trucks nationally, the fatal-crash rate was 1.6 per 100 million vehicle-miles traveled in 2024 — slightly higher than the passenger-vehicle rate of 1.4. This is the only genuinely per-mile figure found in the literature, but it is not broken out by carrier size.

What we checked directly in AskAmerica's own FMCSA data

We queried transport.fmcsa_carriers (FMCSA's Company Census File) to see whether a true crashes-per-mile comparison could be built directly, using the carrier-level recordable_crash_rate field against self-reported mcs150_mileage, grouped by power_units as the fleet-size proxy. The mileage-weighted result ran in the OPPOSITE direction from the literature above — the largest bucket (500+ power units) showed a higher weighted crash rate (3.74) than the smallest bucket (1-5 units, 0.90).

We do not treat this as a competing finding. Two things explain it: (1) recordable_crash_rate is populated for only about 20% of active carriers overall, and that coverage varies sharply and non-randomly by fleet size — from about 10% of the largest carriers (500+ units) up to about 39% of mid-sized carriers (21-100 units) in our pull — so the carriers with a computed rate are not a representative cross-section of each size band; and (2) self-reported annual mileage on the biennial MCS-150 form is exactly the kind of stale, unreliable denominator that leads FMCSA's own Safety Measurement System to normalize by power units rather than miles. This is a genuine data-quality finding worth reporting on its own terms: a literal "per mile" version of this question cannot be answered cleanly from the public carrier census, which is why every credible external source we found substitutes crashes-per-power-unit or crashes-per-driver for crashes-per-mile.

Bottom line

Answering the question as closely as the data allows: small trucking companies are about 1.7x to 2x more dangerous per unit of operating exposure than the largest carriers, consistent across two independent FMCSA-based analyses years apart. A literal crashes-per-mile figure broken out by carrier size does not exist in reliable form in public data — confirmed by our own attempt to build one, which failed for identifiable, non-random data-coverage reasons rather than because no such gap exists.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Specifically 'per mile' normalization: No reliable public dataset breaks out crashes by true vehicle-miles-traveled and carrier size. The cited figures use crashes-per-power-unit and crashes-per-driver instead, which is what FMCSA itself uses because self-reported mileage is unreliable. We attempted to build a genuine per-mile figure directly from AskAmerica's FMCSA carrier census (recordable_crash_rate / mcs150_mileage) and report that attempt and why it failed (non-random data coverage) rather than presenting a per-mile number we could not validate.

  6. AskAmerica connector check: crash rate by power-unit bucket, mileage-weighted
    Show SQL
    SELECT CASE WHEN power_units <= 5 THEN '1-5' WHEN power_units <= 20 THEN '6-20' WHEN power_units <= 100 THEN '21-100' WHEN power_units <= 500 THEN '101-500' ELSE '500+' END AS size_bucket, COUNT(*) AS n_carriers, SUM(mcs150_mileage) AS total_miles, AVG(recordable_crash_rate) AS simple_avg_rate, SUM(recordable_crash_rate * mcs150_mileage) / NULLIF(SUM(mcs150_mileage),0) AS mileage_weighted_rate FROM transport.fmcsa_carriers WHERE status_code = 'A' AND recordable_crash_rate IS NOT NULL AND power_units IS NOT NULL AND power_units > 0 AND mcs150_mileage IS NOT NULL AND mcs150_mileage > 0 GROUP BY size_bucket ORDER BY size_bucket
  7. AskAmerica connector check: recordable_crash_rate data-coverage by power-unit bucket (selection-bias evidence)

    SELECT CASE WHEN power_units <= 5 THEN '1-5' WHEN power_units <= 20 THEN '6-20' WHEN power_units <= 100 THEN '21-100' WHEN power_units <= 500 THEN '101-500' ELSE '500+' END AS size_bucket, COUNT(*) AS n_total, COUNT(recordable_crash_rate) AS n_with_rate FROM transport.fmcsa_carriers WHERE status_code = 'A' AND power_units IS NOT NULL AND power_units > 0 GROUP BY size_bucket ORDER BY size_bucket
