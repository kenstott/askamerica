Bank lending to program-dependent defense contractors cannot be measured from public data; the large primes are mostly bond-financed and program-concentrated at about 27% (F-35 at Lockheed)

Answer

It cannot be quantified from public data. Bank loans are not reported by borrower or by the Pentagon program behind them. The Shared National Credit report (6,857 borrowers, $6.9 trillion commitments, 2025) has industry sub-sectors, but the OCC report page and FDIC charts file I searched contain no aerospace/defense line, and the warehouse has no loan-by-borrower table. A dollar figure would be invented.

What can be said: the largest primes are program-concentrated but mostly bond-financed. Lockheed Martin has about $21.7B total debt (end 2025), 27% of sales from the F-35 (67% of Aeronautics), and 72% of sales from the U.S. government. Its bank exposure is mainly committed revolvers (a $3.0B five-year facility to 2031 and a 364-day facility led by Bank of America), so exposure to a delay is largely contingent and runs through overall corporate credit.

Exposure reasoning

  • Cancellation is typically settled via termination-for-convenience payments, which cushions lenders; delays and fixed-price overrun charges are the more common stress.
  • Higher risk sits in smaller, single-program, leveraged borrowers (leveraged loans, private credit), which I could not size. Leveraged loans are nearly half of SNC commitments overall.
  • Columbia-class matters to General Dynamics (78% of each boat) and HII; no revenue-share figure found.

Path to a real number

SNC Appendix C aerospace/defense row (if published in the full report), FR Y-14 borrower data (not public), or a bottom-up build from each prime's credit-facility exhibits matched to program revenue shares.

Caveats

Lockheed figures come from a secondary summary of its 10-K and aggregator pages, not a parsed filing. The only warehouse query that returned rows (federal obligations by agency) does not bear on lending.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Dollar amount of bank lending to program-dependent defense contractors: No public loan-level or industry-level dataset gives this. The OCC SNC 2025 report page and FDIC charts file contain no aerospace/defense line (searched); the warehouse has no bank loan-by-borrower table and its SEC fact queries returned nothing or timed out.
