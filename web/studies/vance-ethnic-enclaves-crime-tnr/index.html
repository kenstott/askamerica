Vance's crime claims about immigrant 'ethnic enclaves' are checked against research and are false; the article's other claims hold up

Validation of newrepublic.com/post/184971 (Aug 16-17, 2024 posts)

Summary

This piece is a roundup of several short posts from August 16-17, 2024. The central, checkable claim is Vance's assertion that immigrant 'ethnic enclaves' lead to higher crime rates, which the article counters by citing research showing immigrants are less likely to commit crimes than native-born Americans. That counter-claim holds up well against independent literature: a Stanford/NBER study (Abramitzky et al.) finds immigrants roughly 60% less likely to be incarcerated than the U.S.-born since the 1960s, a Cato Institute analysis of Texas found undocumented immigrants 37.1% less likely to be convicted of a crime, and multiple NYT/Marshall Project analyses (2007-2016) found no link between undocumented immigration and rises in violent or property crime. Vance's quote is also accurately reported and corroborated by three independent outlets (Mediaite, Newsweek, Spokesman-Review) carrying nearly identical wording. The article's more cautious framing of the Harris/Minnesota Freedom Fund tweet ('claiming she was personally involved is dubious') is also accurate: Harris tweeted support but never donated, per the fund itself and FEC filings. The specific dollar figures on fake-elector donations (attributed to The Guardian) were not independently re-derived here because matching named individuals to FEC donor records reliably requires entity resolution beyond this session's scope; they are reported as attributed to the original source, not verified against the warehouse.

Claim 1 — Vance's quote and framing

The article quotes Vance: 'Has anybody seen the movie Gangs of New York? ... You know that when you have these massive ethnic enclaves in our country, it can lead to higher crime rates.' Independent reporting from Mediaite, Newsweek, and the Spokesman-Review carries substantially the same quote from the same Aug. 16, 2024 Milwaukee press conference, so the quotation is accurately reported, not fabricated or distorted.

Claim 2 — Immigrants and crime rates (the core empirical claim)

SourceFinding
Abramitzky et al., NBER/Stanford (cited by NPR, Mar. 2024)Immigrants ~60% less likely to be incarcerated than U.S.-born, consistently since the 1960s
Cato Institute, Texas working paper (2020)Undocumented immigrants 37.1% less likely to be convicted of a crime than native-born Texans

This is a well-replicated finding across multiple independent research groups (Stanford economists, a libertarian think tank, and investigative data journalism), so the article's rebuttal to Vance is well-supported, not just a citation of one advocacy source. The askamerica warehouse's crime schema (FBI CDE/BJS/NIBRS tables) does not carry immigration status as a variable, so this could not be independently recomputed from the warehouse — it was checked against the literature instead, which is the correct instrument here since incarceration-by-nativity is not FBI UCR data in the first place.

Claim 3 — Harris and the Minnesota Freedom Fund

The article says Harris 'did tweet out a link' to the Minnesota Freedom Fund but that 'claiming she was personally involved is dubious.' This matches later, more detailed fact-checks (Washington Post 2020, Snopes, CBS News 2024): Harris publicly encouraged donations via a June 2020 tweet but neither she nor her campaign donated, a fact confirmed directly by the Minnesota Freedom Fund and unsupported by any FEC expenditure record. The article's hedged framing is accurate and not overstated.

Claim 4 — Fake-elector campaign donations (not independently verified)

The article cites The Guardian for specific donation totals (e.g., Meshawn Maddock $1,800+, Tyler Bowyer $645, David Hanna $25,000+, Eli Crane $2,900, Yvette Herrell $3,000+$2,900). These are attributed to a named primary source (The Guardian's review of FEC records) rather than presented as the article's own analysis. Reliably matching these individual names to fec.individual_contributions donor records requires entity resolution this session did not attempt (common names, multiple donors, no clean ID linkage), so these figures are reported here as attributed-but-unverified rather than confirmed independently.
