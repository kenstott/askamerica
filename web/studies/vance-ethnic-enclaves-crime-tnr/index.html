Summary

The reported facts in this article are accurate; the 'racist' label is the magazine's editorial opinion, not a testable claim. J.D. Vance did say, at an August 16, 2024 press conference, that 'massive ethnic enclaves in our country... can lead to higher crime rates,' citing the film Gangs of New York — this is accurately quoted and the video is publicly verifiable. The article's rebuttal — that immigrants are actually less likely to commit crime than native-born Americans — is well supported by independent research (NBER, a 150-year Northwestern/Princeton historical study, and Cato Institute analysis of 2024 ACS data all find immigrants incarcerated at substantially LOWER rates than the U.S.-born, not higher). Whether the remark itself rises to 'wildly racist' is a value judgment the piece makes as opinion/commentary (it is published as a New Republic 'post,' i.e., an opinion/blog item), not a fact that can be independently verified true or false.

#2 · true — Immigrants are actually less likely to commit crimes than native-born Americans (the article's rebuttal to Vance's claim, citing NPR).
Independent evidence
Multiple independent studies converge: NBER analysis found immigrants ~60% less likely to be incarcerated than U.S.-born in 2020; a 150-year historical study (Northwestern/Princeton, reported by Northwestern Now, March 2024) found immigrants have never been incarcerated at a higher rate than the U.S.-born since 1870; Cato Institute analysis of 2024 ACS data found illegal immigrants ~44% and legal immigrants ~75% less likely to be incarcerated than native-born Americans; American Immigration Council fact sheet reaches the same conclusion. AskAmerica's crime schema (FBI CDE offenses/arrests, BJS NCVS) carries no immigration-status or nativity field, so this could not be independently recomputed against this warehouse and is checked purely against the published literature.
