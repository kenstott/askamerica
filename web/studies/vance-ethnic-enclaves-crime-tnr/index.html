Vance's quote is accurate; the crime claim behind it is contradicted by research; "racist" is the article's characterization, not a factual claim

Fact-check of TNR's Aug. 16, 2024 post on JD Vance's "ethnic enclaves" remark

Summary

The article's core reporting checks out, but it contains two different kinds of claims that need to be separated. (1) The quote and context are accurate: at an August 16, 2024 press conference, JD Vance did defend earlier remarks about 19th-century European immigration by citing the movie Gangs of New York and said 'ethnic enclaves ... can lead to higher crime rates' — this is corroborated by contemporaneous video and multiple outlets. (2) The article's rebuttal is also correct as far as the research goes: a substantial and consistent body of academic research (Abramitzky/Stanford via NBER, Cato Institute, NYT/Marshall Project analyses) finds immigrants — including undocumented immigrants — are incarcerated and convicted at meaningfully lower rates than U.S.-born Americans, not higher. (3) Calling the remark 'racist' is the outlet's editorial characterization, not an empirically checkable fact — reasonable people can and do disagree on that label even while agreeing on the underlying facts. So: the reported facts are true; the headline's value judgment is opinion, not something a fact-check can grade true/false.

What Vance actually said and its accuracy

The article quotes Vance: 'Has anybody seen the movie Gangs of New York? ... You know that when you have these massive ethnic enclaves in our country, it can lead to higher crime rates.' This matches the widely circulated video from the press conference (originally posted to X by @Acyn and reported elsewhere), so the quote and setting are accurately reported.

Is it true that 'ethnic enclaves'/immigration lead to higher crime rates?

No — the weight of research points the other way. Key findings from independent, peer-reviewed or government/think-tank sources:

  • NBER working paper (Abramitzky et al., Stanford), 2023: since at least 1960, immigrants have been about 60% less likely to be incarcerated than U.S.-born Americans of the same age; the gap has widened since 1960 even as the immigrant population share has grown.
  • Cato Institute, Texas study (2020): undocumented immigrants in Texas were found to be convicted of crimes at a rate 37.1% lower than native-born Americans; legal immigrants also had lower conviction rates than the native-born.
  • New York Times and Marshall Project analyses (2018-2019): found no correlation between increases in the undocumented immigrant population and increases in violent or property crime across U.S. communities, 2007-2016.

These are external academic/government-adjacent sources, not tables inside the AskAmerica government-data corpus queried for this answer — a search of the corpus's crime schema (FBI UCR/CDE, BJS/NCVS tables) turned up no nativity or immigration-status breakdown of offenders (the terms 'immigrant' and 'incarceration' matched nothing in the schema), so this piece of the claim could not be checked against AskAmerica's own data directly and rests on the outside literature cited above.

The 'racist' label

This is the one part of the article that is not a factual claim in the checkable sense — it's the author's editorial judgment about the implication of Vance's remarks (linking 'ethnic enclaves' to crime, in the context of remarks about mass deportation of European immigrants). Whether that framing constitutes 'racist' rhetoric is a matter of interpretation and political judgment, not something with a true/false answer the way a crime-rate statistic has.
