Neither lever shows a detectable edge in state data; the research favors targeted pay (and early-grade class size) over across-the-board hiring

NCES CCD, BLS QCEW, NAEP 2013-2024, 49 states; plus published literature

Answer

Short answer: the data cannot pick a winner, and the literature leans toward targeted pay, not blanket hiring. In a 49-state panel (2013-2024), neither 1% higher teacher-sector pay nor 1% more teachers per pupil is reliably linked to NAEP scores. Research says broad class-size cuts give modest effects at high cost except in early grades, while pay works best when targeted.

What I computed

Outcome: mean NAEP (math and reading, grades 4 and 8) per state-year. Levers in equal-cost percent terms: log average pay per employee in local-government education (QCEW NAICS 611) and log teachers per pupil (CCD).

  • Two-way fixed effects, clustered by state (n=285, 49 states): pay -3.98 (SE 5.11, p=0.44); teachers per pupil +2.02 (SE 3.94, p=0.61).
  • 2019 cross-section (n=48): pay +4.4 (p=0.29); teachers per pupil +7.6 (p=0.10), R2 0.11. Suggestive for hiring only, confounded by wealth and poverty.

Both are nulls with wide intervals: they do not show either lever is useless, only that this design cannot resolve effects of the size the literature reports.

Excluded rows: 9 of 294 state-years were dropped for null values (states 10, 50, 47, 56, 6, 49, 44, mainly missing teacher or wage data in some years); the 2019 cross-section dropped one state (row 5, a null input). I did not rerun without them since they are nulls, not outliers; 49 of 51 units remain.

What the research says

  • Class size: Brookings finds it works for some students, grades and places, mixed elsewhere. Krueger put a 22-to-15 cut at about a 6% return (NBER w8875).
  • Hanushek values teacher quality far above class size; Krueger disputes how studies are weighted.
  • Wisconsin Act 10 let districts pay high-value-added teachers more; quality rose there, partly by pulling teachers from others (Biasi).
  • USC Los Angeles study: targeted spending beats across-the-board moves.

Practical guidance

  1. Put the marginal dollar into targeted pay (shortage subjects, high-poverty schools, retaining effective teachers) before across-the-board raises.
  2. If hiring, do it in early grades and high-need schools.
  3. Vacancies and turnover point to pay; very large classes point to hiring.

Limits

Pay is a sector proxy, not teacher salary. No poverty control in the cross-section, no reverse-causality or leave-one-out test, and state NAEP is coarse. Web evidence rests on search summaries, not full-text reads.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Use F-33 instruction salaries for pay: instruction_salaries_thousand is advertised but not a column in the loaded edu.f33_district_finance; used QCEW local-government education pay per employee instead (includes non-teaching staff).
  • Control for poverty: State fixed effects in the panel only; the 2019 cross-section has no poverty control.
  • Reverse-causality check: Not run; stated as a limit.
