Even in the coal-heaviest US grid regions, an EV emits roughly 40-60% less CO2 per mile than a typical gasoline car

EPA eGRID2023 subregion CO2 output rates (environment.egrid_emission_rates); my own per-mile arithmetic

Summary

Yes. In 2023 the dirtiest eGRID region (MRO East, 1,397 lb CO2/MWh, 40% coal, 43% gas) gives an EV about 220 g CO2/mile from electricity, versus about 355 g/mile for a 30 mpg gasoline car (fuel burned plus upstream), a ~38% cut. In SERC Midwest (51% coal) the cut is ~45%; in ERCOT ~68%. The grid would need roughly 2,260 lb/MWh (30 mpg comparison) before the EV lost, well above any US region.

Method

Computed: EV = 0.33 kWh/mile at the wall x 1.05 transmission/charging factor x eGRID CO2 output rate. Gasoline = 8,887 g CO2/gal / mpg, +20% for refining and delivery. The 0.33 kWh, 1.05 and 20% factors are my assumptions, not warehouse data. Break-even rates: 1,696 (40 mpg), 2,262 (30 mpg), 2,714 (25 mpg) lb/MWh. Even against tailpipe-only emissions of a 40 mpg car (1,414), the worst region (1,397) is barely under.

Caveats: eGRID rates are average, not marginal (the plants actually ramped by extra charging, often gas, may differ); battery manufacturing adds up front emissions that a few years of driving typically repay, with payback longer on coal-heavy grids; grids are getting cleaner while the car lasts; regions are not states; efficient hybrids (40+ mpg) narrow the gap on the worst grids. A web search found the ICCT 2025 update is EU-only, so I cite no US lifecycle figure from it.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Lifecycle battery manufacturing: Not in the warehouse; treated qualitatively, not computed.
  • State grain: Table is eGRID subregion grain, not state; subregions cross state lines.
