## Where pharma/API manufacturing is actually being built right now (vs. merely announced), as of September 2026

**This corpus (AskAmerica/askengine) has no pharmaceutical-manufacturing-facility table** — `search_catalog` confirms "pharmaceutical" matches nothing in any schema (economic tables here go only to NAICS-sector/industry aggregates, e.g. `econ.state_industry`, `econ.industry_gdp`, which can't identify individual facilities or construction status). The answer below is built entirely from company disclosures, local/state government announcements, trade press (FiercePharma, Contract Pharma, PharmExec, Construction Dive), and a UNC Kenan-Flagler tracker built specifically to separate "broken ground" from "announced only" for tariff-exemption purposes. That tracker's table was the single most useful primary source and is cited directly below.

### The short answer
Real, shovels-in-the-ground activity is concentrated in three clusters, plus a scatter of single-site expansions — and it is running well ahead of what most headlines about "reshoring" imply, but a meaningful share of the biggest-dollar announcements (Lilly's Alabama and Virginia API plants, several Novartis/Roche/Sanofi/UCB sites) are still pre-construction as of this writing.

**1. North Carolina's Research Triangle / "Pharma Corridor" (Holly Springs–Clayton–Sanford–Wilson–Durham) — the most active zone in the country.**
At least eight distinct facilities have actually broken ground or are under active construction here, several already partially operational:
- Fujifilm Biotechnologies, Holly Springs — $3.2B biologics site; **Phase 1 opened Sept 2025**, drug-product line coming online in 2026, Phase 2 (doubling capacity) under construction for 2028.
- Novo Nordisk, Clayton — $4.1B second fill-finish plant, **under construction**, targeting 2027–2029.
- Amgen, Holly Springs — $1.5B drug-substance plant, under construction.
- Genentech/Roche, Holly Springs — $700M metabolic-medicine plant, under construction, 2029 target.
- Kyowa Kirin, Sanford — $530M biologics plant, under construction, 2027 target.
- Johnson & Johnson, Wilson — $2B plant, under construction.
- Merck, Durham — $1B vaccine facility, under construction (2025 target).
- Biogen, NC — $2B antisense-oligonucleotide plant, under construction.

**2. Indiana's Lilly footprint (Indianapolis + the new "LEAP" district in Lebanon).**
Both are actively under construction, not just announced — $4.5B in Indianapolis (targeting late 2027) and $4.5B in Lebanon (targeting 2027). This is the largest concentration of confirmed Lilly build-out outside its newer 2025-26 API announcements.

**3. Chicago-area API cluster (AbbVie, North Chicago).**
AbbVie actually broke ground on a $195M API plant in fall 2025 (target: operational 2027), and followed with a second, larger $380M two-plant API expansion on the same campus with construction starting spring 2026 (target 2029) — one of the only *confirmed under-construction* API-specific (as opposed to biologics/fill-finish) sites outside Texas/NC.

**4. Scattered single-site builds actually underway:** Merck biologics/ADC plant, Wilmington DE ($1B); GSK vaccines plant, Marietta PA ($800M, targeting 2028); Roche glucose-medicine plant, Indianapolis ($550M); Vetter fill-finish, Des Plaines IL ($285M, targeting 2029); Hikma, Columbus OH; Gilead biologics, Foster City CA (targeting 2026).

**5. Eli Lilly's Houston, TX API/GLP-1 mega-site ($6.5B) has moved from announcement to real construction** — site permits were filed and construction activity was confirmed starting mid-2026, though the company itself still frames the full buildout as running to 2030.

### What is still announcement-only, not construction
- **Lilly's Huntsville, AL API plant ($6B)** — announced Dec 2025/confirmed Feb 2026; construction is *scheduled* to begin sometime in 2026 (contractually must start by March 2027) but has not broken ground as of this writing.
- **Lilly's Richmond/Goochland, VA API-for-ADCs plant ($5B)** — announced Sept 2025; no groundbreaking reported yet, ~5-year completion horizon.
- **AstraZeneca (Albemarle VA, $3.5B; Tarzana CA cell-therapy site), Novartis (Morrisville NC API plant, Florida/Texas radioligand sites, ~$23B overall US pledge), Sanofi, UCB, and Roche's Pennsylvania gene-therapy site** — all announced in 2024–2025 with no confirmed construction start identified in current reporting.
- Several of these (Novartis's $23B, Sanofi's, Roche's aggregate US pledges) are multi-site "commitment" packages announced under political/tariff pressure without site-specific construction timelines yet disclosed.

### Why the NC Triangle and Indiana stand out
Both clusters had existing pharma-manufacturing infrastructure, state/local incentive packages already in place, and multiple companies co-locating (shared labor pool, supply chains, utilities) — which is why announcement-to-groundbreaking conversion has been fastest there. By contrast, greenfield API announcements in Alabama and Virginia (states without an existing large-scale pharma manufacturing base) are taking longer to convert from announcement to visible construction.

### Caveat on method and confidence
- No government dataset currently tracks individual pharmaceutical facility construction status by location; this answer relies on trade press and company/state press releases, cross-checked against the UNC Kenan-Flagler tracker (compiled specifically to identify "broken ground" projects for the Oct 2025 tariff-exemption policy) and independently verified for several sites (Lilly Houston, Lilly Huntsville, Lilly Richmond, Fujifilm, Novo Nordisk, AbbVie) via separate searches.
- "Under construction" claims reflect the most recent (2025–2026) reporting available; site status can change quickly, and companies do not uniformly disclose formal groundbreaking dates.
- Dollar figures are nominal, as announced, not inflation-adjusted; summing them (~$34B in disclosed capex across roughly 19 confirmed-under-construction projects) is illustrative of scale, not audited.
- Government tariff/trade policy (the Oct 2025 100% branded-drug tariff, later delayed to a "preparing to impose" status per STAT News) is a live incentive shaping which "announced" projects convert to construction fastest — API and antibody/biologics domestic capacity was specifically the intended tariff-exemption category, which raises the risk that some 2025-2026 "under construction" claims are lagging PR rather than site engineering fact.
