Illinois, Kentucky and New Jersey carry the widest pension funding gaps; markets price it at the extremes, but ratings and contribution discipline matter more than the raw ratio

Pension figures: Reason Foundation 2025 report, Equable, CRR. Spreads: Boyer research, Bond Buyer, Schwab/Bloomberg snapshot. Not computed from the AskAmerica warehouse.

Answer

The widest gaps by funded ratio are Illinois (52%), Kentucky (54%) and New Jersey (55%) per Reason Foundation's 2025 report; Equable names Illinois, Kentucky and Mississippi as below 60%. In dollars, California, Illinois, Texas and New Jersey are largest, which partly reflects size. Pennsylvania (66%) is the largest big-state laggard. Nationally the gap is $1.27 trillion at 82.5% funded (Equable, 2025) or about 77.7% (Boston College CRR) depending on method.

Does it show up in bond pricing? Yes on average, but not one-for-one. Boyer's cross-state study finds one standard deviation more net pension liability to GDP is associated with about 18 bp higher CDS spreads (about 28 bp in bond spreads), strongest where state constitutions protect pensions. Illinois, the worst-funded, has traded the widest state spread to the AAA benchmark (163 bp at 10 years in early 2023, narrowing to 145 bp after upgrades, versus 60-70 bp at its early-2022 tightest). But a June 2026 snapshot has Illinois at 62 bp and New Jersey at 21 bp despite similar funded ratios, so ratings, supplemental contributions and budget health drive pricing at least as much as the funded ratio itself.

What I did and did not verify

The AskAmerica warehouse has no state pension funded-status table and no municipal spread series (searches returned nothing relevant; the one table I queried, Census government finance, returned unit counts only and no pension liabilities). All pension and spread figures come from the web sources cited, which I read directly except Equable (403), Schwab's table and the Equable PDF, which were not readable; the Schwab spread figures (62 bp Illinois, 21 bp New Jersey, 12 Jun 2026) and Equable's state statements come from search-result summaries. The full 50-state ranking was not retrievable, so I did not rank all states and ran no regression of my own. Funded ratios differ by source because of discount-rate and fiscal-year conventions (Reason 79% for 2024 vs Equable 78.0% vs CRR 76.2%).

Evidence on pricing

  • Boyer (Chicago Booth, 2018 draft): 18 bp CDS / 28 bp bond spread per SD of net pension liability to GDP; IV exercise suggests causal; stronger with constitutional pension protection.
  • Illinois: March 2023 Bond Buyer reports ratings upgrades tied to supplemental pension payments, rainy-day deposits and debt paydown; spreads narrowed from 90/163/175 bp to 75/145/165 bp (1/10/25-year).
  • CRR (2025): contributions now exceed 100% of actuarially required in aggregate, over 80% of plans pay the full amount; funded status improves only modestly. Hence markets reward contribution discipline.
  • Biggs/AEI (2015, fair-value basis): gaps range from 5% of gross state product in Washington to 37% in Illinois, so Illinois ranks worst on a market-value basis too.

Caveat: Boyer's sample is older (2010s), and spreads also reflect liquidity, ratings, tax base and issuance size. This is descriptive, not a new causal estimate.
