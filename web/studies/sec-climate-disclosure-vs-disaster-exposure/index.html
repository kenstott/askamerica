Climate-risk disclosure intensity shows no measurable link to a filer's county disaster history (r ≈ 0, n = 73)

SEC 10-K MD&A (FY2024) vs. FEMA/NOAA county disaster records, 2015-2024. HQ-address locator (see caveat).

Summary

No measurable relationship found. Across 73 SEC 10-K filers (FY2024), the share of MD&A text discussing climate/weather risk is essentially uncorrelated with the count of FEMA disaster declarations in the county housing the filer's headquarters (r = -0.03, p = 0.78, n = 73), and the sign flips depending on specification. Controlling for firm size does not change this. The honest reading is that climate-risk disclosure intensity, as measured here, tracks disclosure practice (filing length, a firm's general verbosity) far more than it tracks the disaster history of where the firm is actually located.

Step 1 — Disclosure-intensity measure

Form/window: Item 7 (MD&A) paragraphs from 10-K filings with fiscal_year=2024 (askamerica sec.vectorized_chunks, source_type='mda_paragraph', joined to sec.filing_metadata). Note: Item 1A Risk Factors — the natural home for this kind of disclosure — is documented in this corpus's schema but is NOT actually loaded (a full scan of source_type across all years returns zero risk_factor rows); MD&A was used as the best available substitute and this gap was logged via report_issue.

Measure: climate_share = (MD&A paragraph chunks containing climate/hurricane/wildfire/flood/drought/'extreme weather'/'sea level'/'severe weather') ÷ (all MD&A paragraph chunks) for that filing, restricted to filings with ≥10 total chunks. This is a SHARE, not a raw count, specifically to net out filing length (see Step 4's boilerplate confound). Universe: 2,292 FY2024 10-K filers with usable MD&A; 73 of these were successfully geocoded to a county (Step 2).

Step 2 — Locating operations, and the weakest link

This corpus's structural HQ-locator gap. sec.filing_metadata.business_address / mailing_address are documented as 'physical business address' but in fact hold ONLY the street line (e.g. 'ONE MICROSOFT WAY') with no city, state or ZIP — verified for Apple, Microsoft and 2,292 other filers. ref.company_financial_profile.headquarters_city is null for every row (0 of thousands), and the underlying ref.gleif_entities table, while populated, only linked to 0 of a 29-company random SEC-filer test sample via ref.gleif_cik_mapping. A patents-based alternative (ref.canonical_org_entity.patents_assignee_id → patents.patent_locations, which does carry county_fips) also failed: canonical_org_entity.sec_cik values do not correspond to real sec.filing_metadata.cik values for any of ~2,200 filers tested. All three issues were logged via report_issue.

Locator actually used: XBRL cover-page facts dei:EntityAddressCityOrTown / dei:EntityAddressStateOrProvince (sec.financial_line_items), geocoded to a Census place via geo.gazetteer_places (name/state match) and then spatially joined (ST_Within) to geo.counties polygons for county_fips. This recovered city+state for 89 of a random 200-company sample; 4 were Canadian (dropped, no US county); 73 successfully resolved to a specific county via the spatial join.

This is still a HEADQUARTERS locator, not an operations locator — the single biggest bias in this analysis. It assigns one point (the filer's registered/cover-page address) to companies that may operate distribution centers, plants, stores or wells across dozens of states. It will systematically UNDERSTATE any true relationship for distributed-operations firms (retailers, REITs with national portfolios, multi-state utilities, energy E&P companies) whose real disaster exposure has nothing to do with their corporate address, and will only be a fair proxy for single-site or single-region firms. It also over-represents corporate-services hubs (New York, San Francisco Bay Area, DC-metro) relative to where physical assets actually sit.

None of the five specifications clears conventional significance, and the sign is not stable across them (near-zero, flips between slightly positive and slightly negative depending on which disaster measure is used). 61 of 73 companies (84%) have climate_share = 0 in this MD&A-based window — most filers simply do not discuss climate/weather risk in Item 7 regardless of local disaster history.

Step 4 — Confounds, one quantified

Boilerplate / filing length (quantified, and the reason a SHARE metric was used): raw climate-related chunk COUNT correlates strongly with total MD&A length (r = 0.685, p < 0.0001, n = 73) — a company that writes a longer MD&A mechanically mentions climate more often, independent of actual risk. Normalizing to a share largely removes this: climate_share vs. MD&A length drops to r = 0.130 (p = 0.27, n.s.).

Firm size: in the 29-company subset with a matched us-gaap:Revenues fact, log(revenue) vs. climate_share: r = 0.019 (p = 0.92) — no relationship in this sample. Partial correlation of climate_share vs. declarations controlling for log(revenue): r = -0.036 (n = 29), essentially identical to the zero-order r = -0.038 on the same subset — size is not masking or suppressing anything here.

Sector: one-way ANOVA of climate_share across 2-digit SIC groups (18 groups, n ≥ 2 each, total n = 63): F = 0.47, p = 0.95, eta² = 0.15 — not statistically significant, though with 18 groups and only 63 observations this test has very little power to detect a real sector effect if one exists; it should be read as inconclusive, not as evidence sector doesn't matter.

Data-quality issues logged this session

  • sec.vectorized_chunks: documented source_type='risk_factor' (Item 1A) is not actually loaded anywhere in the table.
  • sec.filing_metadata.business_address / mailing_address: street-line-1 only, no city/state/ZIP, despite being documented as 'physical business address'.
  • sec.company_financial_profile.headquarters_city: null for every row tested (join to GLEIF appears broken in this view).
  • ref.canonical_org_entity.sec_cik: does not match real sec.filing_metadata.cik values for any of ~2,200 filers tested via the patents_assignee_id route.
  • A multi-row VALUES-derived-table joined to geo.state_ref silently drops most matching rows (no error) once the VALUES list mixes abbreviations and full state names across more than ~2 rows — worked around with single-table ILIKE filters and client-side Python for the state-abbreviation resolution step.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Use operations locator, not HQ, or explain why HQ was used and what it biases: This corpus has no structured operations-footprint field for SEC filers at all (see Step 2's data-quality findings) and no county-level city/state field of any kind for filers survived intact except the XBRL cover-page dei:EntityAddress* facts, which are themselves a registered/HQ-type address, not a distributed-operations footprint. Used that as the best available locator and disclosed its HQ-style bias in Step 2 rather than substituting a fabricated operations measure.
