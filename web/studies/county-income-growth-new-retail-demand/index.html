Population growth is sustained in a few hundred counties; real income growth almost never persists, so only a short list passes both tests

BEA CAINC4 county market income (net earnings + dividends/interest/rent, excluding transfers) per capita, real 2024 dollars; 2010, 2018, 2023, 2024; 3,104 counties

Answer

Population growth is persistent across counties (correlation of 2010-18 vs 2018-23 annualized growth: 0.67, n=3,104). Real per-capita market income growth is not (-0.03), and a county's 2024 income growth is negatively related to its 2018-23 trend (-0.41), i.e. it mean-reverts. So a one-year income jump is mostly a blip; population trend is the reliable signal. Requiring both periods of population AND income growth, with 2024 still positive, among counties with 25,000+ people, the number of 'sustained' counties falls from 619 (any positive growth) to 189, 65 and 17 as thresholds tighten (pop CAGR 0.5/1.0/1.5%+, real income CAGR 1/1.5/2%+).

Strongest large counties (100k+ people, pop and real market-income CAGR above 1.5% in 2010-18 and 2018-23, income above 1% in 2010-18, 2024 positive), ranked by 2018-23 income growth: Sumter FL, Sumner TN, Douglas CO, Deschutes OR, Williamson TN, Utah UT, Hays TX, Kootenai ID, Canyon ID, Wilson TN, Pasco FL, Williamson TX, Collier FL, Maury TN, Ada ID, Washington UT, Osceola FL, Union NC, Cherokee GA, Travis TX, Limestone AL, St. Lucie FL, Rutherford TN, Wake NC, Mecklenburg NC. Caveats: Douglas CO (2024 income +0.2%) and Utah UT (+0.5%) are soft in 2024; Osceola and St. Lucie have low per-capita income ($37k-$42k), so growth is volume-led. Sumter FL (The Villages) is retiree dividend income.

Exclusions

Units excluded by the queries: counties lacking any of population, net earnings, or dividends/interest/rent in a given year (the p.ne / p.dir NOT NULL predicates) were dropped, as were CPI annual-average rows (period M13) that would double-count monthly values. The persistence sample is the 3,104 counties with complete data in all four years; counts of sustained counties additionally exclude counties under 25,000 people (noisy). I did not compute the statistics without the null-drop, so the effect of those drops on headline correlations is untested.

Method and caveats

  • Income = CAINC4 lines 45+46 divided by line 20 population; transfers (line 47) left out so the 2020-21 stimulus step is not read as growth. Deflated by national annual-average CPI-U.
  • County data lack 2019 (known gap) and 2025; endpoints 2010, 2018, 2023, 2024 avoid 2019.
  • 2024 values are early BEA estimates subject to revision. Income is by place of residence, not where shopping occurs.
  • Not tested: retail sales, store supply, cost-of-living differences.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Retail demand directly (retail sales, spending): No county retail sales series queried; income and population are demand proxies only. County cost-of-living (RPP) adjustment not applied.
