Litigation moved student-loan flows mainly through the SAVE forbearance: defaults came from the payment restart, and a second wave is not yet priced as settled

NY Fed Liberty Street (May 2026), secondary reports of Q2 2026 HHDC, FSA PSLF report (as of 2026-08-31)

Summary

Litigation changed flows through one channel: it parked about 7 million SAVE borrowers in forbearance, so they are neither paying nor delinquent. The default surge since late 2025 (about 1.0M in Q4 2025 and 2.6M in Q1 2026, 3.6M total) came from the end of the pandemic payment pause, not from forgiveness uncertainty. The share of balances past due is about 10%, near pre-pandemic levels. Forgiveness that is already processed is settled: PSLF and related discharges total $96.4B for 1.29M borrowers. Broad forgiveness is not priced in at all. The forbearance only postpones SAVE repayment, so a second wave of delinquency and default is possible once those borrowers reach the nine-month mark. This is a descriptive reading, not a causal estimate: no public series separates the litigation effect from the pause-end effect.

What the evidence shows

  • NY Fed (May 2026): over 17% of borrowers have been 90+ days past due at least once since delinquencies reappeared in 2025:Q1. Default takes 270 days, so defaults began in 2025:Q4. About 30% of new defaulters were current before the pandemic, and about half had no payment due.
  • SAVE: forbearance because of litigation; very few have returned to repayment. The NY Fed flagged about 7M borrowers as a possible second default wave.
  • Q2 2026, per secondary reports (I did not open the primary HHDC release): balances fell about $7B to about $1.65T, and flow into serious delinquency fell to 7.83% from 12.88% a year earlier. The NY Fed cautioned that re-reporting of defaults is still distorting the figures.
  • Collections on defaulted loans are suspended with no resumption date, so default has had less cost for borrowers than usual. That is policy discretion, not litigation settlement.
  • Settled: FSA PSLF combined report (warehouse table edu.fsa_pslf_discharge_summary): $96.4B discharged, 1,291,700 borrowers, average $74,600. These are processed discharges, not the litigated broad forgiveness plans.

Settled versus not

Settled: the pause ended and normal delinquency and default mechanics resumed; PSLF discharges proceed. Not settled: SAVE borrowers' return to repayment under the new Repayment Assistance Plan (launching July 2026) or standard plans, and when collections resume. Because SAVE borrowers' status is frozen, current delinquency rates understate eventual exposure. Limits: the credit-bureau series are not in the warehouse (the table itself notes credit-bureau delinquency is not public), so I cannot compute the counterfactual. Cross-check the Q3 2026 HHDC release due in November.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Causal quantification: No public series isolates litigation effect; descriptive only. Q2 2026 figures are from secondary reports, not the primary NY Fed release.
