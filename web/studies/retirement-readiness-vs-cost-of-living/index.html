Roughly 36-39% of households nearing retirement are on track to fall more than 10% short of their pre-retirement living standard, about 9 million households aged 55-64

Answer

The best measure is Boston College's National Retirement Risk Index (NRRI, 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances, the latest). 39% of households are projected to replace more than 10% less income than they need to keep their pre-retirement standard of living. For the 50-59 group it is 36%. That equals roughly 8.8 million of the ~24.5 million households headed by someone 55-64 (my estimate). The NRRI reports how many fall short, not by how much, so the average size of the gap is not published here.

Method and numbers

  • NRRI: a household is at risk if projected retirement income (Social Security, pensions, annuitized 401(k)/IRA and financial assets, plus home equity via a reverse mortgage) is more than 10% below a consumption-smoothing target. It fell from 47% (2019) to 39% (2022), mostly from home prices. CRR calls it a lower bound: few retirees actually take reverse mortgages, and Vanguard found at least 70% fall short if housing equity is excluded.
  • Groups at risk in 2022: renters 72%, homeowners 24%, low income 64%, middle 29%, high 23%, no plan 65%, DB pension 13%.
  • Savings: 57% of households aged 55-64 held retirement accounts in the 2022 SCF (CRS), so 43% held none. Only 9.2% held over $1 million.
  • Count (computed here): ACS 2024 5-year (census.acs_housing_tenure_by_age) shows 18.40M owner and 6.09M renter households with a 55-64 householder. Applying 24% and 72% gives 4.4M + 4.4M = about 8.8M. Applying 36% to 24.5M gives 8.8M too. The rates are for all NRRI households, not just 55-64, so treat this as illustrative.

Caveats: 2022 was a housing and savings peak and CRR expects the figure to drift back toward 40-50%. The warehouse holds no SCF or NRRI data; the shares come from the published reports and only the household counts come from the warehouse.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • How far short (size of shortfall): NRRI publishes only the share more than 10% below target, not an average dollar or percentage-point gap; no warehouse table holds SCF/NRRI microdata.
