Interest-rate swap clearing is dominated by a handful of firms at two separate layers — one CCP (LCH SwapClear) and a small set of bank clearing members

CCP-layer shares: Clarus CCPView, Q1-Q2 2026. Access-layer concentration: Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago and OCC

Summary

Yes — extremely concentrated, at two separate layers. At the infrastructure layer, one clearinghouse, LCH SwapClear (part of London Stock Exchange Group), clears roughly 90-99%+ of interest-rate swap notional in every major currency except the Japanese yen, where it splits the market with Japan's JSCC (~52/48). Computed directly with this session's HHI tool, USD and EUR swap clearing score 8,750-9,177 out of a possible 10,000 — five times the U.S. Department of Justice's 1,800 threshold for "highly concentrated." But clearing has a second, equally important layer that a CCP-only answer misses: access. Clients cannot clear directly at LCH or CME — they clear through a small set of bank-affiliated clearing members (futures commission merchants), and that layer is just as concentrated. The OCC's most recent quarterly report puts just four U.S. banks at 79-87% of all U.S. bank derivatives notional, and the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago found that for OTC interest-rate swaps and credit default swaps specifically, the top 10 clearing members handle over 99% of client transactions, with five bank-affiliated clearing members holding more than 80% of total client margin globally (US, UK, Japan). So the honest answer to "a handful of firms" is: one firm clears almost everything (LCH), and a handful of the same large global banks control almost all client access to it.

Layer 1 — the clearinghouse (CCP) itself: near-monopoly by currency

  • USD (SOFR/Fed Funds OIS): LCH SwapClear 95.7% vs. CME 4.3% (Q1 2026) — $77.9tn vs $3.46tn notional.
  • EUR (Euribor IRS + €STR OIS): LCH SwapClear ~93.3% vs. Eurex ~6.7% (Q2 2026: LCH €71.2tn, Eurex €5.15tn of €76.3tn). Eurex's share has grown from a low base since the EU's 2025 Active Account Requirement began pushing euro clearing away from London for regulatory-sovereignty reasons — but LCH still dominates by a wide margin.
  • GBP (SONIA OIS): LCH SwapClear 99.995%.
  • CAD (CORRA OIS): LCH SwapClear 99.97%.
  • AUD (BBSW/AONIA): LCH SwapClear 95.7% vs. ASX 4.3%.
  • JPY (TONA OIS): the one real exception — LCH SwapClear ~51.9% vs. Japan's JSCC ~48.1% (Q2 2026), a genuine two-firm split rather than one dominant player, though LCH has recently pulled ahead after CFTC approval in Sept 2025 let US funds access JSCC.

Across all interest-rate derivatives (not just swaps), the BIS put total notional outstanding at $258.7tn as of mid-2023, with LCH SwapClear alone accounting for about $208tn — roughly 80% of the entire global stock. At the U.S. CCP level more broadly (all products, not just IRS), the Chicago Fed computed an initial-margin HHI of 3,179.6 as of December 2023 — still "highly concentrated" by DOJ standards, though lower than the IRS-specific figures above because it blends in less-concentrated product lines (equities, Treasuries) cleared at other CCPs like FICC and OCC.

Layer 2 — clearing members: access is controlled by the same handful of global banks

A market participant cannot clear a swap directly at LCH or CME unless it is itself a clearing member (CM) — in the U.S., a registered futures commission merchant (FCM). Everyone else clears as a client of one of those banks. Both the OCC and the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago independently confirm this access layer is at least as concentrated as the CCP layer:

  • OCC (Quarterly Report on Bank Trading and Derivatives Activities): just four large U.S. banks held 79.1% of total U.S. banking-industry derivatives notional in Q1 2026 (85.1% in Q4 2025, 87.1% in Q1 2025) — out of ~1,190+ insured banks that hold any derivatives at all.
  • Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago ("How Concentrated Is the Clearing Ecosystem," July 2024): the number of FCMs serving U.S. clearing clients fell from a peak of 190 (March 2005) to 63 (December 2023). Across CCPs, the top 5 clearing members handle 48.5-88% of client transactions and the top 10 handle 78.4-100%, depending on product — and "the clearing services for OTC interest rate swaps and credit default swaps both have over 99% of the client transactions cleared by the top 10 CMs." A separate, oft-cited Chicago Fed/CFTC finding: five bank-affiliated clearing members account for more than 80% of total client margin for cleared OTC derivatives in the US, UK, and Japan combined.

In other words, even in currencies/products where the CCP layer looks like a duopoly (USD split LCH/CME) rather than a pure monopoly, the practical experience for an end client is clearing through one of a handful of the same GSIB-affiliated banks (JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Morgan Stanley, etc.) regardless of which CCP the trade lands at.

Why it stays this concentrated

Swap clearing has strong natural-monopoly economics: dealers want to net offsetting trades against the deepest possible counterparty pool at a single CCP to minimize collateral costs, a network effect that entrenched LCH SwapClear's early lead after the post-2008 Dodd-Frank/EMIR mandatory-clearing push. A 2019 BIS working paper (Benos, Huang, Menkveld, Vasios) documents that when liquidity does fragment across CCPs — as with USD swaps split between LCH and CME — a persistent "CCP basis" price distortion emerges, averaging about 2 basis points and roughly $80 million per day in opportunity cost for end users, because dealers cannot net collateral requirements across the two pools. That cost is itself evidence of how strong the pull toward one dominant clearer is: fragmentation is expensive, so liquidity keeps re-concentrating at LCH except where regulation actively works against it (the EU's Active Account Requirement for EUR, JSCC's home-market advantage for JPY). At the clearing-member layer, the driver is different but reinforcing: post-crisis capital and margin rules raised the fixed cost of being a clearing member enough that smaller banks exited (FCM count fell from 190 to 63 since 2005), leaving client access concentrated among the same large, well-capitalized global banks that dominate dealing generally.

Data and methodology notes

AskAmerica's own government-sourced corpus (CFTC swap data repository feed, schema cftc) was checked first via search_catalog and confirmed structurally unable to answer the CCP-layer question directly: under CFTC Part 43 public-dissemination rules, the CCP-facing 'beta/gamma' novation leg that carries the clearer's identity is explicitly excluded from the public data feed, so cftc.clearing_activity and cftc.cftc_trades show near-zero disseminated rows for the cleared field regardless of true clearing volume — a known structural gap in the public U.S. government data, not a bug in this corpus. All HHI figures in the dashboard above were computed directly with the hhi_concentration tool from the currency-level shares reported by Clarus/LSEG (not read off a published index). The Chicago Fed's own U.S.-CCP-wide IM HHI (3,180) and FCM client-funds HHI (891) are quoted directly from that paper rather than recomputed, since the underlying per-CCP/per-FCM dollar figures needed to recompute them were not extracted from the source table in this session.
