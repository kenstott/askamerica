Federal oil and gas royalties average $379 per leased acre nationally — but range from $0.20 to $1,340/acre by state

FY2024 ONRR onshore royalty revenue divided by BLM acres under federal oil & gas lease, by state

Summary

Averaged across all federal onshore oil-and-gas acreage under lease, royalty revenue works out to roughly $379 per leased acre in FY2024 ($8.42 billion in onshore royalties collected by ONRR, divided by the 22.2 million acres BLM reported as under lease at fiscal year-end). But that average is close to meaningless as a description of any actual acre: royalty return per acre varies by more than 6,000-fold across states in FY2024 — from about $0.20/acre in West Virginia to roughly $1,340/acre in New Mexico, whose Permian Basin leases alone produced 66% of all federal onshore royalty revenue from just 19% of leased acreage. A large part of the variance is not geographic at all but binary: only about 56% of leased federal acreage (12.4 of 22.2 million acres) was actually producing at FY2024 year-end, and non-producing acreage returns essentially nothing in royalties (it pays only nominal rent, $3–$15/acre/year). GAO has separately found that noncompetitively-issued leases return only about one-fifth the per-acre revenue of competitively-issued leases, a second, independent axis of variation layered on top of geography.

How the number was built

Numerator — onshore federal oil & gas royalty revenue by state and fiscal year, from ONRR's revenuedata.onrr.gov bulk file (loaded here as lands.onrr_revenues), filtered to revenue_type='Royalties', mineral_lease_type='Oil & Gas', land_category='Onshore' — i.e. excluding bonus bids and rent, which are one-time or acreage-holding payments rather than production royalties, and excluding offshore (Gulf of Mexico/Pacific) revenue, which is collected under a different acreage regime (BOEM, not BLM).

Denominator — BLM's own Table 2 ("Acreage in Effect"), from the FY2024 Oil and Gas Statistics workbook, giving acres under lease by state as of the last day of each fiscal year. National FY2024 total: 22,218,215 acres (a literal total in BLM's own summary tab, not a sum I built by hand). Producing-acreage figures come from the same BLM publication's national totals (BLM's own page text: ~12.4 million of 22.2 million federal acres under lease were producing oil and gas in economic quantities from over 91,000 wells at FY2024 year end).

Division — royalty revenue ÷ acres under lease, computed separately for each state and nationally. This is an average royalty intensity, not a per-lease or per-well figure, and it deliberately does NOT net out the (much smaller) rent and bonus revenue non-producing acreage does generate — the question asked about royalty revenue specifically.

Notice the pattern is not simply "biggest acreage wins": Wyoming holds the single largest federal leasehold in the country (7.1 million acres, 32% of the national total) yet returns only $111/acre — a ninth of California's per-acre return from a leasehold 1/24th the size, and a twelfth of New Mexico's. Acreage size and productivity are close to unrelated across states; what matters is which basin the acreage sits over (Permian, Bakken, and legacy California fields are extraordinarily rich relative to leased area; much of Wyoming, Nevada, and Appalachia is leased but only lightly productive).

The producing/non-producing split, and the competitive/noncompetitive split

Two further, independently-documented sources of variance sit underneath the state numbers above:

  • Producing vs. non-producing acreage. BLM's own FY2024 statistics show 22.2 million acres leased against roughly 12.4 million acres actually producing — meaning close to half of all leased federal acreage nationally generates essentially zero royalty in a given year (only the flat per-acre rental payment, currently $3/acre for the first two years, $5/acre for years 3–8, and at least $15/acre for years 9–10). Any acre-averaged royalty figure, including the $379/acre headline above, is therefore a blend of a large non-producing population returning near $0 and a smaller producing population returning several hundred to well over a thousand dollars per acre.
  • Competitive vs. noncompetitive leasing. GAO has found that noncompetitively-issued leases (those that failed to attract a bid at auction and can then be picked up for the statutory minimum) return only about one-fifth the royalty revenue per acre that competitively-issued leases do — a second axis of variance that cuts across both the producing/non-producing split and the state-level split above.

The FY2024 royalty rate itself is also part of the story: the Inflation Reduction Act (2022) raised the onshore federal royalty rate from the century-old 12.5% statutory minimum to 16.67% for leases issued in the following 10 years, which is one reason the national average nearly tripled from about $121/acre in FY2020 to $379/acre in FY2024 (the other reasons being the 2021–2023 rebound in oil and gas prices and continuing production growth in the Permian).

Caveats and what this does not capture

  • All figures are onshore only. Federal offshore (Gulf of Mexico, Pacific) royalties were $6.4 billion in FY2024, collected under BOEM's separate offshore-leasing regime with a different acreage basis (block-based, not the BLM's onshore parcel system), so onshore and offshore per-acre figures are not directly comparable and were not combined here.
  • ONRR's onshore royalty data for FY2024 carries a $1.08 billion "unassigned/no state" line (about 13% of the onshore royalty total) that this analysis could not attribute to any state's acreage — it is included in the $379/acre national average (numerator) but not in any state-level figure, so state figures undercount the true national total slightly less than they appear to.
  • The state-level acreage-to-royalty match uses each state's total leased acreage as the denominator, not that state's producing acreage specifically — so a state with a higher producing share (e.g. New Mexico) will show a higher $/acre partly for that composition reason, not purely a richer underlying resource.
  • Web fetches to blm.gov, doi.gov, taxpayer.net, and congress.gov intermittently returned connection errors during this research; the BLM acreage figures ultimately used here were confirmed via a successful direct fetch and parse of BLM's own FY2024 Oil and Gas Statistics Excel workbook (Table 2), not a secondary source's summary of it. The $14.5B FY2024 aggregate energy-revenue and GAO noncompetitive-lease figures are cited from search-engine summaries of DOI/GAO material, not from a parsed primary document, and are flagged as such.
