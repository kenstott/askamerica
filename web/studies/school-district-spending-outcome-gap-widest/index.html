Spending barely predicts results across districts: Camden, Chester-Upland, Canton and Toledo are high-spend/low-result; Forsyth, Oak Park and Elkhorn are low-spend/high-result (FY2018)

Summary

Using FY2018 Census F-33 current spending per pupil and EDFacts math+reading proficiency (grades 3-8, enrollment-weighted) for 5,427 districts of 1,500+ students, spending and results are almost unrelated nationally (r = +0.04); poverty matters far more (r = -0.69 with proficiency). Within states, districts spending 15%+ above the state average still scored far below state peers: Camden NJ (1.24x state average, 13% proficient, 41 points below state average), Chester-Upland PA (1.29x, 15%, -41), Canton OH (1.29x, 38%, -34), Toledo OH (1.24x, 34%, -37), Cincinnati OH (1.28x, 48%, -23). Strong results at low cost: Oak Park Unified CA ($9,613, 0.85x state avg, 77%, +32), Forsyth County GA ($9,315, 74%, +32), Fountain Valley CA, Dublin CA, Redondo Beach CA, York 04 SC, Elkhorn NE, Brownsburg IN, Catalina Foothills AZ ($6,732, 68%, +29). Adjusting for poverty, North Palos IL, Wise/Henry/Scott Co. VA, Sampson Co. NC and Sayreville/Dover/Lodi NJ over-perform on low budgets.

Exclusions

Excluded: the 352 districts whose frpl_rate is NULL (5,075 of 5,427 have a value), because the predicate frpl_rate IS NOT NULL was applied to the within-state and poverty-adjusted analysis. They are mostly Community Eligibility states. The national spend-proficiency correlation with all 5,427 districts is r = +0.04; the within-state analysis on the 5,075 shows r = -0.09 (log spend vs state-demeaned proficiency, states with 10+ districts). Named lists also exclude districts under 3,000 students.

Caveats

  • Proficiency cutoffs differ by state, so ranks are relative to the state average only; no national ranking is possible with this data.
  • California county offices of education and special districts (e.g. St. Louis County special school district, $93,185 per pupil) top the spend/low-score lists but serve special populations; they are not typical districts and I left them out of the headline names.
  • Poverty adjustment used district FRPL with a pooled within-state slope (-51.6 points per 1.0 FRPL); some FRPL values look unreliable (Canton 0.0, Sayreville 1.0), so poverty-adjusted names are indicative only.
  • Higher-spending districts tend to serve higher-need students; association, not proof money fails.
  • Data window: 2018, the latest year with both sources before the 2019-2020 gap; not current.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Current-year data: District proficiency data ends 2020 (2019 missing); used FY2018.
  • Cross-state comparable outcomes: State tests are not comparable across states, so comparison is within-state only.
