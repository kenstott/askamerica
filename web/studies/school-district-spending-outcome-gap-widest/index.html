Within each state, high-spending districts with weak results are mostly high-poverty urban districts, and spending barely predicts results once poverty is held fixed

Census F-33 FY2018 per-pupil current spending x EDFacts 2018 grades 3-8 proficiency (math+reading); 6,417 districts, 46 states; enrollment >=1,000

Answer

Using the only district-level spending and results data available here (Census F-33 FY2018 spending; state-test proficiency, 2018), the high-spend/weak-result group is dominated by high-poverty urban districts: Chester-Upland PA ($18,957 per pupil, 128% of its state average, proficiency 39.8 points below the state), Gary IN (143%, -34.1), Flint MI (154%, -33.5), Normandy MO (126%, -33.0), East Chicago IN (135%, -31.7), Pine Bluff AR (127%, -31.2), Battle Creek MI (143%, -30.6), St. Louis City (141%, -26.4), Indianapolis (152%, -25.6); Lansing, Pontiac and St. Paul also appear. Most have 65-100% free/reduced lunch rates.

Strongest results at the lowest spending (spending at 85% of the state average or less, FRPL 40%+): North Palos IL ($11,320, 79% of state, +26.4 points above state), Clinton MS (+24.3, $7,624), Newhall CA (+23.2), Whitehouse TX (+21.4), Etiwanda CA (+20.2), Vermilion Parish LA (+15.3), Webb City MO, Dorchester 2 SC, DeSoto Co MS, Wise County VA (89% proficient on $9,222).

Key finding: within states, after controlling for district poverty, spending has no detectable association with proficiency (coefficient -0.000035 points per dollar, p=0.34, n=6,373); poverty explains most variation (R2 0.64). High-spend districts often spend more because they serve high-need students, so the list shows poverty, not necessarily waste.

Exclusions and caveats

Exclusion disclosure. The predicate frpl_rate IS NOT NULL excluded 404 of 6,821 districts (1,000+ students, 300+ valid test-takers): those whose state publishes no free/reduced lunch rate (Community Eligibility states such as MA, TN, WV, DE). The predicates a.math IS NOT NULL and a.rd IS NOT NULL excluded districts with no usable math or reading proficiency (sentinel/suppressed values). Without the frpl exclusion the raw spending-proficiency correlation is 0.068 (n=6,821); with it 0.054 (n=6,417), so the exclusion changes nothing material. The unfiltered count of districts lacking math or reading was not separately measured. Also excluded: districts under 1,000 students (F-33 includes tiny non-operating units with per-pupil figures in the millions), FRPL values above 100% (miscoded), states with under 20 qualifying districts (regression n=6,373), and county special-education/vocational operators removed by name from the headline lists.

  • Proficiency: enrollment-weighted math and reading, grades 3-8, 2018. State tests have different cutoffs, so each district is compared only to its own state average.
  • Spending: per-pupil current expenditure, FY2018, not adjusted for cost of living or student need.
  • Data end at 2018 (2019 absent, 2020 anomalous); no post-pandemic results. One year, descriptive not causal. Some FRPL values look miscoded (Canton OH at 0%, Sayreville NJ at 99.7%), so the poverty-adjusted ranking is noisy and I used the raw within-state gap for the named lists.
