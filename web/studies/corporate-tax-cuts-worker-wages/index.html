Real pay for production and nonsupervisory workers grew faster after the 2017 corporate tax cut, but the data cannot show the tax cut caused it

BLS CES average hourly earnings (production/nonsupervisory, total private) deflated by CPI-U; annual averages

Summary

Yes in a narrow sense, and no as a causal claim. Real hourly pay for production and nonsupervisory workers rose about 1.1% a year over 2017-2019, versus 0.7% a year over 2015-2017 (equal two-year windows). But on a longer baseline (2014-2017) the pre-period pace was also 1.1%, so the acceleration depends on the window. The pay growth was real but modest, and a single national series with no control group cannot attribute it to the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (corporate rate cut from 35% to 21%, effective January 2018).

Window choice: I used matched-length windows ending/starting at the 2017 pre-effective year and showed alternatives, because a mismatched baseline can manufacture or erase an acceleration. Post-2019 figures are contaminated by COVID (low-wage job losses raise average earnings mechanically) and 2021-22 inflation.

Caveats

  • Pre-trend: real pay was already rising 1-1.5%/yr as the labor market tightened after 2014; the tax cut came late in an expansion.
  • No control group: this is national, so I cannot separate the tax cut from tight labor markets, minimum wage increases, or the business cycle. Studies using firm-level data generally find corporate tax benefits flowed mostly to shareholders and high earners, with small worker gains; I did not re-verify those studies here.
  • Average hourly earnings is a composition-sensitive average, not median pay of the same workers, and excludes bonuses and benefits.
  • Annual averages from CES; table metadata declares 2008-2024 but rows extend into 2026 (2026 is partial).

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Causal attribution to the tax cut: National series has no control group; stated as not identifiable here. Literature on firm-level effects not re-verified.

query — 17 rows — 5182 ms
SELECT w.yr, w.nom, c.cpi, w.nom/c.cpi*100 AS real_wage FROM (SELECT substr(CAST("date" AS VARCHAR),1,4) AS yr, avg("value") AS nom FROM econ.wage_growth WHERE series='CES0500000008' GROUP BY substr(CAST("date" AS VARCHAR),1,4)) w JOIN (SELECT CAST("year" AS VARCHAR) AS yr, avg("value") AS cpi FROM econ.inflation_metrics WHERE series='CUUR0000SA0' GROUP BY CAST("year" AS VARCHAR)) c ON w.yr=c.yr ORDER BY w.yr
