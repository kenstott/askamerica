Manufacturing and professional/tech services lead reported cyber incidents; ransomware's share of breaches has risen to a record 48%

Sources: Verizon 2024-2026 Data Breach Investigations Reports; Black Kite 2026 Ransomware Report; GuidePoint GRIT 2026 Ransomware &amp; Cyber Threat Report

Summary

Two independent industry-tracking approaches agree on the top target: manufacturing is generating the most reported cyber incidents right now, followed by professional/scientific/technical services (including technology), with healthcare, financial services, retail/wholesale, construction and legal rounding out a consistent 'top tier'. Manufacturing alone accounted for 22.0% of all publicly disclosed ransomware victims tracked by Black Kite between April 2025 and March 2026 (1,660 of 7,551), and manufacturing plus professional/technical services together made up roughly 40% of that total. Separately, ransomware's share of confirmed data breaches has been rising for three straight years in Verizon's Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR): from roughly 32% (2023 incident data, ransomware+extortion combined under a revised methodology) to 44% (2024 data) to a record 48% in the 2026 DBIR (2025 incident data) — the highest share in the report's 19-year history, even as the total number of tracked ransomware groups and victims both grew.

Which industries are hit most

Two named, current reports were used because AskAmerica's own warehouse (cyber_vuln/cyber_threat schemas) carries CVE, KEV and IOC data but no industry-attributed breach/incident-count table, so this could not be answered from that corpus alone (confirmed via search_catalog before going external).

  • Black Kite's 2026 Ransomware Report (7,551 publicly disclosed ransomware victims tracked April 2025-March 2026, cross-validated against OSINT): Manufacturing led for a fourth consecutive year with 1,660 victims (22.0% of all disclosures); Professional, Scientific & Technical Services (including much of the technology sector) followed with 1,389 (together ~40% of the year's total); Construction climbed to third with 541 victims. Healthcare, Financial Services, and Wholesale/Retail Trade sat in the 'next tier' of volume, each large enough to matter but well behind the top two.
  • GuidePoint Security's GRIT 2026 Ransomware & Cyber Threat Report independently ranks the five most-impacted industries by ransomware volume for both 2024 and 2025 identically: (1) Manufacturing (1,060 victims, 14% of observed volume in GRIT's count), (2) Technology, (3) Retail & Wholesale, (4) Healthcare, (5) Legal. Legal stood out for growth: victims more than doubled year over year, from 196 in 2024 to 455 in 2025 (+132%), attributed to the volume of privileged client PII/PHI law firms hold.
  • Verizon's 2026 DBIR (31,000+ security incidents, 22,000+ confirmed breaches across 145 countries, Nov 2024-Oct 2025) names finance, professional services, manufacturing, healthcare and local government/public sector as the most heavily targeted sectors overall — broadly consistent with the ransomware-specific rankings above, though DBIR measures all breach types, not ransomware alone.

The two ransomware-specific sources (Black Kite and GuidePoint/GRIT) use different tracking methodologies and slightly different industry taxonomies (NAICS-based vs. GRIT's own categories) but converge tightly: manufacturing first, technology/professional services second, with healthcare, retail and construction/legal filling out the top tier both years.

Ransomware's share of incidents: rising, not falling

The clearest, most authoritative time series for this comes from Verizon's DBIR, which reports the share of confirmed breaches in which ransomware was present, each year drawing on tens of thousands of incidents contributed by ~100 partner organizations (law enforcement, forensic firms, cyber insurers, threat-intel groups):

  • 2024 DBIR (incidents from Nov 2022-Oct 2023): ransomware and extortion combined were present in 32% of breaches, under a revised methodology introduced that year that also changed how insider-caused incidents were counted, so this figure is not perfectly comparable to later years.
  • 2025 DBIR (Nov 2023-Oct 2024 incidents): ransomware alone rose to 44% of confirmed breaches.
  • 2026 DBIR (Nov 2024-Oct 2025 incidents): ransomware rose again to 48% of confirmed breaches — Verizon states this is the highest share in the DBIR's 19-year history.

This rising share held even though the median ransom payment actually fell (from $150,000 to $139,875) and 69% of 2025 victims refused to pay at all — the trend is in incident frequency and breach-type composition, not in dollars extracted per victim. It is corroborated by the raw victim-count data: Black Kite counted 7,551 publicly disclosed ransomware victims in its April 2025-March 2026 window, up 24.9% year over year, the fourth consecutive year of a new high, with the pace accelerating sharply in the second half (861 victims in March 2026 alone, the highest single month on record) and the number of active ransomware groups climbing from 127 to 146 by June 2026 (up from just 61 groups in 2023). More attacker groups, more victims, and a growing share of all breaches involving ransomware are consistent, mutually reinforcing signals of a rising rather than plateauing or falling trend.

Caveats

Ransomware-victim counts from leak-site tracking services (Black Kite, GuidePoint/GRIT) reflect only publicly disclosed victims (posted to dark-web leak sites or otherwise made public); true attack volume, especially for victims who pay quietly or whose data is never posted, is almost certainly higher and could skew the industry mix if under-disclosure is uneven across sectors. The Black Kite industry chart's healthcare/financial-services/wholesale-retail victim counts are approximate 'next tier' values read from descriptive report text rather than an exact published table (the underlying interactive report was not independently pulled beyond the narrative page). The 2024-to-2025 DBIR ransomware share change (32%→44%) spans a methodology revision Verizon itself flags, so the cleanest same-methodology comparison is 2025→2026 (44%→48%), which is the core of the 'rising' finding here. No AskAmerica-warehouse table (cyber_vuln, cyber_threat) carries industry-attributed breach or ransomware incident counts, so this entire answer rests on external primary-source reporting, not this corpus's own data.
