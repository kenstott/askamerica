In the fastest AI-adopting industries, the occupational mix has moved, but no faster than before ChatGPT

BLS OEWS national industry x major-occupation employment, May 2019 / 2022 / 2025; Census BTOS AI use; dissimilarity index in points

Answer

Only weakly, and not in a way that can be pinned on AI. In Information and Finance and Insurance, the two sectors with the highest measured AI use, the occupational mix moved by 5.6 and 4.1 index points between May 2022 and May 2025. In the three years before ChatGPT (2019 to 2022) it moved more: 7.6 and 4.9 points. The mix is shifting, but the pace has slowed rather than accelerated.

Who is adopting fastest

Census BTOS (to May 3, 2026) puts AI use at 39.7% in Information and 33.9% in Finance and Insurance against 19.8% nationally; neither changed significantly since December. Wording changed in November 2025 ('any business function'), so older figures are not comparable. Professional services is also high in other sources but I did not find a BTOS rate for it.

What I measured

Dissimilarity index = half the sum of absolute changes in each of 22 major-occupation shares (the share of jobs that would have to change occupation to restore the old mix).

Where it moved, 2022 to 2025: Information gained management (11.6% to 13.3%) and computer/math (23.7% to 26.0%) and lost arts/media (16.0% to 13.4%) and office support (12.2% to 10.5%). Finance lost office/administrative support (33.6% to 29.6%) and gained management (11.3% to 12.7%). That direction, away from clerical work, is what AI-exposure theory predicts, but Finance's office-support share was already falling from 38.3% in 2019 to 33.6% in 2022. Professional services moved about 2.5 to 3.7 points 2022-25 (the 2022 page I read was truncated before two small groups, so I did not rely on it).

Corroboration

Yale Budget Lab finds the same: Information, Financial Activities and Professional/Business Services show larger mix shifts than the aggregate since November 2022, but the trends began before ChatGPT. Stanford's ADP-based work finds no broad displacement, with declines concentrated among workers aged 22-25 in exposed occupations (about 19% below counterfactual), a pattern that sector-wide OEWS shares would not reveal. An NBER paper finds use above 20% in four of five occupations.

Caveats

  • OEWS is a three-year rolling panel survey; each May estimate pools up to 36 months of data, so it smooths and lags real shifts, and 2025 estimates only partly cover the post-adoption period.
  • Occupation groups are 2-digit; shifts within groups (for example junior versus senior analysts) are invisible.
  • NAICS and OEWS methodology revisions between 2019 and 2025 may move some Information employment across sectors; I did not adjust for this.
  • The warehouse loads only May 2025 for this table; earlier years were transcribed from BLS pages, and the 2019-to-2025 comparison is descriptive, not causal. No test of statistical significance is possible from published point estimates.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Use warehouse for the shift: Warehouse table econ.industry_occupation_employment holds only May 2025; 2019/2022 values were read from BLS OEWS industry pages. Professional services excluded from the pre/post comparison because the 2022 page was truncated.
