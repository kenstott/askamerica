Outage duration has risen modestly in data-center states, but about as much as everywhere else; frequency is flat, and business-interruption exposure cannot be measured here

EIA-861 reliability, 2013-2023, customer-weighted, excluding major event days; IEEE and non-IEEE reporters combined

Summary

Outage minutes per customer (SAIDI, normal conditions) rose from the 2013-15 average to the 2021-23 average in most big data-center states: Virginia +17% (145 to 171 min), Georgia +32% (100 to 132), Nevada +33%, Oregon +18%, Arizona +12%, Texas +8%, Ohio +6%. Illinois fell 32%. All other states combined rose 13% (108 to 122). So Virginia is only modestly above the national drift, and the group is not clearly different from the rest. Outage frequency (SAIFI) is roughly flat: Virginia 1.21 to 1.29, Texas 1.24 to 1.19, Ohio 1.07 to 1.05, rest of US 0.99 to 1.02.

Business-interruption exposure: this warehouse has no data on losses, claims, insurance or commercial outage costs, so that half of the question is not answered with data. Longer outages imply some more exposure, but nothing here ties the change to data centers.

Exclusions

Excluded rows: utility-years where SAIDI, SAIFI, with-MED SAIDI (saidi_m IS NOT NULL) or a customer count was missing under both the IEEE and utility-own standards. Those utilities were not named. Checking the effect: without the saidi_m filter, Virginia SAIDI goes from 146.6 to 170.5 min (+16%) versus 145.4 to 170.6 (+17%) with it, and the non-data-center states rise about 12% versus 13%. Also excluded from the 'rest of US' group are the eight chosen states (VA, TX, GA, AZ, OH, IL, OR, NV), shown separately. A cross-check query also excluded rows where ieee_saidi_without_med IS NOT NULL failed only in the sense of the coalesced saidi being null; it did not use the IEEE column alone, so Dominion remains included. Major event days are excluded by using the without-MED series.

Method and caveats

Table energy.eia_utility_reliability. Each utility-year uses the IEEE value where reported, else the utility's own standard, weighted by covered customers (so Dominion is included). With-MED values swing wildly with storms (Texas 2021 1,197 min, Oregon 2021 1,567 min, Georgia 2017 1,045 min). Data end in 2023, and much data-center build-out is later, so an effect may not show yet. Statewide averages are not data-center-county averages; I chose the eight states from general knowledge, not a load table. Utility reporting coverage varies by year and I did not hold the utility panel fixed or test dropping the largest contributor. Windows are 3 years each, endpoints noisy: Virginia ranged 142-188 between years. Association only; weather, aging lines and reporting changes are not controlled.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Business-interruption exposure for local businesses: No loss, claims or insurance data in the warehouse; not computed and no outside sources fetched.

WITH u AS (SELECT report_year yr, state_abbr st, utility_id,
 COALESCE(ieee_saidi_without_med, other_saidi_without_med) saidi,
 COALESCE(ieee_saifi_without_med, other_saifi_without_med) saifi,
 COALESCE(ieee_saidi_with_med, other_saidi_with_med) saidi_m,
 CASE WHEN ieee_saidi_without_med IS NOT NULL THEN ieee_customers ELSE other_customers END c
 FROM energy.eia_utility_reliability)
SELECT st, yr, SUM(saidi*c)/SUM(c) saidi, SUM(saifi*c)/SUM(c) saifi, SUM(saidi_m*c)/SUM(c) saidi_med, SUM(c) cust
FROM u WHERE saidi IS NOT NULL AND saifi IS NOT NULL AND saidi_m IS NOT NULL AND c>0 AND st IN ('VA','TX','GA','AZ','OH','IL','OR','NV')
GROUP BY st, yr ORDER BY st, yr
