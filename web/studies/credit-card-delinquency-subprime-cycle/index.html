Card delinquency has not returned to pre-pandemic levels; subprime-specific data is not in the corpus, so all-borrower figures are a proxy

NY Fed/Equifax household debt (econ.household_debt_credit) through 2026Q3; Fed bank card delinquency (FRED DRCCLACBS) through 2026Q2

Answer

I could not find a subprime-only series: the warehouse has no credit-score breakdown, and the New York Fed's by-score data and TransUnion's page were not retrievable. For card borrowers overall, delinquency fell to a trough in 2021-22, surged through 2023-24, and has plateaued since, still well above 2019 on most measures. Subprime borrowers, who drive card delinquency, are therefore very likely still above pre-pandemic levels, but that is an inference, not a measurement.

The stock of seriously delinquent balances is about 55% above 2019 and has not fallen. New-delinquency flows peaked in 2024 and are easing slowly (90+ flow 33% above 2019; 30+ flow 23% above). The bank-reported rate, which covers a prime-heavier book, is only about 10% above 2019 and has fallen for eight quarters, so it is nearly back.

Subprime evidence outside the warehouse

  • The New York Fed's Liberty Street Economics (Nov 2026) finds card delinquency above pre-pandemic for every neighborhood-income group, with the steepest rise among the lowest-income quartile; it gives no subprime figures.
  • Origination scores: median 699 in 2017-19, 688 in 2020-22, and higher since 2023, so newer vintages are stronger than pandemic-era ones.
  • Search results report mixed signals: Philadelphia Fed large-bank data above pre-pandemic as of Q4 2025, while some bank-card trackers show early delinquency back below pre-pandemic in 2026. I did not open those primary sources.

Caveats

Subprime-specific rates (e.g., VantageScore, Fitch, TransUnion, NY Fed by score) are the right measure and would likely show higher levels and a larger gap. The balance-based percent is also affected by charge-offs and balance growth, so it is not a pure borrower-level rate.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Subprime-specific series: No credit-score breakdown in the corpus (the word subprime matches nothing); NY Fed's by-score sheets are not loaded. NY Fed Liberty Street piece gives only qualitative by-income findings; TransUnion page returned 403. Used all-borrower and low-income-neighborhood evidence as a proxy.
