No clean subprime-only card series exists in the warehouse; proxies show card delinquency has stopped rising and is near or above pre-pandemic levels depending on the measure

Summary

I could not find a subprime-only card delinquency series, so I cannot give a precise subprime answer. For all card borrowers, new delinquencies rose sharply from the 2021 lows through 2023, then leveled off in 2024 to 2026. On lender-reported 30+ day rates, the bank card delinquency rate was 2.85% in Q2 2026 (3.04% a year earlier, 3.22% two years earlier). The NY Fed's 90+ day stock rate kept rising, from 7.6% (2022Q3) to 12.8% (2026Q1), but the NY Fed says this is driven by stale charged-off balances being reported longer, not by more new delinquency. Whether that is back to pre-pandemic depends on the measure. Secondary sources put the 30+ rate at roughly 2.6% to 2.9% against a Q4 2019 baseline near 2.6%, which I did not verify from a primary source. Prime cardholders are at record lows (Fitch, 0.84% 60+), so any remaining stress is concentrated in subprime. TransUnion attributes its higher borrower-level 90+ rate (2.26%, versus 2.06% in Q2 2023) to a growing subprime population.

Data caveats

The warehouse tables econ.household_debt_credit and a full DRCCLACBS history were not queryable (only 2026Q1 and Q2 returned, 2.91 and 2.85); I reported this as a defect. Figures above come from web sources: Wolf Street (relaying Fed, Equifax and Fitch data), the NY Fed Liberty Street post of Aug 11, 2026, and a TransUnion Q2 2026 summary via search. The PNC report would not load. The Q4 2019 baseline figure is from a search snippet, not a primary read. The subprime card gap is a real finding: ask for Fitch or VantageScore subprime card ABS indices or NY Fed delinquency by credit score for a direct answer.
