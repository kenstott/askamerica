Net interest is now ~14% of federal spending, the third-largest budget item, and it has more than tripled since 2015 even after adjusting for inflation

CBO Feb 2026 Budget and Economic Outlook; Treasury Fiscal Data

Summary

In fiscal year 2025, the federal government paid $970 billion in net interest on the national debt — about 14% of total federal outlays and roughly 18.5% of federal revenue. That makes interest the third-largest item in the federal budget, behind only Social Security and Medicare, and larger than national defense or Medicaid. Interest costs have grown enormously: from $223 billion in FY2015 to $970 billion in FY2025 — a 335% nominal increase, or about 220% even after adjusting for inflation (CPI-U, expressed in 2025 dollars). CBO's February 2026 baseline projects interest will keep growing faster than any other major budget category, reaching roughly $1.04 trillion in FY2026 and $2.1 trillion by FY2036.

How much of the budget interest takes today

Net interest totaled $970 billion in FY2025 (Congressional Budget Office / Treasury data). Relative to the rest of the budget:

  • ~14% of total federal outlays in FY2025 (up from roughly 6% in FY2015, when interest was $223B against about $3.69T in total outlays)
  • 18.5% of federal revenue in FY2025, above the previous historical high of ~18% set in 1991, and up from about 10% in 2021
  • 3.2% of GDP in FY2025, more than double the 1.6% of GDP recorded in 2021, and already above the prior record set in 1991
  • Larger than Medicaid, national defense, or total non-defense discretionary spending; roughly on par with Medicare, and projected by CBO to exceed Medicare starting around 2029

The federal government paid interest at an average pace of roughly $2.7–$2.8 billion per day in FY2025.

How much it has grown

Net interest outlays by fiscal year (CBO/Treasury, nominal dollars): $223B (2015) → $345B (2020) → $352B (2021) → $881B (2024) → $970B (2025) → an estimated ~$1.04 trillion (2026, CBO baseline).

That is a 335% nominal increase from 2015 to 2025. But nominal dollar comparisons across a decade overstate real growth because of inflation. Using AskAmerica's inflation-adjustment tool (BLS CPI-U, annual averages), $223 billion in 2015 is equivalent to about $303 billion in 2025 dollars. Against the actual FY2025 figure of $970 billion, that means net interest costs have grown by roughly 220% in real (inflation-adjusted) terms over the decade — still more than a tripling, just not quite as large as the raw nominal figure implies.

The growth accelerated sharply after 2021: interest nearly tripled between FY2020 ($345B) and FY2024 ($881B) alone, driven by the combination of a much larger debt stock and the return of higher interest rates after 2022. CBO attributes the increase to both a growing debt (held by the public, projected to grow 86% from 2025 to 2036) and a rising average interest rate paid on that debt (projected up about 16%, or 0.5 percentage points, over the same span) — together projected to push interest costs up 121% further by 2036.

Where this is headed

Under CBO's February 2026 baseline (current law, no policy changes assumed):

  • Net interest is projected to roughly double again, from $970B in FY2025 to about $2.1 trillion by FY2036
  • As a share of GDP: 3.2% (2025) → 4.6% (2036)
  • As a share of federal revenue: 18.5% (2025) → 25.8% (2036) — meaning by 2036 roughly one dollar in four of all federal tax revenue would go just to paying interest
  • As a share of total spending, interest is projected to reach about 15.7% by 2029, which would exceed the previous all-time high of 15.4% set in 1996
  • CBO projects interest to become the single largest federal program, surpassing Social Security, by around 2047

These are baseline projections under current law, not certainties — they would change if interest rates, deficits, or policy diverge from CBO's assumptions.

Methodology and sourcing notes

Net interest figures for FY2015–FY2025 and CBO's FY2026–FY2036 projections come from CBO's February 2026 Budget and Economic Outlook, as reported directly by the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget (CRFB) and the Peter G. Peterson Foundation (PGPF), both of which cite CBO's baseline explicitly and were fetched directly for this report (not secondary paraphrases). AskAmerica's own warehouse (econ.federal_debt, sourced from the Treasury Fiscal Data API) was queried and confirmed current total public debt outstanding (~$39.2 trillion as of June 2026) but does not carry a net-interest-outlay-by-year series broken out from the rest of the budget, so the budget-share figures in this report are drawn from CBO/Treasury reporting via CRFB and PGPF rather than computed from AskAmerica tables directly. The FY2015 total-outlays figure used for the ~6%-of-budget comparison is a standard published OMB/Treasury total (~$3.69 trillion) rather than a figure pulled from this connector. The real-dollar (inflation-adjusted) comparison was computed using AskAmerica's adjust_inflation tool against BLS CPI-U annual averages.
