Net interest hit $970B in FY2025 — 13.8% of federal spending, more than double its 2015 share

OMB Historical Table 3.2 (Outlays by Function, FY1962-2031) and Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement, September 2025

Summary

Net interest on the federal debt was $970 billion in FY2025 — 13.8% of total federal outlays and about 19% of federal revenue (Treasury, Monthly Treasury Statement, Sept. 2025). It is now the government's third-largest spending category, behind only Social Security and Medicare, having overtaken defense.

That share has more than doubled in a decade: net interest was 6.1% of outlays in FY2015 and 8.4% in FY2019 (pre-pandemic), fell to roughly 5% in FY2020-2021 as near-zero interest rates and pandemic-inflated total spending both worked in its favor, then rose every year since as the Fed raised rates and outstanding debt kept growing — 7.6% (2022), 10.7% (2023), 13.1% (2024), 13.8% (2025). In dollar terms, net interest nearly quadrupled from $223B (2015) to $970B (2025), a 335% nominal increase and about 220% after adjusting for inflation (CPI-U, constant 2025 dollars). CBO/OMB project the share to keep climbing toward roughly 14.5-14.7% by FY2030-2031, which would approach or exceed the historical peak of about 15.3-15.4% set in the mid-1990s.

How the current level compares historically

Net interest's share of the budget has swung twice before: it rose from about 6.5% in the early 1960s to a first peak of roughly 15.3% in FY1995-1996 (high interest rates plus a debt run-up through the 1980s-90s), then fell steadily as rates dropped and 2000s deficits shrank, bottoming near 5.2-5.7% in FY2020-2021. The current run-up is the third episode and is driven by a different mix: a much larger stock of outstanding debt (nearly $37T total public debt in 2025) combined with the fastest rate-hiking cycle in decades (2022-2023), so each dollar of that larger debt now costs more to service. Unlike the 1980s-90s episode, this one is occurring without the double-digit interest rates of that era — it is a debt-stock story more than a rate-level story, though rates well above the 2010s' near-zero levels are compounding it.

Data and method

Figures are from OMB Historical Table 3.2, Outlays by Function and Subfunction (function code 900, Net Interest), loaded in this session as econ.federal_outlays_by_function, cross-checked against Treasury's FY2025 Monthly Treasury Statement figure of $970B via web search. Net interest is OMB's net-of-offsetting-receipts measure, not gross interest paid on the debt (which is higher). Share-of-outlays is net interest divided by that year's total outlays across all 20 OMB functions in the same table. Inflation adjustment uses BLS CPI-U annual averages (askengine adjust_inflation), base year 2025; the 2025 CPI average used is a partial 11-month figure since December 2025 CPI was not yet available at query time, so the real-dollar figures for 2025 should be read as approximate. FY2026-2031 rows in the source table are OMB's own budget-cycle projections/estimates, not measured actuals, and are shown for trend context only, not as a claim about what will happen.

Every query behind this report

3 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 70 rows — 9837 ms
SELECT f.year, f.outlays_millions AS net_interest_millions, t.total_millions
FROM econ.federal_outlays_by_function f
JOIN (SELECT year, SUM(outlays_millions) AS total_millions FROM econ.federal_outlays_by_function WHERE function_code <> '900' GROUP BY year) t ON t.year = f.year
WHERE f.function_code = '900'
ORDER BY f.year
query — 70 rows — 2057 ms
SELECT f."year", f.outlays_millions AS net_interest_millions, t.total_millions,
ROUND(100.0*f.outlays_millions/t.total_millions,2) AS pct_of_outlays
FROM econ.federal_outlays_by_function f
JOIN (SELECT "year", SUM(outlays_millions) AS total_millions FROM econ.federal_outlays_by_function GROUP BY "year") t ON t."year" = f."year"
WHERE f.function_code = '900'
ORDER BY f."year"
adjust_inflation — 25212 ms
SELECT "year", outlays_millions*1.0 AS net_interest_millions FROM econ.federal_outlays_by_function WHERE function_code='900' AND "year" IN (2015,2019,2020,2025)
