Five firms take about a third of DoD contract dollars, and that top-five share is higher in FY2023-25 than in FY2021

Summary

Five parent companies (Lockheed Martin, RTX, General Dynamics, Boeing, Northrop Grumman) took about 32% of DoD contract dollars in FY2025 and 33% in FY2023, versus about 26% in FY2021. Lockheed alone is 13.7% (FY2025). Quincy/Costs of War put 2020-24 at $771B of $2.4T (one-third). Geographically, five states (VA, CA, TX, FL, PA) received about $176B of DoD contract spending in FY2024. The top-five share is higher than in FY2021, but I sampled only three years, and FY2021 was distorted by pandemic awards, so I cannot call a long-run trend.

The squared-share sums (computed by me in Python from GovTribe's published shares, top 8-10 firms only) are floors under a full HHI. With about 36,700 contractors the full HHI is far below 1,000, so this is a top-heavy tail rather than a monopolized market. FY2021 shares are depressed by COVID vaccine and health awards in the denominator. Between FY2023 and FY2025 the top-five share was flat to slightly down (32.9% to 32.0%) while General Dynamics rose (4.9% to 6.0%). New top-10 names are health and distribution contractors (Humana, Cencora), not new weapons makers.

Places

OLDCC FY2024: Virginia $53.7B, California $42.9B, Texas $34.0B, Florida $25.2B, Pennsylvania $20.6B (sum $176.4B), per a search summary of its Table 3. Place of performance is as designated by the contractor, so it does not show where work is done. I did not obtain a national denominator, a prior-year comparison, or a district-level measure, so I cannot state a share or trend for places.

Caveats and data gaps

The warehouse award-level table (fiscal.usaspending_contracts) timed out on every query, and its state and district rollups cover all agencies, not only defense, so I used external sources: GovTribe FPDS-based rankings (net of deobligations, with GovTribe's own parent roll-up), the Quincy/Costs of War report (July 2025), and OLDCC. Classified contracts are not in these data. Earlier-era shares (2000s-2010s) were not found in a verifiable form.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Change over time: Only 3 sampled years (FY2021, 2023, 2025); no long-run series. The warehouse award-level table timed out and its state/district tables are all-agency, not defense-only.
