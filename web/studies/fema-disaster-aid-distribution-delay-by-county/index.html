Across all 2026 major disasters, Tennessee (DR-4898) and Mississippi (DR-4899) have the most counties with no obligated aid project, 295 days after declaration

FEMA OpenFEMA via AskAmerica disasters schema, loaded through 2026-11-27; window: major disasters (DR) declared 2025-11-28 to 2026-11-28, 50 states+DC

Summary

I ranked every major disaster declared in the last 12 months, not one disaster. The longest waits are in Tennessee (DR-4898), where 52 of 95 declared counties have no obligated Public Assistance project, and Mississippi (DR-4899), where 35 of 82 do not, 295 days after the 2026-02-06 declarations. Kansas DR-4897 (declared 2025-12-19, 344 days) still has 11 of 12 counties with none, including Wyandotte and Barton. Public Assistance pays governments and utilities, not residents. For residents, the household program shows a different picture, covered below.

Public Assistance (government/utility aid): longest waits

  • KS DR-4897, 344 days: 11 of 12 counties with no obligated project (Barton, Rawlins, Ottawa, Comanche, Wyandotte, Edwards, Stevens, Logan, Hodgeman, Morris, Saline).
  • TN DR-4898, 295 days: 52 of 95 counties, e.g. Warren, Washington, Polk, Lauderdale, Sullivan, Carter, Sevier, Blount, Bradley, Putnam, Cumberland.
  • MS DR-4899, 295 days: 35 of 82 (plus the Choctaw reservation), e.g. Perry, Jefferson Davis, Amite, Lamar, Forrest, Jones, Neshoba, Harrison, Jackson.
  • Montana DR-4902: 15 of 28 (235 days). Kentucky DR-4913: 10 of 21 (183 days).
  • Largest counts among the 88-day-old September declarations: Kansas DR-4940 (30 of 30) and DR-4943 (22 of 22), Texas DR-4935 (21 of 23).

Caveat: the table lists only obligated projects and has no obligation date. A county with none may be waiting, or may never have applied or had eligible work. Read it as a waiting signal, not proof.

Household aid (what reaches residents)

In the loaded IHP data, the largest 2026 programs are TN DR-4898 (70,467 registrations, 37,486 awarded, 53%), MS DR-4899 (86,013 registered, 49,142 awarded, 57%) and IN DR-4933 (60,785 registered, 34,492 awarded, 57%). Every IA-declared county in those disasters has registrations, so none is wholly unpaid; roughly 43 to 47 percent of registrants had no IHP award. A low share mixes delay with ineligibility and insurance, and there are no payment dates, so this shows how far behind, not days waited.

Data gap: Texas DR-4935 has 20 IA-declared counties but no IHP rows loaded. That is a data gap, not evidence those residents are waiting. OpenFEMA's IndividualsAndHouseholdsProgramValidRegistrations can be queried by disasterNumber. I did not fetch it.

Method, exclusions and limits

One row per disaster and county from disaster_declarations (DR only, states only), left-joined to public_assistance_projects and ihp_valid_registrations_by_county. The window is the trailing 12 months. Days are counted from the earliest declaration date to 2026-11-28. Fire-management and emergency declarations are not in scope. Newer declarations have had less time, so comparison across disasters is confounded by age.

Excluded rows: the filter county_fips IS NOT NULL (and not ending 000) left out 42 declaration rows across 13 disasters that are statewide or tribal entries with no county code. Without the filter they would add no county to the rankings; the TN, MS and KS headline counts are unchanged because those are county-level counts, and the excluded rows are not counties. Territories (state code 60 and above) were also left out.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Per-county days waited for household aid: No IHP payment dates exist; shown as award share instead.
