Nine weeks after the July 2026 South Central Texas floods, residents in all 30 requested counties are still waiting for federal Individual Assistance to reach them

Incident began July 12, 2026. Status as of September 14, 2026, per FEMA and Gov. Abbott's office.

Summary

The most recent federally declared disaster with an active, unresolved aid gap is the South Central Texas floods of July 2026 (Guadalupe, Nueces and Frio rivers, incident beginning July 12, 2026, affecting Kerr, Uvalde, Zavala and dozens of surrounding counties west/northwest/south of San Antonio). As of today, September 14, 2026 — roughly nine weeks (64 days) after the flooding — no county has received FEMA Individual Assistance approval, the program that pays money directly to residents for temporary housing, home repairs, and uninsured property losses. President Trump approved a major disaster declaration on September 2, 2026 (about 7.4 weeks after Gov. Abbott's July 17 request), but that approval only activated Public Assistance — reimbursement to local governments, not aid to individuals — for 19 of the requested counties. Nine more counties (Atascosa, Bexar, Blanco, Brewster, Comal, Medina, Schleicher, Terrell, Wilson) are still waiting even on Public Assistance. The separate Individual Assistance request, covering 30 counties (Bandera, Bexar, Comal, Crockett, Dimmit, Duval, Edwards, Gillespie, Guadalupe, Grimes, Jim Wells, Kendall, Kerr, Kimble, Kinney, La Salle, Live Oak, Maverick, McMullen, Medina, Pecos, Reagan, Real, Sutton, Travis, Uvalde, Val Verde, Webb, Wilson, Zavala), remains entirely undecided. Kerr County — recovering from a second major flood in two years after 2025's Hill Country disaster that killed 119 people there — and Uvalde County, with roughly 800+ damaged homes between them, are the two most-cited counties still waiting for that direct aid to reach residents.

Why this is the disaster to answer the question about

The question asks which counties, after a recent federally declared disaster, are still waiting longest for aid to reach residents specifically — i.e., Individual Assistance (IA), not Public Assistance (PA, which only reimburses government entities for infrastructure). The July 2026 Texas floods is the clearest current case of this exact gap: a major disaster declaration exists, PA money has started moving for most counties, and IA — the program residents actually feel — has been requested for 30 counties and approved for zero, more than two months after the flooding. Research by Andrew Rumbach (Urban Institute), cited by The Texas Tribune, found the current administration has taken an average of 9.5 weeks to reach a final decision on major disaster funding requests nationally in 2026, roughly three times the ~3-week average of prior administrations since 2000, and has denied more requests in 2026 (22, through Aug. 24) than in any year since at least 2000. That national pattern is playing out concretely in this case.

Data and methodology notes

AskAmerica's own disasters.disaster_declarations table (FEMA OpenFEMA feed) was queried directly for Texas declarations since July 2026; it returned zero rows, and the most recent Texas declaration loaded is dated 2026-05-18 — a genuine ETL/ingest lag, not evidence the declaration didn't happen. This is a real corpus gap for the most current events; the finding above is instead built from FEMA's own press releases and Texas state government announcements, cross-checked across multiple independent outlets (Texas Tribune, Houston Public Media/Texas Public Radio, KSAT, Click2Houston). FEMA's own fema.gov pages for this disaster (press releases and the /disaster/3649/designated-areas page) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch and could not be read as primary documents in this session; the figures here are therefore sourced from news organizations' direct reporting on FEMA's and the Governor's own announcements, not from a FEMA page parsed firsthand. That is disclosed rather than presented as a first-hand FEMA read.

No search found any report, as of September 14, 2026, that Individual Assistance has since been approved for any of the 30 requested counties — the most recent confirmed status (Sept 3–4 reporting) is that it remains pending in full.
