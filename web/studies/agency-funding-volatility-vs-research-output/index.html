Across 23 NIH institutes, budget volatility shows no detectable link to publications per dollar, but the data can only rule out large effects

NIH RePORTER awards FY2010-2025 (research.nih_award_projects) and linked papers (research.nih_publications), pub years 2022-2025

Answer

Within what the warehouse can measure, not much, and not detectably. Across 23 NIH institutes, a more volatile budget history is not associated with fewer publications per grant dollar: the coefficient is -0.33 log points per 1.00 of volatility (SE 2.56, p=0.90, n=92 institute-years, 23 clusters). Per one standard deviation of volatility (about 3 percentage points) that is roughly -1%, but the 95% interval is about +/-16%, so the test cannot exclude moderate effects. Year-by-year, an institute's own larger budget swing did not predict its publications per dollar either (-0.17 per unit of |YoY change|, SE 0.35, p=0.63).

Method

Budget = sum of award_amount per institute and fiscal year (OD excluded, since its budget mixes transfers; 2026 excluded as partial). Volatility = standard deviation of YoY % change, FY2013-2025. Output = distinct PMIDs by publication year linked to the institute's awards, per $M of that year's awards, pub years 2022-2025. Controls: log average budget size (larger institutes show lower pubs/$, p=0.05). Two-way fixed effects for the within-institute version.

Caveats that matter

  • Publication links exist only for grants active FY2022 onward, and RePORTER credits a renewed project's whole history to each year, so output is a portfolio-accumulation measure, not output produced that year. Pre-2022 and 2026 counts are artefacts and were not used.
  • Only 4 output years and 23 institutes: low power. Volatility itself is modest (3-12% SD); NIH never saw the large shocks (e.g. FY2026 drops of 20-30% at several institutes) in the output window, which is too recent for papers to show up.
  • Papers lag funding by years, so contemporaneous matching understates any effect.
  • NIH only; NSF and other agencies have no publication or patent linkage here. Institutes are not independent agencies.
  • I did not run leave-one-out sensitivity; FIC (12 pubs/$M, small budget) is an outlier.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Agencies beyond NIH (NSF, DOE etc.): Only NIH has award-to-publication links loaded; NSF publication linkage and NIH-patent linkage are not in the warehouse. Institutes stand in for agencies.
