The 2021-22 US port supply-chain crisis was severe (100+ ships anchored, 3-week waits) but has fully cleared as of late 2022

BTS container-throughput data plus Marine Exchange of Southern California and port-authority queue statistics

Summary

The 2021-22 US port supply-chain crisis was large by any measure: at its January 2022 peak, roughly 109 container ships were anchored or drifting offshore waiting for a berth at Los Angeles/Long Beach (about 150 across all North American ports), average wait times for a berth stretched to 17.6-28 days, and tens of thousands of import containers sat on terminals for 9+ days (about 65,000 at Long Beach alone in late November 2021). It has essentially fully cleared: the Marine Exchange of Southern California reports zero ship backup off LA/Long Beach since November 22, 2022, and both ports report aged-container counts down 90-96% from their late-2021 peaks. Throughput itself never collapsed — container volume kept rising through the crisis — confirming the bottleneck was processing velocity (labor, chassis, warehouse space, rail drayage), not a drop in freight demand.

How big the crisis was

  • Vessel queue: from an average of roughly 30 ships waiting at anchor for much of mid-2021, the queue built to a record of 60-80 vessels by August 2021 and peaked near 109 off LA/Long Beach (about 150 nationally) on January 9, 2022.
  • Wait times: average time at anchor for a berth reached 17.6-28 days in January 2022, versus essentially no meaningful wait pre-pandemic.
  • Container backlog: Port of Long Beach had about 65,000 loaded import containers sitting on its terminals (any dwell time) in late November 2021, with the 9+ day aged-container count at its highest point of the crisis.
  • Throughput kept climbing, not falling: combined LA + Long Beach monthly TEU volume (BTS Port Performance data, the only currently loaded government series with this detail) rose from about 1.4 million TEU/month pre-pandemic to a peak around 1.9-1.86 million TEU/month in mid-2021/2022 — the crisis was demand outrunning capacity to move it, not a volume shortfall.

How much has cleared

  • Vessel queue: fully cleared. Kip Louttit, executive director of the Marine Exchange of Southern California, has stated there has been no backup off Los Angeles/Long Beach since November 22, 2022; any late arrivals since then are by carrier choice, not port-capacity constraint.
  • Aged containers: down 90-96%. The Port of Long Beach's count of loaded import containers dwelling 9+ days is down 96% from the late-2021 peak; the Port of Los Angeles's equivalent count is down 90% from its own late-October-2021 peak.
  • Volume normalized at a higher plateau, not distressed: the Port of Los Angeles processed 9.4 million TEUs in 2024, 19% above 2023 and 7% above its five-year average — i.e., cargo volumes stayed strong even as the operational backlog disappeared.
  • Caveat — global picture is more mixed in 2026: Red Sea rerouting and record global throughput have created renewed, though smaller and more geographically scattered, congestion at some non-US ports and occasional US terminal-level dwell pressure; this is a different, milder phenomenon than the 2021-22 US-specific crisis, and current public commentary (MARAD's weekly AIS-based anchored-vessel count, terminal operator updates) describes most major US gateways — Oakland, Tacoma, Seattle, LA/Long Beach — as running with no meaningful vessel queue as of September 2026.

Data and methodology notes

The AskAmerica/govdata corpus carries no table for vessel queue counts, anchorage wait times, or container dwell/aging directly (search_catalog returned no match for 'congestion' or 'waiting' against any table). The one government series in this corpus with port-level operational detail, transport.bts_port_teu (BTS Port Performance Freight Statistics Program), only carries monthly TEU throughput, not queue or dwell metrics, and that source itself stopped publishing after October 2022 and has not been refreshed since (confirmed live). It was used here to establish that the crisis was not a volume/demand event. All ship-count, wait-time, and container-aging figures came from public port-authority and Marine Exchange statistics via web research, since no queryable government dataset in this corpus covers them — that is a genuine coverage gap, not a proxy substitution.

Every query behind this report

2 warehouse calls ran in this session, in order. Each is reproducible against the same snapshot.

query — 92 rows — 2125 ms
SELECT report_date, port_name, teu FROM transport.bts_port_teu WHERE port_name ILIKE '%los angeles%' OR port_name ILIKE '%long beach%' ORDER BY report_date
query — 46 rows — 758 ms
SELECT report_date, SUM(teu) as combined_teu FROM transport.bts_port_teu WHERE port_name ILIKE '%los angeles%' OR port_name ILIKE '%long beach%' GROUP BY report_date ORDER BY report_date
