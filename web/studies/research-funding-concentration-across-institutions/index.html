Federal research funding is extremely concentrated among a small set of universities, and that concentration has been remarkably stable for 15 years

NSF NCSES Higher Education R&D (HERD) Survey and Survey of Federal S&E Support, FY2010-2024

Summary

Federal research funding at U.S. universities is highly concentrated, and that concentration has barely moved in 15 years. In FY2024, the top 10 recipient institutions took in 20.8% of all federally funded university R&D expenditure (NSF NCSES HERD survey), and the top 100 institutions (out of 674 reporting) took in 81.2%. A Gini coefficient computed over all reporting institutions is 0.82 in FY2024, essentially unchanged from 0.83 in FY2010 — an 'extreme' concentration level by conventional bands, and flat over the period. A companion NCSES obligations-based series (a related but distinct federal S&E-support measure) tells the same story with a slightly different number: the top-10 share fell modestly from 22.7% (FY2014) to 20.1% (FY2023). Johns Hopkins University has been the single largest recipient throughout the window, and by FY2024 received roughly 3x the next-largest institution (Georgia Tech), driven substantially by its Applied Physics Laboratory, a DOD-funded University Affiliated Research Center.

What 'concentration' means here, and the two measures used

Two federal sources measure a closely related but not identical quantity:

  • NSF NCSES Higher Education R&D (HERD) Survey — R&D expenditures at degree-granting institutions, broken out by funding source. This is the primary series used here (research.nsf_herd_by_institution in AskAmerica), because it is the only one loaded in the connector and it lets us compute the full distribution (Gini, top-N shares) across all ~630-740 reporting institutions per year, not just a top-10/top-25 published list.
  • NCSES Survey of Federal S&E Support to Universities / Federal Funds for R&D — federal obligations for science & engineering support (a superset of R&D, including some non-R&D S&E activity), the basis of NCSES's own December 2025/February 2026 'Trends in Rankings' InfoBytes report. This is a primary-source web fetch (pdf), not an AskAmerica table, and is reported for cross-validation.

The two series disagree slightly on level (HERD's federal-R&D top-10 share of ~21% vs. the S&E-support series' ~20-23%) because they measure different money (R&D expenditure vs. broader S&E obligations) and different accounting bases (expenditure vs. obligation), but they agree on the qualitative finding: concentration in the low-20s percent for the top 10, and roughly stable to modestly declining over the past decade.

Gini coefficient (computed over every reporting institution's federal R&D dollars) moved from 0.825 (2010) to 0.813 (2017) to 0.821 (2020) to 0.823 (2024) — essentially flat within a narrow 0.81-0.83 band across the whole window, all falling in the 'extreme concentration' range. The total federal R&D pool nearly doubled over the period (from ~$37.5B to ~$64.6B, current dollars, not inflation-adjusted), and the number of reporting institutions was also roughly stable (629-737), so the flat concentration measures are not an artifact of a shrinking or expanding population — new dollars have been distributed across the existing hierarchy in roughly the same proportions as old dollars, not disproportionately captured by new entrants or by the very largest incumbents.

The externally-published corroborating series (NCSES, Feb 2026)

NCSES's own February 2026 InfoBytes report ('Federal Funding for Science and Engineering: Trends in Rankings Among Top Universities, FYs 2014-23', NSF 26-310), fetched directly from ncses.nsf.gov, reports federal S&E-support obligations (a related but broader base than HERD's R&D expenditure): in FY2023, the top 10 recipient institutions received $9.9B of $49.0B total obligated to 1,110 institutions — 20.1%. In FY2014 the top 10 received $7.2B of $31.2B — 22.7%. The top 100 institutions received 78.4% of the FY2023 total. This report also documents COVID-era volatility in the rankings (NYU jumped from 25th in FY2020 to 2nd in FY2021 on $877M of HHS/NIH pandemic-response obligations) that is smoothed out in HERD's fiscal-year expenditure accounting but shows up as the small mid-window bump in the HERD top-10 series above (peaking FY2016-2020 before easing).

Caveats

  • HERD figures are R&D expenditure, not obligations, and cover roughly 925 degree-granting institutions expending at least $150,000 in R&D — a narrower population than all federal S&E recipients (colleges, non-degree-granting institutions, and non-university performers like FFRDCs and DOD's own labs are out of scope for both series used here).
  • Figures are current (nominal) dollars, not inflation-adjusted; the roughly 72% nominal growth in the federal R&D pool from 2010 to 2024 includes real growth plus price inflation.
  • FY2020 obligations data is known by NCSES to understate DOD S&E support to universities by an unknown amount; this affects the obligations-based series more than the HERD expenditure series but is worth noting for any year-over-year read around 2020-2021.
  • Johns Hopkins' outsized share is substantially attributable to its Applied Physics Laboratory, a University Affiliated Research Center that functions more like a DOD-funded lab housed at a university than typical campus research — a reader comparing 'universities' should be aware this single line item is doing a lot of the concentration at the very top.
