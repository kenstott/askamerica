Federal university R&D is moderately concentrated: top 25 schools take about 40%, and that share rose only about 3 points from 2008 to 2024

NSF NCSES HERD survey, federal-funded R&D expenditures by institution, 2008-2024, nominal USD

Summary

In FY2024 the top 10 universities performed 20.8% of the $64.6B of federally funded academic R&D, the top 25 performed 40.5%, and the top 100 performed 81.2%, out of 674 reporting institutions. In 2008 the shares were 19.4%, 37.5% and 79.6% (685 institutions). Concentration rose mainly 2008-2016, then plateaued. HHI (hhi_concentration tool, 2024, n=674) is 111 on a 0-10,000 scale, 'unconcentrated' by DOJ/FTC bands (98 in 2008 by my SQL). No single school dominates, but Johns Hopkins alone holds 5.6% (4.7% in 2008).

Findings

  • Top-10 share: 19.4% (2008), 21.2% (2016-17 peak), 20.8% (2024).
  • Top-25 share: 37.5% to 40.5%. Top-100: 79.6% to 81.2%.
  • HHI: 98 (2008), peak 115 (2020), 111 (2024).
  • Total federal academic R&D doubled, $31.3B to $64.6B nominal. By agency: HHS $17.5B to $35.5B, DOD $3.1B to $10.2B, NSF $3.8B to $7.2B. DOD growth largely lifted Johns Hopkins (APL), $1.45B to $3.62B, and Georgia Tech into the top 2.
  • Top 10 in 2024: Johns Hopkins, Georgia Tech, Michigan, UC San Diego, Washington, Columbia, Duke, UCSF, Penn, Stanford. Below Hopkins the ranks are bunched at 1.6-1.9% each.

Caveats

Rollup note: a diagnostic flagged the federal_agency column, where 'Total' sits beside the individual agencies. The concentration queries filtered to federal_agency='Total' only. In the agency-comparison query I selected 'Total' as its own row next to the agency rows, so the 2008/2024 agency figures are read separately and not summed. Measure is expenditures (HERD), not awards; dollars are nominal; shares are unaffected by inflation. Institution count varies by year (2011 shows 896 rows, likely campus reporting differences), so top-N shares are more reliable than HHI over time. Johns Hopkins includes APL. Not tested: Gini, NIH or NSF award-level concentration, or post-2024 funding disruptions. Descriptive only, not causal.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Award-level (NIH/NSF) concentration: Used HERD expenditures only; award tables not analyzed.
