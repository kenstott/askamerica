In gas, speculative positioning moves before and alongside inventory surprises; in crude it shows no reliable timing relationship

CFTC Disaggregated COT (managed money, WTI 067651 and NYMEX nat gas 023651) vs EIA weekly stocks, 2010-2026, n about 870 weeks

Answer

It depends on the commodity. In natural gas, weekly changes in managed-money net positioning (share of open interest) line up with the storage surprise of the same week (r=-0.19) and the next two weeks (r=-0.19, -0.15), but not with surprises already published. Positioning moves before or with the physical change, never after it. In crude oil, no lag from -4 to +4 weeks is clearly distinguishable from zero (all |r| at most 0.07, about 2 standard errors).

Method and results

Supply signal: weekly change in U.S. working gas (Lower 48, Bcf) or U.S. crude stocks ex-SPR, minus the same-week-of-year average change. Positioning: weekly change in (managed-money long minus short)/open interest. COT Tuesday matched to the EIA Friday week-ending three days later. Lagged correlations k=-4..+4 (k>0: positioning at t vs supply at t+k), then an HC1-robust regression on surprises at t-2..t+2 and own lag (n=843).

k-2-10+1+2+3
Crude r-0.068-0.012-0.063-0.070-0.0460.001
Gas r-0.010-0.082-0.188-0.187-0.151-0.002

2-SE band about 0.068. Gas regression: surprise at t p=0.002, t+1 p=0.016, t+2 p=0.001; t-1 p=0.97, t-2 p=0.26; own lag 0.21 (p<0.001); R-squared 0.10. Crude: no surprise term below p=0.05; own lag 0.15; R-squared 0.03.

Caveats

  • Likely common driver (weather) rather than speculators causing storage changes; the sign is bullish positioning before smaller-than-normal injections.
  • EIA stocks for a week ending Friday publish the next Wednesday, after the Tuesday COT snapshot, so the lead is not a reporting artifact.
  • Small explanatory power; not a trading signal. Production (monthly), prices, Brent and products not tested. Seasonal mean uses the full sample; data are current revised values.

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Production (monthly) as the supply measure: EIA production is monthly and state-level, too coarse to time against weekly COT; weekly inventory changes used instead.
  • Causality: Only timing/predictive association shown; weather is a likely common driver for gas.
