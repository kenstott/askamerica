Speculative positioning in energy futures tracks fundamentals weakly and shows no reliable lead or lag, in either crude oil or natural gas

CFTC COT managed-money net positions vs EIA production and inventories, monthly grain, Jan 2019-Dec 2024

Summary

Once positioning and fundamentals are aligned to a common monthly grain and tested in first differences (the stationarity-appropriate test), speculative positioning in crude oil and natural gas futures shows only weak, statistically fragile correlation with contemporaneous or lagged/leading physical production and inventory changes — nowhere close to the strong relationship a naive level-on-level correlation implies. Across 7 lags (−3 to +3 months) and 4 commodity×fundamental pairs (28 tests), the single largest coefficient was r=−0.244 (n=69, crude oil managed-money net position vs. a commercial crude stock build three months later) — right at the nominal p≈0.05 threshold for this sample size, and exactly the kind of result multiple testing across 28 comparisons would produce by chance alone (≈1.4 expected false positives at α=0.05). Natural gas showed essentially no relationship at any lag (|r| ≤ 0.16 throughout). Crude oil positioning is modestly more inventory-sensitive than natural gas positioning, but neither market shows a coefficient large or lag-consistent enough to describe positioning as reliably leading, lagging, or moving in lockstep with the physical market. This is consistent with the peer-reviewed literature (Kilian & Murphy 2014; Byun 2017), which finds the crude oil inventory-price relationship stable through the post-2004 financialization era and rules out destabilizing speculation as a primary driver of oil market fundamentals.

Step 1 — Trader positioning, energy contracts in scope

Scope: NYMEX WTI crude oil (CFTC contract code 067651) and NYMEX Henry Hub natural gas (023651), CFTC Disaggregated Commitments of Traders (Futures-Only) report, managed-money net position (long minus short contracts), aggregated from weekly Tuesday reports to a monthly average to match the coarsest fundamentals series. Window: January 2019 – August 2026 fetched; the correlation analysis below uses Jan 2019 – Dec 2024, the window where EIA crude production data is also available (see Step 2 caveat).

Net managed-money length swung from roughly +410,000 contracts in WTI crude (early 2021, post-COVID demand recovery) down to roughly −19,000 (Sept 2025, net short) — a decisive multi-year reversal. Natural gas net positioning ranged from about +200,000 (mid-2021) to about −170,000 (2024-2025 supply glut), reflecting the market's much larger seasonal swing between winter-demand length and shoulder-season short positioning. Source: CFTC Commitments of Traders public reporting (Socrata dataset 72hh-3qpy), an external source the askamerica cftc schema does not carry — that schema holds DTCC OTC swap transaction data, not exchange futures positioning; this is a genuine coverage gap in the corpus (see Data quality notes).

Step 2 — Physical production and inventory, alignment grain

Alignment method: the CFTC COT report publishes weekly (Tuesday snapshots); EIA petroleum stocks and natural gas storage publish weekly; EIA crude oil production publishes monthly; a comparable EIA natural gas production series is not loaded in this corpus at all (data quality gap, reported). Weekly positioning against monthly production is exactly the grain mismatch the task warns about, so every series was aggregated to monthly before any correlation was computed: weekly COT reports were averaged within each calendar month, and weekly EIA stock/storage levels were averaged within each calendar month. First differences (month-over-month change) were then taken on the monthly fundamentals series to test the stationarity-appropriate relationship, since both positioning and inventory/production levels trend for multi-year stretches (a known source of spurious level correlation, Granger & Newbold 1974).

  • Crude production: EIA series MCRFPUS2 (U.S. field production of crude oil, kbbl/day), askamerica energy.eia_fossil_fuel_production. Data quality finding: this table's declared national series contained exact duplicate rows for parts of 2023, 2025, and Jan–Mar 2026 that doubled a naive SUM() — reported via report_issue; all production figures here use SELECT DISTINCT before aggregating. Production rose from ~11.9M bbl/d (Jan 2019) to ~13.4M bbl/d (Dec 2024).
  • Crude inventory: EIA series WCESTUS1 (U.S. ending stocks of crude oil excluding SPR, kbbl), askamerica energy.eia_petroleum_stocks. Ranged roughly 412M–538M bbl over the window, with the largest single move the COVID-era build (Jan-Jun 2020, +107M bbl).
  • Natural gas inventory: EIA series for Lower-48 working gas storage (region R48, storage_type SWO), askamerica energy.eia_natural_gas_storage, Bcf, strongly seasonal (roughly 900 Bcf spring trough to 4,000 Bcf fall peak each year — the seasonal swing dwarfs any speculation-driven component, which is exactly why the first-differenced test below controls for it by looking at month-over-month change rather than level).
  • Natural gas production: not available in the askamerica corpus — the energy.eia_fossil_fuel_production table's description claims natural gas rows are unioned in, but only fuel_type='Crude Oil' is actually loaded (reported as a second data quality finding). Substituted EIA's public dnav series n9070us2 (U.S. dry natural gas production, MMcf/month), fetched directly from eia.gov and flagged here as an external, non-corpus source per the workflow's external-source rules.

For reference, a two-tailed test at n≈70 requires |r| ≥ ~0.235 to clear p<0.05 for a single comparison — only crude-vs-stocks at lag +3 (r=−0.244) crosses that line, and with 28 total comparisons run here, roughly 1.4 such crossings are expected from chance alone even if the true relationship is zero everywhere. As a cross-check, the raw LEVEL correlation between crude net positioning and crude stock levels is r=0.43 to 0.61 across the same lags — far larger than any differenced result — which is the signature of two independently trending series (both drifted with the multi-year 2019-2024 price/production cycle) rather than a genuine causal or informational relationship; reporting that level correlation as the answer would have manufactured a strong finding purely from the grain/detrending choice, exactly as the task warned.

Step 4 — What this does and does not support causally

Does not support: a claim that speculative positioning causes, anticipates, or drives physical production or inventory decisions in either market, or the reverse. Lead-lag correlation in observational monthly time series is not evidence of causation in either direction — it is consistent with a common third factor (price, weather, OPEC+ decisions, refinery maintenance schedules) driving both series independently, with no requirement that either one informs the other.

Main confound: price is the obvious omitted common cause — both managed-money positioning and physical decisions (production ramp/curtailment timing, discretionary inventory draws) respond to the same price signal, which was not held constant in this bivariate test. A partial-correlation or multivariate design netting out price (and, for natural gas, heating/cooling degree days) would be needed before attributing any of the small residual correlation to positioning-fundamentals feedback specifically rather than both reacting to price in parallel.

Does support: a description of the observed co-movement pattern — weak and lag-inconsistent for both commodities in first differences, moderately positive at a 3-month lag between crude positioning and a following inventory draw (but not significant net of multiple-comparisons correction), and negligible for natural gas at every lag tested. This is directionally consistent with peer-reviewed findings (Kilian & Murphy 2014; Byun 2017) that the crude oil price-inventory relationship remained fundamentals-driven and stable through the post-2004 period of heavy financial-investor participation, i.e. speculative positioning is not a strong independent driver of, or strongly driven by, physical crude fundamentals at monthly grain. It also matches practitioner commentary that crude oil managed-money positioning is largely momentum/trend-following on price rather than fundamentals-anticipatory, while natural gas positioning is dominated by weather-driven seasonal storage dynamics that a simple linear monthly correlation does not capture well.

Data quality notes (reported to askamerica via report_issue)

  • energy.eia_fossil_fuel_production contains exact duplicate national crude-production rows for parts of 2023, 2025, and Jan–Mar 2026, which silently double a naive SUM(); the declared primary key does not include the partition metadata columns that actually differ between the duplicates.
  • energy.eia_fossil_fuel_production's fuel_type column only ever contains 'Crude Oil' despite the table description stating natural gas production is unioned in from a separate EIA endpoint; natural gas production is entirely absent from the corpus and had to be sourced externally (EIA dnav n9070us2, not an askamerica-versioned or DQ-checked source).
  • The askamerica cftc schema does not cover CFTC Commitments of Traders (exchange futures positioning) at all — it holds DTCC OTC swap-transaction data. All positioning data in this report is from the external CFTC Socrata endpoint (72hh-3qpy), not the askamerica corpus.
