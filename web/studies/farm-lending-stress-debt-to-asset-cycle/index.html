Farm sector debt-to-asset ratio has ticked up modestly since 2023, with stress concentrated in highly leveraged row-crop operations in the Corn Belt and northern Plains

USDA ERS Farm Sector Income & Wealth Statistics (national balance sheet), 2010-2026F; Kansas City Fed Ag Credit Survey and University of Illinois farmdoc daily for regional/crop detail

Summary

The U.S. farm sector's debt-to-asset ratio — the standard national gauge of leverage — has risen only modestly in the last few years: from a recent low of 12.94% in 2023 to about 13.3-13.4% in 2024-2025 (USDA ERS forecast), still well below the 2020 pandemic-era peak of 14.10% and far below the ~22% levels of the 1980s farm crisis. In dollar terms, sector-wide farm debt is projected at roughly $592 billion in 2025 (+5.0% year over year) against roughly $4.42 trillion in assets (+4.7%), so debt and asset values have been rising at nearly the same pace and the aggregate solvency picture looks stable, not stressed. That national number, however, hides real concentration: financial stress is falling disproportionately on the most highly leveraged row-crop (grain) operations, and is most pronounced in crop-heavy regions of the Corn Belt and northern/central Plains (the Minneapolis and St. Louis Federal Reserve districts in particular), while cattle and other livestock producers have been comparatively insulated by strong livestock prices.

So the ratio actually fell from the 2020 pandemic-era peak (14.10%) through 2023 (12.94%, aided by record 2021-22 farm incomes and rising land values), then has ticked back up about 0.4 percentage points since 2023 as farm income has softened, interest rates have stayed elevated, and producers have leaned more on operating debt. ERS's own 2026 first-look forecast (reported by Purdue's Commercial AG update) shows debt growth accelerating again into 2026 — the third acceleration in the last four years. Independently, University of Illinois farmdoc daily (Sept. 2025), citing the same ERS release, reports debt-to-asset at 13.36% for 2024 and 13.40% for 2025 — matching this corpus's figures to within rounding/forecast-vintage differences, which is a good cross-check on the ERS series as loaded here. Over the full 2003-2025 span the ratio has stayed inside a narrow 11.28%-14.1% band, so by the national aggregate alone this is a modest re-leveraging, not a solvency crisis.

2. Is that stress concentrated in particular crops? Yes &mdash; row crops / grain, and specifically the most leveraged quartile

ag.ers_farm_income carries the debt-to-asset ratio only at the national (US) grain. To confirm this before relying on outside sources, we queried for any non-US rows on that same series (SELECT DISTINCT state FROM ag.ers_farm_income WHERE variable_description_total='Debt to asset ratio, excl. operator dwellings' AND state <> 'US', and the equivalent for the underlying debt/asset dollar levels): both returned zero rows. The only geography this table's solvency series actually holds is state='US' — no state, region, or crop breakdown exists for debt, assets, or the debt-to-asset ratio anywhere in this table, so nothing was dropped by choice; the state/crop cut the question asks for simply is not published at this grain by USDA ERS to begin with. That is a genuine coverage gap in this corpus, not a filtering decision, and it is why the crop/region findings below come from Federal Reserve district surveys and University of Illinois farm-level data instead of a table query.

University of Illinois farmdoc daily's farm-level Illinois grain-farm data (Farm Business Farm Management, FBFM, grain operations with $40,000+ gross revenue) shows the aggregate stability masks a widening split by leverage tier. From 2022 to 2024:

  • The upper-quartile (most leveraged 25%) of grain farms saw their debt-to-asset ratio rise from 31.0% to 34.1% (moving further into the "cautionary" 30-60% band) and their interest expense per tillable acre roughly double, from $37.08 to $59.86, as the federal funds rate rose from near zero to 5.25-5.50%.
  • The median grain farm moved only slightly, from 17.5% to 18.2% (still "strong," below 30%).
  • The lower-quartile (least leveraged) farms stayed essentially flat at ~6.3-6.4%.

In other words, the stress is not evenly spread across grain producers — it is concentrated in the minority of already-highly-indebted row-crop operations, for whom debt-servicing costs have surged even though their balance-sheet leverage ratio remains in the "cautionary," not "vulnerable," range. Kansas City Fed district reporting corroborates this at a broader geographic scale: "conditions have been particularly challenging for the most highly leveraged crop operations," while "strength in cattle and other livestock industries provided support to finances in some areas" — i.e., livestock producers have been comparatively insulated relative to row-crop (corn/soybean/grain) operations.

3. Is that stress concentrated in particular regions? Yes &mdash; crop-heavy Federal Reserve districts, especially Minneapolis and St. Louis

The Federal Reserve's regional agricultural credit surveys (Kansas City, Chicago, Minneapolis, St. Louis Fed districts) are the standard source for sub-national farm financial stress, since USDA/ERS balance-sheet data in this corpus is US-total only (see Section 2 above for the confirmation query). Per Kansas City Fed reporting (2025): "Farm income deteriorated most in areas more heavily concentrated in crop production... around 30% of respondents in the Chicago and Kansas City Districts reported that repayment rates were lower than a year ago, while that share was around 40% in the Minneapolis District and 50% in the St. Louis District." Separately, growth in farm debt at commercial agricultural banks stayed elevated into 2025, with loan delinquency rates rising modestly, and total farm debt up more than 8% year-over-year among banks with farm-loan concentrations above 300% of capital — i.e., the banks most exposed to agriculture. Nationally, Chapter 12 farm bankruptcy filings rose 55% in 2024 (216 filings) versus 2023, though that remains below the 595 filings seen in 2019 during the prior trade-war-driven downturn.

Putting it together: the Minneapolis Fed district (Minnesota, Montana, North/South Dakota, northwestern Wisconsin, Upper Michigan — heavy spring wheat, corn, and soybean country) and the St. Louis Fed district (Missouri/southern Illinois/Indiana/Kentucky/Tennessee/Arkansas/Mississippi row-crop belt) are showing the weakest repayment trends, consistent with these being disproportionately row-crop regions rather than livestock- or specialty-crop-heavy areas. The Kansas City and Chicago districts (which blend more cattle/livestock with crops) are showing comparatively milder deterioration.

Methodology and caveats

What was computed directly from this corpus: the 2010-2026 national debt-to-asset ratio series and the underlying farm-debt and farm-asset dollar levels, queried from ag.ers_farm_income (USDA ERS Farm Sector Income & Wealth Statistics, excl. operator dwellings), filtered to state='US' and the "Debt to asset ratio, excl. operator dwellings" / debt and asset value series. A follow-up query filtering state <> 'US' on the same series confirmed zero rows exist outside the national total — this table simply does not carry a state/crop breakdown of the solvency series, so no state-level or crop-level rows were excluded by choice from the ERS-sourced part of this analysis; that entire cut is absent from the source data itself.

What was added from outside research to answer the crop/region clause: University of Illinois farmdoc daily's analysis of Illinois FBFM grain-farm quartile data (debt-to-asset by leverage tier, 2003-2024), and Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City Ag Credit Survey reporting on district-level repayment-rate deterioration and bank-level farm-debt/delinquency trends. These are farm-level survey data (Illinois only, as one representative Corn Belt state) and regional bank-survey data respectively — not a full 50-state or all-commodity census, so treat the regional/crop findings as representative rather than exhaustive; a true national crop-by-crop, state-by-state debt-to-asset breakdown does not appear to be published by any single source at that grain.

2025 and 2026 ERS figures are forecast vintages (ERS publishes about 1 year behind final); the 13.34%/13.40% and 13.54% figures for 2025/2026 in this corpus and in farmdoc's citation of the same ERS release differ by a few hundredths of a point, consistent with different forecast-revision dates rather than any data discrepancy.
