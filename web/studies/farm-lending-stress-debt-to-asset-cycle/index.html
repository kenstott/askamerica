Farm debt-to-asset is back near 13.4% after a 2020 peak, but crop margins are deeply negative and interest burden has risen in the Northeast, Mountain and Corn Belt hot spots

USDA ERS Farm Income and Wealth Statistics (Feb 2026 release; 2026 = forecast) and Commodity Costs and Returns

Summary

National farm debt-to-asset (ERS) rose from 11.3% (2012) to a 14.1% peak in 2020, fell to 12.9% in 2023 as land values jumped, and has risen again to 13.4% (2024-25) and 13.5% (2026 forecast). Debt is $579B in 2025 against $4.34T of assets: debt up about 22% since 2021 ($474B to $579B), assets up about 27%. Leverage is moderate; the pressure is liquidity: current debt share rose from 19% (2021) to 26% (2025) and times-interest-earned fell from 8.7 to about 5-6.

Stress is better seen in income than the balance sheet, and there are no state or crop balance sheets. Using interest expense as a share of net cash income by state (a proxy): Northeast rose 11.8% to 16.8% (2019 to 2025), Mountain 17.1% to 18.7%; Lake States and Southern Plains fell (2019 had weak income). Corn Belt and N. Plains slightly lower overall but IL (18% to 33%) and ND (20% to 27%) sharply worse. State change correlates with crop share of production value at r=0.41 (n=48; level r=0.27), weak evidence only.

Crops: per-acre net returns after all costs are negative in 2025 for all 9 crops: corn -$118, soybeans -$98, wheat -$128, cotton -$201, rice -$277 (from +$196 in 2022), barley -$130. Row-crop, rice and cotton economics are the weak spot, while 2022 was strongly positive for corn, soy and rice.

Exclusions

The US national total row (state='US') was excluded from the state panel because it is the sum of the states; Alaska and Hawaii were dropped as tiny, and states with non-positive net cash income in 2019 or 2025 were also left out, leaving n=48 (DC is not in the ERS state rows). The headline national ratio uses the US row and no exclusions. Regions are my own grouping of states into ERS-style farm production regions.

Caveats: ratio is sector-wide; burden is a proxy; government payments prop up net cash income; small states (MA, NH) are volatile.
