Farm debt-to-asset ratio has barely moved (13.8% in 2019, 13.3% in 2025); the stress is in crop returns, not the balance sheet

USDA ERS Farm Income and Wealth Statistics (national balance sheet), ERS Costs and Returns, NASS land values; warehouse snapshot checked 2026-11-11

Summary

Farm debt relative to assets is roughly flat. The national debt-to-asset ratio was 13.79% in 2019, peaked at 14.10% in 2020, fell to 12.94% in 2023, and was 13.34% in 2025 (ERS forecast 13.54% for 2026). Debt rose 38% from 2019 to 2025 ($420B to $579B), but assets rose 42% ($3.05T to $4.34T), mostly land values. The pressure is on the income side: ERS per-acre net returns turned negative for corn, soybeans, wheat, cotton and sorghum by 2024-25, and debt service ratios are back up (0.27 in 2024). Stress is broad across crops and regions, most severe for cotton and wheat; the Prairie Gateway soybean estimate is the one clear positive.

Limit: the warehouse carries debt and assets only for the US total. State-level farm debt is not loaded, so a regional debt-to-asset ranking is not computed here. Regional evidence is a proxy (returns and land values), not measured leverage.

Since the 2023 low the ratio rose about 0.4 to 0.6 points: debt grows faster than assets (2022 to 2026: debt +22%, assets +18%). Real-estate debt is up 42% since 2019. Nominal dollars. Data-quality note: the ERS subcategory column mixes 'All' rollup rows with lender-level parts; I selected only the single 'All' rollup rows (category Farm sector debt, Farm sector assets, Real estate), so nothing is double counted in these figures.

Exclusions

Two queries excluded units. A state query with state <> 'US' (excluding the national total) returned zero rows because state-level debt and asset rows do not exist in the table, so the national figures above are the only balance-sheet data; no headline changes with or without the exclusion. A regional query with region <> 'U.S. total' removed the national row so regions could be compared; the national row is reported separately in the crop table (corn 2025 -118 national versus regional range -185 to -12).

Costs include allocated overhead and land, so negative values do not mean cash insolvency, but equity erodes unless land values keep rising.

Regions (proxy)

By ERS region, 2025 corn net returns are negative everywhere (Northern Crescent -185, Heartland -124, Prairie Gateway -117, Eastern Uplands -115) except near zero in the Northern Great Plains (-12). Cotton is worst in the Heartland (-633) and Mississippi Portal (-388). Wheat is worst in the Fruitful Rim and Northern Great Plains (about -172). NASS land values 2020 to 2025 rose most in Kansas (+70%), Nebraska (+59%), Missouri (+46%), Iowa (+43%) and least in New Mexico, Nevada, Wyoming, Montana and Louisiana (about +19-22%). Plains and Corn Belt balance sheets were cushioned most by asset gains; cotton, rice and weak-appreciation areas have less buffer. This is inference, not a measured state leverage ratio.

Caveats

The sector ratio hides the distribution of highly leveraged operators. The last two years are ERS forecasts and revisable. Not computed: state debt-to-asset ratios; crop-specific debt (ERS does not publish it).

What This Report Does Not Answer

  • Concentration by region: State farm debt/assets not loaded; delivered regional returns and land value proxies instead
