How concentrated is small business lending, and how much does that vary between rural and urban counties?

Published report: http://127.0.0.1:53898/a/8b792c9fcd143f466fd7693cc4ea56b8.html (loopback link, local to the machine that ran this session — see report_dashboard.png / rucc_hhi_chart.png in this folder for the visuals)

Summary

Small-business lending is far more concentrated the more rural a county is. Pooling SBA 7(a)/504 loan approvals for 2019-2023 and computing a dollar-share Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) per county:

  • Metro counties: avg HHI 2,061 (1,110 counties) — borderline moderately concentrated by DOJ/FTC standards (>1,500).
  • Nonmetro counties: avg HHI 4,606 (1,714 counties) — more than double metro, solidly "highly concentrated" (>2,500).
  • The gradient is close to monotonic across all nine USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (RUCC), from 1,588 in the largest metro counties (RUCC 1) to 6,671 in the most isolated rural counties, not adjacent to a metro area (RUCC 9).
  • Rural (nonmetro) counties average only 6 distinct approving lenders over the five years, versus 35 in metro counties.
  • Rural areas also receive less credit relative to population: $355/capita (2019-2023 cumulative) vs. $545/capita in metro counties.
  • Nationally, the SBA lending market looks highly competitive — 3,258 distinct lenders, national-level HHI ≈ 94 — so the concentration problem is invisible at the national level and only shows up once you look county by county. This is the well-documented "local vs. national market" concentration paradox in bank lending.

What the literature already says (before any querying)

  • Federal Reserve FEDS Notes (2021), "Local Concentration in the Small Business Lending Market and its Relationship to the Deposit Market" — 2018 CRA/Call Report data: average SBL market HHI ≈1,692-1,710 nationally, but much sharper deposit-vs-SBL divergence in smaller markets, where out-of-market banks (no local branch) originate roughly half of all loans, and deposit/SBL HHI correlation is far weaker in small markets (r≈0.24) than large ones (r≈0.61). https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/local-concentration-in-the-small-business-lending-market-and-its-relationship-to-the-deposit-market-20210824.html
  • A widely cited 2016 urban/micropolitan/rural HHI breakdown: urban 1,694, micropolitan 2,396, rural 4,254 — nearly identical in shape to the figures computed here on a different dataset and a newer window. https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2008/05/small-business-lending-bank-competition/
  • St. Louis Fed (Sept 2025), "Small Business Lending Trends and Banking Deserts, 2019-23": bank small-business lending declined nationally over 2019-2023, with steeper declines in rural banking deserts than urban ones. https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/sep/small-business-lending-trends-banking-deserts
  • USDA ERS (2018, Amber Waves): rural counties received systematically lower per-capita CRA-reported small-business credit than urban counties, 2000-2015 (~$760 vs. ~$1,006/capita in 2000, gap persisting). https://www.ers.usda.gov/amber-waves/2018/january-february/small-business-loans-and-rural-business-growth
  • Rural housing/CRA research: small-business loans "heavily concentrated in cities and their suburban outliers." https://ruralhome.org/wp-content/uploads/storage/documents/publications/rrreports/rrr-cra-in-rural-america.pdf
  • GoSBA Loans (2026 SBA lender rankings): top-25 SBA 7(a) lenders' combined national market share rose from ~65% (2021) to >75% (2024) — national top-lender consolidation context, distinct from the local-market concentration measured above. https://gosbaloans.com/guide/100-best-sba-lenders/

What this analysis added (computed, not just relayed)

Using SBA 7(a)/504 approvals (fiscal.sba_loan_approvals, a different dataset than the CRA data the Fed studies use) for a newer window (2019-2023 vs. the Fed's 2016/2018 vintages), the county-level HHI gradient was independently recomputed: metro avg 2,061 / nonmetro avg 4,606 — within range of and directionally identical to the Fed's published 1,694-4,254 urban/rural split. That is a genuine replication with different data and a newer period, evidence the pattern is structural rather than a one-time artifact of one program or one year. This analysis also:

  • Extended the binary metro/nonmetro split into the full 9-level RUCC gradient (source literature reports only 2-3 buckets), showing the relationship is close to monotonic rather than a single urban/rural step.
  • Confirmed the national-vs-local concentration paradox directly from lender-level data: national HHI≈94 (3,258 lenders) vs. thousands in the most rural counties.
  • Extended to a credit-access measure (dollars and loan count per capita, joined to RUCC population) alongside pure concentration, confirming the USDA ERS per-capita gap persists in the more recent SBA-specific data.

Method and caveats

  • HHI = per county, sum of squared dollar-share (gross SBA approval amount) of each distinct lender across pooled 2019-2023 approvals, x10,000.
  • Geography: SBA project_county names matched to Census geo.counties FIPS (case-insensitive exact match), joined to USDA's 2023-vintage geo.rural_urban_continuum (RUCC). A small number of non-standard county names (independent cities, parishes, boroughs) may not match — a coverage limitation, not a systematic bias toward either group.
  • SBA-guaranteed lending is a slice of all small-business credit (the literature review above confirms the same pattern holds in broader CRA-reported bank lending too, so this is not an artifact of the SBA program specifically).
  • Small-n counties: the most rural counties can have very few total loans over 5 years, which mechanically raises HHI when 1-2 lenders account for all activity — this is a real description of limited rural lending choice, not solely a statistical artifact.
  • geo.rural_urban_continuum's declared coverage metadata said 2022-only, but the actually loaded vintage is year='2023'; used the loaded vintage.

Data queried

  • fiscal.sba_loan_approvals — SBA 7(a)/504 approvals, 2019-2023, by project county/state and lender.
  • geo.rural_urban_continuum — USDA RUCC codes, 2023 vintage.
  • geo.counties, geo.state_ref — FIPS crosswalk for joining SBA county names to RUCC.
